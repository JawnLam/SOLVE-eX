# Skin in the Game

**One-line summary:** Trust signals where the signal-sender bears asymmetric downside — verbal-only commitments without consequence to the source are weaker evidence.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating advice, predictions, recommendations, and partnerships; designing incentive structures; choosing who to trust on a high-stakes call.

## Purpose

Information about a person's beliefs is weak when there's no cost to being wrong. People who put real capital, reputation, time, or wellbeing on the line filter their statements more carefully, integrate downside considerations, and provide more reliable signal. The discipline applies both to others (whose advice to weight) and to oneself (whose plans to commit publicly to).

## How To Use

1) When weighing advice or a forecast, ask: what does the source lose if wrong? 2) Heavily discount predictions from forecasters with no exposure to error. 3) When designing institutions, build in personal stakes for decision-makers. 4) When making a high-confidence prediction, expose yourself to downside if wrong — public commitment, bet, contractual obligation.

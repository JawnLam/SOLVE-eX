# Visuddhimagga Seven Purifications

**One-line summary:** Buddhaghosa's developmental scaffold for the Theravada path — seven sequential purifications from virtue through knowledge-and-vision.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging long-arc Theravada practice and seeking a structured developmental map of where one is on the path and what comes next, rather than relying on intuition about progress.

## Purpose

The *Visuddhimagga* ('Path of Purification') by Buddhaghosa (~5th c. CE) is the most influential Theravada systematization. It lays out the path as seven sequential purifications: (1) sila — virtue/ethical conduct; (2) citta — consciousness, through concentration; (3) ditthi — view, correct understanding; (4) kankha-vitarana — overcoming doubt; (5) maggamagga-nanadassana — knowledge of path and non-path; (6) patipada-nanadassana — knowledge of the way of progress; (7) nanadassana — knowledge and vision (culminating insight). Used as a developmental scaffold for mapping one's position on the path.

## How To Use

Study the seven stages in order with their detailed sub-stages. For one's current practice, identify honestly which stage is the current work: is sila established? Has citta (concentration) reached sufficient stability? Is ditthi (right view) clear? Have doubts been overcome? Can one distinguish path from non-path experiences? Is the way of progress being seen? Has knowledge-and-vision matured? The map disciplines progress assessment — many practitioners assume they are further along than they are; the *Visuddhimagga* offers a sober structure for honest evaluation. Best used in dialogue with a teacher familiar with the framework.

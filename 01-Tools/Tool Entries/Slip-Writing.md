# Slip-Writing / Crawford Slip Method

**One-line summary:** An anonymous parallel-ideation method — participants write each idea on a separate slip, slips are pooled, categories emerge — predecessor of Brainwriting and Nominal Group.

**When to reach for it:** Large-group ideation where participation should be anonymous; idea generation in cultures where face-loss concerns inhibit voiced contributions; rapid surfacing of many ideas.

## Purpose

Crawford developed the slip method at USC in the 1920s as an anonymous, parallel, written ideation technique. Each idea goes on its own slip, allowing physical sorting and clustering after the ideation phase. The anonymity addresses dominance effects; the parallel writing produces high idea volume; the physical slips enable affinity-diagramming on the output. Many later structured-brainstorming techniques (Brainwriting 6-3-5, NGT, sticky-note workshops) descend from this method.

## How To Use

1) Pose the question. 2) Each participant writes one idea per slip, as many slips as they can produce in the time. 3) Slips pooled centrally, ideally anonymously. 4) Slips sorted into emergent categories (affinity diagramming). 5) Category-level synthesis.

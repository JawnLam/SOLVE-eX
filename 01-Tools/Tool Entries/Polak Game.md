# Polak Game

**One-line summary:** Workshop game where participants physically position themselves in a room according to two questions: (1) Is the future getting better or worse? (2) Are we in control or not? Surfaces the implicit images-of-the-future that shape strategy.

**When to reach for it:** Opening a futures workshop — when participants need to surface their differing assumptions about what kind of future is plausible before committing to scenarios. Useful for groups whose disagreement is rooted in different futures-orientations rather than in different facts.

## Purpose

Frederik Polak's 1973 thesis: civilizations rise and decline with the vitality of their dominant image-of-the-future. Cultures with positive, agentic images flourish; cultures with negative, fatalistic images decline. The Polak Game makes individual images-of-the-future visible as physical position. Participants discover that their colleagues hold radically different baseline orientations — and that those differences explain a lot about strategic disagreement.

## How To Use

1. **Set up the room** — Two intersecting axes on the floor: vertical (better future ↔ worse future), horizontal (we are in control ↔ we are not).
2. **Question 1** — "Is the future getting better or worse?" Each participant moves to their position on the vertical axis.
3. **Question 2** — "Are we in control of the future?" Each participant moves to their position on the horizontal axis.
4. **Discussion in quadrants** — Group by quadrant. "What's it like to stand here? What does this position mean for how you approach the future?"
5. **Cross-quadrant dialogue** — Each quadrant shares; others listen. Surface the assumptions that explain the positions.

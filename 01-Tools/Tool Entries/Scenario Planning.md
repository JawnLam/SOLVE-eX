# Scenario Planning (Shell / Schwartz)

**One-line summary:** Construct a small set (typically 4) of internally-consistent, strategically-different future scenarios; use them to stress-test strategy against uncertainty.

**When to reach for it:** Strategic planning under high uncertainty; challenging organizational assumptions about the future; preparing for strategic surprises; competitive analysis against multiple futures.

## Purpose

Standard forecasting projects a single most-likely future; Scenario Planning produces multiple internally-consistent futures, each plausible, none claimed as 'the' future. Shell pioneered the method in the 1970s, famously preparing for the 1973 oil shock. Schwartz codified the approach. The discipline forces engagement with alternative futures the organization would otherwise ignore. Strategies that survive stress-testing across all scenarios are more robust; strategies that depend on one specific future are exposed.

## How To Use

1) Identify the focal question and time horizon. 2) Identify key driving forces (typically PESTEL or similar). 3) Rank forces by impact and uncertainty. 4) Choose the two most-uncertain high-impact forces as axes. 5) The 2x2 produces 4 scenarios; flesh each out as a coherent world. 6) Stress-test strategy against each. 7) Identify robust strategies and early-warning signals.

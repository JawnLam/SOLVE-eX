# Joik as Cognitive-Memorial Method

**One-line summary:** Sámi vocal-musical tradition in which a joik does not describe but enacts the person, place, or animal it sings — a constitutive rather than descriptive cognitive method.

**When to reach for it:** Studying or engaging cognitive methods in which the act of representation is itself the representation — and where externalized memory of person/place/relation takes the form of enacted sound rather than descriptive text.

## Purpose

Joik (yoik) is the Sámi vocal tradition, sung not about something but as something. One does not joik *about* a person, place, or reindeer — one joiks the person, place, or reindeer. The joik is the entity in sonic form; performing it brings the entity into present relation. Used for memory, identity-marking, communication across long Arctic distances, ecological reasoning (joiks of specific places encode that place's character), and contemporary Sámi cultural resilience. Linguistic-philosophically: a constitutive, not descriptive, cognitive method.

## How To Use

(In observation/study rather than practice for non-Sámi.) Notice the cognitive distinction between description-of and enactment-of. A descriptive song *says* something about X; a joik *is* X performed. Consider: in your own work, are there practices that constitute rather than describe? (Naming ceremonies, certain prayers, performance art, ritual address.) Joik trains attention to the difference and opens cognitive method beyond representation. For Sámi practitioners, joiks are learned in lineage and carry specific cultural ownership — outsider engagement should be respectful and not appropriative.

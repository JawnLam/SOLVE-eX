# Zhu Xi's Quiet Sitting (Jing Zuo)

**One-line summary:** Neo-Confucian (Cheng-Zhu school) practice of sitting quietly before each period of study or reasoning, to settle the heart-mind. Foundational scaffold for sustained inquiry; differs from Buddhist meditation in its preparation-for-engagement framing.

**When to reach for it:** Before any extended study, writing, or deliberation, especially when the practitioner notices agitation or distraction. The Neo-Confucian use is preparatory rather than terminal — the sitting prepares for activity rather than being the activity.

## Purpose

Buddhist meditation aims at the meditation itself or at insight liberation. Confucian quiet sitting aims at *preparing* the heart-mind for moral and intellectual engagement with the world. The practice is brief, regular, and instrumentally framed: the practitioner sits to settle before reading, before writing, before family interaction. The settled state is the substrate for the next activity, not an end-state to be achieved.

## How To Use

1. **Choose a brief duration** — 10-20 minutes is typical; not a multi-hour retreat-style sit.
2. **Sit upright** in a stable, alert posture.
3. **Let the heart-mind settle** — notice agitation and let it subside; don't pursue thoughts.
4. **Maintain the inner posture of *jing* (reverent attentiveness)** — alert, responsive, neither dull nor scattered.
5. **Conclude when settled enough** — open the eyes, begin the next activity (reading, writing, meeting).
6. **Daily before key activities** — the practice's value emerges over weeks of repetition.

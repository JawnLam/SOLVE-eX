# Mussar Cheshbon HaNefesh (Soul-Accounting)

**One-line summary:** Daily moral self-accounting practice from the Mussar tradition (Israel Salanter, 19th c.) using tracked middot — character traits — observed across daily incidents and refined over weeks and years.

**When to reach for it:** When someone wants slow, deliberate character development rather than situational coping — building specific virtues (patience, generosity, humility, equanimity) into stable dispositions through structured daily attention.

## Purpose

Character is built by attention to specific traits, one at a time, over time. The Mussar practice pairs *learning about* a trait (text study) with *living with* the trait (daily observation), creating a feedback loop. The accounting is non-judgmental data collection — not "how did I fail?" but "what did I notice?" Refinement happens slowly; the practice is lifelong.

## How To Use

1. **Choose a middah (trait)** to work with for a week or longer — e.g., savlanut (patience), anavah (humility), zerizut (alacrity).
2. **Study the trait** — short daily reading from Mussar texts (Cheshbon HaNefesh, Mesillat Yesharim, etc.) framing what it looks like.
3. **Observe through the day** — notice incidents where the trait is in play. No correction; just notice.
4. **Evening accounting** — write briefly about the day: where did the trait show up? What did I notice? No judgment.
5. **Rotate traits** — over 13 weeks (Salanter's traditional cycle), work through 13 middot, then begin again.

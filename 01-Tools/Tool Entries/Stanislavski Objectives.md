# Stanislavski Objectives-Units-Beats

**One-line summary:** Constantin Stanislavski's script-analysis method — break script into units (scene-segments with single intent), find character's objective in each unit, trace through-line to super-objective.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing a performance role where the character's psychological architecture must be discovered and made playable, and where unstructured reading would leave the actor without a through-line.

## Purpose

Constantin Stanislavski (early 20th c.) developed the most influential modern actor-training method. The script-analysis core: break the script into units (scene-segments with a single intent); find the character's objective within each unit (what they want); trace through-lines that connect unit-objectives into the character's super-objective (the deepest motivational arc across the whole play). Each beat is a single intention-shift; each unit is a continuous intention. The cognitive method: psychological architecture is discovered and made playable through this structured decomposition. Influenced virtually all subsequent modern acting (Lee Strasberg's Method, Sanford Meisner's Repetition, Stella Adler's psychological work) even where the descendants disagree on method.

## How To Use

Read the script multiple times before annotation. Then mark unit boundaries — where does the character's intent shift? A unit might be three lines or three pages. For each unit, identify the character's objective in action verb form: 'to provoke,' 'to comfort,' 'to escape.' Be specific; avoid generic verbs like 'to be sad.' Identify the obstacle in each unit (what is in the way of the objective?). Then trace through-lines: where does this character's objective in this scene serve a larger arc? What is the super-objective — the deepest motivation across the whole role? Play scene by scene with active pursuit of unit-objectives, layered with awareness of the super-objective. The decomposition gives the actor a play-able architecture rather than a felt-vague intention.

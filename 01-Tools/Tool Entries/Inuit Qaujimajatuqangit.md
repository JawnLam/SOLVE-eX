# Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (IQ)

**One-line summary:** Inuit traditional knowledge codified as a structured epistemological-governance frame with six guiding principles.

**When to reach for it:** Designing decision processes, organizational structures, or research methodologies in contexts where Inuit knowledge holders and Western institutions co-govern, or where Western frames feel rootless in place.

## Purpose

IQ ('that which Inuit have always known') was codified by the Government of Nunavut (2000 onward) to structure territorial decision-making according to Inuit cognitive and ethical norms. Its six guiding principles function as a substrate for governance, education, and research: pijitsirniq (serving others); aajiiqatigiingniq (consensus through discussion); pilimmaksarniq (skill development through observation/practice); piliriqatigiingniq (collaborative working); avatittinnik kamatsiarniq (environmental stewardship); qanuqtuurniq (resourceful problem-solving). Each is a distinct cognitive method as well as a value.

## How To Use

For an organization, project, or inquiry: walk through each of the six principles in turn. Pijitsirniq — whose service is this for, and is service the primary frame? Aajiiqatigiingniq — has discussion been allowed to reach consensus, or has voting cut it short? Pilimmaksarniq — are participants developing real skill, or just credentials? Piliriqatigiingniq — is the work genuinely collaborative or coordinated-individual? Avatittinnik kamatsiarniq — what is the work's relation to the land it depends on? Qanuqtuurniq — are we resourceful with what we have, or escalating to new resources? Use as a periodic review frame.

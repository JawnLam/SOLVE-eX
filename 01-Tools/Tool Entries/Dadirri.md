# Dadirri (Aboriginal Deep Listening)

**One-line summary:** Ngangikurungkurr (Northern Australia) concept of deep contemplative listening to country, to others, and to inner stillness — "learning by watching and listening, waiting and then acting."

**When to reach for it:** Cross-cultural research, place-based inquiry, trauma-informed work with Indigenous communities, contemplative practice. Approached with cultural respect; the form belongs to its tradition and is shared by Miriam-Rose Ungunmerr-Baumann as a gift to others.

## Purpose

Dadirri is Aboriginal Australian deep listening. It is not the same as Western mindfulness — though it shares quietness with it. Dadirri is a relational stance toward country, ancestors, story, and stillness simultaneously. The practice cultivates a kind of attention that knows where it is, who it belongs to, where it came from, and where it is going. Learning by watching and listening rather than by asking questions.

## How To Use

The stance is enacted, not stepped through. Ungunmerr-Baumann offers it as a way of being:
1. **Sit quietly in country** — outside if possible. Just be present.
2. **Notice the watching-and-listening orientation** — not seeking information, just listening.
3. **Carry the stance into encounters** — listen to others the same way; let them speak in their own time.
4. **Wait and then act** — don't rush to respond. The right action emerges from the listening.
5. **Daily orientation** — dadirri is a way of life more than a discrete practice. Cultivate it as a habit.

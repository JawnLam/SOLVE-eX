# Nominal Group Technique (Delbecq-Van de Ven)

**One-line summary:** Silent idea generation, then round-robin sharing, then group discussion, then private ranking — in-person, structured, balances participation and synthesis.

**When to reach for it:** Small-group decision processes (5-9 people) where ideation, clarification, and ranking are needed in one session; problem-solving meetings where minority voices should not be drowned out.

## Purpose

Conventional discussion-based decision processes have multiple failure modes: dominant personalities crowd out others; early-anchored ideas constrain later thinking; consensus pressure flattens disagreement. NGT addresses all three: silent ideation prevents premature anchoring; round-robin sharing gives every voice equal time; private ranking prevents social-pressure conformity. The technique gives small groups a structured path from a question to a prioritized output.

## How To Use

1) Frame the question. 2) Silent ideation: each writes ideas privately (5-10 min). 3) Round-robin: each shares one idea at a time, recorded on flip chart, until all are exhausted. 4) Discussion: clarification only, not evaluation. 5) Private ranking: each ranks the top N ideas. 6) Aggregate rankings.

## Sources

- Delbecq, Van de Ven, & Gustafson (1975) Group Techniques for Program Planning.

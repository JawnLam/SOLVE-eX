# Chochinov Dignity Therapy

**One-line summary:** Brief individualized psychotherapy (Harvey Chochinov, 2002) for patients with advanced illness — structured interview about meaning and legacy produces an edited generativity document.

**When to reach for it:** A patient with advanced illness is experiencing existential distress, loss of meaning, or a wish to leave something behind for loved ones — and a structured therapeutic intervention can support legacy work.

## Purpose

Dignity Therapy was developed by Harvey Chochinov (2002, 2012) for patients with advanced or terminal illness. A trained therapist conducts a 1-2 session structured interview using questions about life history, accomplishments, lessons learned, hopes for loved ones, things that should be remembered. The interview is audio-recorded, transcribed, edited (with the patient's input), and given back to the patient as a 'generativity document' that the patient can give to family. Empirical research shows reduced existential distress and improved quality of life in patients near death. The cognitive payload: structured legacy-articulation as therapeutic intervention.

## How To Use

(For trained therapists, generally palliative care chaplains, social workers, or psychologists.) Schedule one or two sessions of 60-90 minutes each, in a place comfortable for the patient. Use Chochinov's standard question protocol: 'Tell me a little about your life history... When did you feel most alive? What are the most important roles you've played? Your most important accomplishments? Things you've learned about life that you want to pass along? What advice do you want to give to your loved ones? Are there hopes and dreams you have for them? Anything else you want them to know or remember?' Audio-record. Transcribe verbatim. Edit collaboratively with the patient — removing repetition, organizing thematically, preserving voice. Give the final document to the patient.

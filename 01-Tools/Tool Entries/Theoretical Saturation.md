# Theoretical Saturation

**One-line summary:** Continue sampling until additional cases produce no new categories or properties — the stopping criterion in grounded-theory and qualitative research.

**When to reach for it:** Qualitative research design; literature review scope decisions; deciding when an enumeration is comprehensive enough to act on; any context where 'we surveyed enough' must be defended.

## Purpose

Without a stopping criterion, qualitative work either stops too early (missing categories) or never stops (infinite incremental cases). Theoretical Saturation provides a principled stopping rule: keep sampling until N consecutive new cases produce no new categories or new properties of existing categories. The criterion forces explicit tracking of what each case adds, distinguishing 'we surveyed enough' (defended) from 'we got tired' (not defended).

## How To Use

1) Identify the categorization being built. 2) For each new case, ask: does this introduce a new category, or extend a property of an existing one? 3) Track 'novelty rate' across cases. 4) When novelty rate falls below threshold and stays low for N consecutive cases, declare saturation. 5) Document the stopping criterion in writeup.

# Words of Estimative Probability (Kent)

**One-line summary:** Standardized probability-vocabulary mapping (Sherman Kent, CIA, 1964): "almost certain" = 87-93%, "probable" = 63-87%, "even chance" = 47-53%, etc. Disciplines analytic language to avoid linguistic ambiguity in assessments.

**When to reach for it:** Writing or reading any analytic product where verbal probability words ("likely," "possible," "probable") risk being read with widely different meanings by different audiences. Standard in US intelligence community assessments since IC Directive 203.

## Purpose

Words like "likely" mean wildly different things to different people — studies show interpretations from 30% to 80%. Without a shared scale, the analyst and the policymaker may understand the same words to mean different probabilities. Kent's scale pins each phrase to a numerical band. Practitioners learn to write within the bands; readers learn to parse them.

## How To Use

1. **For each probability claim** in an assessment, choose the appropriate phrase from the standardized list.
2. **Kent's bands** (one common mapping): Almost certain (95±5%), Highly likely (87%±5), Likely (75%±12), Probable (63%±12), Even chance (50±5%), Probably not (37%±12), Unlikely (25%±12), Highly unlikely (13%±5), Almost certainly not (5%±5%).
3. **Reserve numerical ranges** for cases where precision is warranted ("60-80% chance").
4. **Train readers** as well as writers — the discipline only works if the audience reads the words as the writer meant them.

## Sources

- Office of the Director of National Intelligence (2007). *IC Directive 203: Analytic Standards*.
- Friedman, J. A., & Zeckhauser, R. (2012). "Assessing Uncertainty in Intelligence." *Intelligence and National Security* 27(6): 824–847.

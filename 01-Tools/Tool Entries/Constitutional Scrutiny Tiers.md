# Constitutional Scrutiny Tiers

**One-line summary:** US constitutional decision frame applying one of three scrutiny levels to government actions restricting rights — Strict, Intermediate, or Rational Basis — determining burden of proof and likely outcome.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating a US constitutional challenge to government action that restricts a right or classifies persons, where the appropriate scrutiny level must be identified before substantive analysis proceeds.

## Purpose

The US constitutional scrutiny tiers are a categorical decision frame developed in 20th-century equal-protection and substantive-due-process jurisprudence (with roots in *Carolene Products* footnote 4, 1938). Three tiers: (1) Strict Scrutiny — applied to suspect classifications (race, national origin) and fundamental rights (interstate travel, marriage, voting). Government must show: narrowly tailored, compelling government interest, least restrictive means. Most government actions fail. (2) Intermediate Scrutiny — applied to quasi-suspect classifications (sex, illegitimacy). Government must show: substantially related, important government interest. (3) Rational Basis — the default. Government must show: rationally related, legitimate government interest. Most government actions pass. The tier-selection often determines outcome.

## How To Use

For the challenged government action: identify what right is being restricted or what classification is being used. Map to tier: race or national origin classification → strict; fundamental right restriction → strict; sex or illegitimacy classification → intermediate; other classifications or non-fundamental rights → rational basis. Apply the tier's specific test: under strict, can government show a compelling interest narrowly tailored? Under intermediate, an important interest substantially related? Under rational basis, a legitimate interest rationally related? The burden of proof and standard differ at each tier. Be aware: the categorization itself is often contested (is X a fundamental right? Is Y a suspect classification?), and the tiers have evolved (most recently in same-sex marriage cases). Useful as a foundational frame for US constitutional analysis.

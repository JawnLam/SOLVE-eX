# Zuowang (Sitting and Forgetting)

**One-line summary:** Daoist meditation: drop body, drop intellect, identify with the Way. "Sitting" until the discursive self forgets itself; the practitioner becomes what they sit *with*, not what sits.

**When to reach for it:** Daoist contemplative practice across schools; relevant to anyone seeking a non-grasping deep-rest practice distinct from the focused-attention emphasis of Buddhist concentration practices.

## Purpose

Where Western meditation often emphasizes the watcher ("observe your thoughts"), Zuowang dissolves the watcher. The practitioner sits until even the self that is sitting is forgotten. The result is identification with the Way (*dao*) — the unconditioned source from which the differentiated self arose. The practice is paradoxical: it seeks not a state to acquire but the dissolution of the seeker.

## How To Use

Zhuangzi's depiction in chapter 6 (Yan Hui's progression):
1. **Forget benevolence and righteousness** — let go of conventional ethical anxiety.
2. **Forget rituals and music** — let go of culturally-shaped identity.
3. **Sit and forget** — let go of body and intellect themselves.
4. **The result** — "I cast aside my limbs, drive out my intelligence, separate from form, free from knowledge — and identify with the Great Thoroughfare. This is Zuowang."

In practice:
1. **Sit quietly** in a stable posture.
2. **Let go progressively** — first thoughts, then sensations, then the sense of being a sitter.
3. **Rest** in what remains when even "sitting" has been forgotten.
4. **Cultivate over years** — the practice deepens slowly.

# Bradford Hill Criteria

**One-line summary:** Austin Bradford Hill's (1965) nine criteria for inferring causation from epidemiologic association — strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, dose-response, plausibility, coherence, experiment, analogy.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating whether an observed statistical association between an exposure and an outcome represents true causation, especially in observational epidemiology where direct experimental manipulation is not possible.

## Purpose

Austin Bradford Hill's 1965 presidential address to the Royal Society of Medicine articulated nine criteria for inferring causation from epidemiologic association: (1) Strength — larger associations are more likely causal; (2) Consistency — replication across populations, settings; (3) Specificity — the association is for a particular disease, not anything; (4) Temporality — exposure precedes outcome; (5) Biological gradient — dose-response relationship; (6) Plausibility — biological mechanism is plausible; (7) Coherence — fits with what is known about disease natural history; (8) Experiment — experimental evidence (where possible); (9) Analogy — similar exposures cause similar outcomes elsewhere. Hill explicitly framed these as 'aspects' rather than rules, but they have become foundational for causal reasoning in epidemiology.

## How To Use

For an observed association between exposure and outcome: walk through each criterion. Is the association strong (large effect size)? Is it consistent across studies, populations, settings? Is it specific to a particular outcome? Does exposure clearly precede outcome (the only criterion Hill called essential)? Is there a dose-response gradient (more exposure, more outcome)? Is there a plausible biological mechanism? Is the association coherent with what is known about the disease? Is there experimental evidence (RCT, intervention study)? Is there analogous evidence from related exposures and outcomes? An association meeting many criteria is more likely causal; none of the criteria are independently sufficient or strictly necessary (except temporality).

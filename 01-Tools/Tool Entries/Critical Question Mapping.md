# Critical Question Mapping

**One-line summary:** Brainstorm questions (not answers); categorize (broad / narrow / impact); prioritize; build a hierarchy.

**When to reach for it:** You suspect you're answering the wrong questions; need to surface and select the questions worth asking.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

The purpose of the "Critical Question Mapping" thinking tool is to enhance effective thinking in problem-solving situations by identifying the most impactful or "perfect" questions. This approach uses the technique of "Question Brainstorming," which is similar to traditional brainstorming but focuses on generating questions rather than ideas. After brainstorming, the tool guides participants in categorizing, prioritizing, and analyzing these questions to determine the most critical ones for their specific situation. By doing so, it aids in uncovering key insights and solutions, steering the problem-solving process towards a more structured and comprehensive understanding of the issue at hand.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

To enhance effective thinking, it's essential to ask the right questions. The goal is to identify the "perfect" questions, but how can you achieve this? The technique of "Question Brainstorming" can help. This method is similar to traditional brainstorming but concentrates on generating questions instead of ideas. After listing all possible questions, choose the top 3-4. Then, apply a structured question hierarchy to these selected questions. This process aids in pinpointing the most impactful or "perfect" questions to ask for your specific situation.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

To effectively utilize this thinking tool in a facilitated problem-solving workshop, follow these steps:

1. **Initial Engagement and Brainstorming:** Introduce the tool's concept and explain the types of questions. Conduct a brainstorming session focused on generating diverse questions related to the problem, without immediate judgment or dismissal. Encourage participants to ask as many questions as possible without seeking to answer them immediately. Document all questions without filtering.
2. **Categorization of Questions:** Group brainstormed questions into predefined categories for structured analysis (e.g., Broad Scope Exploration, Narrow Scope Focus). Eliminate duplicates and streamline by focusing on unique, impactful questions.
3. **Prioritization Process:** Use criteria like relevance, impact, and urgency to prioritize questions, focusing on those aligning with workshop goals. Facilitate in-depth discussions for each category to refine questions and identify actionable insights. Select the top 3-4 questions per category. Use the "Prioritization of Key Questions" table for this process.
4. **Selection of Key Questions:** Identify and agree upon the most critical questions, ensuring consensus. Organize these key questions in a hierarchical order, progressing from broad to specific.
5. **Impact Analysis and Discussion:** Perform an impact analysis on the selected questions to determine their potential influence on the problem-solving process.
6. **Development of a Hierarchical Question Structure:** Apply a structured hierarchy to the selected key questions, starting with broad topics and progressively narrowing.
7. **Identification of the "Perfect" Questions:** After applying the hierarchical structure, identify the questions most critical to understanding and resolving the problem.
8. **Action Plan for Answering Key Questions:** Develop a comprehensive plan that details the steps and methodologies necessary to address the key questions.
9. **Review and Iteration:** Conduct regular reviews of the answers and insights obtained. Stay flexible and ready to revise the questioning strategy as new information arises.
10. **Documentation:** Keep a detailed record of all questions, discussions, and the rationale behind the selection of key questions.
11. **Feedback and Closure:** Consolidate feedback to evaluate the effectiveness of the questioning process.

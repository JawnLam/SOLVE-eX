# Awe Walks

**One-line summary:** A 15–30 minute walk taken with attention deliberately oriented toward awe — vastness, beauty, mystery, the more-than-self — using a structured looking-protocol that converts ordinary outdoor time into a measurable contemplative practice.

**When to reach for it:** Daily contemplative practice that needs to be embodied (not seated); coming out of a period of self-rumination or status anxiety; before high-stakes decisions where ego-decentering improves judgment; in therapeutic contexts where the practitioner needs more accessible practice than seated meditation; when grief or transition has narrowed attention to the self and you need a wider container.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Awe Walks** are a small-stakes, evidence-based contemplative intervention. The structure (per Sturm et al. 2020):

1. **Set the orientation before starting.** Say to yourself: *"On this walk I'll try to approach what I see with fresh eyes — as if for the first time."* (This is the Keltner-lab framing; alternatives use shoshin or beginner's-mind language.)
2. **Take the walk.** Outdoors. 15 minutes minimum; longer is fine.
3. **Look up.** Most awe-eliciting features are above the horizon line — trees, sky, buildings, weather.
4. **Look for the eight wonders.** Keltner's research catalogs eight common awe-triggers: nature, moral beauty (others' courage / kindness), collective effervescence, music, visual design, spirituality / religion, encounter with the threshold of life and death, encounter with epiphany / sudden understanding.
5. **Notice the small-self response.** Awe characteristically produces "small-self" — a felt-recognition that the self is one element in a vastly larger system. Don't grasp for it; just notice when it arrives.
6. **Don't photograph.** Photographing reorients the attention from awe to self-as-photographer. Keep your phone in your pocket.

The non-obvious operational insight is that **awe is cultivable.** Keltner's lab work establishes that the practice produces *replicable* well-being effects across a week-long intervention, comparable to or exceeding standard mindfulness interventions on some prosocial measures (Sturm et al. 2020, n=60). Awe is not a rare lightning-strike. It's a daily-attention regimen.

A second insight: **awe is somatic-cognitive.** It involves identifiable body markers (chills, "small smile," widened visual focus). The walk-protocol exploits this by adding gentle physiological activation (walking) to the attention shift. Seated awe practice is harder; walking awe is easier.

A third insight: **the small-self response is the active ingredient.** The well-being effect tracks with experienced self-decentering, not with how "awesome" the trigger objectively was. A maple tree in the front yard does this as reliably as the Grand Canyon, provided the attention is right.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the practice prevents:

1. **The contemplative-practice-feels-too-hard failure.** Seated meditation has a high entry barrier (posture, attention, expectation). Awe Walks are easier to start and easier to sustain.
2. **The exotic-practice failure.** Many awe-tools require pilgrimage / nature retreat / unusual context. Awe Walks work from your front door.
3. **The grim-meditation failure.** Some contemplative traditions emphasize the difficult; awe is unambiguously pleasant. This makes it more likely to be practiced.

For anyone wanting a contemplative entry point, for therapists looking for low-cost prescriptions, for leaders trying to maintain perspective under pressure, Awe Walks are the most evidence-based small-dose intervention available.

```

|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | Set the orientation. "Fresh eyes" framing.                                      |
|  2   | Go outside. Walk at a relaxed pace.                                             |
|  3   | Look up. Above the horizon line.                                                |
|  4   | Watch for the eight wonders (nature / moral beauty / collective effervescence  |
|      | / music / design / spirituality / threshold / epiphany).                        |
|  5   | When you notice the small-self response, sit with it for a few breaths.         |
|  6   | Phone stays in pocket. No photos.                                               |

|  8   | After: brief mental note of what produced the strongest awe-trigger today.      |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

> **Operational notes:** Two failure modes to watch for. (1) "Awe-hunting" — trying to manufacture awe through effort; this defeats the practice. Awe arrives unbidden; the practice is creating the conditions for it. (2) "Photographing the awe" — converting the experience into shareable content reorients attention back to self. Both are common in beginners. The 15-minute floor matters because too-short walks don't allow attentional settling.

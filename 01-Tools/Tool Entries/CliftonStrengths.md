# CliftonStrengths

**One-line summary:** A self-report instrument that surfaces an individual's dominant *talent themes* — recurring patterns of thinking, feeling, and behaving that energize them — from a catalogue of 34, providing operational vocabulary for "what comes naturally and what drains me."

**When to reach for it:** Onboarding new hires; team composition discussions; career transitions where role-fit is the question; coaching contexts where the goal is amplifying strengths rather than remediating weaknesses; team-development workshops.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

Donald Clifton, working at Gallup in the 1990s, proposed a *strengths*-based alternative to deficit-focused development. His observation: people improve faster by leaning into what comes naturally than by remediating weaknesses. The instrument identifies dominant talent themes from 34 — patterns like Strategic, Empathy, Achiever, Learner, Activator, Relator, etc.

The non-obvious operational insight is the *energy* signal. CliftonStrengths' practical value is less in the specific theme labels and more in the question they pose: which kinds of work *energize* you (you finish a long session feeling more alive than at the start) versus which *drain* you (you finish exhausted regardless of how much you did)? Designing roles and projects to align with energizing themes produces durable engagement; ignoring this produces burnout regardless of how "good" someone is at the work.

Caveats: the psychometric rigor is weaker than the Big Five's; theme labels can become identity claims that limit rather than expand; and the proprietary nature limits academic study. Treat the instrument as descriptive vocabulary for self-knowledge and team conversation rather than as scientific assessment.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the strengths frame addresses:

1. **Deficit-only development.** Time invested in remediating weak skills produces less return than time leveraging strong ones; the strengths frame redirects.
2. **Role-energy mismatch.** Many roles ask for behaviors that drain the incumbent; identifying energizers helps redesign the role or the person's allocation within it.
3. **Generic team-fit.** Vague "diversity of thinking" becomes specific when teams can name the talent themes present and absent.

For consulting and team-design work, CliftonStrengths' shared vocabulary helps teams talk about complementarity productively. The labels matter less than the conversation they enable.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Take the assessment (paid; allow 30–45 min). Receive top 5 (or full 34).        |
|    2 | Read the descriptions. Notice which resonate; which don't fit.                  |
|    3 | For each top theme, identify recent moments when it was active and energizing.  |
|    4 | Identify themes you wish you had more of — these are often *aspirations*       |
|      | rather than gaps; consider whether you'd actually want to be that person.       |
|    5 | Map current role / projects against your themes. Where's the alignment?         |
|      | The drain?                                                                      |
|    6 | Identify "complementary partner" themes — themes that naturally support yours. |
|    7 | At team level, list each member's top themes; check coverage and gaps.          |
|    8 | Re-allocate work where possible to maximize theme-alignment.                    |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

      Individualization, Positivity, Relator

      ___________________________________________________________________

    Move 1: where can I do MORE of theme-aligned work?
      ___________________________________________________________________

      ___________________________________________________________________

      ___________________________________________________________________

TEAM COVERAGE MAP

    For each team member, list their top 3–5 themes.
    Identify:
      Domains over-represented: ________________________________________
      Domains under-represented: _______________________________________
      Specific gaps (no Strategic Thinking; no Influencing): _____________
      Hiring or development priorities to fill gaps: ___________________

THEME PAIRINGS (productive complementarity)

      Achiever + Strategic    : execution with foresight
      Empathy + Communication : connection turned into communication
      Learner + Ideation      : curiosity becomes generative
      Restorative + Maximizer : fix what's broken AND amplify what works
      Includer + Activator    : "everyone in" + "let's go"

      Look for pairings between you and others — your gap may be their strength.

CAVEATS

    [ ] Theme labels can become identity claims that limit growth

    [ ] Treat as descriptive vocabulary, not scientific assessment
    [ ] Some individuals' top-5 doesn't quite match self-recognition;
        trust the introspective check above the algorithmic output

> **Operational notes:** Three disciplines. (1) Use as conversation starter, not verdict. The labels create useful shared vocabulary but should not be treated as scientific fact. The introspective question — "what energizes vs. drains me?" — is the operative one regardless of what the assessment said. (2) Watch the shadow side. Every theme has a failure mode at high expression: Achiever → workaholic; Empathy → self-loss; Strategic → analysis paralysis; Significance → status-anxiety. Map each top theme's shadow alongside its strength. (3) Pair with values and skills work. Strengths describe natural patterns; they don't describe whether those patterns serve your values or whether you have the skills to execute. The full picture needs all three. Fourth: at team level, the Big Five may be more rigorous; for team conversations and individual coaching contexts where shared vocabulary matters, CliftonStrengths' theme language is operationally useful. Use the right tool for the right scope.

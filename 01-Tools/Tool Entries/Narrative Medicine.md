# Narrative Medicine

**One-line summary:** Rita Charon's method of close reading of patient narratives plus parallel chart-writing — reading-as-clinical-method that surfaces what quantitative notes miss.

**When to reach for it:** When a clinician wants to engage with the patient as a whole person rather than as a presenting complaint. Used in medical-humanities curricula, palliative-care training, and chronic-disease management.

## Purpose

Conventional medical charting strips the patient's story to fit predefined fields. Narrative medicine treats the patient's account as a literary text worth close attention — what's said, what's not, what metaphors organize it. The parallel chart (clinician's reflective writing alongside the official record) creates space for the meaning the form excludes.

## How To Use

1. **Close-reading exercise** — Read a short literary text with a colleague; attend to form, voice, gaps. Practice the close-reading move.
2. **Listen for narrative** — In a patient encounter, attend not just to symptoms but to how the patient frames their illness: what story are they telling about themselves?
3. **Parallel chart** — Write briefly (often 5-10 minutes) about an encounter in a register the official record forbids: what struck you, what you felt, what you don't know.
4. **Share with peers** — In a structured group, read parallel charts; respond to each other's writing rather than critiquing.

# Mediation Style Selection

**One-line summary:** Three mediation styles — Transformative, Facilitative, Evaluative — chosen strategically; style mismatch sabotages mediation outcomes.

**When to reach for it:** Selecting a mediator or designing a mediation process, where the style of mediator-intervention is itself a strategic variable that should match the parties' goals, relationship, and dispute type.

## Purpose

Three primary mediation styles, each with distinct cognitive method: (1) Transformative (Bush & Folger) — focuses on party empowerment and recognition, with the mediator stepping back to support party-driven communication; success measured by relational shift rather than settlement. (2) Facilitative (Riskin) — focuses on interest-based problem-solving with the mediator helping parties identify underlying interests, generate options, evaluate against criteria; success measured by mutually-satisfying agreement. (3) Evaluative (Riskin) — the mediator assesses the case (legal merits, likely outcomes) and offers prediction or recommendation; success measured by efficient resolution informed by neutral expert assessment. Choice of style is strategic; style mismatch sabotages outcomes.

## How To Use

Before mediation: assess what the parties most need. If the underlying issue is relational (ongoing relationship matters, communication has broken down), transformative may be appropriate. If parties want creative problem-solving and have capacity for it, facilitative. If parties are deadlocked and need neutral assessment, evaluative. Match style to dispute: long-term relationships (employment, family, neighbors) often warrant transformative or facilitative; commercial disputes with no ongoing relationship often warrant evaluative. Select the mediator whose style matches. If the mediator's style is misaligned (e.g., evaluative mediator pushing settlement in a transformation-needing situation), parties leave dissatisfied even if 'settled.' The choice of style is a strategic move, not a personality preference.

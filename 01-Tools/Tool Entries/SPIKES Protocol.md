# SPIKES Bad-News Protocol

**One-line summary:** Six-step protocol for delivering bad medical news — Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotion, Strategy/Summary — originated in oncology, now standard across medicine.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing to deliver bad news to a patient or family — a diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment failure — where structure helps prevent the well-documented failures of unstructured delivery.

## Purpose

SPIKES (Baile et al., 2000) is a six-step protocol developed for delivering bad medical news with skill rather than improvisation. Each letter is a step: (S)etting — private space, sit at eye level, prepare; (P)erception — what does the patient already know or suspect?; (I)nvitation — how much do they want to know?; (K)nowledge — deliver clearly with warning shot ('I have some difficult news...'), plain language, pauses; (E)motion — acknowledge with empathic response (silence, NURSE statements); (S)trategy and summary — plan next steps, check understanding. Originated in oncology; now standard across medicine and increasingly taught in non-medical bad-news contexts (HR, leadership).

## How To Use

Before the encounter: arrange a private space; ensure adequate time; sit so eye levels match. Setting established. Begin with perception: 'What have you been told about your condition?' Listen. Invitation: 'How much detail would you like me to share?' Calibrate accordingly. Knowledge: give a warning shot, then deliver in plain language in 1-2 sentences. Pause. The patient needs time to absorb. Emotion: when emotion arises (and it will), acknowledge it — name it, normalize it, sit with the silence. Don't rush to information. When the patient is ready, move to Strategy and Summary: what comes next; check understanding by asking the patient to summarize.

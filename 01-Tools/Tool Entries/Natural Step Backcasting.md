# Natural Step Backcasting

**One-line summary:** A strategic-planning method — Karl-Henrik Robèrt's Natural Step framework — that defines a desired future state (often anchored to sustainability principles) and works backward through milestones to determine present actions, distinct from forecasting which extrapolates current trends.

**When to reach for it:** Long-horizon strategic planning where the desired future differs structurally from current trajectory (sustainability transitions, decarbonization, industry transformation); organizational change with a clear vision; corporate or municipal sustainability strategy; climate-action planning; and any context where current-trend extrapolation would land somewhere undesirable and explicit work is needed to redirect.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Natural Step Backcasting** plans by working backward from a defined future. The structure:

1. **Define the future state** — anchored to clear principles or vision (in TNS: four sustainability principles).
2. **Assess current state** against the future state. Where are we vs. where we need to be?
3. **Identify the gap** — specific dimensions where current ≠ future.
4. **Work backward** — what milestones must be reached to get from now to future? In what sequence?
5. **Identify present actions** — what must be done now to begin the trajectory?
6. **Test actions** against decision criteria (in TNS: do they reduce dependence on the unsustainable; provide flexibility for future steps; provide return on investment).

The non-obvious operational insight is that **forecasting and backcasting produce different strategies.** Forecasting extrapolates current trends; if current trends produce undesirable futures (climate breakdown, resource exhaustion, organizational decay), forecasting recommends incremental adjustment. Backcasting starts from where you want to be; it can produce different actions than forecasting suggests, especially when the gap is large and structural change is needed.

The Natural Step's specific anchor: four sustainability principles defining a sustainable future:

1. Eliminate the systematic increase of substances extracted from the Earth's crust (e.g., fossil fuels, heavy metals).
2. Eliminate the systematic increase of substances produced by society (e.g., plastics, persistent chemicals).
3. Eliminate the systematic physical degradation of ecosystems (e.g., deforestation, soil loss).
4. Eliminate barriers to people meeting their needs (poverty, exclusion, exploitation).

A society / organization aligned with these principles is "sustainable." Backcasting from this defined future produces strategy.

A second insight: **the framework is generalizable beyond sustainability.** Any organization with a clearly-defined desired future state can use backcasting. The Natural Step's contribution is the rigorous principle-based future-definition; the backcasting method is independent of those specific principles.

A third insight: **backcasting reveals when current direction is wrong.** Forecasting often suggests "more of the same with adjustments." Backcasting can reveal that the current direction leads away from the desired future and a structural shift is needed. This is uncomfortable but important.

A fourth insight: **the framework integrates with day-simulation.** Backcasting defines the future; day-simulation tests its desirability and coherence. Used together: backcasting strategically; day-simulation experientially.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The "extrapolate-the-current-trajectory" trap.** Current direction may lead somewhere undesirable; forecasting hides this by treating trajectory as fixed.
2. **The "incremental-improvement-only" failure.** When the gap to the desired future is structural, incremental improvement won't close it. Backcasting reveals the need for non-incremental moves.
3. **The "vague vision" weakness.** Backcasting requires defined future state; the discipline of definition exposes vague visions that "we want to be sustainable" hides.

For sustainability strategists, climate planners, corporate transformation leaders, futures-focused planners, and anyone whose long-horizon strategy differs structurally from current trajectory, backcasting is foundational.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Define the future state precisely. What principles, characteristics, conditions|
|      | must hold?                                                                        |
|    2 | Assess current state against the future state. Where do we already align?     |
|      | Where do we diverge?                                                              |
|    3 | Identify the structural gaps — dimensions where present ≠ future.               |
|    4 | Work backward through milestones. What state must we be in 1 year, 5 years,    |
|      | 10 years out to be on trajectory?                                                |
|    5 | Identify present actions. What can be done now toward the first milestone?     |
|    6 | Apply TNS decision criteria (or your equivalent): reduce dependence on the     |

|    7 | Implement and monitor. Are we on the trajectory toward the future?            |
|    8 | Iterate. Backcasting isn't one-time; revisit as understanding improves.       |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE BACKCASTING STRUCTURE

   Future state (defined precisely)
        |
        | (work backward)
        v
   Milestones (10-year, 5-year, 1-year)
        |
        v
   Present actions (start now)

   Contrasted with forecasting:

   Present trajectory → extrapolation → future state
   (Where current path leads)

   The two often produce different futures and
   different actions.

THE TNS FOUR PRINCIPLES (as future-state anchor)

   1. Society's actions don't systematically increase
   concentrations of substances from the Earth's
   crust (fossil fuels, heavy metals, etc.).

   2. Society's actions don't systematically increase
   concentrations of substances produced by society
   (plastics, persistent chemicals).

   3. Society's actions don't systematically degrade
   ecosystems (deforestation, soil loss, biodiversity
   collapse).

   4. People aren't subject to conditions that
   systematically undermine their capacity to meet
   their needs (poverty, exploitation).

   A sustainable future = society aligned with these
   four. Organizations / municipalities backcasting
   from these principles produce different strategies
   than forecasting from current trajectories.

THE TNS DECISION CRITERIA (for present actions)

   For each candidate action:

   1. Does this action move toward sustainability
   compliance? (Aligned with the four principles?)

   2. Does this action provide flexibility for future
   moves? (Doesn't lock in unsustainable infrastructure
   for decades?)

   3. Does this action provide adequate return on
   investment? (Economically viable; doesn't bankrupt
   the firm in pursuit of sustainability.)

   Actions passing all three are "good first steps."
   Actions that fail one (especially the second —
   future flexibility) deserve scrutiny.

   This balances ambition with pragmatism: progress
   toward the future without unsustainable costs in
   the present.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE — IKEA SUSTAINABILITY

   Future state (TNS principles): all materials
   sustainably sourced; all energy renewable; supply
   chain doesn't exploit workers.

   Current state assessment: significant percentage of
   wood from non-FSC sources; significant fossil-fuel
   energy in operations; some supply-chain
   transparency gaps.

   Gap: structural, not incremental.

   Milestones backward:
       2030: 100% renewable energy in own operations
       2025: All wood from FSC or recycled sources
       2020: Materials transparency across product
       lines
       2015: Carbon-footprint baseline established

   Present actions (decision criteria applied):
       Invest in solar capacity at stores (passes all 3)
       Phase out tropical hardwoods (passes 1, 2; ROI
       initially negative; pursue anyway as strategic)
       Supplier code-of-conduct (passes all 3)

   Without backcasting, IKEA might have continued
   incremental improvement; with backcasting, IKEA
   committed to structural change with explicit
   trajectory.

THE FORECAST-VS-BACKCAST CONTRAST

   FORECASTING:
       "Current trends suggest 5%/year decarbonization."
       "Continue current programs; add minor
       optimizations."
       "Goal: stay ahead of regulation."

   BACKCASTING:
       "Future state requires net-zero by 2050."
       "Current trajectory falls short by 60%."
       "Required: 7%/year decarbonization, with
       structural shifts in product mix."
       "Strategy: phase out high-carbon product lines;
       invest in alternatives now."

   The two approaches produce different strategies.
   Backcasting is more demanding but matches the
   structural-shift situation.

THE COMMON FAILURE MODES

   1. VAGUE FUTURE STATE
        "We want to be sustainable" without TNS-style
        principles. Recovery: precise definition.

   2. NO MILESTONE STRUCTURE
        Future state defined; present-state assessed;
        no path between. Recovery: explicit
        milestones.

   3. MILESTONES TOO INCREMENTAL
        10-year plan that's actually forecasting
        dressed as backcasting. Recovery: confirm
        milestones bridge the gap, not just continue
        trend.

   4. UNREALISTIC PRESENT ACTIONS
        Actions that bankrupt the firm in pursuit of
        sustainability. Recovery: TNS decision
        criteria including ROI.

   5. NO MONITORING
        Plan made; never tracked. Recovery: explicit
        progress review.

   6. ABANDONMENT AT FIRST OBSTACLE
        Hard to maintain backcasting through political
        / economic shocks. Recovery: institutional
        commitment; senior champion.

   7. SINGLE-FUTURE BACKCASTING
        One vision; ignores uncertainty. Recovery:
        multiple-scenario backcasting; resilience to
        different futures.

THE OPERATIONAL TEMPLATE

   Future state (precisely defined):
       Principle 1: __________________________________
       Principle 2: __________________________________
       Principle 3: __________________________________
       Principle 4: __________________________________

   Current state assessment:
       Principle 1: alignment ___% — gap: ____________
       Principle 2: alignment ___% — gap: ____________
       Principle 3: alignment ___% — gap: ____________
       Principle 4: alignment ___% — gap: ____________

   Milestones (working backward):
       10-year: ______________________________________
       5-year: _______________________________________
       1-year: _______________________________________

   Present actions (with TNS decision criteria):
       Action 1: _____________________________________
           Toward future: Y / N
           Future flexibility: Y / N
           Adequate ROI: Y / N
           → proceed: Y / N
       Action 2: _____________________________________
           [same checks]

   Monitoring schedule:
       Annual progress review on milestones
       Trajectory adjustment as needed
```

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) Define the future state precisely. Vague visions ("be sustainable") don't anchor backcasting. The TNS principles are one set; any precise principle-based definition works. The discipline is the precision. (2) Distinguish backcasting from forecasting. They produce different strategies in different ways. Forecasting extrapolates current trajectory; backcasting starts from desired future. The choice depends on whether current trajectory leads where you want to go. (3) Apply decision criteria to present actions. Pure ambition without ROI bankrupts; pure ROI without ambition fails to reach future. The TNS three-criterion test (toward future + flexibility + ROI) balances. (4) Iterate. Backcasting is a planning discipline, not a one-time exercise. Revisit annually; adjust trajectory as understanding deepens and conditions change.

# Bloom's Mastery Learning

**One-line summary:** Hold the level of mastery constant while letting time-to-mastery vary — provide formative feedback and corrective instruction until learners reach the criterion.

**When to reach for it:** Course design where uniform competence matters more than uniform timing; tutoring; situations where letting weaker students fall behind is unacceptable; learning that compounds (math, programming, languages).

## Purpose

Standard age-graded instruction holds time constant and lets achievement vary, producing a Gaussian distribution of mastery. Bloom's Mastery Learning inverts this: hold mastery constant (set a criterion), let time vary. Provide formative assessments, give corrective instruction for those who don't yet meet criterion, retest. Bloom's empirical work showed that mastery-learning classrooms could compress the achievement distribution dramatically. The method underlies modern competency-based education and adaptive learning systems.

## How To Use

1) Define mastery criterion for each unit (typically 80-90% on a formative assessment). 2) Teach the unit. 3) Formative assessment. 4) For those meeting criterion: enrichment activities. 5) For those not yet: corrective instruction targeting specific deficits. 6) Re-test. 7) Repeat until criterion. 8) Move to next unit.

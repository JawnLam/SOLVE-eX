# Oblique Strategies (Eno)

**One-line summary:** Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt's deck of 100+ cards with cryptic prompts — "Honor thy error as a hidden intention," "Use an old idea," "Distorting time" — drawn randomly when creatively stuck, the prompt forcing a perspective shift or constraint that breaks the block.

**When to reach for it:** Creative blocks during writing, music, design, art; when iteration on the current path produces diminishing returns; team brainstorming that's stuck in a rut; coaching creatives to find new angles; and any context where systematic generation has failed and a random / oblique nudge is needed to surface different approaches.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Oblique Strategies** is a deck of cryptic prompts for creative unblocking. The structure:

1. **Get stuck** in creative work.
2. **Draw a card** at random from the deck.
3. **Read the prompt** — short, cryptic, sometimes contradictory.
4. **Apply** to your current work — interpret the prompt liberally; force connection.
5. **Continue** with the unblocking direction.

Sample prompts:

- "Honor thy error as a hidden intention"
- "Use an old idea"
- "Distorting time"
- "What would your closest friend do?"
- "Look at the order in which you do things"
- "Don't be afraid of things because they're easy to do"
- "Repetition is a form of change"
- "Reverse"

The non-obvious operational insight is that **random / oblique prompts break self-reinforcing creative ruts.** When stuck, the mind tends to keep trying variations of the same approach. A random prompt — especially one that doesn't seem to apply — forces a different angle by interpretation. Sometimes the prompt is directly applicable; sometimes interpretation produces unexpected connections; either way, the rut is broken.

Eno developed the deck during his work as an ambient-music pioneer. David Bowie famously used it during the Berlin trilogy recordings. The technique has spread to designers, writers, programmers, and creative practitioners broadly.

A second insight: **the framework is anti-systematic on purpose.** Most creative-thinking tools (TRIZ, SCAMPER) are systematic. Oblique Strategies is deliberately oblique — the very randomness is the mechanism. When systematic approaches have been exhausted, randomness sometimes finds what they couldn't.

A third insight: **interpretation is part of the technique.** The prompts often don't directly apply. Forcing yourself to find an interpretation produces creative work. "Honor thy error as a hidden intention" applied to a piece you think is failing might mean: maybe the failure is actually pointing at something you didn't intend but should pursue.

A fourth insight: **the deck format is the medium.** Drawing randomly mimics a creative external voice. Apps and digital decks exist; physical deck has tactile + ritual quality some prefer.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The creative-rut iteration trap.** Variations of the same approach when a different approach is needed.
2. **The "no fresh ideas" stall.** Systematic generation exhausted; needing genuine novelty.
3. **The "stuck and frustrated" cycle.** Frustration tightening the rut rather than escaping it.

For musicians, writers, designers, programmers, and any creative practitioner facing blocks, Oblique Strategies is practical, lightweight, time-tested unblocker.

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE PROMPT-INTERPRETATION DISCIPLINE

   When you draw a card:

   1. READ literally.
      Take the prompt at face value.
      How does it apply to current work?

   2. READ metaphorically.
      What's the prompt's deeper suggestion?
      "Distorting time" might mean changing tempo,
      or jumping ahead in the narrative, or treating
      a static moment as if extended.

   3. READ as a question.
      What would I do if I followed this prompt?
      Even if I rejected it, what does it suggest
      about what I should do?

   4. INVERSION.
      What's the opposite of the prompt? Sometimes
      that's the unblocking move.

   The prompt isn't an instruction; it's a generative
   nudge.

THE SAMPLE PROMPTS (with interpretation)

   "HONOR THY ERROR AS A HIDDEN INTENTION"
       The mistake you've been trying to fix may
       actually be pointing at something. Examine
       it as if it were intentional.

   "USE AN OLD IDEA"
       Stop trying to invent new; recycle / repurpose
       something you've used before.

   "DON'T BE AFRAID OF THINGS BECAUSE THEY'RE EASY TO
   DO"
       Resistance to obvious solutions because they
       feel beneath you. Often the obvious thing is
       right.

   "REPETITION IS A FORM OF CHANGE"
       Adding more of the same can produce different
       effects (texture, intensity, monotony as
       statement).

   "REVERSE"
       Run the process backward; switch roles; change
       direction.

   "WHAT WOULD YOUR CLOSEST FRIEND DO?"
       Borrow another's perspective; imagine them
       attempting your work.

   Each prompt is interpretive scaffolding, not
   prescription.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE — STUCK ON A WRITING PROJECT

   Writer working on chapter 3 of a book; the chapter
   isn't working; multiple revisions haven't fixed it.

   Draws Oblique Strategy: "Look at the order in
   which you do things."

   Interpretation: maybe the chapter's problem is
   not its content but its position. What if it
   were chapter 5 instead of 3? What if the events
   in the chapter were reordered?

   Re-thinking reveals: this chapter is supposed to
   establish a relationship that the reader shouldn't
   meet until later; moving it works.

   Block broken. The prompt didn't directly say
   "move the chapter"; it suggested examining order;
   interpretation found the move.

THE WHEN-TO-USE SIGNALS

   Signs you're stuck and Oblique Strategies might
   help:

   Multiple iterations producing minimal change
   Frustration mounting; productivity dropping
   Same approach being applied repeatedly
   Conviction that "the answer is here, I just can't
   see it"
   Creative work felt to be in a rut

   Signs to use a different tool:
   Stuck on technique you don't yet have (need
   skill-building, not unblocking)
   Stuck because of fundamental wrong direction
   (need re-strategizing, not nudge)

THE COMMON FAILURE MODES

   1. LITERAL-ONLY READING
        Prompt doesn't directly apply; declared
        useless. Recovery: interpret metaphorically.

   2. MULTI-DRAW FISHING
        Drawing many cards; looking for "the right
        one." Recovery: 1-3 max; engage with what's
        drawn.

   3. PROMPT-AS-DEMAND
        Treating prompt as instruction; following
        rigidly. Recovery: prompt is suggestion;
        engage critically.

   4. TOO EARLY USE
        Drawing cards before genuinely stuck.
        Recovery: exhaust systematic approaches first.

   5. NO INTEGRATION
        Card prompts stay separate from work; no
        application. Recovery: actually try the
        prompt's suggestion in your work.

   6. SUPERSTITION
        Treating cards as oracular. Recovery: the
        cards are tools; the creative work is yours.

THE OPERATIONAL TEMPLATE

   Creative project: __________________________________

   Block description: _________________________________

   Drew card: _________________________________________

   Literal interpretation: ____________________________

   Metaphorical interpretation: _______________________

   Application to work: _______________________________

   Did it help? Y / N
   What changed: ______________________________________

   If not unblocked after 1-3 cards, try different
   tool or break.
```

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) Engage interpretively. The prompts rarely apply literally; the value is in interpretation. Force yourself to find a meaning even when none seems obvious. (2) Draw few; engage with what's drawn. Multi-card fishing for "the right card" defeats the technique. The randomness is the mechanism. (3) Use when stuck, not by default. The technique excels at unblocking; doesn't replace systematic approaches. Exhaust ordinary approaches first. (4) Treat cards as suggestions, not oracles. The creative work is yours; the cards are tools. The interpretation and application are what matter; the prompt itself is just a nudge.

# Irving Younger's 10 Commandments of Cross-Examination

**One-line summary:** Irving Younger's classic commandments for cross-examining a witness — Be brief; Use plain words; Use only leading questions; Be prepared; Listen; Don't quarrel; Don't ask the witness to explain; Don't ask one question too many; Save it for argument; Don't repeat direct.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing for or conducting cross-examination of an adverse witness, where the goal is witness control and the production of testimony useful to one's case, and where unstructured cross would let the witness control the room.

## Purpose

Irving Younger's (1976) 10 Commandments of Cross-Examination encode hard-won courtroom lessons about witness control. Each commandment addresses a specific failure mode: (1) Be brief — long cross loses the jury; (2) Use plain words — jargon distances; (3) Use only leading questions — the witness should not be educating the jury; (4) Be prepared — wing it and the witness wins; (5) Listen — the witness will sometimes hand you gold; (6) Don't quarrel — argument with a witness is jury-losing; (7) Don't ask the witness to explain — they'll explain away your point; (8) Don't ask one question too many — when you have the answer, stop; (9) Save it for argument — don't argue the case during cross; (10) Don't repeat direct testimony — you reinforce it. Each is anti-instinct; the instinct fails; the commandment succeeds.

## How To Use

Before cross: prepare the desired admissions from the witness as a series of leading questions, each calling for a yes or no. During cross: ask leading questions only (not 'Did you see X?' but 'You saw X, didn't you?'). Keep questions short and plain. When the witness gives the answer you wanted, stop — don't ask the follow-up that lets them explain away. When the witness gives a damaging answer to your case, don't argue; move on; save the argument for closing. When tempted to ask 'one more' to make sure the jury got it — don't; the jury got it. Listen for unexpected openings the witness creates and respond if they offer real gold, but don't probe blindly. The cumulative effect: witness controlled, key admissions secured, argument saved for closing.

# OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

**One-line summary:** A goal-setting framework — set aspirational Objectives and 3-5 measurable Key Results per objective; cascade and align across the organization; review quarterly.

**When to reach for it:** Organizational goal-setting in rapid-growth contexts; alignment across teams in growth-stage companies; replacing MBO when MBO has become bureaucratic.

## Purpose

OKRs originated at Intel under Andy Grove and were popularized at Google via John Doerr. The framework's discipline: Objectives are qualitative and aspirational; Key Results are quantitative and graded (typically 0-1, with 0.7 being a strong result for stretch goals). The cadence is typically quarterly. OKRs are public across the organization (alignment), separated from compensation (aspirational), and continuously visible (focus). The method's risks (cargo-cult adoption, measurement gaming) are well-known; correct use requires commitment to its design principles.

## How To Use

1) Set 3-5 Objectives at organizational level — qualitative, aspirational, time-bound. 2) For each, set 3-5 Key Results — quantitative, gradeable. 3) Cascade: each team sets aligned OKRs. 4) Make all OKRs public. 5) Check progress weekly; grade at end of quarter. 6) Set new OKRs each quarter, with strong learning from the previous. 7) Keep separate from individual performance review.

# Wang Yangming's Investigation of Things (Gewu)

**One-line summary:** Wang Yangming (1472-1529) reinterpreted Zhu Xi's "gewu" (investigation of things) as inward inquiry into the heart-mind's already-present principle (li), not outward investigation. Practice: silent sitting plus reflection on the unity of knowing and acting (zhixing heyi).

**When to reach for it:** Self-cultivation work, ethical reflection, or any inquiry where the practitioner suspects that the answer is internal and obscured by habits of misuse — rather than external and yet-to-be-discovered. Particularly relevant when Western analytical methods have exhausted their utility.

## Purpose

Zhu Xi's Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucianism took "gewu" (investigation of things) literally — study external things to discover their li (principle). Wang Yangming, after a famous failed attempt to investigate bamboo until enlightenment, reversed direction: the principle is already within the heart-mind; gewu is the work of rectifying the heart-mind so the principle becomes visible. Crucially, knowing and acting are unified — to truly know a virtue is to enact it. The practice is therefore inseparable from daily ethical life.

## How To Use

1. **Quiet sitting** — Begin or punctuate the day with brief silent sitting; let the heart-mind settle.
2. **Examine a specific situation** — Recall a recent ethical moment (where you acted, hesitated, failed, succeeded).
3. **Reflect on the heart-mind's response** — What did the heart-mind incline toward? Was the inclination obscured by self-interest, habit, fear?
4. **Clarify** — What does the heart-mind, rectified, indicate is right action here?
5. **Act accordingly** — Knowing without acting is not yet knowing.
6. **Cycle** — Daily; over years, the heart-mind becomes increasingly clear.

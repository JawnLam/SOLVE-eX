# Mimamsa Five Pramanas

**One-line summary:** Prabhakara Mimamsa's five accepted means of knowledge — perception, inference, analogy, postulation, and reliable testimony — used as an epistemic checklist.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating a knowledge-claim and asking what kind of justification supports it, especially in contexts where multiple modes of justification might apply or where one is being substituted for another.

## Purpose

The Mimamsa school (Kumarila Bhatta and Prabhakara, 6th-8th c. CE) developed one of the most rigorous Indian epistemologies. Prabhakara recognizes five pramanas (means of valid knowledge): (1) pratyaksa — direct perception; (2) anumana — inference from sign to inferred (smoke→fire); (3) upamana — comparison or analogy; (4) arthapatti — postulation from circumstance (an abductive move: 'the fat man Devadatta doesn't eat during the day yet stays fat; therefore he must eat at night'); (5) sabda — testimony of reliable persons/texts. Used as systematic epistemic checklist against which any knowledge-claim can be tested.

## How To Use

For the claim under evaluation, ask which pramana supports it. Is it grounded in direct perception (and if so, is the perception itself reliable)? Is it inferred from a sign (and is the sign reliably linked)? Is it analogical (and is the analogy valid)? Is it postulated to explain a puzzling fact (and is the postulation the simplest)? Is it accepted on testimony (and is the testimony reliable)? Where a claim has support from multiple pramanas, confidence rises; where it has support from only one, examine that one carefully. Where it has support from none, the claim is unjustified.

# MI Change Talk Elicitation (DARN-CAT)

**One-line summary:** Targeted listening for and amplification of client's own change talk — Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need (preparatory); Commitment, Activation, Taking steps (mobilizing).

**When to reach for it:** Behavior-change conversations in clinical, coaching, and counseling contexts; when persuasion has produced reactance and the client's own motivation must be elicited rather than imposed.

## Purpose

MI's central insight: people are more likely to change when they hear themselves articulate change reasons than when others articulate those reasons for them. Change talk has predictable categories: preparatory (DARN — desire, ability, reasons, need) and mobilizing (CAT — commitment, activation, taking steps). The clinician listens for any instance of change talk and reflects it, elaborates it, or asks for more — strengthening it rather than letting it pass.

## How To Use

1) Listen for change talk in any of the DARN-CAT categories. 2) When you hear it, reflect it back explicitly. 3) Ask elaboration questions: 'Tell me more about that.' 4) Summarize accumulated change talk. 5) Move from preparatory to mobilizing categories as the conversation develops. 6) Avoid pushing — let the change talk emerge.

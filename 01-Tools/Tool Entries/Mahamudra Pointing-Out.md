# Mahamudra Pointing-Out Instruction

**One-line summary:** Direct teacher-to-student transmission method in Kagyu Tibetan Buddhism. The teacher uses specific contemplative-experiential setups to "point out" the nature of bare awareness (rigpa); the student recognizes through direct experience rather than concept.

**When to reach for it:** Within a sustained student-teacher relationship in the Kagyu (Mahamudra) or Nyingma (Dzogchen) lineages of Tibetan Buddhism. Not a self-administered method. Requires significant prior contemplative training (preliminaries — *ngöndro*) before the pointing-out is given.

## Purpose

The nature of mind, in Mahamudra/Dzogchen understanding, is bare awareness itself — present in every moment but obscured by conceptual elaboration. No description can convey this; understanding from words is not the same as recognition. The pointing-out instruction is the moment when the qualified teacher uses experiential setups (specific instructions, brief contemplative experiments, sometimes shock or surprise) that allow the student to glimpse what is being indicated — directly, briefly, but unmistakably. The path is then to stabilize that recognition.

## How To Use

Pointing-out can only be given in person by a qualified teacher; outside that, here is the structural pattern:
1. **Preliminaries** — Student undertakes ngöndro (preliminary practices) and develops stable shamatha (concentration).
2. **The setting** — Teacher offers pointing-out at a moment of student readiness.
3. **The instruction** — Various specific forms (looking at the mind looking, recognizing thoughts as they arise, resting in awareness without grasping). The exact form depends on student and lineage.
4. **Recognition** — Student briefly recognizes bare awareness. The recognition is unmistakable when it occurs.
5. **Stabilization** — Long-term practice deepens and stabilizes recognition into sustained realization.

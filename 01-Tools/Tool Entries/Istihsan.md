# Istihsan (Juristic Preference)

**One-line summary:** Hanafi-school method of departing from strict qiyas-derived rulings when analogical results conflict with general principles of justice, public benefit, or established custom.

**When to reach for it:** A qiyas (analogical) derivation produces a ruling that conflicts with broader maqasid (purposes), maslaha (public benefit), or established custom — and the analogical strictness needs a principled escape valve.

## Purpose

Istihsan ('juristic preference' or 'seeking the better') is associated particularly with the Hanafi school but used (under different names) by others. It functions as a controlled escape valve from strict qiyas. When an analogical derivation would produce a ruling that conflicts with broader principles — necessity, public welfare, customary practice, justice — the jurist may prefer an alternative on principled grounds. Controversial: the Shafi'i school criticized istihsan as preferring opinion over text; defenders argue it is itself a principled method, not arbitrary. Methodologically: istihsan requires explicit identification of the conflicting principle and demonstration that it carries jurisprudential weight.

## How To Use

(1) Apply qiyas to the case at hand. (2) Examine the result — does it conflict with another principle (necessity, public benefit, custom, justice)? (3) If yes, identify the conflicting principle with precision. (4) Show that the conflicting principle has jurisprudential authority equal to or greater than the qiyas-derived result. (5) Articulate the istihsan: depart from the strict qiyas result and adopt the alternative grounded in the conflicting principle. The articulation must be transparent — istihsan is not pretextual. Used by Hanafi jurists in many areas of fiqh; the methodological discipline is what distinguishes principled istihsan from arbitrary preference.

# Patanjali Eight Limbs (Ashtanga) Developmental Scaffold

**One-line summary:** Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (~2nd c. BCE - 4th c. CE) lay out eight progressive disciplines — yama (ethical restraints), niyama (observances), asana (posture), pranayama (breath), pratyahara (sense withdrawal), dharana (concentration), dhyana (meditation), samadhi (absorption). Used as a cognitive-developmental ladder.

**When to reach for it:** Designing a long-term contemplative or somatic-cognitive development arc; locating where one is currently working; recognizing what scaffolding is missing. The eight-fold structure is widely adopted across yoga lineages, including secularized contemporary practice.

## Purpose

Most contemporary contemplative training drops directly into meditation (limb 7) without the prior limbs. Patanjali's insight: meditation builds on concentration, which builds on sense-withdrawal, which builds on breath, which builds on posture, which builds on observances, which builds on ethical restraints. Skipping the foundation produces wobble in the upper limbs. The ashtanga scaffold makes the sequence and dependency explicit.

## How To Use

1. **Yama** — Five ethical restraints (non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, brahmacharya, non-possessiveness). Daily life groundwork.
2. **Niyama** — Five observances (purity, contentment, austerity, self-study, surrender to the divine).
3. **Asana** — Postural practice that produces a steady, comfortable body.
4. **Pranayama** — Breath regulation to balance the autonomic system.
5. **Pratyahara** — Withdrawal of attention from sense-objects.
6. **Dharana** — Concentration on a single object.
7. **Dhyana** — Sustained meditation; the object remains but the meditator's separate experience dissolves.
8. **Samadhi** — Absorption; subject-object distinction dissolves.

In practice, work all limbs simultaneously, but recognize which is foundational for your current development.

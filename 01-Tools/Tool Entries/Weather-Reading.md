# Weather-Reading (Sailing)

**One-line summary:** Mariner's cognitive practice of reading weather signs before instrumented forecast — cloud morphology, wind shifts, swell direction, barometric trends, sea-surface texture, bird behavior.

**When to reach for it:** Operating in environments where instrumented forecasts are unavailable, unreliable, or too coarse-grained for the time-and-place decisions being made — and where many weak signals must be integrated into directional inference.

## Purpose

Weather-reading is the mariner's traditional cognitive practice of reading weather signs from the natural world. Cloud morphology (mares' tails predicting wind, mackerel sky predicting deterioration); wind shifts (veering versus backing, predicting low- or high-pressure approach); swell direction (telling of storms hundreds of miles away); barometric trends (rate of change as significant as level); sea-surface texture (cat's paws ahead predicting wind line); bird behavior (frigatebirds before storms, gannets diving where fish run). The cognitive method: integrate many weak signals into a directional inference about what's coming. Survives in modern sailing as a backup-cognitive-method when instruments fail and as a primary method for short-term tactical decisions where forecasts are too coarse.

## How To Use

Build a habit of looking at sky, water, wind, and life-signs continuously while on the water. Don't focus on one signal; notice many. Cloud type and direction of movement. Wind direction and trend (use a wind vane and feel on the cheek). Swell direction and period. Barometric pressure now and trend over hours. Sea state and texture. Bird behavior. Each is a weak signal; together they integrate. With experience, an interior model forms: 'wind shifting right and clouds building from the southwest with the barometer falling — front approaching in 6-8 hours.' Initially, use a reference text (Watts) to calibrate signal-to-prediction. With practice, the reading becomes intuitive. Useful as both primary and backup decision-method in any field-operation context.

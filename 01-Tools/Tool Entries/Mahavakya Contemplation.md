# Mahavakya Contemplation

**One-line summary:** Sustained contemplative engagement with one of the four Vedantic 'great sayings' — Prajnanam Brahma, Aham Brahmasmi, Tat Tvam Asi, Ayam Atma Brahma — as stable abiding in the recognition each points to.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging Vedantic contemplation where a terse declarative pointing-statement is the practice-object, and the work is not understanding the saying but abiding as the recognition it points to.

## Purpose

Each of the four Vedantic mahavakyas ('great sayings') from the Upanishads encapsulates the non-dual recognition: 'Prajnanam Brahma' (Consciousness is Brahman); 'Aham Brahmasmi' (I am Brahman); 'Tat Tvam Asi' (Thou art That); 'Ayam Atma Brahma' (This Self is Brahman). Each Vedanta lineage associates one with one of the four Vedas. The contemplation is not analytical — one does not 'work out' what the saying means. The contemplation is stable abiding in the recognition the saying points to. Used as the central practice of jnana yoga.

## How To Use

Select a mahavakya, traditionally the one transmitted by one's teacher. Sit quietly. Recite the saying internally. Don't analyze. Don't paraphrase. Let the saying point. Notice what is being pointed to. Rest there. When mind drifts, recite again. Over time, the saying becomes less a statement and more a doorway. The recognition the saying points to stabilizes as recognition-itself, independent of the saying. This is the saying's intended purpose: to render itself unnecessary. Traditionally given in transmission with the right context and confirmation.

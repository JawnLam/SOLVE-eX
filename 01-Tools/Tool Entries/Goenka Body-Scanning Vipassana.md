# Goenka Body-Scanning Vipassana

**One-line summary:** Goenka/U Ba Khin lineage technique — systematic head-to-feet (and back) scanning of body sensations, observing each with equanimity as anicca (impermanence) is the object.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging extended seated meditation where the body itself is the practice-object and the cognitive payload is direct experience of impermanence rather than abstract concept of it.

## Purpose

In the S. N. Goenka / U Ba Khin lineage of Theravada Vipassana, the body-scan is the primary technique. Attention sweeps systematically from head to feet (and back) over a fixed duration, observing each region's sensations (or apparent absence of sensation) with equanimity. The cognitive payload: anicca (impermanence) is the object. Sensations are observed to arise-pass-arise-pass; the scanner's relationship to them — clinging or pushing-away — is what binds. By holding equanimity across the sweep, the practitioner trains the relationship rather than the sensations. Distinct from Mahasi noting (which uses six sense doors) and from open-awareness practice.

## How To Use

Sit upright on a cushion or chair. Begin attention at the crown of the head. Notice whatever sensation is present (or apparent absence). Move attention systematically: forehead, sides of head, face, neck, shoulders, down each arm, torso (front, back), pelvis, down each leg, to the feet. Then reverse. Spend 30 seconds to several minutes on each region depending on retreat structure. Throughout, maintain equanimity: pleasant sensations are not preferred, unpleasant ones not pushed away. The discipline is the equanimity, not the sweep itself. Typically practiced 1-3 hours daily, 10+ hours daily on retreat. Tradition: 10-day silent residential retreat free of charge.

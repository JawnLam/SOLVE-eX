# Trekchö (Cutting Through Solidity)

**One-line summary:** Dzogchen's first major practice — resting in the discovered nature of awareness without effort to maintain or modify it, the resting itself dissolving apparent solidity of phenomena.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging Dzogchen practice after pointing-out introduction by a qualified Nyingma teacher, where the work is to stabilize the discovered nature of awareness rather than to cultivate a state.

## Purpose

Trekchö ('cutting through solidity' or 'spontaneous cutting of tension') is the first of Dzogchen's two main practices (the second being Tögal). After the disciple receives pointing-out instructions and recognizes the nature of awareness (rigpa), trekchö is the practice of resting in that recognition without effort to maintain or modify it. The resting itself dissolves the apparent solidity (the seeming reification) of phenomena and self. Distinct from Mahamudra pointing-out (which has Kagyu lineage and somewhat different methodology); trekchö is specifically Nyingma/Dzogchen. Foundational for the subsequent practice of Tögal.

## How To Use

Within Nyingma Dzogchen lineage: after receiving pointing-out instruction and confirmation that one has recognized rigpa, settle into the recognized nature. Don't try to maintain it (that introduces grasping). Don't try to modify it (that introduces effort). Don't watch for it (that introduces a subject-object split). Simply rest. When recognition fades into ordinary thinking, gently return — without effort or apology — to the recognized state. Practiced in formal sitting and increasingly integrated with all activity. The discipline is the non-doing; the difficulty is that the mind keeps trying to do something. Sustained over years with teacher's guidance.

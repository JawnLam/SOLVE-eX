# EMDR 8-Phase Protocol

**One-line summary:** The clinical protocol for EMDR therapy — 8 phases from history-taking through reprocessing of traumatic memories using bilateral stimulation, to integration and follow-up.

**When to reach for it:** Trauma therapy for trained EMDR clinicians; treating PTSD, complex trauma, anxiety disorders with traumatic origin.

## Purpose

EMDR therapy follows a specific 8-phase protocol that distinguishes it from generic talk therapy. Phase 1 (history) identifies targets; Phase 2 (preparation) builds resources and stabilizes; Phase 3 (assessment) locks in the specific target; Phase 4 (desensitization) is the bilateral-stimulation reprocessing; Phase 5 (installation) strengthens positive cognitions; Phase 6 (body scan) checks for residual somatic activation; Phase 7 (closure) reorients; Phase 8 (re-evaluation) follows up. The protocol structure is what makes EMDR a treatment, not just an exposure technique.

## How To Use

1) Phase 1: History and treatment planning. 2) Phase 2: Preparation and stabilization. 3) Phase 3: Target assessment (image, NC, PC, VOC, SUD, body location). 4) Phase 4: Desensitization with bilateral stimulation. 5) Phase 5: Installation of positive cognition. 6) Phase 6: Body scan. 7) Phase 7: Closure. 8) Phase 8: Re-evaluation in next session.

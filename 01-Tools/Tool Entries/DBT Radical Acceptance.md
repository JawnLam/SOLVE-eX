# DBT Radical Acceptance

**One-line summary:** Fully accept reality as it is, including unchangeable past and present circumstances — distinct from approval, but a prerequisite to effective action.

**When to reach for it:** Suffering arising from refusal to accept what cannot be changed; grief work; trauma recovery; situations where struggle against reality compounds the pain.

## Purpose

Suffering often has two components: pain (from the event) and struggle (against acceptance of the event). Radical Acceptance addresses the second. It does not mean approval, agreement, or passivity — it means recognizing that reality is what it is and that fighting acceptance of that fact deepens suffering. The stance is contemplatively related to Buddhist non-attachment but operationalized for therapeutic context. From acceptance, effective action becomes possible; without acceptance, action is reactive and brittle.

## How To Use

1) Identify what you are refusing to accept. 2) Notice the struggle and its costs. 3) Practice statements: 'This is what is.' 'I cannot change what has happened.' 'I can choose what to do now.' 4) Body practices: relax against the reality rather than tensing into it. 5) Recognize that acceptance is a recurring choice, not a one-time event.

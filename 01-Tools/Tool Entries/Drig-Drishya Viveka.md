# Drig-Drishya Viveka (Seer-Seen Discrimination)

**One-line summary:** Advaita Vedanta inquiry method — distinguish in each cognitive event the seer (drig) from the seen (drishya), recursing until the unconditioned seer is recognized.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging contemplative-philosophical inquiry into the nature of the experiencer, where direct attention (Atma Vichara) alone is not yielding clarity and systematic recursive analysis can.

## Purpose

Drig-Drishya Viveka (often attributed to Shankara) treats every cognitive event as having two poles: the seer (drig) and the seen (drishya). The body is seen by the mind; the mind is seen by deeper awareness; that awareness in turn can be made an object of attention by something more fundamental. The method recurses: at each level, whatever is observed must not be the ultimate observer (since it is being observed). The regress terminates when the observer can no longer be made an object — there one rests as the unconditioned seer. Related to but methodologically distinct from Ramana Maharshi's Atma Vichara (which is direct attention rather than systematic recursion).

## How To Use

In quiet sitting, choose any object of cognition (a sensation, a thought, an emotion). Ask: is this the seer or the seen? It is the seen — it is observed. Then ask: what observes this? Mind. Now make 'the mind' the object: is mind the seer or the seen? Mind is observed; it is the seen. What observes mind? Awareness. Now examine 'awareness' — can it be observed? If yes, it too is the seen, and something more fundamental observes it. Continue until you reach what cannot be made an object — there you rest. The regress is short; the difficulty is honesty about whether the supposed-seer is actually being seen.

# BCG Growth-Share Matrix

**One-line summary:** Classify a firm's business units on market-growth × market-share axes into Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, and Dogs — and allocate capital accordingly.

**When to reach for it:** Multi-business-unit portfolio review; capital-allocation decisions; product-line pruning analysis. Now considered dated by some but still pedagogically useful.

## Purpose

The BCG matrix encodes a portfolio-management theory: invest in Stars (high growth, high share), milk Cash Cows (low growth, high share) to fund Stars and Question Marks, divest Dogs (low growth, low share), and choose carefully among Question Marks (high growth, low share). The framework forces the multi-business firm to make explicit decisions about cross-subsidization rather than fund every unit reflexively.

## How To Use

1) Plot each business unit on the 2x2 by current market growth and relative market share. 2) Classify into the four quadrants. 3) Apply the canonical strategic prescription for each. 4) Refresh annually as positions shift. 5) Treat as a starting point for portfolio strategy, not the final answer — many businesses defy easy classification.

# Ecosystem Thinking

**One-line summary:** Treat a business/social/biological system as an ecosystem of interdependent species — analyze keystone roles, niche structure, and co-evolutionary dynamics.

**When to reach for it:** Platform strategy, supply-chain design, regional-economic analysis, organizational dependency-mapping, conservation planning; any analysis where the unit of action is a network of interdependent agents.

## Purpose

Single-firm strategy treats competitors as adversaries and the firm as a unit. Ecosystem thinking treats the broader network as the relevant unit, with firms playing roles (keystone, dominator, niche player, hub landlord). Strategic moves in an ecosystem-aware frame consider not just direct competition but the health of the network the firm depends on. The frame transfers naturally from biological ecosystems and brings the toolkit (keystone species, niche analysis, food web dynamics).

## How To Use

1) Map the ecosystem: who are the agents, what flows between them, what depends on what? 2) Identify keystone agents (whose removal would collapse the network) and niche agents (specialized roles). 3) Classify your firm's role. 4) Strategically: a keystone shouldn't behave like a dominator; a niche player shouldn't behave like a keystone. 5) Invest in ecosystem health, not just firm health.

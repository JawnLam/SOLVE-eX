# Compassion-Focused Therapy Three-Systems Model

**One-line summary:** Paul Gilbert's emotion-regulation map distinguishing three motivational/affective systems — Threat (red), Drive (blue), Soothing (green). Diagnoses an under-developed soothing system and uses compassion-imagery practices to build it.

**When to reach for it:** When a client's threat system is chronically over-activated (anxiety, shame, self-criticism) and their soothing system is undeveloped. Common in trauma, depression, and high-shame populations. Evidence base for shame-prone presentations.

## Purpose

Many psychological problems are framed by Gilbert as imbalance among three evolved affective systems. The threat system (alarm) and drive system (goal-pursuit) are well-developed; the soothing/affiliation system (safe connection, calm) is undertrained. The clinical move is to cultivate the soothing system through specific practices — compassion imagery, compassionate self-talk, embodied breathing — that activate parasympathetic pathways.

## How To Use

1. **Psychoeducation** — Map the three systems and their evolutionary functions.
2. **Soothing-rhythm breathing** — Slow, deep breathing to activate parasympathetic state. Daily practice.
3. **Compassionate-image work** — Build an internal compassionate figure (ideal compassionate other) and engage with it.
4. **Compassionate self** — Shift from threat-mode self-talk to compassionate self-talk in specific situations.
5. **Multimodal practice** — Compassion-letter writing, compassionate-other dialogues, body-based grounding.
6. **Generalization** — Bring soothing-system access into real-time stressors.

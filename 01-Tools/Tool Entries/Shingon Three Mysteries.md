# Shingon Three Mysteries (Sanmitsu)

**One-line summary:** Japanese Shingon Esoteric Buddhism's integration practice — simultaneous engagement of body (mudra), speech (mantra), and mind (mandala visualization).

**When to reach for it:** Engaging esoteric Buddhist practice in the Shingon tradition where the integration of three modalities — somatic gesture, vocal mantra, and mental imagery — is the cognitive method, rather than working through one at a time.

## Purpose

Shingon ('true word'), founded by Kukai (Kobo Daishi, ~835 CE), teaches that the cosmic Buddha Dainichi (Vairocana) is continuously expressing reality through three mysteries: body (form), speech (sound), and mind (consciousness). The practitioner's three mysteries are mysteries because their integration brings the practitioner into resonance with Dainichi. The technique: simultaneously perform a specific mudra (hand-gesture), recite a specific mantra (sacred sound), and visualize a specific mandala (symbolic image), each coordinated with the others. Kukai's claim: this enables Buddhahood in this very body (sokushin jōbutsu).

## How To Use

Within Shingon lineage transmission: receive empowerment for a specific deity-practice. Learn the mudra (specific finger and hand positions), the mantra (in Siddham or Sanskrit pronunciation), and the visualization (specific deity, color, posture, attributes). In formal practice, perform all three simultaneously: hands in mudra, mouth reciting mantra, mind holding visualization. The coordination is initially difficult; with practice it becomes integrated. The integration is the cognitive payload — the practitioner's body-speech-mind aligning with cosmic body-speech-mind. Daily practice may be 30-60 minutes; extended ritual takes hours. Outside lineage, study as cultural-religious phenomenon.

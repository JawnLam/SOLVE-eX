# Dhikr (Sufi Remembrance)

**One-line summary:** Repeated invocation of divine names or formulas (Allah; La ilaha illa Allah; etc.), aloud or silently, sometimes synchronized with breath or movement. Anchors awareness; reshapes attentional habits.

**When to reach for it:** Within Sufi practice as part of daily devotional life; also in formal *hadra* gatherings where the group performs dhikr together (often with rhythm, movement, or music depending on the *tariqah*). Forms vary widely — silent Naqshbandi, vocal Chishti, kinetic Mevlevi.

## Purpose

The Qur'an enjoins remembrance (*dhikr*) of God; Sufi practice develops this into a structured contemplative method. By repeatedly invoking the divine name, the practitioner trains attention to return continually to its source; over time, the dhikr becomes self-acting and the practitioner experiences continuous awareness of the divine presence. The form (silent, vocal, kinetic) is matched to lineage and to the practitioner's temperament.

## How To Use

1. **Receive the dhikr** from a qualified teacher (within lineages where this is the protocol).
2. **Form**: choose the name or formula (Allah; La ilaha illa Allah; Ya Hayy Ya Qayyum; etc.) per teacher's instruction.
3. **Sit or stand** in a position conducive to the form.
4. **Repeat** — silently or aloud — with attention on the heart or wherever the lineage specifies.
5. **Synchronize with breath** if directed (often the formula on inhale, name on exhale).
6. **Continue** — typical sessions are 30-60 minutes; some forms are continuous throughout daily life.
7. **Group dhikr** (hadra) — pace, rhythm, and movement intensify under collective practice.

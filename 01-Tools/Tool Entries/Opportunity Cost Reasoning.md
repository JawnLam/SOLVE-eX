# Opportunity Cost Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Every yes implicitly says no to alternatives — evaluate decisions by what they preclude, not just by what they directly cost.

**When to reach for it:** Resource-allocation decisions (time, attention, capital, hiring slots) where the constrained dimension makes the foregone alternative the relevant comparison.

## Purpose

Accounting cost is what you pay; opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative you didn't pursue. The latter is almost always the more relevant comparison for strategic decisions because cash costs underrepresent the full economic consequence. The discipline is naming the foregone alternative explicitly — vague 'we could have done other things' framing produces poor decisions.

## How To Use

1) Identify the resource being committed (hours, dollars, slots). 2) Name the single best alternative use of that resource. 3) Estimate the value of that alternative. 4) Compare the proposed action's value against the alternative's, not against zero. 5) Choose the higher-value option, accepting that 'do nothing' is sometimes the right answer.

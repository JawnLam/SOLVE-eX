# Panchakosha Analysis (Five-Sheath Inquiry)

**One-line summary:** Vedantic decomposition of the self into five concentric sheaths (koshas) — food, vital, mental, intellect, bliss — each examined as object rather than ultimate subject.

**When to reach for it:** Inquiring into the layered structure of personal identity, especially when the question 'what am I?' resolves differently at different levels of subtlety.

## Purpose

The *Taittiriya Upanishad* introduces the five koshas — concentric sheaths through which the unconditioned Self appears as a person. Annamaya kosha (food-sheath, the physical body sustained by nourishment); pranamaya (vital-sheath, the energy of breath and life); manomaya (mental-sheath, sense-mind and emotions); vijnanamaya (intellect-sheath, discrimination and witness); anandamaya (bliss-sheath, the subtlest covering). The contemplative method: inquire into each in turn, recognizing that each is an object of awareness and thus not the ultimate subject, until the awareness underlying all five is recognized.

## How To Use

Sit quietly. Begin with attention to the physical body — its weight, sensations, breath in the tissues. Note: this is annamaya, the food-sheath. It is observed; it is not the observer. Move to attention to vital energy — the flow of breath, the alertness, the felt aliveness. Note: this is pranamaya. It too is observed. Move to mind — thoughts, emotions, the chatter and silence. This is manomaya. Observed. Move to intellect-witness — the one who is observing all of this. Is the witness itself a kosha? It is vijnanamaya, still a sheath. Move to the subtlest bliss-quality. This too is anandamaya. What underlies all? Rest there.

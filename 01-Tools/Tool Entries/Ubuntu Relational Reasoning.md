# Ubuntu Relational Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Sub-Saharan African philosophical frame: 'umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu' — a person is a person through other persons. Decisions are evaluated by their effect on the relational web.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging ethical or institutional decisions where individual-rational utility calculation feels rootless or extractive, and where the cognitive frame of personhood-as-relational-web is more truthful to the situation.

## Purpose

Ubuntu (Zulu/Xhosa, with cognates across many Bantu languages) is a sub-Saharan African philosophical frame articulated in scholarly form by Mogobe Ramose, John Mbiti, Desmond Tutu, and others. The core: 'umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu' — a person is a person through other persons. Personhood is not given by individual birth but constituted in the relational web. Decisions are not evaluated by individual-rational calculation but by what they do to/for the web of relations. Action is good insofar as it deepens ubuntu; bad insofar as it diminishes it. Used as theoretical foundation for the South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission's restorative-justice method.

## How To Use

As stance: when facing an ethical or institutional decision, reframe the question from 'what's the most rational option for me/us as individuals?' to 'what does each option do to the relational web?' Identify the web: who is in relation here, including extended kin, community, future generations, ancestors? Trace each option's effect — does it deepen ubuntu (build trust, repair fracture, sustain mutual flourishing) or diminish it (extract value, sever ties, prioritize one over the web)? The frame disciplines decisions that look efficient by individual-utility metrics but are corrosive to the relational ground. Particularly important in organizational, restorative-justice, and policy contexts.

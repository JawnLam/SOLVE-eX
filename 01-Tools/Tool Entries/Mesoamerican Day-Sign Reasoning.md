# Tonalamatl / Tzolkin Day-Sign Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Mesoamerican 260-day sacred calendar where each day's name+number carries divinatory and decisional meaning; consulted by daykeepers for life decisions.

**When to reach for it:** Studying or engaging Mesoamerican divinatory cognition, or seeking a structured frame for life-decision reflection that leverages calendar-day-as-question rather than calendar-date-as-coordinate.

## Purpose

The 260-day calendar (Aztec Tōnalpōhualli, K'iche' Maya Cholq'ij, Yucatec Tzolkin) couples 20 day-names with 13 numbers, generating 260 distinct day-signatures. Each carries traditional associations — auspicious activities, attentions required, energetic quality. Daykeepers (ajq'ijab') consult the calendar for naming children, scheduling agriculture, divining illness causes, and addressing conflicts. Distinct from Western horoscope: the calendar is a cognitive scaffold for situating action in cyclical time, not a fate-predictor.

## How To Use

Encounter the day through its sign (e.g., 7 Imox/Crocodile, 4 Aq'ab'al/Dawn). Sit with the day's traditional associations as a meditation. Ask: what does this day invite? What does its quality reveal about the decision I face? Use as a frame for daily examen and for situating major decisions. Practitioners insist the calendar is not deterministic — it surfaces qualities for the practitioner to engage with, not outcomes to fear. Note: traditional consultation belongs to trained ajq'ijab'; outsider use should be respectful, descriptive, and tradition-aware.

# Affinity Diagram (KJ Method)

**One-line summary:** Organize a large set of ideas/observations by clustering similar items into themes that emerge from the data rather than from a predetermined taxonomy.

**When to reach for it:** Synthesizing large numbers of interview notes, brainstormed ideas, customer feedback items, or research findings into emergent themes.

## Purpose

Top-down categorization imposes an a priori structure that may not fit the data. The KJ Method (Kawakita Jiro) clusters from the bottom up: each item gets a sticky note, items are silently grouped by similarity, and themes are named only after clusters stabilize. The result is grounded in the data rather than the analyst's expectations. The method also engages group members non-verbally, reducing dominance effects.

## How To Use

1) One idea/observation per sticky note. 2) Silently arrange notes on the wall by similarity. 3) Move notes when you see better clusters; expect rearrangement. 4) When clusters stabilize, name each. 5) Identify relationships between clusters. 6) Use the emergent structure for next-step analysis.

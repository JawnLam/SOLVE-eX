# Alexander Technique

**One-line summary:** F. Matthias Alexander's (1890s-1940s) method of conscious-control re-education. Teaches "inhibition" of habitual reflex plus "directing" — mental cues that release patterns of postural misuse.

**When to reach for it:** Chronic tension or pain caused by habit (musicians, actors, computer workers), Parkinson's gait/balance support, performance enhancement, or as a general somatic-awareness practice. Best learned in one-on-one lessons with a certified teacher.

## Purpose

Most postural patterns are reflexive — initiated before conscious thought. Alexander discovered that the act of *intention* (preparing to speak, stand, sit) triggers a startle-pattern of neck tension that cascades through the whole body. The Technique trains a pause ("inhibition") between intention and action, then "directing" — gentle mental cues (neck free, head forward and up, back lengthen and widen) that release the pattern. Over time, the new pattern becomes habitual.

## How To Use

1. **Take a lesson** — the teacher's hands guide directly; this can't be learned solely from books.
2. **Inhibition practice** — Before any movement, pause. Notice the impulse to act. Don't act.
3. **Directing** — Send a mental cue toward release: "Neck free, head forward and up, back lengthen and widen."
4. **Move while directing** — Carry the directions into the action (standing, sitting, speaking).
5. **Daily practice** — Apply during ordinary activities: chair-work, walking, computer use.

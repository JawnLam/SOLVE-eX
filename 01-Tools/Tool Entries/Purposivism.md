# Purposivism (Constitutional / Statutory)

**One-line summary:** Interpret legal text in light of the purpose the legislature was attempting to achieve — text is evidence of purpose, not the sole anchor.

**When to reach for it:** Statutory interpretation where text is ambiguous or applied to circumstances the legislature didn't anticipate; arguments that text alone produces results contrary to evident purpose.

## Purpose

Purposivism contrasts with textualism: where text alone produces unclear or absurd results, the legislative purpose should guide interpretation. Hart and Sacks's classic articulation: assume the legislature was reasonable and pursuing reasonable purposes; ask which interpretation best serves those purposes. Breyer extends this with active liberty (the constitutional purpose of democratic self-government). Critics argue that purpose attribution is judicial activism by another name.

## How To Use

1) Identify the statute's purpose from text, structure, legislative history, and context. 2) When text is ambiguous, ask which interpretation best serves that purpose. 3) When text seems to produce absurd results, consider whether the legislature would have written differently if it had anticipated. 4) Use purpose to gap-fill rather than to override clear text.

# Image Theater (Boal)

**One-line summary:** Companion technique to Forum Theatre. Participants sculpt their bodies (and others' bodies) into still images representing oppressions or aspirations. Surfaces unconscious assumptions through embodied tableaux; transitions into Forum scenes via "dynamization."

**When to reach for it:** Early-stage workshop exploration of a theme — when participants need to surface what they feel/sense before verbalizing. Useful when verbal discussion has reached its limit; the body knows what the mouth can't yet say.

## Purpose

Speech often defaults to ideological scripts; bodies do not. Image Theater leverages this: a participant uses other participants as clay to sculpt an image of an oppression or hope. The sculpted image carries information the speaking participant didn't know they had. When the image is then shown to the workshop, observers also see what wasn't articulated — and the dialogue that follows is grounded in shared visceral data rather than abstract argument.

## How To Use

1. **Sculptor and clay** — One participant (sculptor) silently positions others (clay) into a still image. No words.
2. **Show the image** — Other participants observe; the sculptor stands aside.
3. **Discussion of what's seen** — What's the relationship in the image? Who has power? What's missing? What's possible?
4. **Dynamization** — Bring the image to motion in slow, small movements. What does it want to do?
5. **Transition** — Image becomes the starting point for a Forum Theatre scene (if pursuing change-experimentation).

# ACT Hexaflex (Six Core Processes)

**One-line summary:** The six interconnected core processes of psychological flexibility — defusion, acceptance, present-moment contact, self-as-context, values, committed action.

**When to reach for it:** ACT therapy planning; teaching ACT to trainees or clients; case conceptualization; identifying which process is currently underdeveloped.

## Purpose

The Hexaflex unifies the six ACT processes into a single map. Each process is both a target and a tool: clients can be stuck because any of the six is underdeveloped (cognitive fusion, experiential avoidance, dominance of conceptualized past/future, attachment to conceptualized self, vague values, inaction). Therapy aims at flexibility — fluent movement across all six. The map provides a diagnostic frame: which process needs the most work today?

## How To Use

1) Conceptualize client presentation across the six processes. 2) Identify which is most underdeveloped. 3) Choose ACT interventions targeting that process. 4) Move flexibly across the six as new material emerges. 5) Periodically re-assess the full hexaflex to track flexibility gains.

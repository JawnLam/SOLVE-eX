# Comparative Advantage

**One-line summary:** Even when one party is better at everything, both gain from specialization — the relevant comparison is opportunity cost, not absolute ability.

**When to reach for it:** Trade-policy analysis; division-of-labor decisions in teams; partnership structuring; outsourcing/insourcing debates; founder/employee task allocation in startups.

## Purpose

Ricardo's insight contradicts the absolute-advantage intuition: even the best generalist gains by specializing in the activity where their *relative* advantage is greatest, leaving other activities to lower-absolute-skill counterparts. Inside teams: founders who do everything themselves underperform founders who specialize where their relative advantage is highest. The tool is the explicit opportunity-cost framing of every 'should I do it or delegate?' decision.

## How To Use

1) For each pair of agents and each task, compute the opportunity cost of doing the task (what else could each be doing?). 2) Assign each task to the agent with the lower opportunity cost — not the higher absolute skill. 3) Both parties gain. 4) Apply to team task allocation, partnerships, and outsourcing.

## Sources

- Ricardo, D. (1817) *On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation*.

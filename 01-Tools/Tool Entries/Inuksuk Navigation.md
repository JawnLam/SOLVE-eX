# Inuksuk Navigation

**One-line summary:** Inuit stone-figure landmarks as externalized cognitive aids for wayfinding, danger-marking, hunting choreography, and cache-marking.

**When to reach for it:** Studying or designing externalized-spatial cognition systems where information must be transmitted across time to future travelers (including future-self) in featureless or extreme environments.

## Purpose

An inuksuk ('that which acts in the capacity of a human') is built from stones to mark direction, indicate danger, signal cache locations, choreograph caribou drives, or commemorate persons. The cognitive method: rather than carrying or memorizing information, externalize it into the landscape in a form readable by anyone who shares the encoding tradition. Distinct configurations carry distinct meanings; an inuksuk speaks across centuries to those who know to read it.

## How To Use

As an active method: identify information that must persist over time, be readable by others (including non-literate future readers), and survive environmental conditions. Externalize it into the landscape using locally-available, persistent materials in distinguishable configurations. Train the readership in the encoding. Generalizes from Arctic navigation to any context where information must be place-bound and tradition-readable rather than text-bound and individually-memorized — wayfinding markers, cairns, trail blazes, monument-scale signage.

# Nava-Vidha Bhakti (Nine Forms of Devotion)

**One-line summary:** Bhakti yoga's nine-form catalog of devotional practice — listening, singing, remembering, serving, worshiping, prostrating, serving, friending, self-surrendering — used as a daily-rotation cognitive frame.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging structured devotional practice where the practitioner wants a varied and complete map of devotional modes rather than reliance on a single favored mode.

## Purpose

From the *Bhagavata Purana* (7.5.23-24), the nine forms of bhakti are: (1) sravana — listening to sacred narrative; (2) kirtana — singing or chanting praise; (3) smarana — remembering the divine throughout activity; (4) pada-sevana — service to feet/presence; (5) archana — formal worship/puja; (6) vandana — prostration and reverence; (7) dasya — attitude of servitude; (8) sakhya — friendship with the divine; (9) atma-nivedana — total self-surrender. Used as a developmental map (each stage prepares the next) and as a daily-rotation cognitive frame (different practices on different days, or all nine in compressed form).

## How To Use

Map the nine modes onto your week or month. Sravana on Mondays — read or listen to sacred texts. Kirtana on Tuesdays — chant. Smarana woven through each day — remember during ordinary activity. Pada-sevana on weekends — actual service in a temple or to a teacher's lineage. Archana on Wednesdays — formal worship. Vandana daily on rising and sleeping. Dasya as an internal attitude during work. Sakhya in personal prayer. Atma-nivedana at moments of difficulty. Over time, the modes interpenetrate; you don't need the explicit rotation, but the catalog ensures none atrophies.

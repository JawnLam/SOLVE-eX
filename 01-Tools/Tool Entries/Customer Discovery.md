# Customer Discovery

**One-line summary:** The encompassing early-stage validation method — Steve Blank's — for surfacing whether a problem actually exists in the market and who has it, through structured conversations with potential users, behavioral observation, and disciplined hypothesis-testing; sub-methods (Mom Test, Jobs-to-Be-Done, persona work, segment analysis) live inside this umbrella.

**When to reach for it:** Pre-MVP product / business idea-validation; early-stage founder decisions about which problem to solve; segment-and-persona work where the segment isn't yet known; pivot-or-persist decisions that depend on what users actually need vs. what the founder assumed; and any context where you need an organized approach to "go talk to users" that doesn't collapse into anecdote-gathering.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Customer Discovery** is the umbrella method for problem-and-market validation. Within it, specific sub-methods operate at finer grain:

- **Mom Test** — interview protocol for getting truthful behavioral data from conversations (three rules: talk about their life not your idea; ask about past specifics not future generics; talk less listen more).
- **Jobs-to-Be-Done** — reframing customer behavior as "hiring" a product/service to do a specific job, redefining competitive analysis and innovation focus.
- **Persona and segment work** — clustering observed behaviors into representative archetypes for cross-segment comparison.
- **Behavior-vs-opinion discipline** — treating past behavior as data and future-tense opinions as noise.
- **Quantification of demand** — moving from individual interview data to aggregate signals (commitments / pre-orders / waitlist sign-ups / pilot fees) that constitute evidence of demand.

The procedure at the umbrella layer:

1. **State the problem hypothesis explicitly.** A testable claim about who has what problem in what context. "Founders of small-team revenue-bearing SaaS companies face conversion-rate cliffs around the 100-employee customer size" is testable; "we need to improve conversion" is not.
2. **Identify the target user segment.** Specifically. Demographics + behavior + context. Avoid segments defined as "people who would benefit from X" — that's a hypothesis, not a segment.
3. **Design the discovery conversations.** Apply Mom Test rules. Aim for ≥10 conversations across the segment. Cold-network where possible to avoid politeness contamination.
4. **Observe behavior, not just hear opinions.** What do users actually do today? What workarounds have they built? What does their day look like in the relevant domain?
5. **Look for repeated patterns.** Patterns across ≥3 independent users are evidence; single anecdotes are not.
6. **Test for commitments.** Time, money, social capital, follow-up scheduled. Compliments are noise; commitments are signal.
7. **Form a refined hypothesis or pivot.** If the data does not support the original hypothesis, refine it or abandon it. The honest failure mode is "no one actually has this problem" — surface it before building.

The non-obvious operational insight is that **Customer Discovery is a research method, not a sales method.** The conversations are not pitches. The goal is to learn what is true about the user's world, not to validate the founder's idea. Founders who treat discovery conversations as "tell people about my idea and see if they want it" produce contaminated data and false-positive validation.

A second insight: **the umbrella relationships matter.** Mom Test is the conversation protocol; Jobs-to-Be-Done is the analytical frame for understanding the conversations; persona work is the synthesis step; the umbrella Customer Discovery is the method that connects them. Using one without the others tends to produce thinner outputs — Mom Test conversations without JTBD analysis produce raw transcripts; JTBD analysis without Mom Test conversations produces speculation; either without persona work produces ungeneralizable anecdotes.

A third insight: **Customer Discovery is precursor to Customer Validation, not a substitute for it.** Discovery surfaces whether a problem exists; Validation tests whether enough people have it that a business can be built. Founders who confuse Discovery with Validation ship product based on n=5 interview enthusiasm and discover the market is smaller than the seed round assumed.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The "I'll just build it and see" anti-method.** Skipping Discovery entirely and building based on assumption. Discovery costs weeks of conversations; skipping costs months or years of misallocated build.
2. **The anecdote-collection trap.** Talking to 3 friends who liked the idea is not Discovery. The umbrella imposes structure: representative segment, ≥10 conversations, Mom Test rules, behavior-not-opinion, patterns-not-anecdotes.
3. **The contaminated-data false positive.** Warm-network conversations without Mom Test discipline produce systematic over-enthusiasm. The umbrella's Mom Test sub-method addresses this directly.

For founders pre-MVP, product managers running discovery sprints, segment researchers, and anyone whose downstream decisions depend on understanding what is actually true about a market and its users, Customer Discovery is the canonical umbrella method — and the entry point to the specific sub-methods it contains.

|      | segments defined by their fit to your hypothesis ("people who'd benefit from X").|

|    4 | Identify ≥10 conversations across the segment. Bias toward cold-network         |

|    5 | Run the conversations. After each, log behavioral data only (not opinions);     |

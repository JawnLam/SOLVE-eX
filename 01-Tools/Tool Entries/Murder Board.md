# Murder Board

**One-line summary:** Adversarial review session where a single analytic product (estimate, brief, plan) is subjected to hostile questioning from a panel chosen to stress-test it. Heavily used in the US intelligence community.

**When to reach for it:** Before releasing or briefing an analytic product whose conclusions matter — congressional testimony, intelligence estimate, executive brief, public-policy memo. Also for high-stakes nominee preparation ("murder boarding" a witness or candidate).

## Purpose

Red Teaming is broad and structural — assume an adversary's perspective on a plan. A Murder Board is narrower and more pointed — *this specific product*, before *this specific release*, vetted by *this panel*. The intensity is calibrated to the stakes: every weak point will be exposed in real questioning, so better here than there.

## How To Use

1. **Recruit the panel.** Senior analysts unfamiliar with the product, ideally with intellectual investment in being adversarial. Optimal size 3-6.
2. **Brief the panel** on the product (paper or oral).
3. **Panel attacks.** Question every assumption, sourcing, conclusion, alternative explanation. Time-pressure simulates real Q&A.
4. **Analyst defends or absorbs.** Note every weak point that wasn't anticipated.
5. **Revise the product** based on panel critique. Sometimes a second pass with a different panel.

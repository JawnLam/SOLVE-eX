# Trust Equation (Maister)

**One-line summary:** David Maister's diagnostic formula for trustworthiness — Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy, divided by Self-Orientation — that decomposes trust into four factors and identifies self-orientation in the denominator as the most powerful determinant: high self-orientation can collapse trust regardless of how high the other three factors are.

**When to reach for it:** Building client relationships in professional services; diagnosing why a relationship feels distant despite competence; coaching consultants / advisors / sales; leadership relationship-building; partner / vendor selection; and any context where understanding why someone is or isn't trusted matters more than just demonstrating capability.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**The Trust Equation:**

```
Trustworthiness = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation
                  = (C + R + I) / S
```

The four factors:

1. **Credibility** — words and content. Do they know what they're talking about? Credentials, expertise, intellectual rigor.
2. **Reliability** — actions and follow-through. Do they do what they say? Consistency, dependability over time.
3. **Intimacy** — emotional safety. Do I feel safe sharing concerns with them? Can I be vulnerable?
4. **Self-Orientation** — focus on themselves. Are they more interested in their own status / commission / approval than in serving me?

The non-obvious operational insight is that **self-orientation is the trust-killer.** The other three factors add; self-orientation divides. A practitioner with high credibility, reliability, and intimacy can still have low trustworthiness if their self-orientation is high — the relationship feels mercenary or self-serving. Conversely, lower competence with low self-orientation often produces stronger trust than higher competence with self-focus.

A second insight: **the four factors are interventionable separately.** Diagnosis: which factor is weak? Then specific intervention:

- **Credibility low:** demonstrate expertise (specific knowledge displays, accurate analysis, intellectual honesty)
- **Reliability low:** make small commitments and reliably deliver; build track record
- **Intimacy low:** initiate vulnerability; share something personal; create space for them
- **Self-Orientation high:** reduce self-focus; make conversation about them; be willing to lose business / not pitch

A third insight: **the framework explains professional-services dynamics.** Senior professionals often have high CRI and low S; relationships are sticky. Junior professionals often have decent CR but low I and high S; relationships are transactional. The framework prescribes the development path.

A fourth insight: **the equation is heuristic, not literal math.** Don't try to score each factor 1-10 and compute. Use the structure to diagnose qualitatively. Where's the weakness?

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The "competence-only" focus.** Demonstrating expertise without building intimacy or reducing self-orientation; produces shallow trust.
2. **The "high-S blindness."** Practitioners unaware their self-orientation is undermining all their other strengths.
3. **The "ineffable trust" mystery.** Treating trust as inscrutable rather than diagnosable.

For consultants, advisors, lawyers, doctors, salespeople, leaders, and anyone whose effectiveness depends on being trusted, the Trust Equation is foundational diagnostic.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Identify a relationship to assess. Specific person.                            |
|    2 | Assess the four factors from their perspective:                                 |
|      |    Credibility: do they think you know what you're talking about?              |
|    3 |    Reliability: do they think you do what you say?                              |
|    4 |    Intimacy: do they feel safe being vulnerable with you?                      |
|    5 |    Self-Orientation: do they think you're focused on them or on yourself?      |
|    6 | Identify the weakest factor.                                                     |
|    7 | Design an intervention specific to that factor.                                 |
|    8 | Track over time; trust builds (or erodes) gradually.                            |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE EQUATION

   Trustworthiness = (C + R + I) / S

   Numerator (additive):
       Credibility (words / content)
       Reliability (actions / consistency)
       Intimacy (emotional safety)

   Denominator (divisive):
       Self-Orientation (about you, not them)

   High S can collapse the whole equation regardless
   of high CRI. Low S amplifies whatever CRI you have.

THE FACTOR-BY-FACTOR INTERVENTIONS

   IF CREDIBILITY IS LOW:
       Demonstrate expertise specifically
       Articulate insights they didn't have
       Be intellectually honest (admit what you don't
       know)
       Display rigor in analysis

   IF RELIABILITY IS LOW:
       Make smaller commitments first; deliver them
       Build track record before major commitments
       Communicate proactively about timing
       Don't promise what you might not deliver

   IF INTIMACY IS LOW:
       Initiate vulnerability (share something
       personal first)
       Create space for them to share
       Listen without solving
       Address emotional / personal issues, not just
       work

   IF SELF-ORIENTATION IS HIGH:
       Notice your focus during conversations
       Make conversation about them
       Be willing to refuse business / engagement that
       doesn't serve them
       Recommend competitors / alternatives where
       appropriate

   The fourth is hardest because it requires
   self-awareness and willingness to act against
   immediate self-interest.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE — CONSULTING RELATIONSHIP

   Senior consultant assessing relationship with key
   client.

   Credibility: high (deep expertise; client values
   analytical rigor)
   Reliability: high (delivers on time consistently)
   Intimacy: medium (professional relationship;
   personal disclosure limited)
   Self-Orientation: medium (occasional pitches for
   additional engagements feel transactional)

   Diagnosis: trust is constrained by intimacy
   ceiling and occasional S spikes.

   Interventions:
       Initiate more personal sharing (intimacy)
       During pitches, structure as "this might or
       might not serve you" rather than "here's what
       you should buy" (lower S)
       Make explicit no-pitch periods (build
       intimacy without commercial agenda)

   Over months: relationship deepens; client brings
   harder problems and personal advice questions.
   Trust has compounded.

THE NEAR-NEIGHBOR DISTINCTIONS

   CREDIBILITY vs. ARROGANCE:
       Credibility is demonstrated knowledge.
       Arrogance is over-claiming or dismissing
       others.

   RELIABILITY vs. RIGIDITY:
       Reliability is consistent follow-through.
       Rigidity is inflexibility regardless of
       circumstances.

   INTIMACY vs. OVER-FAMILIARITY:
       Intimacy is appropriate emotional safety.
       Over-familiarity is presumption of closeness
       not earned.

   LOW SELF-ORIENTATION vs. SERVITUDE:
       Low S is genuine focus on the other.
       Servitude is loss of self in service.

   The framework's elements are calibrated, not
   maximized.

THE COMMON FAILURE MODES

   1. COMPETENCE-ONLY FOCUS
        High CR, low I, possibly high S; relationship
        feels transactional. Recovery: invest in
        intimacy; reduce S.

   2. HIGH-S BLINDNESS
        Self-focus invisible to self. Recovery: ask
        for honest feedback; observe own behavior.

   3. INTIMACY WITHOUT COMPETENCE
        Likable but not credible; trusted personally
        but not professionally. Recovery: build
        credibility evidence.

   4. RELIABILITY WITHOUT INTIMACY
        Dependable but cold; transactional. Recovery:
        humanize.

   5. INTIMATE BUT UNRELIABLE
        Warm but flaky; trust limited by inconsistent
        follow-through. Recovery: smaller commitments,
        consistently delivered.

   6. NO INTERVENTION DESIGN
        Recognizing weak factor without specific
        action. Recovery: factor-specific interventions.

   7. TRUSTWORTHY-IN-VACUUM
        Building trust without context; trust depends
        on relationship history and pattern. Recovery:
        invest over time, not in one moment.

THE OPERATIONAL TEMPLATE

   Relationship: ______________________________________

   From their perspective, assess:
       Credibility: low / medium / high
       Reliability: low / medium / high
       Intimacy: low / medium / high
       Self-Orientation: low / medium / high

   Weakest factor: ____________________________________

   Intervention specific to weakest factor:
       _________________________________________________

   Risk: high-S blindness. Honest self-check:
       Am I more focused on my agenda than theirs?
       What would I do differently if I genuinely
       prioritized their interests?

   Tracking: trust over time
       Month 1: ___________________________________
       Month 3: ___________________________________
       Month 6: ___________________________________
```

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) Diagnose all four factors. Trust failures are usually weakness in one factor; identifying which enables targeted intervention. (2) Watch self-orientation. The most powerful factor; the most invisible to oneself. Honest self-check matters because S is hard to see in self. (3) Match intervention to weak factor. Generic "build trust" doesn't work; factor-specific interventions do. The framework's value is the diagnostic precision. (4) Build over time. Trust compounds; one strong moment isn't enough; consistent strength across time builds genuine trust. The horizon for serious trust-building is months to years.

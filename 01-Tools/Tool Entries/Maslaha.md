# Maslaha (Public-Interest Analysis)

**One-line summary:** Islamic legal method of evaluating rulings against their effect on public welfare — al-Ghazali's five essentials: religion, life, intellect, offspring, property.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating a legal or policy ruling for its real-world effect on public welfare, where strict textual or analogical derivation alone may produce results that damage what the law is supposed to protect.

## Purpose

Maslaha (literally 'benefit') is the Islamic legal principle of evaluating rulings against their effect on public welfare. Al-Ghazali (~1100) codified the five essentials (al-darurat al-khams) that maslaha must protect: religion (din), life (nafs), intellect ('aql), offspring (nasl), property (mal). Any ruling must protect these; rulings that harm them in particular cases can be modified through maslaha-reasoning (sometimes called istislah). Used in classical jurisprudence and revived in modern Islamic law for addressing contemporary questions (banking, medicine, governance).

## How To Use

For the ruling under evaluation: (1) Map its effect across the five essentials. Does it protect or threaten religion? Life? Intellect? Offspring? Property? (2) For each essential, distinguish three levels of need: darurat (necessities — without which life or essential function fails), hajiyat (needs — without which serious hardship occurs), tahsiniyat (refinements — without which character is diminished). (3) Where the ruling threatens necessities, weight against extending it. Where it protects necessities, weight strongly toward. (4) Use maslaha as the principled override when strict qiyas-derivation would damage what the law is meant to protect. Best applied by qualified jurists; the temptation toward unprincipled maslaha-as-anything-I-prefer is the methodological hazard.

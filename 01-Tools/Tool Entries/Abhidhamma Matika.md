# Abhidhamma Matika Analysis

**One-line summary:** Theravada Abhidhamma's foundational matrices (matika, 'matrix') — every mental event run through the matrix to classify what kind of dhamma it is.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging systematic introspective taxonomy of mental states, where one wishes to classify cognitive-emotional events with precision rather than describe them in folk-psychological terms.

## Purpose

The Theravada Abhidhamma uses 'matika' (matrices) — exhaustive lists of mental phenomena and their classifications. The *Dhammasangani* opens with such matrices and uses them to categorize every cognitive-emotional event. Key matrices include the dyadic (e.g., wholesome/unwholesome/neutral), the triadic (e.g., past/present/future, internal/external/both), and the more complex multi-dimensional categorizations. Used by Theravada practitioners as a systematic introspective taxonomy: any mental event arising in meditation is run through the matrix to classify what kind of dhamma it is. The classification trains discriminating awareness.

## How To Use

Study a specific matrix (the simplest is the triadic kusala/akusala/avyakata — wholesome/unwholesome/indeterminate). During practice or daily life, notice a mental event arising. Pause. Apply the matrix: is this wholesome, unwholesome, or indeterminate? On what basis? Move to more complex matrices over time: is this past-rooted, present, future-oriented? Internal, external, or both? Associated with greed, hatred, delusion, or their opposites? The matrix application is the practice; over time, the discriminating awareness becomes more rapid and precise. Used in conjunction with vipassana but as a distinct cognitive-analytical method.

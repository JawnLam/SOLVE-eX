# Living Constitution Doctrine

**One-line summary:** The Constitution should be interpreted as a document whose meaning evolves with changing social understanding — common-law constitutionalism rather than fixed original meaning.

**When to reach for it:** Constitutional interpretation in living-constitution traditions; arguments that the Constitution must adapt to circumstances unanticipated by its framers; contrast with originalism.

## Purpose

The Living Constitution doctrine treats constitutional interpretation as analogous to common-law evolution: judges apply text to new circumstances, building precedent that shapes future readings. Strauss's account argues that the actual practice of constitutional law has always worked this way, regardless of theoretical commitments. The school accommodates the necessary application of 18th-century text to 21st-century problems by treating constitutional meaning as itself historical-evolutionary.

## How To Use

1) Recognize that interpretation always involves applying old text to new circumstances. 2) Use precedent to anchor interpretation, building on prior judicial reasoning. 3) Consider contemporary moral, political, and practical context. 4) Maintain coherence with constitutional structure and core commitments while allowing application to evolve. 5) Justify departures from precedent rigorously.

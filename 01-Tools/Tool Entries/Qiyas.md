# Qiyas (Analogical Reasoning)

**One-line summary:** Islamic legal method of extending a scripturally-based ruling to a new situation by identifying the operative cause (illa) and reasoning analogically.

**When to reach for it:** Reasoning about a novel case where direct scriptural ruling does not exist, and the appropriate method is to find the operative principle behind an existing ruling and extend it.

## Purpose

Qiyas is the third source of Sunni Islamic jurisprudence (after Quran and Sunna), formalized particularly by al-Shafi'i (~9th c.). The method: identify the 'illa (operative cause or rationale) of an existing scripturally-grounded ruling; verify that the new case shares this 'illa; extend the ruling. Classic example: Quranic prohibition on wine (khamr) is extended to all intoxicants because the 'illa is intoxication, not wine specifically. Methodologically rigorous: the 'illa must be derivable from the original ruling, not posited ad hoc. Distinct from istihsan (juristic preference, which departs from strict qiyas).

## How To Use

(1) Identify the existing scriptural ruling (asl). (2) Identify the new situation (far'). (3) Determine the 'illa (operative cause) of the existing ruling — what is the ruling tracking? Is it stated explicitly, derivable from the text, or inferred from contextual evidence? (4) Verify that the new situation shares this 'illa. (5) If it does, extend the ruling. Each step is subject to scholarly scrutiny: the 'illa might be misidentified, the new case might not actually share it, the ruling might not be extensible. Used by trained jurists; quality depends on the 'illa-identification.

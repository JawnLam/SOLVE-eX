# Mengzi Four Sprouts Mapping

**One-line summary:** Mengzi's moral psychology: four natural affective sprouts — compassion, shame, deference, approval/disapproval — present in every human and to be cultivated into mature virtues.

**When to reach for it:** Inquiring into one's own moral psychology, especially identifying which inherent affective tendencies (sprouts) are well-developed and which are stunted and in need of cultivation.

## Purpose

Mengzi (Mencius, ~4th c. BCE) developed a moral psychology that disputes Xunzi's view of human nature as initially crooked. Mengzi: every human has four natural affective 'sprouts' (siduan) — the sprout of compassion (which grows into the virtue of benevolence, ren); the sprout of shame (which grows into righteousness, yi); the sprout of deference (which grows into propriety, li); the sprout of approval/disapproval (which grows into wisdom, zhi). Ethical development is not the imposition of external rules but the cultivation of these innate seeds into mature virtues. Used as introspective self-mapping: which sprouts are flourishing in me, which are stunted?

## How To Use

As reflective practice: review recent days. Where have I felt compassion arise spontaneously? Where shame? Where deference? Where approval or disapproval? The sprouts are visible in spontaneous emotional response, not only in deliberate action. Note which sprouts arise easily for you and which are quiet. The quiet ones are not absent — they are the sprouts most in need of cultivation. Choose practices that exercise the quiet sprouts: deliberately attend to suffering you would normally turn from (compassion), to your own failings (shame), to others' rightful authority (deference), to your spontaneous evaluations (approval/disapproval). The cultivation is gentle attention to what is already there, not the manufacture of what isn't.

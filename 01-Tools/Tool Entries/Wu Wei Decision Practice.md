# Wu Wei Decision Practice

**One-line summary:** Daoist 'non-forced action' applied as a decision protocol — what response would arise if I were not pushing? — sharply contrasting Western strategic-rational decision-making.

**When to reach for it:** Facing a decision where the rational-strategic instinct to push, to force, to engineer the outcome feels suspect, and where the question is whether non-forced action would yield a different and more truthful path.

## Purpose

Wu wei ('non-action' or more precisely 'non-forced action') is a central Daoist principle (Laozi, Zhuangzi). Applied as a decision protocol, it asks: what response would arise if I were not pushing? What is the natural shape of this situation, and what action follows from it without strain? Strongly contrasts Western strategic-rational decision-making which assumes the decider should engineer outcomes through deliberate intervention. Wu wei treats deliberate intervention as often counterproductive — adding force where the situation's natural flow would otherwise produce a better outcome. Used by Daoist sages, in Chan/Zen practice, and increasingly in Western psychology (flow, effortless attention).

## How To Use

Facing a decision: pause. Notice the strategic-rational impulse — the calculation, the planning, the engineering of outcome. Without suppressing it, set it aside briefly. Ask: if I were not pushing, what would arise? What is the natural shape of this situation? What action follows from it without strain? Don't manufacture an answer; wait for one to surface. Often, in this listening, a different action becomes obvious — often quieter, more responsive, more aligned with the situation's actual contours. Sometimes the strategic-rational option remains the right one; sometimes the wu wei option is. The discipline is not abandoning strategy but adding the wu wei question as a check against forcing.

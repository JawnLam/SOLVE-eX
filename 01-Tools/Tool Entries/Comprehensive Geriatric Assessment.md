# Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA)

**One-line summary:** Multi-dimensional structured evaluation of an older adult — medical, functional, psychological, and social — by an interdisciplinary team, used to guide individualized care plans for frail elders.

**When to reach for it:** Assessing an older adult — especially one who is frail, hospitalized, has fallen, or is being considered for major intervention — where single-domain assessment would miss the interactive factors that determine outcomes.

## Purpose

The Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment is a multi-dimensional structured evaluation of an older adult conducted by an interdisciplinary team. Four core domains: (1) Medical — medications (often polypharmacy), comorbidities, nutritional status, sensory function; (2) Functional — Activities of Daily Living (ADLs: bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, continence, feeding) and Instrumental ADLs (IADLs: medications, finances, transportation, shopping, cooking, housework, telephone); (3) Psychological — cognition (often via MMSE or MoCA), mood (often GDS), behavior; (4) Social — caregiver capacity, social support, financial resources, housing environment, advance care planning. Cochrane review shows CGA improves outcomes for hospitalized older adults.

## How To Use

The interdisciplinary team (typically geriatrician, nurse, social worker, pharmacist, often PT/OT) coordinates assessment across the four domains. Medical: comprehensive medication review with Beers Criteria check; comorbidity inventory; nutritional screen; vision/hearing/dentition. Functional: ADL and IADL assessment by direct observation when possible. Psychological: cognitive screen (MMSE/MoCA); mood screen (GDS or PHQ-9); behavioral observations. Social: caregiver capacity and burden; financial resources; housing and home safety; goals of care and advance directives. Team meeting integrates findings into a problem list with prioritized interventions. The CGA outputs an individualized care plan, not a list of diagnoses.

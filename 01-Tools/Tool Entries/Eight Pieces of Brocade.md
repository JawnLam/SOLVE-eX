# Eight Pieces of Brocade Qigong (Ba Duan Jin)

**One-line summary:** Ba Duan Jin — sequence of 8 specific movement-breathing practices (standing or seated variants) traditionally attributed to Marshal Yue Fei (Song dynasty); each addresses a specific organ-meridian system.

**When to reach for it:** Establishing a daily integrated body-mind cultivation practice that is accessible to beginners but produces deepening effects over years, and where the discrete-piece structure makes practice easy to remember and complete.

## Purpose

Ba Duan Jin ('Eight Pieces of Brocade') is one of the most widely practiced qigong sets, traditionally attributed to Marshal Yue Fei (Song dynasty, 12th c.). The 'brocade' image conveys the woven coordination of breath, movement, and intention. Each of the eight pieces is a specific posture sequence with associated breath pattern, addressing a specific organ-meridian system in Traditional Chinese Medicine: liver, lung, spleen, kidney, etc. Used as an integrated body-mind cultivation method; each piece produces a distinct cognitive-physiological effect; the full set takes 15-25 minutes. Accessible to beginners; deepens over years.

## How To Use

Learn the eight pieces from a teacher or careful video instruction. The standing version is most commonly practiced. The full set in order: (1) Two Hands Hold Up the Heavens; (2) Drawing the Bow; (3) Single Hand Holds Up the Heavens; (4) Looking Back; (5) Sway the Head and Swing the Tail; (6) Touch Toes and Bend Backwards; (7) Punch with Angry Eyes; (8) Bouncing on the Toes. Each piece is performed 8-16 times, with attention to breath coordination, soft strength, and connection to the relevant organ. Daily practice for 15-25 minutes. Effects accumulate; gentle vigor and clear-mindedness commonly reported after weeks. Seated variants exist for those with mobility limitations.

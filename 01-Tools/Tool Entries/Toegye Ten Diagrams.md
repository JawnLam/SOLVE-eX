# Toegye Ten Diagrams of Saint Learning

**One-line summary:** Yi Hwang's (Toegye, 1568) ten diagrams illustrating the Neo-Confucian path to sagehood — each diagram a visual contemplative gateway summarizing a stage of self-cultivation.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging Neo-Confucian self-cultivation as a long-arc developmental practice and seeking a structured visual scaffold for daily contemplation across stages.

## Purpose

Yi Hwang, known by his pen name Toegye (1501-1570), was Korea's most influential Neo-Confucian philosopher. In 1568 he presented King Seonjo with the *Songhak Sipdo* (Ten Diagrams on Sage Learning), summarizing the Neo-Confucian path to sagehood across ten illustrated stages — from cosmological foundations through investigation of principle, self-cultivation, and culminating sagely virtue. The diagrams are not decorative illustrations of a textual treatise; they are themselves the practice-objects, each held in extended contemplation as a visual gateway into the corresponding stage of development. The cognitive method: visual structure shapes cognitive-emotional movement of the practice.

## How To Use

Obtain the ten diagrams with their accompanying commentary (Kalton's translation is standard). For each diagram in turn: study the visual structure carefully — what does each element represent, how do they relate? Then sit with the diagram as contemplative object. Don't analyze; let the visual structure guide attention through its parts and relations. Move to the next diagram only when the current one has stabilized — i.e., the contemplation no longer feels like effortful study but like recognized terrain. Move through all ten in order. The progression maps the developmental arc; revisiting earlier diagrams later reveals new depths.

# Surgical M&M Conference

**One-line summary:** Regular peer-review meeting (typically weekly) where surgical complications and deaths are discussed for educational and quality-improvement purposes; structured peer scrutiny as cognitive method.

**When to reach for it:** Operating in a surgical or other clinical specialty where regular structured peer-review of complications and deaths is the appropriate quality-improvement and educational mechanism.

## Purpose

The Morbidity and Mortality Conference is a regular (typically weekly) meeting in surgical and other clinical specialties where recent complications and deaths are presented and discussed by the department. A surgeon presents the case with relevant clinical detail and decision-points; the group analyzes the decisions, technique, and system issues that contributed to the outcome. The cognitive method: structured peer scrutiny applied to one's own practice as ongoing learning rather than punishment. Charles Bosk's classic ethnography (*Forgive and Remember*, 1979) studied this practice as a form of professional self-policing distinct from blame-based review.

## How To Use

A senior member of the department designates cases for review. The presenting surgeon prepares a structured presentation: pre-operative status, intra-operative findings and decisions, post-operative course, outcome, contributory factors. The group asks: what would you do differently? What systems issues contributed? What did you anticipate that didn't happen, or didn't anticipate that did? The discussion is professional, frank, and protected by quality-improvement privilege (in many jurisdictions). The norm is that errors of technique are addressed personally; errors of clinical judgment are addressed didactically; errors of system are addressed organizationally. The combination of personal accountability and shared learning is the cognitive payload.

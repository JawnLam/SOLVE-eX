# Steelmanning

**One-line summary:** Construct the strongest possible version of an opposing argument — even stronger than its actual proponents argue — before evaluating or responding.

**When to reach for it:** Adversarial debates where the opposing argument is being dismissed; internal deliberation where one side is dominant; pre-mortem-style stress-testing of one's own conclusions.

## Purpose

Strawmanning (the opposite practice) reduces an opponent's argument to its weakest form, defeats that weak form, and declares victory — leaving the actual disagreement unresolved. Steelmanning forces engagement with the strongest version, which is more often what the disagreement actually depends on. The practice both improves the analysis and serves a relational function: people respond more cooperatively when their best argument is acknowledged.

## How To Use

1) State the opposing position. 2) Strengthen it: what charitable assumptions, what additional evidence, what reframing makes it strongest? 3) Argue the strengthened version yourself, sincerely. 4) Only after the strongest version is on the table, attempt rebuttal. 5) If you can't rebut the steelmanned version, update your belief.

# Pratyahara (Sense-Withdrawal)

**One-line summary:** Patanjali's fifth limb — deliberate withdrawal of senses from their objects, sustained internally without going dull, bridging external practices and meditation.

**When to reach for it:** Transitioning from outward yoga practices (asana, pranayama) to inward contemplative work, and finding that direct concentration (dharana) doesn't yet stabilize because senses remain hooked to objects.

## Purpose

Pratyahara is the fifth of Patanjali's eight limbs (Yoga Sutras 2.54), positioned between the outward four (yama, niyama, asana, pranayama) and the inward three (dharana, dhyana, samadhi). Often translated as 'sense-withdrawal,' it is the deliberate retraction of senses from their objects — not by physical isolation but by interior attention. Distinct from concentration (dharana — which has an explicit object) and meditation (dhyana — which has flowing absorption). Pratyahara is the bridge: attention is internal but no specific object is fixed; the senses are present but unhooked. Often the most difficult limb because it lacks the structure of object-focused practice.

## How To Use

Sit comfortably with breath stabilized. Notice the senses operating: sounds arriving, sensations on skin, visual impressions even with eyes closed. Without suppressing or fighting them, draw attention inward. Don't focus on a specific object; let attention rest interior to the senses' reaching-outward. Notice when attention is captured by a sensation, and gently withdraw without aversion. Sustain this withdrawal-without-dullness. Initially difficult — practitioners often slip into either object-focus (which is dharana) or drowsiness. With practice, a stable middle emerges; this stable middle is the gateway to concentration that no longer struggles with sensory pull.

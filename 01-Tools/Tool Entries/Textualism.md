# Textualism (Constitutional / Statutory Interpretation)

**One-line summary:** Interpret legal text according to its plain meaning to a reasonable reader of the relevant time — privilege the text itself over legislative history or purpose.

**When to reach for it:** Statutory and constitutional interpretation where the text is precise enough to bear weight; arguments about whether courts should look behind text to legislative history.

## Purpose

Textualism is methodologically related to originalism but operates at the level of any legal text: statutes, regulations, contracts, constitutional provisions. The commitment is to the words on the page rather than the legislators' intent (which may not exist as a coherent group fact) or the law's purpose (which can be reconstructed in many ways). Textualists argue that text discipline produces predictable, rule-of-law-respecting interpretation. Critics note that text often underdetermines outcomes.

## How To Use

1) Read the text closely, in its full context. 2) Apply standard linguistic conventions, dictionaries, and legal canons of construction. 3) Resolve ambiguity within the text first; turn to legislative history only as a last resort. 4) Avoid reading purpose into text not supported by it. 5) Maintain consistency across cases.

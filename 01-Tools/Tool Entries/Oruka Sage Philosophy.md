# Oruka Sage Philosophy Method

**One-line summary:** Kenyan philosopher Henry Odera Oruka's method — academically-trained philosophers interview non-academic 'sages' (wise community elders) about ethical-metaphysical questions, treating their responses as primary-source philosophy.

**When to reach for it:** Conducting philosophical research where the assumption that primary-source philosophy must come from academic philosophers is itself the obstacle, and where elder wisdom in oral traditions warrants engagement as philosophy rather than ethnography-as-data.

## Purpose

Henry Odera Oruka (1970s onward) developed the Sage Philosophy method to preserve and elevate Indigenous African philosophical thought. The method: academically-trained philosophers conduct extended structured interviews with non-academic sages — wise community elders. The sages' answers are treated as primary-source philosophy, not as ethnographic data about belief systems. Oruka distinguished 'folk sages' (who know and transmit tradition) from 'philosophic sages' (who also critique and reformulate it). The method addresses the colonial-era denial that pre-literate African societies produced philosophy by demonstrating that philosophical reflection is present and articulate in non-academic forms.

## How To Use

(1) Identify recognized sages in a specific community — through community consultation, not external designation. (2) Conduct extended structured interviews on philosophical questions (ethics, metaphysics, epistemology, social philosophy). (3) Record verbatim. (4) Treat responses not as data-about-beliefs but as philosophical positions, with the same critical engagement one would offer an academic philosopher. (5) Where sages disagree with each other or with academic tradition, take the disagreement as philosophical rather than as confusion or error. (6) Publish with sages credited as the primary thinkers. Method has been extended beyond Africa to other oral traditions; raises careful ethical-methodological questions about credit, ownership, and translation.

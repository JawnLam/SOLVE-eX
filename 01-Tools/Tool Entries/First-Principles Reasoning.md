# First-Principles Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Decompose a problem down to physically/logically irreducible axioms, then rebuild from those axioms rather than from analogy to existing solutions.

**When to reach for it:** When inherited solutions feel constrained by tradition or analogy, when cost structures look fixed but might not be, or when a domain demands re-architecture rather than refinement.

## Purpose

Most reasoning proceeds by analogy: 'This is like that, so do what worked there.' First-principles reasoning bypasses analogy and asks 'what do the underlying physics, economics, or logic actually require?' This exposes hidden assumptions, often reveals that the inherited solution carries costs that the underlying constraints don't justify, and licenses new architectures that look impossible from analogical reasoning.

## How To Use

1) State the problem in inherited form. 2) List the assumptions baked into that framing. 3) For each assumption, ask 'is this a law of physics/economics/logic, or a contingent tradition?' 4) Drill until you reach genuinely irreducible constraints. 5) From those constraints alone, design forward. 6) Cross-check the new design's resource requirements against the original's.

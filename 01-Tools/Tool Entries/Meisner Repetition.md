# Meisner Repetition

**One-line summary:** Sanford Meisner's core acting-training exercise — two actors repeat a simple observation back and forth, responding truthfully to what shifts in the partner, training active listening and in-the-moment response.

**When to reach for it:** Building the foundational acting skill of present-moment responsiveness, especially against the tendency toward internal-rehearsal and pre-planned line-delivery that disconnects from the scene partner.

## Purpose

Sanford Meisner (mid-20th c.) developed the Repetition exercise as the entry to his training method. Two actors stand facing each other. One observes: 'You're wearing a blue shirt.' The other repeats: 'I'm wearing a blue shirt.' Repeat back and forth. As each repetition lands, the actors respond truthfully to whatever shifts in the partner — a flicker of doubt, a smile, a heaviness. The repetition shifts as response shifts: 'You're not sure.' 'I'm not sure.' 'You're nervous.' Etc. The cognitive payload: train active listening (the partner is the working material) and in-the-moment response (the actor cannot pre-plan, only respond to what is). Inverts Stanislavski's introspection — for Meisner, internal psychology emerges from external responsiveness, not the other way.

## How To Use

Pair with a partner. Stand facing each other, eye contact, comfortable distance. One makes a simple observation about the other: physical, factual. The partner repeats it from their point of view. Repeat back and forth. Don't perform; don't act. Just observe and repeat. As either of you genuinely responds to the other (a flicker, a shift, a question, a doubt), let that shift the repetition: 'You're smiling now.' 'I'm smiling now.' Continue. The exercise typically goes 5-15 minutes. The discipline: respond to what actually is, not what you think should be. Variants increase complexity (point-of-view exercises, independent activities under repetition). The foundation is the same: presence in the partner, not the script.

# Ayni Reciprocity Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Andean cognitive-ethical frame in which every action is part of an ongoing cycle of reciprocal exchange with people, land, ancestors, and spirit.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating a decision, project, or relationship in a context where individual-rational utility calculation feels incomplete or extractive.

## Purpose

Ayni reframes action from 'what do I gain?' to 'what cycle is this part of and what completes it?'. Predating the Inka and continuous in contemporary Quechua/Aymara communities, it operationalizes mutual obligation as the basic unit of reality. The frame surfaces hidden debts, expected returns, and the temporal arc of exchange — including non-human partners (mountains, water, ancestors).

## How To Use

For the decision or action under consideration: (1) name the parties involved, including non-human (land, water, ancestral lineages, future generations). (2) Identify what each party has given or is giving. (3) Identify what is owed back and to whom — across what time-horizon? (4) Ask: does this action complete a cycle, open one, or break one? (5) Where cycles are unfinished, what would close them? Used as a daily examen in Andean communities; can be adopted as a workplace or project-planning frame to surface unaccounted-for reciprocity.

# Hozho (Navajo Beauty Way)

**One-line summary:** Diné/Navajo cosmological principle and design method — harmony, balance, beauty as the criterion for action and craft. Not just an aesthetic standard; an action-philosophy expressed in sand painting, weaving, prayer ("may I walk in beauty").

**When to reach for it:** When an aesthetic or practical decision calls for evaluation against deep cultural standards of harmony — Navajo cultural contexts, cross-cultural design work, or any practitioner seeking an alternative to Western beauty-as-form aesthetics. Approach with cultural respect; not a method to extract and apply outside its context without acknowledgment.

## Purpose

Western aesthetic philosophy separates beauty from function and from cosmos. Hozho refuses this separation: the beautiful is the harmonious, which is the right relationship among people, place, ancestors, and ceremonies. To act in hozho is simultaneously aesthetic, ethical, ecological, and spiritual. The Beauty Way prayer ("in beauty I walk, with beauty before me, with beauty behind me…") is a daily orientation toward this integration.

## How To Use

The stance is enacted, not stepped through. Indicative practices:
1. **Orient toward harmony** — At the start of action, ask: does this restore or disrupt balance among people, place, time?
2. **Notice disharmony** — Symptoms (illness, conflict, ugly artifact) point toward broken relationship. The work is to restore it.
3. **Sand painting / weaving traditions** — Ceremonial restoration of hozho through symbolic representation; the artifact participates in healing.
4. **Daily orientation** — Beauty Way prayer or its equivalents.
5. **Caveat** — Hozho is a Diné concept; non-Diné learners approach it with respect, study with knowledge-keepers when appropriate, and don't extract the form from its context.

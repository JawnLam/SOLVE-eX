# Hakuin Koan Curriculum

**One-line summary:** Rinzai Zen's structured koan progression (Hakuin Ekaku, 18th c.) — students progress through 500-1700 koans organized in five categories with teacher confirmation at each.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging extended Rinzai Zen practice within a formal lineage, where koan work is structured by curriculum rather than the practitioner's selection.

## Purpose

Hakuin Ekaku (18th c.) systematized koan practice into a structured curriculum within Japanese Rinzai Zen. Koans are organized into five categories of increasing subtlety: hosshin (essence — early breakthrough koans like 'Mu' or 'What is your original face?'); kikan (functional dynamism — applying the breakthrough); gonsen (verbal expression — testing through paradox); nanto (difficult-to-pass — testing in extreme situations); goi-jujukin (Five Ranks — final integration). Students progress through ~500-1700 koans over years, working each with a teacher in private interview (sanzen / dokusan). The curriculum is testing-by-living, not testing-by-knowing.

## How To Use

Within a formal Rinzai lineage: receive the first koan from the teacher. Sit with it in zazen — not analyzing, not interpreting, but holding it as great-doubt while letting the mind work on it. When response or breakthrough comes, present it to the teacher in private interview. Teacher confirms passage or asks more. Receive the next koan in sequence. Work through years; some koans pass quickly, others take months. The curriculum cannot be self-administered effectively — the teacher's confirmation is essential because the practitioner's own assessment will mislead. Tradition emphasizes 'great doubt, great determination, great faith' as the three essentials for sustained work.

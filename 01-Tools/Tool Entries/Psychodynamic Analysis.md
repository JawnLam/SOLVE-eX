# Psychodynamic Analysis

**One-line summary:** A depth-oriented therapeutic tradition that treats current psychological difficulty as expressing unconscious patterns — derived from early relationships, structured by characteristic defenses, and surfacing in transference within the therapeutic relationship.

**When to reach for it:** Long-running characterological patterns; difficulties that resist symptom-focused therapy; complex relational and identity questions; therapeutic process for those drawn to depth and meaning over symptom-targeted protocol.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

Psychodynamic analysis is the modern descendant of psychoanalysis, retaining its core moves — attention to the unconscious, transference and counter-transference, defense mechanisms, the formative role of early attachment — while shedding much of Freud's specific theoretical apparatus (drive theory, Oedipus, etc.). Modern psychodynamic therapy operates with object relations (the internalized templates of early relationships), self psychology (the development of cohesive self), and relational frames (therapy as a real relationship that surfaces patterns).

The non-obvious operational insight is *transference*: the patterns clients bring to the therapeutic relationship are the same patterns they bring to other relationships, in real-time. By noticing transference (and the therapist's own counter-transference) the work has a live laboratory rather than a remembered one. This is what distinguishes psychodynamic work from purely retrospective interpretation — the patterns are *present* in the room.

Modern psychodynamic therapy is briefer and more focused than classical analysis (typically 1–2 sessions/week, several months to several years). The evidence base is real but less extensive than CBT's for specific symptoms; psychodynamic work is more often chosen for its breadth (working with whole-person patterns) than for targeted symptom relief.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three contributions psychodynamic depth offers that CBT does not:

1. **Unconscious as substrate.** Many patterns operate below cognitive access; psychodynamic work surfaces them via dreams, slips, free association, transference.
2. **Defense recognition.** Defenses (denial, projection, intellectualization, splitting) are mostly invisible to the user; naming them is itself liberating.
3. **Early-attachment lens.** Current relational patterns reflect templates laid down in early relationships. Working at template level changes more than working at instance level.

For coaching and self-development (with strong clinical-scope caveats), the vocabulary of defenses and transference offers ways of understanding interpersonal dynamics that other frames don't.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Notice patterns across relationships, not just within one. The same dynamic     |
|      | with different people often points to an internalized template.                |
|    2 | Examine current interpersonal difficulties for *transference* — am I treating  |
|      | this person as if they were someone earlier?                                    |
|    3 | Notice defenses — what am I avoiding feeling, knowing, or saying? What are     |
|      | the typical patterns (denial, projection, intellectualization)?                 |
|    4 | Track dreams, slips, and "irrational" reactions — these are unconscious        |
|      | communications.                                                                 |
|    5 | Free associate — what comes to mind when I sit with this material? Don't       |
|      | filter; record.                                                                 |
|    6 | For deeper work, engage a psychodynamic therapist. The relationship is the     |
|      | active ingredient; self-work captures only part of the practice.                |
|    7 | Allow long timelines. Pattern shift at this depth is months-to-years, not      |
|      | weeks.                                                                          |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
DEFENSE-MECHANISM CATALOGUE (psychoanalytic core, condensed)

    Defense                  | Operation                                    | Example
    -------------------------|----------------------------------------------|---------------------------
    Denial                   | refusing to acknowledge a fact               | "It's not that bad"
    Repression               | excluding from consciousness                  | forgetting a traumatic event
    Projection               | attributing one's feelings to another        | "He's angry at me"
    Reaction formation       | acting opposite to felt impulse               | overly nice to disliked person
    Rationalization          | justifying with cognitive reasons             | post-hoc explanation
    Intellectualization      | thinking about, not feeling                   | analyzing emotion in the abstract
    Sublimation              | redirecting impulse into accepted activity   | aggression → competitive sport
    Displacement             | redirecting feeling to safer target           | yelling at family after work
    Splitting                | seeing self/other as all-good or all-bad     | idealize → devalue cycle
    Identification           | taking on traits of another                   | becoming like the abuser
    Regression               | returning to earlier developmental level      | childlike behavior under stress
    Acting out               | enacting impulse instead of feeling           | dramatic behavior in distress

TRANSFERENCE PATTERN WORKSHEET

    Current relationship: __________________________________________________

    My emotional reaction (often disproportionate or "off"):
      _____________________________________________________________________

    Who does this person remind me of, even slightly? _______________________

    What pattern from earlier life might be replaying?
      _____________________________________________________________________

    What was unfinished or unmet in the earlier relationship?
      _____________________________________________________________________

    How might this be showing up here?
      _____________________________________________________________________

    What new response would be possible if I weren't in transference?
      _____________________________________________________________________

OBJECT-RELATIONS LENS (Klein / Winnicott / Fairbairn)

    Internalized "objects" (relational templates) influence how I experience
    current relationships. The classic patterns:

      Good object / bad object splitting:
        Tendency to see people as all-good (idealize) or all-bad (devalue);
        rapid switching between these.

      Persecutory object:
        Felt sense of being attacked, undermined, even when objectively safe.

      Depressive position:
        Capacity to see others as whole — combining good and bad — with
        accompanying capacity for guilt, repair, gratitude.

DREAM AS ROYAL ROAD (Freud's term — generally apt)

    Record the dream. Apply free association: what comes to mind for each
    image, not "what does it mean?". The unconscious's organization is
    associative; following the associations follows the structure.

    See also: Jungian Dream-Work for a structured method.

WHEN TO ENGAGE A PSYCHODYNAMIC THERAPIST

    [ ] Patterns repeat across multiple relationships
    [ ] Difficulty with intimacy, trust, anger
    [ ] Identity / meaning questions that resist symptom-focused approach
    [ ] Long-term curiosity about depth, motivation, formative patterns
    [ ] CBT-equivalent approaches haven't reached the underlying pattern
```

> **Operational notes:** Three disciplines. (1) Psychodynamic work is *long*. Quick relief isn't its mode. The trade-off is depth: pattern-level change vs. symptom relief. Choose intentionally; for symptom relief, CBT is usually faster. (2) Self-application is partial. Many of the technique's most powerful moves (transference, working through) require an actual relationship — a therapist who notices what's happening in the room. Self-reading and journaling cover part of the territory; the relational layer requires another person. (3) Don't pathologize defenses. They served a purpose; the work is updating them, not eradicating them. The healthy adult uses defenses flexibly, not rigidly. Fourth: the modern psychodynamic field is plural. Object relations, self psychology, and relational psychoanalysis are different subsidiary frames; if the first you encounter doesn't fit, others might. Match the frame to the questions you're carrying.

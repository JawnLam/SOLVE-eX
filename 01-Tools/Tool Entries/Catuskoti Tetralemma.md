# Catuṣkoṭi (Madhyamaka Tetralemma)

**One-line summary:** Nāgārjuna's four-cornered logic: for any proposition P, reject all four positions — (1) P, (2) not-P, (3) both P and not-P, (4) neither P nor not-P. Used to point past discursive grasping toward śūnyatā (emptiness).

**When to reach for it:** Philosophical inquiry into deep questions where binary or even ternary frames seem inadequate; contemplative practice within Madhyamaka Buddhism; logical analysis of paradoxes (parallel to dialetheism in modern logic).

## Purpose

Standard logic accepts that for any P, either P or not-P (excluded middle). Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi widens the structural possibilities to four — and then rejects all four. The point is not nihilism ("nothing is true") but a deconstructive move: any proposition the mind grasps is partial; reality (śūnyatā) is what remains when all four corners of conceptual grasping are released. The practice trains a habit of letting go of conceptual finality.

## How To Use

1. **Take a proposition P.** (e.g., "The self exists.")
2. **Negate P.** Articulate "The self does not exist." Notice the grasping in this position too.
3. **Both P and not-P.** Articulate "The self both exists and does not exist." (Reality is paradoxical.)
4. **Neither P nor not-P.** Articulate "The self neither exists nor does not exist." (Beyond the categories.)
5. **Release all four.** Don't settle into any. Sit with what remains — śūnyatā, the empty openness that grounds all conceptual moves.
6. **In contemplative use**, the practice is performed slowly with each "corner" felt fully and released; in philosophical use, the four are tools for surfacing the limits of conceptual analysis.

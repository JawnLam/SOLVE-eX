# Maqasid al-Shariah (Higher Purposes Analysis)

**One-line summary:** Islamic legal-theory move (al-Shatibi, Ibn Ashur, al-Qadri) that examines not just the letter but the purposes of Sharia — the five essentials plus justice, freedom, dignity.

**When to reach for it:** Approaching contemporary Islamic legal questions where the letter of classical rulings risks frustrating the underlying purpose of the law, and where renewal requires going to the maqasid rather than micro-adjusting rulings.

## Purpose

Maqasid al-Shariah ('higher purposes of Sharia') developed in classical Islamic legal theory (rooted in al-Ghazali, formalized by al-Shatibi in the 14th c., revived by Ibn Ashur and others in the 20th c.) examines the purposes underlying Sharia rather than focusing only on its surface rulings. The five essentials of maslaha (religion, life, intellect, offspring, property) are augmented in modern formulations by justice, freedom, dignity, and equality. Used to address cases where literal application of classical rulings would frustrate the underlying purpose — particularly important in modern contexts where social conditions differ from classical contexts.

## How To Use

For the legal or ethical question at hand: (1) Identify the relevant classical ruling and its scriptural and qiyas-derived basis. (2) Identify the maqasid the ruling was intended to serve (which of the essentials does it protect; what purpose does it advance?). (3) Examine the contemporary application — does literal compliance still serve the maqasid, or does it frustrate them? (4) Where it frustrates them, reason from the maqasid back to a modified ruling that better serves the original purpose. The method requires both classical jurisprudential training and contemporary social analysis. Modern proponents (Tariq Ramadan, Jasser Auda, Khaled Abou El Fadl) apply it to questions of human rights, governance, gender, and bioethics.

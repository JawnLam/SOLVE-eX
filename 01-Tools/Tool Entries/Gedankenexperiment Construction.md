# Gedankenexperiment Construction Method

**One-line summary:** A method for constructing imagined scenarios that isolate a specific concept or relationship by stripping confounding variables — the imaginary scenario forces a yes/no answer that exposes hidden commitments or principles.

**When to reach for it:** Conceptual problems where empirical testing is impossible (physics-at-the-limit, ethics, personal-identity edge cases); strategic decisions where the relevant scenario hasn't happened yet; teaching contexts where a vivid case clarifies a principle; surfacing latent assumptions in a position you (or another) hold.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

A thought experiment is **not** a hypothetical, a guess, or a vague "imagine if." It's a precisely-constructed imaginary scenario designed to isolate a single relationship or principle. The construction discipline matters: a well-built thought experiment forces clear conclusions; a sloppy one produces sloppy intuitions.

Mach's foundational observation: **physical experiments and thought experiments share an underlying logic** — both vary a parameter, hold others constant, and observe a result. The thought experiment substitutes imaginative construction for physical apparatus, which makes it powerful where physical experiment is impossible (Galileo's falling bodies: no friction; Einstein's elevator: arbitrary acceleration) and treacherous where intuition rather than physics is the input (most ethical thought experiments).

Construction discipline:

1. **State the question or principle to be tested.** Be precise. "Is X morally permissible?" / "Does Y necessarily depend on Z?" / "Is the speed of light frame-invariant?"
2. **Construct the scenario.** Specify the world's relevant features. Be explicit about what is held constant and what varies.
3. **Strip confounders.** Identify variables that could contaminate the test. Imagine them away (frictionless surfaces, infinite resources, ideal observers, etc.).
4. **Run the scenario.** What follows? What would the participant / system / world actually do? Surface the intuition.
5. **Examine the intuition.** Is it tracking the principle you wanted to test, or is it tracking a confounding feature you failed to strip?
6. **Vary the scenario.** Adjust parameters. Where does the intuition shift? Where does it persist? The shifts and persistences map the underlying structure.

The non-obvious operational insight: **intuitions are the data, not the conclusion.** The thought experiment generates intuitions; the analysis examines what the intuitions are tracking. Famous failure mode (in ethics): treating the intuition itself as the conclusion. The Trolley Problem is informative; the answer "you ought to flip the switch" is downstream of *which features of the scenario the intuition is responding to*.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the method prevents:

1. **Vague hypotheticals.** "What if AI gets smarter?" — the scenario is too underspecified to generate clear intuitions. The construction discipline forces specificity.
2. **Confounded intuitions.** A thought experiment whose intuition is being driven by an unintended scenario feature. Stripping confounders is what makes the test valid.
3. **Intuition-as-conclusion.** Treating the gut response as the answer rather than as the input to analysis. The method's last steps (examine + vary) catch this.

The framework is foundational to physics, ethics, philosophy of mind, and personal identity — domains where empirical experiment is constrained.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | State the precise question to be isolated.                                      |
|  2   | Construct a minimal scenario that brings exactly this question to the surface.  |
|      | Specify the world's relevant features explicitly.                               |
|  3   | List what you're stripping (potential confounders). For each, state your        |
|      | grounds for stripping it.                                                       |
|  4   | Run the scenario. What follows? Generate the intuition.                         |
|  5   | Probe the intuition: which scenario feature is it tracking? Test by varying     |
|      | features one at a time.                                                         |
|  6   | Map the intuition surface. Where does the intuition shift across variants?      |
|      | Where does it persist? The shifts are the underlying structure.                 |
|  7   | Decide what the experiment shows. (Often: a clearer formulation of the          |
|      | original question, not a final answer.)                                         |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **The strip step is the practice.** Most failed thought experiments have one or two un-stripped confounders driving the intuition. Identifying what was stripped (and why) is the discipline. (2) **Vivid > clean.** A thought experiment that's hard to picture won't generate intuitions. Schrödinger's Cat is famous because the image is vivid, not because cats are physics-relevant. (3) **Intuition pumps vs. arguments.** Daniel Dennett's distinction: an intuition pump (e.g., the philosophical zombie) sets up vivid imagery to drive a conclusion you'd reject on dry argument. Be alert to whether the experiment is helping you think or hijacking your thinking via vividness. (4) **In ethics especially, intuitions are not authoritative.** A thought experiment that "shows" something deeply counterintuitive may be tracking a confounding feature; revisit the construction.

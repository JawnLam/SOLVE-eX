# Yarning Circles

**One-line summary:** Aboriginal Australian dialogue method — circular, non-hierarchical, contemplative-paced conversation where speakers are not interrupted and silence is part of the discourse.

**When to reach for it:** Convening collective inquiry, research interviews with Aboriginal communities, or any group conversation where rapid-fire dialogue would short-circuit the depth being sought.

## Purpose

Yarning treats conversation as relational sense-making, not information-extraction. The circular form removes hierarchy; the non-interruption rule lets speakers find their own pace; silence is not awkward but generative. Used as community decision-making, as legitimate research methodology in Aboriginal-led scholarship, as an educational protocol, and increasingly as a cross-cultural dialogue frame. Distinguished from Western 'circle' protocols by its embedded ethical commitments to story, relationship, and place.

## How To Use

Form a circle (literal or virtual). Establish norms: speak when ready, no interruption, silence is welcome, the talking is shared. A facilitator may seed an opening question but does not direct flow. Speakers may yarn about the topic, about related stories, about their relationship to the topic — relational sense-making is the point. Sessions may last hours. Document with consent and according to community protocol. Used in research as a legitimate methodology (yarning method) by Aboriginal scholars; respect the cultural ownership when applying outside Aboriginal contexts.

# Korean Seon Hwadu (Kanhwa Seon)

**One-line summary:** Korean Seon (Zen) method of holding a single huatou (critical phrase) with sustained great-doubt — not seeking the answer but sustaining doubt-as-state until it breaks open.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging Zen-koan practice within the Korean Seon (Kanhwa) tradition, where the discipline is one sustained great-doubt rather than progression through many discrete koans.

## Purpose

The Korean Seon (Zen) hwadu method developed in the lineage of Chinul (12th c.) and Hyesim, refined under the influence of Chinese Caodong-Dahui synthesis. The practitioner takes one huatou — the critical phrase of a koan, such as 'What is this?' (i mwogo?) or 'What was your original face before your parents were born?' — and holds it not for intellectual investigation but to sustain 'great doubt' (uijŏng) as a continuous interior state. The doubt is not skepticism but unresolved engagement that doesn't seek closure. When the doubt becomes complete and total, it breaks open. Distinct from Hakuin's many-koan progression; Korean tradition emphasizes one hwadu held for years.

## How To Use

With a teacher in the Seon tradition, receive a hwadu (often 'What is this?' or 'Who is the one chanting Buddha's name?'). Sit in stable zazen. Don't analyze the question. Don't compose answers. Hold the question — let it be a felt, embodied unresolved engagement. The intellect will offer answers; recognize them as moves away and return to the held question. The doubt is to fill the entire field of awareness — not just a problem to solve but a quality of attention. Over time the doubt becomes round, ripe, total. The breakthrough, when it comes, is sudden. May take years; in formal Seon practice, retreats last 3 months at a time.

# Compounding Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Reasoning by compounding — when growth rates apply iteratively, small differences in rate produce large differences in outcome over time.

**When to reach for it:** Long-horizon decisions (savings, skill-building, relationship investment, technology adoption) where the iteration count is large enough that exponential effects dominate linear effects.

## Purpose

Compounding inverts the linear intuition that 'doubling effort doubles outcome.' Under iterated growth, small rate advantages produce dramatic long-run differences, and small rate disadvantages destroy positions. The discipline is to think about decisions in terms of their effect on the rate, not the level.

## How To Use

1) Identify the iteration count (months, years, cycles). 2) Estimate the per-iteration multiplier under each option. 3) Compute the cumulative product. 4) Note the ratio of outcomes across options — typically wider than the underlying rate spread suggests. 5) Bias toward options that protect rate sustainability over time, even at cost to short-term level.

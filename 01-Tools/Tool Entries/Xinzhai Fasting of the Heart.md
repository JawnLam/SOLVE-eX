# Xinzhai (Fasting of the Heart)

**One-line summary:** Zhuangzi's (~4th c. BCE) practice of "emptying the heart" so it becomes a mirror — responsive to the world but not affected by it. Distinct from Buddhist *śūnyatā* by its receptive-clarity emphasis.

**When to reach for it:** Daoist contemplative practice, especially for practitioners drawn to the *mirror-heart* image. Adaptable as a metaphorical orientation for anyone wanting to develop receptive clarity without the goal-orientation of Buddhist liberation.

## Purpose

The grasping mind colors everything it touches. Xinzhai is the discipline of releasing the grasping at the level of the heart-mind itself. The metaphor is the polished mirror: it reflects whatever passes before it perfectly, but holds nothing. Zhuangzi's sage acts from this state — responsive, fully present, unconditioned by attachment. The practice is less a sequence of steps than a way of being cultivated through years of letting-go.

## How To Use

Zhuangzi's instruction is metaphor more than procedure; the practice is enacted as a stance:
1. **Sit quietly**; let body settle.
2. **Begin with the ear's attention to sound** — then move attention to the heart-mind itself.
3. **Empty the heart-mind** — release thoughts, judgments, desires, even the desire to release. Don't suppress; let go.
4. **The heart becomes mirror-like** — receptive to what arises, holding nothing.
5. **From this state**, engage with whatever comes — speech, action, encounter — without losing the mirror-clarity.
6. **Daily cultivation** — over years, the mirror-quality stabilizes.

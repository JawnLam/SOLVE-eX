# Mid-Cycle Interest Pivot

**Also known as:** Abductive reformulation; interest re-specification

**One-line summary:** A reformulation move that updates a cycle's interest mid-execution when real-time observation reveals a different interest would serve the agenda better than the originally-specified one — preserving the agenda while abandoning a now-obsolete interest, distinct from giving up the cycle and distinct from continuing on inertia.

**When to reach for it:** Partway through executing a planned cycle, when observation reveals that the cycle's intended action would close a different (and better) interest than the one originally specified; when an actor reports that "the conversation took an unexpected turn" and the strategic question is whether to redirect or continue; when sunk-cost framing is producing pressure to stay the course even though staying would serve the now-wrong interest; in research / discovery work where the original objective and the actual finding have diverged. The pivot is most valuable when the actor is feeling that the planned cycle has become a sunk cost — that feeling is the trigger to examine whether the interest has shifted underneath the action.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Mid-Cycle Interest Pivot** is the operational analog of the abductive-reasoning move that drives Grounded Theory Methodology — the imaginative reformulation in response to observed data. The dissertation captures it explicitly in the Sarah → Srini saturation test (Lam 2020 Table 14, pp. 195-196):

> "Sarah observed that it may be possible reduce the number of viable competitors if she could convince Srini to step out of the competition. And so the interest for this interaction cycle and the associated decision matrix is adjusted to be as follows."

The non-obvious operational property: **the pivot updates the interest, not the agenda.** The agenda is the long-running ambition; the interest is what the agenda produces at the moment. When mid-cycle observation shows that a different interest would serve the same agenda better, the cycle's interest is reformulated; the decision matrix is rebuilt with the new interest's PP and AA; the action either continues toward a now-clarified target or is reshaped to fit the new interest.

A second insight: **the pivot is anti-sunk-cost-fallacy at the interaction-cycle level.** The pressure to continue is that the cycle has already invested resources — preparation, positioning, a meeting in progress, a conversation already in motion. The pivot's discipline is that the agenda, not the cycle's prior investment, is what determines whether the current course serves the actor. If the agenda is no longer served, the prior investment is sunk; the pivot recovers what value the cycle's current position can deliver toward the new interest.

A third insight: **the pivot is distinct from abandonment.** Abandoning the cycle means leaving the original interest and its target unaddressed, and re-entering the planning funnel for a new cycle. The pivot keeps the cycle live but reformulates what the cycle is for. This requires that the actor's current positioning still has political reach toward the new interest — that is the precondition for pivot rather than abandon.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **Inertia continuation.** Staying with the original interest because the cycle is already in motion, even though observation has shown that the original interest is no longer the right one for the agenda.
2. **Premature abandonment.** Leaving the cycle entirely when the right move was a reformulation. The cycle's prior investment may still have political reach; the pivot recovers it.
3. **Pivot to a different agenda.** Reformulating not just the interest but the agenda underneath — this is not a pivot; it is a strategic restart. The pivot's discipline is that the agenda is preserved.

For executives, founders, and any actor running political work in conditions where information surfaces mid-execution, the pivot is the discipline that converts unexpected observation into agenda-serving redirection.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

The pivot is a short, structured move executed in real time when observation has revealed a shift.

1. **Recognize the trigger.** Mid-cycle observation has produced data that changes what the cycle's intended action would accomplish. Common triggers: a counterparty reveals a different motivation than expected; a sub-cycle target becomes accessible that was not previously; a key constraint becomes movable that was previously fixed.
2. **Test the trigger against the agenda.** Is the agenda still served by the original interest? If the agenda has not changed but the interest's relevance has, the conditions for pivot are present. If the agenda has changed, this is not a pivot — return to the planning funnel.
3. **Name the candidate new interest.** What interest, if pursued instead of the original, would better serve the agenda? Be specific; "do something different" is not a new interest.
4. **Test reach.** Does the cycle's current positioning have political reach toward the new interest's target? If the actor's positioning, relationships, and ongoing action still influence the new target, pivot is viable. If not, the cycle must close and a new cycle must open.
5. **Rebuild the decision matrix for the new interest.** New PP, new AA, new options if the option set has changed. The matrix's discipline applies fully to the pivot.
6. **Execute the pivot in observable form.** The pivot is rarely silent — counterparties register the redirection. Make the pivot explicit (in the actor's own internal frame and, where appropriate, to the counterparty as a reasoned shift) so the cycle continues coherently.

A useful test: if the actor cannot describe how the new interest serves the same agenda as the original, the pivot has not occurred — this is a strategic restart that should be treated as such.

## Worked Example

A senior executive at a Series A target enters a coffee meeting with a partner from a competing Series A firm under the interest "gather information about this firm's deal preferences to inform my own term-sheet negotiation." Partway into the conversation, the partner reveals that they are not currently planning to fund any company in the executive's segment due to internal portfolio rebalancing.

The original interest (gather information about the competing firm's deal preferences) has become low-value: this firm is not a viable lead, so its deal preferences will not influence the executive's negotiation. But the executive's positioning — sitting across from a senior VC partner in a candid conversation — still has political reach.

Pivoting: the new interest becomes "secure an introduction to the partner's network — specifically other VCs who are funding in the segment." The agenda is unchanged (build the company into a category-defining firm; close this Series A at favorable terms). The cycle's positioning is preserved (the conversation is still in progress; the partner's goodwill is intact). The pivot is observable: the executive shifts the conversation explicitly from "term-sheet preferences" to "who in your network has been funding aggressively here." The partner, recognizing the candor, makes one warm intro by end of meeting. (Dissertation analog: Sarah → Srini, Table 14 — Sarah enters under "gather information about Srini" and pivots mid-conversation to "convince Srini to step out of the competition" when Srini's career-stage preference makes elimination feasible.)

## Common Failure Modes

- **Pivot used as rationalization for abandonment.** "I pivoted" used to justify leaving a cycle the actor lost confidence in is not a pivot — it is abandonment with a different name. The pivot preserves the agenda; abandonment leaves the agenda's interest unaddressed.
- **Inertia mistaken for discipline.** "Staying the course" when the interest no longer serves the agenda is not strategic patience — it is inertia continuation. The Disciplined Hold stance applies when the gate has returned No, not when the interest has shifted.
- **Pivot without testing reach.** Reformulating the interest without verifying that the cycle's positioning still has political reach toward the new interest's target produces an interest the cycle cannot serve. Close the cycle and open a new one in that case.
- **Pivot at every observation.** Treating every mid-cycle datum as a potential pivot trigger produces strategic instability — the actor never finishes any cycle. The pivot is reserved for shifts that materially change what the cycle's intended action would accomplish.
- **Silent pivot.** Failing to make the redirection observable (at minimum in the actor's own internal frame, often in the counterparty conversation as well) leaves the cycle ambiguous about what it is now for.

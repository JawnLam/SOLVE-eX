# Sankhya 25-Tattva Enumeration

**One-line summary:** Sankhya's analytical decomposition of reality into 25 tattvas — categories from pure consciousness through subtle and gross elements.

**When to reach for it:** Inquiring systematically into the layered structure of manifest experience, especially as a contemplative ladder from gross sense-objects toward subtle cognitive-faculty awareness.

## Purpose

Sankhya, one of the six classical orthodox Indian philosophical systems (Ishvarakrishna's *Sankhya Karika*, ~4th c. CE), decomposes reality into 25 tattvas (categories): Purusha (pure consciousness) and Prakriti (manifest nature) plus 23 evolutes — buddhi (intellect-discrimination), ahamkara (I-maker), manas (sense-mind), five jnanendriyas (sense-faculties), five karmendriyas (action-faculties), five tanmatras (subtle elements), and five mahabhutas (gross elements: earth, water, fire, air, ether). Used as systematic inquiry into manifest reality and as a developmental contemplative ladder from gross to subtle awareness.

## How To Use

Memorize the 25 categories and their order of evolution from subtle to gross. In contemplative practice, begin with a sense-object and trace it inward: this perception is reaching me through a sense-faculty; the sense-faculty is enabled by the sense-mind; the sense-mind serves the I-maker; the I-maker reports to the intellect-discriminator; the intellect-discriminator is illumined by Purusha. The reverse direction yields creation; the forward direction yields liberation-inquiry. Used by Sankhya philosophers as analytical scaffolding and by Yoga and Vedanta practitioners as a meditation-map.

# Avicenna's Floating Man Thought Experiment

**One-line summary:** Ibn Sina's (Avicenna) thought experiment: imagine you are created suddenly in mid-air, deprived of all sensory input — would you still be aware of yourself? — used to argue for the soul's distinction from body.

**When to reach for it:** Reasoning philosophically about the nature of self-awareness, especially whether self-awareness depends on or is independent of bodily and sensory grounding.

## Purpose

Avicenna (Ibn Sina, ~1027 in *Kitab al-Shifa*) offers one of the most famous thought experiments in pre-modern philosophy. Imagine you are created suddenly, in mid-air, with no clothing touching you, no breeze, no sounds, no sights, no smells, no taste, no proprioception of limbs against each other — complete sensory deprivation from the moment of creation. Avicenna argues: you would still be aware of yourself. This self-awareness is therefore not built up from sensory data; it is direct and prior to sense. The cognitive method: use radical sensory-deprivation imagination to surface what awareness is, independent of its usual contents. Foreshadows phenomenological inquiry by 900 years.

## How To Use

In quiet reflective attention: construct the imaginative scenario as precisely as Avicenna does. You exist. You are floating in space. Nothing touches you — no air, no fabric, no temperature, no proprioception. You have no past memory (you were just created). You have no body image (you have never seen yourself). Now ask: is there still self-awareness? Is there an 'I' that knows it is? If yes (Avicenna's claim), what does this tell us about self-awareness — that it is direct, not constructed from sensory input? If no, what does that tell us? The thought experiment is a philosophical instrument for testing the dependency or independence of self-awareness on bodily grounding. Used by phenomenologists, philosophers of mind, and contemplatives.

# Mauri Assessment

**One-line summary:** Māori method of assessing mauri (life-force/vital essence) in places, ecosystems, projects, and people, with restoration of degraded mauri as the implicit goal.

**When to reach for it:** Evaluating an ecosystem, project, place, or team where conventional metrics feel partial — where 'is this alive and flourishing?' is the real question — and where restoration rather than measurement is the work.

## Purpose

Mauri is a Māori concept of vital essence — that which makes a thing 'itself' and alive. Rivers, mountains, ecosystems, communities, projects, and persons each have a mauri that can be strong, weak, polluted, or restored. The Mauri Model assesses this not by single quantitative metric but by structured feeling-thinking with the entity. Increasingly adopted in NZ environmental management, Māori-led research, and indigenous-engineering decision-support — including for industrial projects co-governed by iwi (tribes).

## How To Use

Convene a group that includes both knowledge-holders (mana whenua, those with whakapapa to the place/entity) and practitioners. For the entity in question, walk through four dimensions: (1) Hauora (community wellbeing); (2) Whānau (family/social health); (3) Wairua (spiritual integrity); (4) Taiao (environmental integrity). Each dimension is rated on a -2 to +2 scale via deliberative discussion, not measurement. Aggregate gives a current mauri snapshot. Track over time; intervene to restore degraded dimensions. Used as decision-support for resource consents, restoration projects, and organizational health checks.

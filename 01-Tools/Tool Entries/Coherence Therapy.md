# Coherence Therapy

**One-line summary:** Bruce Ecker's brief therapy method using memory-reconsolidation neuroscience (Nader et al, 2000) to update emotional schemas via experiential juxtaposition. Claims transformational change rather than coping.

**When to reach for it:** When a client's symptoms persist despite cognitive insight — i.e., the symptom is producing a benefit the client isn't conscious of ("pro-symptom position"). Coherence Therapy targets this implicit logic directly.

## Purpose

Conventional therapy treats symptoms as problems to suppress. Coherence Therapy reframes: every persistent symptom is coherent with an unconscious emotional learning that the client experiences as necessary. The therapy surfaces this learning, then juxtaposes it with a present-day contradiction — and memory reconsolidation does the rest. Empirically, symptoms cease (not just diminish) when the underlying schema is updated.

## How To Use

1. **Symptom identification** — Name the specific symptom (anxiety, avoidance, anger) and its triggers.
2. **Pro-symptom position discovery** — Through experiential techniques (sentence completion, imagery, somatic noticing), surface the unconscious emotional logic that makes the symptom necessary.
3. **Verification** — Confirm the discovered position via the client's direct experience ("Yes, that's exactly it").
4. **Mismatch experience** — Create a present-moment experience that directly contradicts the schema (Nader-Schiller condition: same activation channel, mismatched content).
5. **Verification of resolution** — At follow-up, symptom should be absent, not just reduced.

# Biopsychosocial Formulation (Engel)

**One-line summary:** George Engel's (1977) systems-medical method — every clinical presentation is formulated across biological, psychological, and social dimensions simultaneously, reversing biomedical reductionism.

**When to reach for it:** Formulating a clinical case where biomedical reductionism (purely biological framing) would miss the psychological and social factors that materially shape illness, treatment, and outcome.

## Purpose

George Engel (1977) proposed the biopsychosocial model as a systems-theoretic alternative to biomedical reductionism. Every clinical presentation is to be formulated simultaneously across three dimensions: biological (anatomy, physiology, genetics, pathology); psychological (cognition, emotion, behavior, personality); social (family, work, culture, socioeconomic). The dimensions are not additive but interactive — biological factors shape psychology and society; social factors shape biology through stress, nutrition, exposure; psychology mediates how biology is experienced and reported. Initially advanced in psychiatry; increasingly adopted across medicine. Distinguished from purely biomedical model and from purely psychosocial model.

## How To Use

For the clinical case at hand: construct a three-column formulation. Biological column: what biological factors are operating? Genetics, organ pathology, neurochemistry, medication effects, sleep, nutrition, infection, etc. Psychological column: what psychological factors? Personality traits, coping style, beliefs about illness, mood state, cognitive patterns, trauma history. Social column: what social factors? Family system, work, financial stress, cultural context, social support, access to care. For each, note current contributing factors and how each interacts with the others. The treatment plan should address contributors across all three columns, not just the most accessible one. Used in psychiatric formulation and increasingly in primary care, internal medicine, and chronic disease management.

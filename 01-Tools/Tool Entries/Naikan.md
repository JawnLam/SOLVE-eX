# Naikan Self-Reflection (Yoshimoto)

**One-line summary:** Japanese psychotherapeutic practice (Ishin Yoshimoto, 1950s, from Jodo Shinshu Buddhism) — three questions applied to specific relationships, sustained over a 7-day retreat.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging structured self-reflection where the work is to recover an accurate account of one's relational ledger — what has been received, given, and troubled — rather than abstract personal-development work.

## Purpose

Naikan ('looking inside') was developed by Ishin Yoshimoto (1916-1988), a Jodo Shinshu Buddhist layperson, by adapting older mishirabe practices for accessible secular use. Three questions are applied to specific relationships in one's life (mother, father, spouse, others): (1) What have I received from this person? (2) What have I given to this person? (3) What troubles or difficulties have I caused this person? The questions are sustained for hours daily over a 7-day silent retreat (sesshin). The cumulative effect produces unusual character-developmental shifts, often a deep reordering of relational understanding and a softening of resentment patterns.

## How To Use

In a structured Naikan retreat: spend 14 hours per day in introspection in a small private alcove. Take one relationship at a time (typically chronological, starting with mother in earliest memory). For each relationship and time-period, apply the three questions in order, in proportion 60-60-20 (most time on received and given, smaller time on troubles caused). Record concrete instances, not generalizations. A guide (sensei) visits every 90 minutes to receive a brief report of what has been recalled. Over the 7 days, work through multiple relationships and life-periods. The detailed recall combined with the asymmetric proportions (heavy on received and troubles-caused, light on troubles-suffered) produces the characteristic shift. Lay versions exist; full retreat is more effective.

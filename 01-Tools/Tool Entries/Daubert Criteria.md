# Daubert Criteria (Expert Testimony Gatekeeping)

**One-line summary:** US standard from Daubert (1993) under which trial judges as gatekeepers evaluate expert testimony for reliability and relevance using factors: testability, peer review, error rate, standards, general acceptance.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing to admit or oppose expert testimony in US federal court (and most state courts), or evaluating whether a proposed scientific method or expert opinion meets the gatekeeping standard for admissibility.

## Purpose

*Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals* (1993) established the standard for admissibility of expert testimony in US federal courts under Federal Rule of Evidence 702. The trial judge as gatekeeper evaluates expert testimony for two prongs: reliability (is the methodology sound?) and relevance (does it help the trier of fact?). Reliability is assessed through factors including: (1) Testability — can the theory be tested and has it been? (2) Peer review and publication — has the methodology been subjected to peer review? (3) Known or potential error rate; (4) Existence and maintenance of standards controlling the technique; (5) General acceptance in the relevant scientific community (this last is the *Frye* test, now a Daubert factor rather than a standalone standard). Used to admit or exclude expert testimony.

## How To Use

As proponent of expert testimony: anticipate Daubert challenge. Be prepared to demonstrate the methodology is testable and has been tested; the underlying theory has been peer-reviewed; the error rate is known and acceptable; standards govern the technique; the methodology is generally accepted in the relevant scientific community. Educate the expert on the standard so direct testimony addresses these factors. As opponent: identify weaknesses in any factor and file a Daubert motion to exclude. The standard varies in application across federal districts and state courts; many states have adopted Daubert, some retain Frye (general acceptance only), some have their own variants. Know which standard applies. As a general cognitive frame, the Daubert factors function well beyond court as a check on the rigor of any scientific or technical claim.

# 4P Formulation (Predisposing-Precipitating-Perpetuating-Protective)

**One-line summary:** Psychiatric formulation method organizing case factors across four columns — Predisposing, Precipitating, Perpetuating, Protective.

**When to reach for it:** Formulating a psychiatric case where the question is not 'what type of disorder is this?' but 'what factors made this person vulnerable, triggered this episode, are maintaining it, and provide resilience?'

## Purpose

The 4P Formulation organizes case factors across four columns: Predisposing (long-term vulnerability factors — genetic, developmental, personality, early trauma); Precipitating (recent triggers that initiated the current episode — life event, illness, substance use, medication change); Perpetuating (current factors maintaining the problem — ongoing stressors, secondary gain, avoidance, family dynamics, ruminative cognition); Protective (resilience factors — strengths, supports, prior recovery, treatment engagement). Distinct from biopsychosocial: orthogonal frame, can be used inside any clinical-content frame. Used in psychiatric formulation, increasingly in chronic-disease and behavioral-health contexts.

## How To Use

Draw a 4-column table. For the case at hand: Predisposing column — what made this person vulnerable to this kind of problem? Family history, developmental adversity, personality traits, biological vulnerabilities. Precipitating column — what happened recently that triggered the episode? Life event, loss, illness, transition, substance use. Perpetuating column — what is currently maintaining the problem? Ongoing stressors, avoidance behaviors, cognitive patterns, family dynamics, secondary gain. Protective column — what strengths and resources exist? Personal resilience, supports, prior coping, treatment engagement, hope. Treatment can address any column; for stuck cases, the perpetuating column is often the highest-leverage. The frame disciplines comprehensive formulation.

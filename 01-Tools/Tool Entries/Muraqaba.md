# Muraqaba (Sufi Watchfulness)

**One-line summary:** Sufi practice of sustained attention to the divine presence and to one's inner states. Often paired with dhikr; develops witness-awareness of thoughts and states without identification.

**When to reach for it:** Within Sufi contemplative practice (multiple lineages — Naqshbandi, Chishti, Mevlevi, etc. each have specific forms). Adaptable for non-Muslim contemplative practitioners interested in Islamic mystical traditions, with respectful study.

## Purpose

*Muraqaba* literally means "watchfulness" or "surveillance." In Sufi practice, it is the cultivated attention to two simultaneous facts: (1) one is in the presence of God; (2) one's own thoughts and states are passing phenomena. The dual attention develops a witness-consciousness — the practitioner is not their thoughts; the practitioner is the one watching the thoughts in the presence of God. Long-term practice transforms the relationship to ordinary mental contents.

## How To Use

Forms vary by lineage; a common pattern:
1. **Settle** in a quiet place; orient body (often toward qibla); brief opening prayer.
2. **Set the intention** — attention to divine presence + watchfulness of inner states.
3. **Sustained witnessing** — observe what arises (thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations) without grasping or rejecting. The witness is the practice.
4. **Optionally pair with dhikr** — repeat a divine name or formula silently while watching.
5. **Continue for 20-60 minutes**, then close with brief prayer.
6. **Throughout the day** — bring the watchfulness into ordinary activity ("muraqaba in the marketplace").

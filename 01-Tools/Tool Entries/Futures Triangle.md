# Futures Triangle (Inayatullah)

**One-line summary:** Sense-making tool that maps three forces shaping any future-oriented issue: weight of history (pulls back), push of present (current drivers), pull of future (desired/feared aspirations). The triangle's balance reveals tensions and openings.

**When to reach for it:** Early-stage foresight workshops, when participants need to understand the dynamic forces around an issue before committing to scenarios. Effective when a future feels stuck — the triangle reveals which force is dominating.

## Purpose

Most strategic thinking attends to one or two forces at a time — usually present drivers and desired future. The Futures Triangle adds the *weight of history* as a third force — the inertia of institutional, cultural, and structural patterns that resist change. By making all three visible together, the triangle reveals where intervention has leverage and where it's stuck.

## How To Use

1. **State the issue** — frame the futures question.
2. **Weight of history** — What's pulling the present *backward*? Institutional habit, sunk infrastructure, cultural memory, regulatory baggage.
3. **Push of present** — What forces are *driving* change *now*? Technological, demographic, economic, ecological pressures.
4. **Pull of future** — What desired or feared futures are *pulling*? Aspirations, dystopias, narratives of inevitability.
5. **Draw the triangle** — Position the three on a triangle; which is dominant? Where are tensions?
6. **Implications** — What scenario emerges from the current balance? What would shift if one force weakened or strengthened?

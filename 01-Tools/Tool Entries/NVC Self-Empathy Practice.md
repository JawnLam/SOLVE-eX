# NVC Self-Empathy Practice

**One-line summary:** A four-step self-inquiry — Observation, Feeling, Need, Self-Request — that uses the NVC structure inwardly to diagnose what underlies a reactive emotional state and to articulate the unmet need driving the reaction.

**When to reach for it:** When you're emotionally activated and about to react in a way you'll regret; before NVC dialogue with another (to clear your own unmet-need backlog first); when "I just feel angry" or "I just feel hurt" isn't actionable and you need to know *what is the need underneath*; in coaching, journaling, or therapy contexts.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

The dialogue-side NVC tool (separate entry) uses Observation-Feeling-Need-Request to make a *request of another*. Self-Empathy uses the same four-step structure to *listen to yourself*. The non-obvious move is that the structure works inward as well as outward — and inward use is often the precondition for productive outward use.

The four-step internal flow:

- **Observation** — What concretely happened? Strip evaluation. ("He didn't respond to my text for two days" — not "he ignored me.")
- **Feeling** — What is the actual emotion? Use NVC's feelings inventory; "rejected," "abandoned," "hurt" are not feelings (they're interpretations) — the feelings are *sad, scared, lonely, anxious*.
- **Need** — What universal human need is unmet? Connection, recognition, autonomy, safety, contribution, rest, predictability, etc. (NVC posits a small universal inventory of ~30 needs.)
- **Self-Request** — What can I do for myself right now to honor this need (or at least acknowledge it)?

The non-obvious operational insight: **Feelings point to needs.** Strong negative feeling = an unmet need. The feeling itself is not the problem; it's a signal pointing at the underlying need. Self-Empathy uses the feeling as a direction-finder for the need.

A second insight: **Needs are universal; strategies are not.** "I need him to text me back" is a strategy, not a need. The underlying need might be *connection* or *predictability* — and once named, you can find other strategies for meeting it, not just the one you were attached to.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the practice prevents:

1. **Reactive dialogue.** Trying to NVC-dialogue another person while your own needs are screaming. The dialogue collapses because you can't listen empathically from inside an unmet-need flood. Self-empathy first; dialogue after.
2. **Strategy attachment.** Treating "I want X specific thing from this person" as the need itself. The strategy is replaceable; the need is universal. Naming the need opens new options.
3. **Affect-without-direction.** "I just feel bad" doesn't tell you what to do. Naming feeling → need → self-request converts the felt-signal into a usable next move.

This tool's lineage is Rosenberg + the NVC trainer community; the practice is well-established in NVC pedagogy but underspecified in academic literature.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | Pause. Get a piece of paper or a journal. Settle.                               |
|  2   | OBSERVATION. Write what concretely happened. Camera-language only — what would |
|      | a camera see? Strip "he was rude" / "she ignored me" etc.                       |
|  3   | FEELING. What am I actually feeling? Use the NVC feelings list. Distinguish     |
|      | feelings (sad, scared, angry) from interpretations (rejected, betrayed, used). |
|  4   | NEED. What universal need is unmet here? Connection, autonomy, safety,         |
|      | recognition, contribution, predictability, rest, meaning, agency, etc.         |
|  5   | SELF-REQUEST. Given this need, what is one concrete thing I can do for myself  |
|      | right now to acknowledge or honor this need?                                    |
|  6   | Optional: once self-empathized, decide whether to engage the other person via   |
|      | dialogue-NVC, or whether self-empathy alone was sufficient.                     |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
NVC Self-Empathy Worksheet

OBSERVATION (camera-language only; what would a video show?):
________________________________________________________________
________________________________________________________________

FEELING (NVC feelings inventory — pick precise words):
   Common feelings when needs are MET:
     content, grateful, hopeful, peaceful, energized, curious, moved,
     proud, relieved, tender, satisfied
   Common feelings when needs are UNMET:
     anxious, lonely, sad, scared, angry, frustrated, hurt, ashamed,
     resentful, exhausted, helpless, irritated

   "I'm feeling _____________________________."
   (NOT: rejected, ignored, betrayed, used, abandoned — those are
    interpretations of others' behavior, not your feelings)

NEED (universal human need under the feeling):
   Connection      Autonomy        Safety           Meaning
   Recognition     Rest            Predictability   Contribution
   Belonging       Honesty         Play             Mourning
   Authenticity    Competence      Beauty           Mattering
   Agency          Support         Trust            Order

   "The unmet need is _____________________________."

SELF-REQUEST (one concrete thing I can do for myself NOW):
________________________________________________________________

LATER (optional): does the other person need to hear this?
   [ ] No — self-empathy was sufficient
   [ ] Yes — proceed to dialogue NVC (or write the OFNR statement)
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **Feeling vs. interpretation is the hardest distinction.** "I feel betrayed" looks like a feeling but is doing work as a story about the other person's intent. The feeling underneath might be *hurt + scared*. Keep peeling. (2) **Don't skip Need.** The protocol's power lives in the Need step — naming the universal need behind the strategy. Most users stop at Feeling and miss the move. (3) **Self-Request can be small.** Often the request is just "let me acknowledge this need exists" or "let me hold this with kindness." It doesn't have to fix the situation.

## Related Tools and Frameworks

- **Nonviolent Communication** — the dialogue-side companion; this self-empathy practice is the inward precondition for productive dialogue NVC.
- **Affect Labeling** — sharpens the Feeling step.
- **Lazarus Appraisal Framework** — sharpens the appraisal underneath the feeling; complementary lens.
- **RAIN** — alternative protocol when the emotion is acute; RAIN is contemplative-somatic, NVC Self-Empathy is verbal-cognitive.

# Critical Response Process (Liz Lerman)

**One-line summary:** Four-step structured peer-feedback protocol for creative work: (1) statements of meaning from responders, (2) artist asks questions, (3) responders ask neutral questions, (4) permissioned opinions.

**When to reach for it:** Critique sessions in any creative discipline — choreography, writing, design, music, software, organizational work — where the goal is to support the maker's development without imposing the critic's agenda. Increasingly used in research, leadership development, and educational settings.

## Purpose

Standard critique gives critics primary voice ("here's what I think"). CRP inverts this — the artist directs the inquiry. Responders first state what was meaningful, then artists ask their own questions, then responders ask neutral (non-leading) questions, and only finally — with permission — offer opinions. The structure prevents the most common failure mode: critics imposing their aesthetic agenda on a work-in-progress.

## How To Use

Four steps, in order, never skipped:
1. **Statements of meaning** — Responders describe what was meaningful, evocative, memorable. "What stayed with me…" "I noticed…" Specific and personal, not evaluative.
2. **Artist asks questions** — The artist raises their own questions about the work; responders answer.
3. **Responders ask neutral questions** — Non-judgmental questions to understand the artist's intentions. ("What were you trying to do with the ending?") Not leading questions in disguise.
4. **Permissioned opinions** — Responders may now offer opinions, but only after asking permission for each one. The artist may decline.

Facilitator keeps the discipline.

# Tonglen (Giving and Taking)

**One-line summary:** Tibetan Buddhist compassion practice: breathing in suffering (one's own or others'), breathing out relief, peace, and joy. Reverses the natural avoidance pattern; cultivates bodhicitta.

**When to reach for it:** Compassion development; supporting someone who is suffering when verbal help isn't enough; daily contemplative practice. Used in mainstream contemplative training (including secular versions taught by Pema Chödrön, Trungpa Rinpoche's students, and others).

## Purpose

The default response to suffering — our own and others' — is avoidance: turn away, push down, distract. Tonglen reverses this on the breath: the in-breath actively receives the suffering (visualized as heavy, dark, hot), and the out-breath actively offers relief (light, bright, cool). The reversal interrupts the avoidance habit at the somatic-respiratory level, training a compassionate orientation that becomes available outside formal practice.

## How To Use

1. **Settle briefly** in awareness; rest in open spaciousness.
2. **Bring to mind a specific suffering** — your own pain, a friend's grief, a stranger's hardship, or all beings.
3. **Breathe in the suffering** — visualize it as heavy, dark, hot, claustrophobic. Take it fully into yourself. Don't filter.
4. **Hold briefly in your heart** — the suffering meets the spaciousness; both transform.
5. **Breathe out relief** — light, cool, fresh, spacious. Send it out to the suffering being(s).
6. **Continue for the period** — 10-30 minutes. Expand from a specific person to circles of beings (all in pain, all everywhere).
7. **Close** by resting in spaciousness without the visualization.

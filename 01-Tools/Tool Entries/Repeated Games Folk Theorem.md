# Repeated Games / Folk Theorem

**One-line summary:** In infinitely-repeated games with sufficient patience, any feasible payoff above the minmax can be sustained as an equilibrium — explains how cooperation emerges among rational agents.

**When to reach for it:** Long-term business relationships; international cooperation; reputation games; community enforcement of norms; any context where one-shot game predicts defection but observed behavior shows cooperation.

## Purpose

One-shot Prisoner's Dilemma predicts mutual defection; observed behavior in long-term relationships shows extensive cooperation. The Folk Theorem resolves the puzzle: if the game is repeated indefinitely and players value future payoffs sufficiently, cooperative outcomes can be sustained by trigger strategies (cooperate until defection, then permanently defect or punish). The theorem is non-constructive — it shows many equilibria exist — and is the formal foundation for understanding reputation-based cooperation.

## How To Use

1) Specify the stage-game payoffs. 2) Choose a target cooperative outcome. 3) Design a trigger strategy that punishes deviations with future minmax play. 4) Verify the present-value gain from sticking to cooperation exceeds the one-shot gain from deviating. 5) The strategy supports the cooperative outcome as subgame-perfect equilibrium.

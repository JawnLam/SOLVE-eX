# Hesychasm (Jesus Prayer)

**One-line summary:** Eastern Orthodox practice of inner stillness centered on continuous repetition of the Jesus Prayer ("Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner"), often synchronized with breath, with the heart as the locus of attention.

**When to reach for it:** For Orthodox Christian practitioners seeking the path to *theosis* (union with God). The somatic-respiratory form requires spiritual direction; the simpler verbal form is accessible to lay practitioners as continual prayer through daily life.

## Purpose

Hesychasm — from *hesychia*, stillness — aims at unceasing prayer of the heart. The Jesus Prayer is the vehicle. Repetition saturates ordinary discursive thought; over time, the prayer moves from mind to heart (the spiritual center, not the organ). Practitioners describe the prayer becoming self-acting — repeating itself within while the practitioner is engaged in other activities. The somatic dimension (breath synchronization, posture) is taught carefully by experienced guides.

## How To Use

1. **Stand or sit quietly**, with attention on the heart-center.
2. **Recite the prayer silently**: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner." Or shorter: "Jesus, have mercy."
3. **Synchronize with breath** (under direction) — first half on inhale, second on exhale.
4. **Keep attention in the heart** (not the head). When the mind wanders, gently return.
5. **Continue for an extended period** — initially 15-30 minutes; advanced practitioners maintain it through the day.
6. **Seek guidance** — the practice has subtle psychological dimensions (*prelest*, spiritual delusion) that benefit from a teacher.

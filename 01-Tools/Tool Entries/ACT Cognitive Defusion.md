# ACT Cognitive Defusion

**One-line summary:** Set of techniques from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (Hayes et al) for breaking literal fusion with thoughts — "I'm having the thought that…", milk-milk-milk repetition, leaves-on-a-stream, and ~50 other specific defusion exercises.

**When to reach for it:** When a client (or oneself) is fused with self-critical, anxious, or restrictive thoughts — treating them as literal truths rather than mental events. Defusion creates psychological distance without arguing against the content.

## Purpose

Conventional CBT challenges the *content* of distorted thoughts. ACT challenges the *relationship* to thought: even an accurate negative thought ("I might fail") need not control behavior if the thinker can hold it as a thought rather than as a command. Defusion is the technical skill that breaks literal fusion. The shift is from "I am unlovable" to "I'm having the thought that I'm unlovable."

## How To Use

Pick one technique for each situation; rotate to avoid habituation:

1. **Linguistic frames**: "I'm having the thought that [X]." / "I'm noticing I'm having the thought that [X]."
2. **Repetition**: Say the troubling word/phrase aloud rapidly for 30 seconds until it loses meaning ("milk… milk… milk…").
3. **Imagery**: Visualize thoughts as leaves on a stream, clouds in the sky, cars passing — observe each, don't grasp.
4. **Singing**: Sing the thought to a familiar tune.
5. **Naming**: "There's my anxiety mind doing its anxiety thing."
6. **Physicalizing**: "If this thought had a color, shape, weight — what?"

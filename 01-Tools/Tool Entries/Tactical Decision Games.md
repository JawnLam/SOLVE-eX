# Tactical Decision Games (TDGs)

**One-line summary:** Compressed-time vignette exercises (1-3 minutes) where a participant — often an officer-in-training — is given an ambiguous situation and limited info, then must articulate a decision plus rationale aloud.

**When to reach for it:** Building decision skill under time pressure and ambiguity. Used at USMC Quantico, US Army CGSC, and increasingly in business and medicine for naturalistic decision-making training. Specifically targets Klein's Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) capability — the ability to recognize a situation as similar to known patterns and act.

## Purpose

Real decision skill cannot be acquired by reading about decisions. TDGs put the trainee in time pressure with incomplete info, force a decision, then explore the reasoning. The repeated exposure builds the pattern library that RPD requires. The debrief — "What did you see? Why did you choose that?" — extracts learning that the trainee couldn't articulate without the prompt.

## How To Use

1. **Setup** — Brief a vignette in 30-60 seconds. Map, mission, situation, friendly + enemy disposition. Crucial: information is incomplete.
2. **Decision time** — 1-3 minutes for trainee to articulate orders or course of action.
3. **Articulation** — Trainee says what they would do AND why. The why is the cognitive payload.
4. **Debrief** — Group discusses: what cues did the trainee notice? What was overlooked? What pattern was recognized? What alternative would have served? No "right answer" by default.
5. **Repeat** — Multiple TDGs per session build the pattern library.

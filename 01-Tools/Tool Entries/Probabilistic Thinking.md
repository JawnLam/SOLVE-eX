# Probabilistic Thinking

**One-line summary:** Hold beliefs in probability terms rather than binary terms — update them when evidence shifts, accept that being right on probabilities is distinct from being right on outcomes.

**When to reach for it:** Forecasting, planning under uncertainty, evaluating past decisions where outcomes are partly stochastic, communicating with collaborators about confidence levels.

## Purpose

Binary 'true/false' belief structures produce overconfidence, slow updating, and post-hoc rationalization. Probabilistic belief structures track uncertainty explicitly, update smoothly under new evidence, and decouple outcome-quality from decision-quality. The stance is practiced through forced-probability self-questioning ('how confident am I really?'), explicit base-rate consideration, and willingness to be 'somewhat wrong, somewhat right.'

## How To Use

1) Before stating a belief, attach a numerical probability to it. 2) When new evidence arrives, ask 'how should this shift my probability?' and update. 3) Track outcomes to assess calibration over time. 4) Separate 'did the decision optimize expected value given known information?' from 'did the outcome turn out well?'

- Kahneman, D. (2011) *Thinking, Fast and Slow*.
- Tetlock, P. & Gardner, D. (2015) *Superforecasting*.

# Pacha (Time-Space) as Cognitive Frame

**One-line summary:** Quechua/Andean conception of time-space as a single dynamic reality — cyclical, relational, with past visible in front and future behind.

**When to reach for it:** Reasoning about long-horizon decisions, ancestral obligations, or generational stewardship where Western linear-time framings feel inadequate.

## Purpose

In Quechua, *pacha* fuses what English separates into 'time' and 'place.' Time is not an empty container moving forward; it is the unfolding of relational reality across cycles. The past is described as *ñawpaq* — 'in front' — because it is what has been seen; the future is *qhipa* — 'behind' — because it is unseen. This inversion restructures decision-reasoning: what's 'ahead' is ancestral, known, instructive; what's behind catches up. Used implicitly across Andean philosophy and explicitly in modern decolonial scholarship.

## How To Use

When facing a long-horizon or generational decision, deliberately invert the linear-time frame: place the past visibly 'in front' (what has been done, by ancestors and predecessors, that bears on this moment). Place the future 'behind' (unseen, approaching from beyond visibility). Ask: what front-of-me past is informing this choice? What might be approaching from behind that I cannot see? The frame disciplines forecasting by re-grounding it in remembered cycle rather than projected line.

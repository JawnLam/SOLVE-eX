# Statutory Canons of Construction

**One-line summary:** Body of interpretive rules (ejusdem generis, expressio unius est exclusio alterius, noscitur a sociis, etc.) used to parse statutory text. Scalia & Garner catalog ~57 canons grouped by category.

**When to reach for it:** When interpreting any statutory or contractual text whose meaning is contested. Used by appellate courts, regulatory drafters, and contract litigators; also by careful private contract-readers.

## Purpose

Statutes don't interpret themselves. The canons are crystallized interpretive principles — some textual (ejusdem generis says a general term following specific ones is limited to the same kind), some substantive (lenity says ambiguous criminal statutes favor the defendant), some contextual (whole-text canon reads provisions in light of the whole act). Llewellyn's classic critique noted that canons come in opposing pairs — but Scalia/Garner argue this is a feature: canons are aids to argument, not algorithms.

## How To Use

1. **Read the disputed provision.** Identify the textual ambiguity precisely.
2. **Apply textual canons** — what does the structure imply? (ordinary-meaning, whole-text, surplusage, ejusdem generis, expressio unius, noscitur a sociis…)
3. **Apply substantive canons** — does a constitutional or interpretive preference apply? (lenity, constitutional avoidance, federalism, expressio unius in the negative…)
4. **Apply contextual canons** — does the legislative purpose or specific-vs-general resolve it?
5. **Note which canons cut which way.** The advocate explains why their canons should prevail.

# Spanda Vibration Practice

**One-line summary:** Kashmir Shaivism's 'vibration' practice — attend to the dynamic spontaneous pulsation (spanda) that is consciousness itself, especially at gaps between thoughts, breaths, sleep-and-waking.

**When to reach for it:** Seeking direct experiential entry to the source-of-experience, where philosophical analysis (Pratyabhijna) is not the favored mode and somatic-energetic attention to spontaneous pulsation is.

## Purpose

The Spanda tradition (Vasugupta's *Spanda Karika*, 9th c.) treats consciousness not as static awareness but as dynamic spontaneous pulsation. The practitioner attends to this spanda in the natural gaps of experience: between two thoughts, between in-breath and out-breath, in the moment of waking, in the catch between fear and recovery. These gaps are where consciousness is revealed without the cover of content. Sustained attention to spanda stabilizes recognition of consciousness as its source. More practical-somatic than Pratyabhijna's philosophical method.

## How To Use

In quiet sitting, attend to the natural gaps: at the turn between in-breath and out-breath (the brief pause), between thoughts (the brief silence), at the moment of waking before content arrives, at the moment of orgasm, in the gap of a sudden startle. In each gap, notice the pulsation that remains — not a thing but a quality of aliveness. Don't try to extend the gap; let it close and reopen. Over time, the recognition extends from gaps into continuous experience. Traditionally transmitted within a teacher-student relationship; precision matters because mistaking conceptual silence for spanda is easy.

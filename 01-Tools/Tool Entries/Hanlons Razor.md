# Hanlon's Razor

**One-line summary:** Never attribute to malice what is adequately explained by stupidity, incompetence, or circumstance.

**When to reach for it:** Interpreting frustrating behavior by colleagues, customers, institutions, or counterparties; conflict-de-escalation; reading ambiguous communications.

## Purpose

Attributing hostile intent feels emotionally appropriate but is usually wrong. Most adverse outcomes from other agents are products of incompetence, distraction, miscommunication, or system pressure — not deliberate hostility. The razor exists as a default-prior shift toward charitable interpretation, which both calibrates the probability assessment and produces better behavioral responses (one responds differently to a malicious actor than to a confused one).

## How To Use

1) When you encounter behavior that feels like deliberate harm, pause. 2) Enumerate non-malicious explanations (overload, miscommunication, ignorance, system constraints). 3) Estimate the probability that one of those, rather than malice, is operative. 4) Respond consistent with the most-probable cause. 5) Reserve the malicious-intent attribution for evidence that survives the razor.

# RAIN (Recognize-Allow-Investigate-Nurture)

**One-line summary:** A four-step protocol — Recognize the feeling, Allow it to be present, Investigate it with curiosity, Nurture with kindness — that moves the practitioner through difficult emotion without trying to fix, analyze, or suppress it.

**When to reach for it:** Acute emotional reactivity (anger, shame, anxiety, grief); when CBT-style cognitive restructuring feels forced or premature; when an emotion has been suppressed and resurfaces; in coaching, therapy, or self-practice when "thinking about it" hasn't worked.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

RAIN's distinctive operational claim is that **emotions move through on their own timing if allowed**, and that the practitioner's job is to provide safe attentional containment, not solve the emotion. This is contemplative rather than cognitive — closer to Buddhist Vipassana than to CBT.

The four steps map to four common failure modes:

- **Recognize** addresses the failure of *not knowing what you feel*. Pause and name what's here.
- **Allow** addresses the failure of *fighting the feeling*. Permit the felt-signal to be present without trying to change it.
- **Investigate** addresses the failure of *generic emotion*. Ask "where is this in the body?" / "what is it touching?" / "what does it want me to know?" — but the investigation is curious, not analytic.
- **Nurture** addresses the failure of *self-criticism amplifying the suffering*. Bring kindness to the part of you that's feeling this.

The non-obvious operational insight: **investigation is not analysis.** Investigation in RAIN is somatic and curious — "What does this feel like in the chest? What is the texture?" Analysis ("Why do I feel this? What does it mean?") often re-engages thinking and pulls the practitioner out of the felt-experience. RAIN keeps investigation embodied.

A second insight: **Nurture is not a wrap-up move.** Tara Brach added the N because beginners completed the R-A-I and then dropped the practice with the emotion still present. Nurture is what makes the feeling release: meeting it with warmth.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the practice prevents:

1. **Bypassing.** Spiritual or cognitive moves that try to skip over the feeling ("I should know better," "let me just refocus"). RAIN refuses the bypass; the feeling is honored before any move beyond it.
2. **Drowning.** Getting flooded by the emotion with no structure. RAIN gives the practitioner four discrete handholds.
3. **Self-criticism doubling.** Most people layer judgment on top of difficult emotion ("I shouldn't feel this," "why am I so weak?"). RAIN's Nurture step interrupts this layer directly.

The tool is especially useful for emotions where cognitive restructuring has been tried and failed — shame, deep grief, chronic anxiety, trauma-adjacent activation.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | Pause. Find a private moment. Settle posture.                                   |
|  2   | RECOGNIZE. What is here? Name the feeling. ("Fear. Shame. Grief.")              |
|  3   | ALLOW. Silently say "yes" or "let it be" or "this belongs here." Stop fighting.|
|  4   | INVESTIGATE. Where in the body? What's the texture? What is it touching? Stay  |
|      | curious, somatic, embodied. Don't ask "why" — ask "what."                       |
|  5   | NURTURE. Place a hand on your chest. Offer the part feeling this what it needs: |
|      | "I'm here." "You're not alone." "This is hard." Whatever fits.                  |
|  6   | After RAIN: rest. Notice what's shifted. Often the felt-intensity has dropped.  |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
RAIN Session Card (use whenever a difficult emotion arises)

R — RECOGNIZE
   What is here right now? Name it as specifically as you can.
   "I'm noticing __________________."

A — ALLOW
   Permit it to be here. Stop trying to fix, change, or avoid it.
   Phrase: "This belongs."  /  "Let it be."  /  "Yes."

I — INVESTIGATE (somatic, curious, NOT analytic)
   Where in the body do I feel this? __________________
   What's its quality? (heavy / hot / tight / fluttery / sharp / cold)
   __________________
   If the feeling could speak, what does it want me to know?
   __________________

N — NURTURE
   What does the part of me feeling this most need right now?
   [ ] Reassurance        ("I'm here.")
   [ ] Compassion         ("This is so hard.")
   [ ] Permission         ("It's okay to feel this.")
   [ ] Protection         ("I won't let you handle this alone.")
   [ ] Other: ____________

   Offer it. Touch hand to chest. Wait.

AFTER:
   What shifted? __________________
   What part of the emotion remains? __________________
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **Don't skip Allow.** This is where most beginners falter — they recognize, then immediately try to fix. The Allow step is where the practice actually works. (2) **Investigate is the body, not the mind.** If you find yourself analyzing causes or constructing narratives, return to "where in the body?" (3) **If a trauma response activates** (dissociation, panic surge, freeze), back off the protocol and use a grounding move (5-4-3-2-1 senses, cold water on face) — RAIN can re-trigger if the system isn't regulated enough to allow. (4) **You may have to repeat RAIN multiple times** for a strong emotion; this is normal. Each pass releases a layer.

## Related Tools and Frameworks

- **Affect Labeling** — sits inside RAIN's Recognize step but is also useful standalone.
- **Loving-Kindness Meditation (Metta)** — provides the warmth-vocabulary used in Nurture.
- **Gendlin's Focusing** — adjacent somatic-attentional protocol; the Investigate step in RAIN draws on Focusing's felt-sense work.

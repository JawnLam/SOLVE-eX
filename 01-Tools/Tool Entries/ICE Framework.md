# ICE Framework (Ideas-Concerns-Expectations)

**One-line summary:** A three-prompt consultation tool — Ideas, Concerns, Expectations — that elicits the patient's framing of their own presentation rather than imposing the clinician's.

**When to reach for it:** Within any patient encounter, especially when the clinician suspects there's an unspoken concern ("I came because of my back, but I'm really worried about cancer"), or when the clinician wants to align the plan with what the patient actually came for.

## Purpose

Patients often don't volunteer their real concern. They present a symptom and wait for the doctor to ask the question that gets at the deeper worry. ICE makes the question explicit. Skipping it is the most common cause of patient dissatisfaction even when the clinical care is excellent — the patient leaves with their actual question unanswered.

## How To Use

1. **Ideas** — "What do you think might be going on?" or "Have you had any thoughts about what could be causing this?" (Surface the patient's hypothesis.)
2. **Concerns** — "What's worrying you most about this?" (Surface the fear, often unspoken.)
3. **Expectations** — "What were you hoping we'd do today?" (Align the plan with the patient's request — sometimes diagnostic certainty, sometimes reassurance, sometimes referral.)

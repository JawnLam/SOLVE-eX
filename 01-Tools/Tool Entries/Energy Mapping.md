# Energy Mapping

**One-line summary:** A productivity technique — Tony Schwartz's frame — that tracks and manages energy across four dimensions (physical, mental, emotional, spiritual) rather than just time, mapping personal energy curves through the day and aligning high-leverage work with energy peaks rather than fitting work into available hours regardless of state.

**When to reach for it:** When time-management approaches plateau and output doesn't improve; people who put in long hours but don't accomplish more; coaching executives or knowledge workers; performance optimization in athletic / cognitive contexts; and any context where matching work to energy state would improve outcomes more than improving time-allocation alone.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Energy Mapping** treats energy, not time, as the operative resource. The framework's elements:

1. **Four energy dimensions:**
   - **Physical** — sleep, exercise, nutrition, hydration
   - **Mental** — focus, cognitive freshness, decision-making capacity
   - **Emotional** — mood, regulation, social energy
   - **Spiritual** — purpose, meaning, motivation
2. **Energy curve mapping** — most people have predictable energy patterns through the day. Map yours.
3. **Match work to energy** — schedule high-leverage work for peaks; recovery / lower-stakes work for dips.
4. **Restore between blocks** — energy is renewable but requires recovery; without it, output degrades.

The non-obvious operational insight is that **time managed without energy management produces sustained mediocre output.** A 12-hour workday at 50% energy produces less than a 6-hour day at 90% energy plus 6 hours of recovery. Yet "more hours = more output" is the dominant assumption; energy management challenges this.

A second insight: **energy curves are individual.** Most people have a morning peak (typical 9-11am or so), a post-lunch dip (~1-3pm), and variable afternoon. But chronotypes vary — some are night owls, some morning larks. Mapping yours specifically beats generic advice.

A third insight: **the four dimensions are interdependent.** Physical energy affects mental capacity; emotional regulation affects everything; spiritual sense of purpose affects motivation across other dimensions. Improving one often improves others; depleting one often cascades.

A fourth insight: **energy management requires explicit recovery.** Sleep, breaks, exercise, nutrition aren't optional luxuries; they're the input that makes high-energy output possible. Skipping recovery looks productive short-term but degrades sustained output.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The "more hours" misallocation.** Working longer without considering energy state; output flatlines or declines.
2. **The "high-energy on low-leverage" mismatch.** Using your best hours on email; doing strategic work when depleted.
3. **The "recovery is laziness" misconception.** Skipping breaks, sleep, exercise; treating recovery as wasted time; degrading sustained output.

For executives, knowledge workers, athletes, and anyone whose output is energy-dependent, energy mapping is foundational productivity discipline.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Track your energy hourly for 1-2 weeks. Rate physical, mental, emotional       |
|      | energy on 1-10 scale.                                                             |
|    2 | Identify patterns: when are you peaked? When are you depleted?                  |
|    3 | Map work types by energy demand. Strategic work needs high mental; routine     |
|      | needs low.                                                                         |
|    4 | Align: high-leverage / high-demand work in peaks; low-leverage / low-demand   |
|      | in dips.                                                                          |
|    5 | Schedule recovery: sleep, exercise, breaks, nutrition.                          |
|    6 | Reduce energy drains where possible (toxic relationships, low-purpose work,    |
|      | physical issues).                                                                 |
|    7 | Build energy capacity over time: better sleep, regular exercise, stress       |
|      | management.                                                                       |
|    8 | Iterate. Energy mapping refines with practice and self-knowledge.              |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

       Larks: peak ~7-9am

   HIGH MENTAL ENERGY required:
       Strategic thinking
       Hard problem-solving
       Important writing
       Difficult conversations
       Creative work

       Networking events
       Difficult conversations

       Standing meetings, walking calls when low
       Sitting concentration when high

   try strategic thinking when depleted. Switching

   SLEEP: 7-9 hrs for most adults. Consistent timing.
   Affects all other dimensions.

   EXERCISE: regular cardio + strength. Restorative,
   not depleting.

   BREAKS: short breaks every 90-120 min. Walk,
   hydrate, change posture. Ultradian rhythm.

   NATURE: even brief outdoor time restores attention.

   SOCIAL: positive relationships restore emotional
   energy.

   STILLNESS: meditation, prayer, downtime restore
   mental energy.

   Skipping recovery looks productive; degrades
   output.

       12-1pm: lunch + walk (recovery)

       3-4pm: 1:1s (recovery)

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) Track to map. Generic advice ignores individual variation; tracking your own energy reveals your specific pattern. (2) Match work to energy. The single most impactful intervention. High-leverage / high-demand work in peaks; routine in dips. Switching from typical inverse pattern often produces dramatic output gains. (3) Schedule recovery. Recovery isn't optional time stolen from work; it's the input that makes high-energy work possible. Sleep, breaks, exercise, nutrition. (4) Build long-term capacity. Day-to-day optimization is bounded by underlying capacity; investing in better sleep, regular exercise, stress management, and meaningful work raises the ceiling.

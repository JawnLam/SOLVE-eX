# Laban Movement Analysis (LMA)

**One-line summary:** Rudolf Laban's framework for analyzing movement quality across four categories — Body, Effort, Shape, Space — used in dance, acting, choreography, somatic therapy, and behavioral analysis.

**When to reach for it:** Analyzing or designing movement (in performance, ergonomics, animation, somatic therapy) where the quality of movement — not just its mechanics — must be made articulable and discussable.

## Purpose

Rudolf Laban (1879-1958) developed a comprehensive framework for analyzing movement quality. Four categories: (1) Body — what moves (which body parts initiate, follow, connect, isolate); (2) Effort — quality of movement across four dimensions (Weight: strong/light; Time: sudden/sustained; Space: direct/indirect; Flow: bound/free); (3) Shape — how the body changes form (rising/sinking, advancing/retreating, opening/closing); (4) Space — body's relationship to the environment (level, direction, pathway). Used by dancers, choreographers, actors, somatic therapists; increasingly in ergonomics, animation (for character-movement design), and even behavioral analysis (for distinguishing observable movement patterns).

## How To Use

Observe a movement — your own, a performer's, a character's. Apply each LMA category systematically. Body: what initiates the movement? What follows? Where is the movement concentrated? Effort: how does it land on each of the four dimensions — is it strong or light, sudden or sustained, direct or indirect, bound or free? Shape: how does the body change form during the movement? Is it rising, sinking, advancing, retreating, opening, closing? Space: in what direction does it travel? At what level? What pathway? The four-category description articulates qualities that vague language ('graceful,' 'forceful') misses. Used as analytic tool for understanding existing movement and as design tool for choreographing or coaching new movement.

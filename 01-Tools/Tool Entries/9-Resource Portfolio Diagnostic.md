# 9-Resource Portfolio Diagnostic

**One-line summary:** A nine-category resource audit derived from the ADAPT Loop's underlying taxonomy of political capital — Money, Hard Assets, Credibility, Attributes, Legitimacy, Information, Access, Title, Tribe — that names what the actor controls, what they lack, and which of their resources are politically useful (compound across cycles) versus intrinsically valuable but non-compounding.

**When to reach for it:** At the start of any strategic conversation where the actor's resource position is being assessed (Phase 1 Origin clarification, Phase 5 capability gate); when an actor's framing of their resource state is defaulting to "I don't have enough money / time"; when the strategic conversation is treating Money and Title as the only resources that matter and the politically-decisive resources are sitting unnamed in the actor's portfolio; before opening a sub-cycle to procure a missing resource (the diagnostic names what is actually missing). Especially valuable when an actor feels under-resourced — the diagnostic often surfaces that they have more than they think, distributed in categories they were not auditing.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**9-Resource Portfolio Diagnostic** is the audit tool the ADAPT Loop's recursive prescription depends on. The dissertation names nine resource types (Lam 2020 pp. 154-156):

1. **Money** — capital available for direct exchange. Most fungible, most commonly over-weighted.
2. **Hard Assets** — physical goods or property under the actor's control. Less fungible than money but more stable.
3. **Credibility** — the actor's track record of delivering what they commit to. A politically-useful resource because it compounds across cycles.
4. **Attributes** — personal capacities (intelligence, judgment, social skill, charisma) that travel with the actor and produce ongoing convertibility.
5. **Legitimacy** — standing to act on behalf of an interest. Formal authority, role-based permission, sanctioned representation.
6. **Information** — knowledge of facts, relationships, agendas, decision-matrix compositions that the actor controls or can access asymmetrically.
7. **Access** — ability to reach decision makers directly. Meeting time, communication channels, calendar priority, independent of formal title.
8. **Title** — formal role, position, rank in the organizational hierarchy. Produces legitimacy and access but is bounded by tenure.
9. **Tribe** — the coalition of others who will reliably support the actor. A politically-useful resource because it amplifies all other resources and is the hardest to deplete.

The non-obvious operational insight is the **recursive prescription** that organizes the taxonomy (Lam 2020 p. 226):

> "Control the resources that facilitate the ability and opportunity to control more resources."

Not all resources hold equal political value. The politically-useful resources are those that compound — that enable acquisition of further politically useful resources. Money and Hard Assets are intrinsically valuable but compound only weakly (they buy more of their own type). Credibility, Attributes, Information, Access, and Tribe compound across categories — a Credibility win produces Access; an Access pattern produces Information; a Tribe accumulates Credibility on the actor's behalf.

A second insight: **most users default to a Money + Title audit.** "Resources here means everything spendable: cash, time, attention, political capital, relationships, credibility, information" (Field Manual p. 14). The 9-type lens is what recovers Credibility, Attributes, Information, Access, and Tribe — the resources that most often determine whether a political move succeeds, and that are most often missing from the actor's self-assessment.

A third insight: **the diagnostic surfaces convertibility.** A missing resource is sometimes convertible from a resource the actor has in surplus. Credibility can be converted into Access; Access can be converted into Information; Tribe amplifies whatever is being spent. The diagnostic's value is often not "you are missing X" but "you have X, which is convertible into the Y that the gate is asking for."

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **Money-and-time-only audit.** The most common political-capital depletion is invisible from a Money-and-time audit. Credibility decay, Access erosion, and Tribe fragmentation are the silent depletions.
2. **Resource framed as fixed.** Treating the resource portfolio as a fixed inventory misses that Credibility, Attributes, and Tribe are produced by every cycle's outcome. The recursive prescription is what makes the portfolio dynamic.
3. **Wrong sub-cycle.** When the capability gate returns No and a sub-cycle is opened to procure the missing resource (see `Nested Sub-Cycle Discipline`), naming the missing resource imprecisely produces a sub-cycle aimed at the wrong target. The diagnostic disciplines the naming.

For executives, founders, and any actor at the threshold of a political move, the diagnostic is the lens that converts the question "do I have enough?" into the precise question "do I have enough of the right type?"

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

The diagnostic is a structured nine-row audit, typically done in 10-20 minutes. The discipline is the explicit row-by-row pass — skipping any of the nine is the canonical violation.

1. **Define the cycle's resource requirement.** What does the intended action specifically require? (Specificity matters: "more support" cannot be audited; "the CEO's verbal endorsement at next month's executive committee" can.)
2. **Audit Money.** Cash, budget, runway. Note both the level and the discretion (a budget the actor cannot spend without sign-off is partially-controlled).
3. **Audit Hard Assets.** Physical infrastructure, tools, properties under the actor's control. Often low-relevance in political contexts; not always.
4. **Audit Credibility.** Track record of delivery. Specifically: with whom, on what, and how recent? Credibility is target-specific (credibility with the CEO is not credibility with the CFO).
5. **Audit Attributes.** Personal capacities relevant to this cycle (judgment under pressure, social calibration, narrative skill, technical competence). Watch for self-flattery and self-deprecation; both distort.
6. **Audit Legitimacy.** Standing to act. Is the actor authorized to make this move, or are they freelancing? Authorization is sometimes implicit (custom, precedent) and sometimes explicit (formal mandate).
7. **Audit Information.** What does the actor know that other actors do not? Asymmetric information is a politically-useful resource; symmetric information is operational, not strategic.
8. **Audit Access.** Calendar priority with decision-makers. Hallway-conversation availability. Speed of reply. The 8 political-capital observable signals (Lam 2020 p. 222) are largely Access markers.
9. **Audit Title.** Formal role, tenure, hierarchical position. Note the half-life: title decays at retirement, organizational restructure, or change-of-control.
10. **Audit Tribe.** Who will defend the actor's interest when the actor is not in the room? Tribe is the hardest to assess honestly because the actor may feel more support than they actually have.
11. **Read across the audit.** Where is the actor over-resourced? Where under-resourced? Where is the gap between Money/Title (what the actor was auditing) and Credibility/Access/Tribe (what the gate actually depends on)?
12. **Identify convertibility.** Can a surplus in one category be converted to address a deficit in another?

A useful test: if the actor's self-assessment matches their formal job description, the diagnostic has not actually been run. The diagnostic's value is in surfacing the discrepancy between formal resources (Title, Money) and political resources (Credibility, Access, Tribe).

## Worked Example

A founder running an early-stage company is preparing to raise Series A. Standard self-assessment frame: "do I have enough runway and a strong-enough product story?"

Running the 9-resource audit:
- **Money:** nine months of runway. Adequate margin; can walk away from a bad term sheet if needed.
- **Hard Assets:** none material to this cycle.
- **Credibility:** strong with the existing seed investors (they will refer). Untested with target Series A lead partners.
- **Attributes:** strong pitch craft, weak negotiation experience.
- **Legitimacy:** sole founder; full authorization.
- **Information:** familiar with one target partner from prior conversations; minimal information on the other two interested firms' actual deal preferences.
- **Access:** active conversations with three firms; can secure additional intro meetings if needed.
- **Title:** CEO of a $4.5M ARR Series A-ready company; tenured 4 years.
- **Tribe:** seed board is two friendly investors and herself. **No senior CFO. No experienced corporate counsel.** The gate that her runway and product credibility would otherwise pass returns a qualified Yes — she can act, but her negotiation infrastructure is thin.

The audit's diagnostic value is in the gap between the Money-and-Title frame ("I have nine months and a great product") and the Tribe + Information frame ("I have no senior advisor with corporate-counsel-grade pattern memory of Series A negotiations"). The sub-cycle that surfaces is procurement: bring on a corporate counsel before the term-sheet negotiation moves into its serious phase. (Adapted from Field Manual ch. 8, Cathna / Anna Pelham resource check, p. 27.)

## Common Failure Modes

- **Skipping rows.** Auditing Money and Title and calling the diagnostic done. The omitted rows are usually where the diagnostic's value lives.
- **Self-flattery in Attributes.** "I am a strong communicator" without specific evidence inflates the assessment. The discipline is to anchor each Attribute claim in a concrete recent example.
- **Treating Credibility as universal.** Credibility is target-specific. Credibility with the CEO is not credibility with the board chair.
- **Confusing Title with Legitimacy.** A title may not include the legitimacy to make a particular move. Legitimacy is the move-specific authorization; Title is the position-level role.
- **Auditing the wrong cycle's resources.** The diagnostic audits resources for the *cycle in front of the actor*, not their overall career resource portfolio. A different cycle's audit would weight categories differently.
- **Audit without convertibility read.** Listing resources without asking what converts into what reduces the diagnostic to inventory-taking. The strategic move is often in the convertibility.

# Synectics

**One-line summary:** Group creative-problem-solving method (William J.J. Gordon, 1961) using systematic analogy-making — four types: direct, personal, symbolic, fantasy. "Make the strange familiar and the familiar strange."

**When to reach for it:** Innovation workshops, product design, scientific problem-solving where the solution is likely to come from a non-obvious analogical leap. Used in pharmaceutical, materials science, engineering, and design contexts. Strong when the problem is well-defined but standard solutions are exhausted.

## Purpose

Most creative problem-solving stays within the conceptual category of the problem. Synectics exits the category systematically by inviting four kinds of analogy. "How is this problem like X?" — where X is something concrete (direct), something the participant feels (personal), something compressed and oxymoronic (symbolic), or something fantastical (fantasy). The analogies generate raw material; the group then translates back to the original problem.

## How To Use

1. **State the problem** as given.
2. **Reformulate** — get distance from the original framing.
3. **Apply each analogy type** in turn:
   - **Direct**: How does nature/another field solve a similar problem? ("How does a sunflower track the sun?")
   - **Personal**: If I were the problem, what would I feel? ("If I were the bottle that won't open…")
   - **Symbolic**: What two-word compressed oxymoron captures the problem's essence? ("violent harmony," "sticky lubricant")
   - **Fantasy**: What's the wildest, most unrealistic solution? ("It opens itself when it senses thirst")
4. **Force-fit** — Take a raw analogy and force-fit it back to the problem. The translation generates novel proposals.
5. **Evaluate** — Triage proposals; develop the most promising.

# Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) Confederacy Decision Protocol

**One-line summary:** Haudenosaunee "Great Law of Peace" governance protocol: consensus-seeking across clans/nations, requiring decisions to consider impact on Seven Generations forward. Influenced US constitutional thinking.

**When to reach for it:** Studying collective-decision systems with intergenerational horizons. Approached with cultural respect; specific protocols are held by the Confederacy and not freely transferable. The architectural lessons can inform other consensus practices.

## Purpose

Western democratic systems aggregate individual preferences quickly via voting. The Haudenosaunee system instead seeks decisions that the Confederacy as a whole can stand behind, with explicit weight on the well-being of seven generations forward. Decisions ripen through council and consultation across clan mothers, sachems, and the broader community. The pace is slow; the durability is high.

## How To Use

Structural elements (described from outside the tradition, with respect):
1. **Clan mothers** select sachems (chiefs) and may remove them; women hold authority over land/resources.
2. **Sachems deliberate** in council across nations.
3. **Decisions seek unity, not majority** — proposals are refined until all nations can stand behind them.
4. **Seven Generations principle** — every decision is evaluated for its effect on the seventh generation forward.
5. **Speaking protocol** — speakers address the council with formal opening ("thanksgiving address"); the form orients participants toward gratitude and relationship before debate.

*Engage with Haudenosaunee teachers and texts for primary instruction.*

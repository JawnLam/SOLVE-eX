# Mantra-Shastra (Mantra Science)

**One-line summary:** Hindu tantric discipline of structured mantra practice — selection by practitioner's nature and goal, japa count, graded deepening from vocal to silent to non-vibration.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging structured mantra practice as a sustained discipline (rather than ad-hoc affirmation), and seeking the codified science of how mantras are selected, recited, deepened, and integrated.

## Purpose

Mantra-shastra is the systematic discipline of mantra practice within Hindu and tantric traditions. Each mantra carries specific cognitive-energetic effects; each is matched to practitioner's temperament (adhikara), life-stage, and goal (sadhya). Practice proceeds through stages: vaikhari (vocal recitation), upamshu (whispered), manasa (mental), and ajapa-japa (the mantra continues without effort). Specific count requirements — typically 108 per session, often building to lakhs (100,000s) of repetitions of a single mantra — develop deep engagement. Initiation by a teacher is traditionally required for the more potent mantras; ad-hoc adoption produces shallow effect.

## How To Use

Receive a mantra in initiation, or select one with care based on traditional teaching. Commit to a daily count (108 per session is common; higher for serious practice). Begin in vaikhari (spoken). After a period (weeks to months), transition to upamshu (whispered). Then to manasa (mental, silent). Use mala beads to keep count without effortful tracking. Notice the mantra working in the field of awareness — eventually the mantra arises on its own (ajapa-japa). Through this progression, the mantra ceases to be something one does and becomes something that is happening. Tradition emphasizes patience and consistency over intensity.

# Lectio Divina

**One-line summary:** Benedictine four-movement practice of sacred reading: Lectio (read), Meditatio (meditate), Oratio (respond), Contemplatio (rest). 6th c. Rule of St. Benedict; codified by Guigo II (12th c.) into the "ladder of monks."

**When to reach for it:** Daily contemplative engagement with scripture or other sacred text, for Christian practitioners. The method also adapts to lay reading of poetry or wisdom literature where slow, savoring reading is the goal.

## Purpose

Modern reading is information-extraction at speed. Lectio Divina is the opposite — a small passage held over four phases of progressively deepening engagement. Reading becomes praying; reading becomes presence. The four-stage scaffold prevents collapse into one mode (most often, analytical reading) by structurally requiring the move from intellect to heart to silence.

## How To Use

1. **Lectio (reading)** — Read a short passage (a verse or two) slowly, aloud or silently. Read it again. Listen for the word or phrase that draws you.
2. **Meditatio (meditation)** — Hold that word or phrase. Turn it over. Let it speak.
3. **Oratio (prayer)** — Respond from the heart. Speak to God about what the word has surfaced.
4. **Contemplatio (contemplation)** — Rest in silent presence. Let go of word and prayer. Be with the Presence.
5. **(Sometimes added: actio)** — Carry the fruit of the practice into the day.

## Sources

- Guigo II (~1150). *Scala Claustralium* / *The Ladder of Monks*. Trans. Edmund Colledge & James Walsh (1981). Cistercian Publications.
- Casey, M. (1996). *Sacred Reading: The Ancient Art of Lectio Divina*. Triumph.

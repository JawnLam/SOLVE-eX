# Five Whys

**One-line summary:** Iteratively ask 'why?' five times (approximately) to drill from a surface symptom down to a root cause that, if changed, eliminates the recurrence.

**When to reach for it:** Quality incidents, recurring operational failures, post-incident reviews; any context where the obvious cause may be a symptom of an upstream cause.

## Purpose

The technique forces depth that the obvious-cause reflex skips. Stopping at the proximate cause produces fix-the-symptom solutions that recur; iteratively pushing 'why?' reveals system-level causes (training gaps, process design flaws, measurement misalignment) that fix-once. The exact number five is heuristic — keep going until further whys produce 'because that's how the system works' rather than 'because of X.'

## How To Use

1) State the problem concretely. 2) Ask 'why did this happen?' — name the immediate cause. 3) Ask 'why?' of that cause. 4) Continue, recording each level. 5) Stop when further whys are not productive or you hit a system-design issue. 6) Address the root, not the surface. 7) Verify by checking whether the recurrence stops.

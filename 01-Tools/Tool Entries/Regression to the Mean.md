# Regression to the Mean

**One-line summary:** Extreme outcomes are usually followed by less-extreme ones — because extreme observations are partly noise, and noise doesn't repeat.

**When to reach for it:** Interpreting performance reviews, sports streaks, hot startups, viral content, exam scores; designing rewards/punishments that may have been calibrated to noisy peaks.

## Purpose

When an outcome is a mix of skill and luck, an extreme observation reflects both. The skill component will recur; the luck component (by definition) regresses to its mean. Failing to account for this produces predictable errors: rewards that punish reversion-to-mean look ineffective, punishments after extreme failures look effective when they aren't. The discipline is asking 'what part of this was repeatable?' before extrapolating from extremes.

## How To Use

1) Identify the noisy outcome and the extreme observation. 2) Decompose conceptually into 'true value' and 'noise.' 3) For predictions, regress the observation toward the prior mean by an amount proportional to the noise share. 4) Resist the urge to interpret reversion as evidence that an intervention worked or failed. 5) Apply the same logic to one's own peaks and troughs.

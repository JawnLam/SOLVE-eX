# Buddhist Analytical Meditation

**One-line summary:** Tibetan Gelugpa-emphasized practice: deliberately reason through a doctrinal claim (impermanence, emptiness, dependent-origination) until conviction arises in body and mind. Alternates with stabilizing meditation. Distinct from non-discursive awareness practices.

**When to reach for it:** Within Buddhist contemplative training, especially in Gelugpa (Tsongkhapa lineage) and Lamrim-style practice. The method generalizes to any deep philosophical claim the practitioner wants to deeply internalize beyond intellectual assent.

## Purpose

Western philosophical reasoning typically aims at *intellectual conviction*. Buddhist analytical meditation aims at *embodied conviction* — where the claim has become so vividly seen that it shapes feeling and action. The practitioner reasons through a topic (e.g., "all phenomena are impermanent") with concentrated attention, generating examples, refuting counter-positions, until insight stabilizes. Then alternates with stabilizing (non-discursive) meditation to integrate.

## How To Use

1. **Choose a topic** that has a defined doctrinal claim (impermanence, no-self, dependent origination, the four immeasurables).
2. **Stabilize the mind** briefly via shamatha.
3. **Begin the analysis** — work through the claim with examples, refutations of opposing views, applications to one's own life.
4. **Hold the insight** when it arises with felt-conviction.
5. **Rest in stabilizing meditation** — non-discursive rest in the felt-conviction.
6. **Alternate** analytical and stabilizing phases through the session.
7. **Daily over weeks/months** — the topic ripens into stable insight.

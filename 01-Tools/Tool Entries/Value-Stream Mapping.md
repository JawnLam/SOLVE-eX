# Value-Stream Mapping (VSM)

**One-line summary:** A visualization of the end-to-end flow of materials, information, and value through a process — distinguishing value-added from non-value-added activities.

**When to reach for it:** Lean transformation; process redesign; identifying waste in any end-to-end process; teaching the discipline of seeing the whole rather than optimizing parts.

## Purpose

Rother and Shook's contribution to Lean is the standardized notation for visualizing complete value streams. The map distinguishes value-added time (when the customer would pay for what's happening) from non-value-added time (waiting, transport, rework, inspection). A current-state map exposes the waste; a future-state map shows the desired flow. The discipline of mapping forces the team to follow the actual process rather than the imagined one — usually revealing dramatic gaps between idealized process and reality.

## How To Use

1) Choose a value stream (typically a product family). 2) Walk the entire flow from customer request back to raw input. 3) Draw the current-state map using standard VSM notation (process boxes, data boxes, inventory triangles, information flow). 4) Calculate process time vs. lead time. 5) Identify waste. 6) Design future-state map. 7) Plan implementation.

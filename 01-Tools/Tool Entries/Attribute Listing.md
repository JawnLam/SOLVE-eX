# Attribute Listing (Crawford)

**One-line summary:** List every attribute of an object/idea/process; then systematically vary each attribute to generate variants.

**When to reach for it:** Product redesign, idea elaboration, classical creativity exercises, early-stage divergent ideation; teaching structured creativity to novices.

## Purpose

Free-association brainstorming tends to circle nearby variants and miss systematic exploration. Attribute Listing forces the explorer to enumerate the attributes first (size, color, function, material, time of use, user type) and then vary each independently. The combinatorial explosion is the point — many resulting variants will be useless, but the systematic coverage surfaces variants that free association would have skipped.

## How To Use

1) Pick the object to redesign. 2) List its attributes exhaustively (10-20). 3) For each attribute, list alternative values. 4) Generate variants by varying one attribute at a time. 5) Score variants for desirability. 6) Combine attractive variations.

## Sources

- Crawford, R.P. (1954) The Techniques of Creative Thinking.

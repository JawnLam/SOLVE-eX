# Dual-Process Clinical Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Pat Croskerry's framework for clinical reasoning that distinguishes System 1 (intuitive pattern-match) from System 2 (deliberate analysis) — with explicit cognitive forcing strategies for switching modes when pattern-match fails.

**When to reach for it:** When a clinician suspects their initial impression may be wrong, when novel features don't fit the pattern, or when stakes warrant deliberate verification. Also a teaching frame for trainees learning to recognize the *kind* of reasoning they're doing.

## Purpose

Pattern recognition is fast and accurate for typical presentations but fails predictably on atypical cases and rare diseases. Deliberate analytical reasoning catches what pattern recognition misses but is slow and effortful. The expert diagnostician toggles between modes — and crucially, knows when to *force* a switch from intuitive to analytical. Cognitive forcing strategies are the practical hinges.

## How To Use

1. **Diagnose the diagnostic situation.** Typical pattern (System 1 safe) or atypical/high-stakes/ambiguous (System 2 required)?
2. **If System 1**: name your pattern-match, then ask *what's the strongest alternative?*
3. **If System 2**: explicitly run a differential, falsify each hypothesis with available data, calibrate confidence.
4. **Cognitive forcing strategies** — "What else?" (broaden differential), "What can't I miss?" (life-threats), "What does this have in common with the case I just saw?" (anchoring check).

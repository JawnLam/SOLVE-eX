# Pa Auk Jhana Practice

**One-line summary:** Burmese Forest tradition (Pa Auk Sayadaw) method for systematic development of absorbed concentration states (jhanas) via breath visualized as a luminous nimitta sign.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging deep concentration practice within the Theravada tradition where the goal is systematic attainment of the four (or eight) jhanas, and where dry-vipassana approaches do not develop sufficient samatha depth.

## Purpose

Pa Auk Sayadaw's method (Burmese Forest tradition) emphasizes systematic development of absorbed concentration states (jhanas) using the breath as the primary object that eventually transforms into a luminous nimitta (sign-image). The practitioner watches the breath at the nostrils until the breath becomes very fine; eventually a luminous sphere arises in the visual field (the nimitta). Sustained attention on the nimitta produces access concentration, then first jhana, then second through fourth (and beyond in the immaterial jhanas). Preserves the older Visuddhimagga jhana method against contemporary dry-vipassana tendencies that bypass deep samatha.

## How To Use

Within a Pa Auk-tradition retreat or under teacher guidance: develop concentration on the breath at the nostrils for extended periods (often hours per day). As concentration deepens, the breath becomes finer and a nimitta (initially gray, becoming bright white, then radiant) arises in the visual field. Allow attention to transfer from breath to nimitta. Sustained attention on the stable nimitta produces access concentration, then absorption. Practice the five masteries for each jhana (entering, sustaining, emerging, reviewing). Progress through four material jhanas, then four immaterial jhanas. Takes weeks to months of intensive retreat; teacher-checked at each stage.

# Evolutionary Stable Strategy / Evolutionary Game Theory

**One-line summary:** A strategy is evolutionarily stable if a population playing it cannot be invaded by a rare mutant — the dynamic-stability refinement of Nash equilibrium.

**When to reach for it:** Biological co-evolution analysis; cultural-norm dynamics; strategic positioning in populations of agents; understanding why cooperation can or cannot be sustained.

## Purpose

Nash equilibrium is static — assuming everyone plays optimally. Evolutionary game theory asks dynamic stability: in a population playing strategy A, can a rare mutant playing strategy B invade and grow? An ESS resists invasion. The concept is more demanding than Nash and helps explain why certain biological equilibria persist (Hawk-Dove, asymmetric contests) while others don't. The framework has migrated to cultural evolution and to AI agent-population dynamics.

## How To Use

1) Specify the payoff matrix for strategies in pairwise interactions. 2) For each candidate strategy, check whether a rare mutant can invade — invading requires the mutant's fitness against the population strategy to exceed or equal the population's fitness against itself. 3) The strategies that resist all invaders are ESS. 4) Use replicator dynamics to simulate population trajectories.

# Empathy Maps

**One-line summary:** A quadrant-based design-thinking template — Says, Thinks, Does, Feels (sometimes extended to Sees, Hears, Pains, Gains) — that surfaces a user's inner experience by separating what they verbalize from what they think privately, what they do from what they feel.

**When to reach for it:** User-research synthesis after interviews; design-thinking workshops; customer-development sessions; coaching teams to surface user experience beyond stated needs; building empathy with a target user; and any context where "what users say" diverges from "what users actually think and feel."

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Empathy maps** capture user experience across multiple dimensions. The basic four-quadrant version:

1. **SAYS** — verbatim quotes from interviews / surveys. What the user articulates.
2. **THINKS** — what's going on internally that may not be said. Private thoughts, beliefs, assumptions.
3. **DOES** — observed behavior. What they actually do, regardless of what they say.
4. **FEELS** — emotional state. Frustration, excitement, anxiety, satisfaction.

Extended six- or eight-quadrant versions add:
- **SEES** — environment, peers, market signals
- **HEARS** — influencers, advice, media
- **PAINS** — fears, frustrations, obstacles
- **GAINS** — wants, success criteria, hopes

The non-obvious operational insight is that **what users say and what they actually think / do often diverge.** Stated preferences in surveys ("I'd pay more for sustainability") often differ from revealed behavior (purchasing the cheapest option). Empathy maps make this gap visible by separating Says from Thinks and Does — surfacing the latent dynamics that drive actual behavior.

A second insight: **the framework lives at the synthesis stage of research.** After interviews, the analyst sorts material into the quadrants. The mapping exposes contradictions (Says X but Does Y) and emotional undercurrents (Feels frustrated when discussing Z) that linear interview notes don't reveal.

A third insight: **empathy maps complement personas and journey maps.** Personas describe who; journey maps describe what happens; empathy maps describe inner experience. The three together produce a richer picture than any one alone.

A fourth insight: **the framework is participatory.** Done as a team workshop — with sticky notes — the empathy map becomes a shared synthesis exercise, not an analyst's solo work product. The team's collective sense-making is part of the value.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **The "stated-need-only" research synthesis.** Treating what users say as a complete picture; missing what they think and feel.
2. **The "behavior without context" failure.** Documenting actions without the inner state that drives them.
3. **The "no shared synthesis" issue.** Each team member has a different impression from research; empathy mapping creates shared understanding.

For UX researchers, design-thinking facilitators, product teams, and anyone synthesizing qualitative user research, empathy maps are foundational synthesis tool.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Select the user / persona to map. One map per persona.                          |
|    2 | Gather raw material — interview transcripts, observation notes, support logs.  |
|    3 | Set up the quadrants on a wall or whiteboard.                                    |
|    4 | Sort material into quadrants. Verbatim quotes → Says; observed actions → Does;|
|      | inferred thoughts → Thinks; affective signals → Feels.                          |
|    5 | Look for tensions. Where does Says diverge from Thinks or Does?                |
|    6 | Look for patterns. Recurring themes within or across quadrants?                |
|    7 | Translate insights into design implications. What does this user need?         |
|    8 | Use as input to design decisions, paired with personas and journey maps.       |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE FOUR-QUADRANT MAP

   ┌─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┐
   │       SAYS          │       THINKS        │
   │ (verbatim quotes)   │ (private thoughts)  │
   │                     │                     │
   │  - "..."            │  - "..."            │
   │  - "..."            │  - "..."            │
   ├─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┤
   │       DOES          │       FEELS         │
   │ (observed action)   │ (emotional state)   │
   │                     │                     │
   │  - [behavior]       │  - frustrated by ...│
   │  - [behavior]       │  - excited about ...│
   └─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┘

   The four quadrants together capture the user's
   external and internal experience.

THE EXTENDED SIX-QUADRANT VARIANT

   Adds:

   SEES — what's in the user's environment
       Peers, market trends, competitors, products,
       physical surroundings.

   HEARS — what influences they're absorbing
       Friends, family, colleagues, media, social
       feeds.

   These outer dimensions ground the user in context.

THE EIGHT-QUADRANT (FULL) VARIANT

   Adds:

   PAINS — fears, frustrations, obstacles
   GAINS — wants, success criteria, hopes

   The pain-gain dimension explicitly surfaces what
   the user is trying to avoid and achieve.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE — SAAS USER

   User: Sarah Chen, marketing director at SaaS company.

   SAYS:
       "I don't need another tool."
       "Our reporting takes too long."
       "I love when something just works."

   THINKS (inferred):
       "If I add another tool, my team will resent me."
       "The real problem is leadership doesn't get
       marketing."
       "Maybe I should look for a new job."

   DOES:
       Opens 5 different dashboards each morning
       Manually copies data into spreadsheets
       Schedules 1:1s with team to recap meetings
       Hasn't tried the new feature shown in last
       email

   FEELS:
       Frustrated by tool sprawl
       Anxious about being seen as incompetent
       Excited when a piece of work clicks
       Tired

   Tensions:
       Says: "I don't need another tool"
       Does: opens 5 different dashboards (drowning
       in tools she has)
       Implication: she means tools that don't work
       together; integration is the actual need.

   Says: "I love when something just works"
       Feels: anxious about competence
       Implication: she values tools that make her
       feel competent — design for her win moments.

   Design implications:
       Lead with integration narrative
       Onboarding that produces fast wins
       Avoid feature-density that signals complexity

THE SAYS-VS-DOES TENSION DETECTION

   Empathy maps shine when surfacing divergence:

   Says: "Price isn't a major factor for me."
   Does: cancels at the trial-to-paid moment.
   Says-Does tension reveals: stated price-insensitivity
   masks actual price-sensitivity.

   Says: "I'm comfortable with technology."
   Does: never adopts new features; calls support
   for basic configurations.
   Says-Does tension reveals: comfort claim is
   defensive; actual tech-comfort is lower than
   stated.

   These tensions are gold for design — they reveal
   the latent reality.

THE COMMON FAILURE MODES

   1. SHALLOW QUADRANTS
        Generic phrases per quadrant; no specificity.
        Recovery: source from real material; verbatim
        quotes.

   2. NO TENSION DETECTION
        Quadrants filled but no synthesis. Recovery:
        explicitly look for Says-Does, Says-Thinks
        divergences.

   3. INVENTED CONTENT
        Map built without research. Recovery:
        empathy maps are synthesis tools; need raw
        material.

   4. ONE-MAP-MANY-USERS
        Single map for diverse user types. Recovery:
        per-persona map.

   5. NO TRANSLATION TO ACTION
        Map produced; no design implications drawn.
        Recovery: explicit "so what?" step.

   6. STATIC ARTIFACT
        Map in PowerPoint; never revisited. Recovery:
        living document, refresh after new research.

   7. INFERRED-WITHOUT-EVIDENCE
        Thinks/Feels filled with team's projections,
        not user signals. Recovery: ground inferences
        in observed cues (tone, hesitation, body
        language, product behavior).
```

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) Ground in real material, not invention. The map's value comes from synthesis of evidence, not from team imagination. Verbatim quotes, observed behaviors, affective cues from interviews. (2) Look for Says-Does and Says-Thinks tensions. The divergence is where insight lives. Surface them explicitly; design responses tend to live at the latent layer (Thinks, Does, Feels) rather than the surface (Says). (3) Translate to design action. Maps without "so what?" are decoration. Each insight should suggest a specific design implication. (4) Pair with personas and journey maps. Empathy maps describe inner experience; personas describe who; journey maps describe what happens. The three together give richer picture than any one alone.

# Tit-for-Tat (Axelrod)

**One-line summary:** Cooperate on the first move; thereafter, copy the opponent's last action — Axelrod's tournament-winning strategy for iterated Prisoner's Dilemma.

**When to reach for it:** Long-term reciprocal relationships, negotiation tactics, international diplomacy, online cooperation; any sustained interaction with another agent.

## Purpose

Axelrod's computer tournament pitted strategies for iterated Prisoner's Dilemma against each other. Tit-for-Tat won by being nice (cooperative first), retaliatory (punishes defection immediately), forgiving (returns to cooperation when opponent does), and clear (its rule is obvious). The strategy embodies a powerful principle: reciprocal behavior aligns incentives toward cooperation without requiring trust. Generalized Tit-for-Tat (with occasional forgiveness for error) is even more robust in noisy environments.

## How To Use

1) On the first interaction, cooperate. 2) On each subsequent interaction, do what the opponent did last time. 3) For noisy environments, occasionally forgive (return to cooperation after a single defection) to avoid lock-in to mutual defection. 4) Make the strategy visible — its power partly comes from being readable.

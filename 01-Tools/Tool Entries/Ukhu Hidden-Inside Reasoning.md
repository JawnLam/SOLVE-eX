# Ukhu — Hidden-Inside Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Quechua frame in which the inside (ukhu) — even when invisible — actively shapes the whole; trust no surface without asking what the unseen interior is doing.

**When to reach for it:** Investigating a situation where surface appearances seem complete but feel deceptive, or where stable patterns suggest hidden structure-generating processes.

## Purpose

Ukhu is a stance that disciplines attention against surface-reading. In Andean textile cognition, the interior structure of a weave determines the exterior pattern; in social reasoning, what is unsaid drives what is done; in ecology, subterranean processes (water tables, root networks, soil microbiomes) generate surface phenomena. The frame trains a habitual question: 'what is the ukhu of this?' Practitioners report it as protection against being deceived by visible coherence.

## How To Use

Encounter a situation, person, system, or pattern. Note its surface — what is plainly visible. Then ask: (1) What is the ukhu of this — the inside, hidden structure? (2) What weaves or forces are operating below the visible? (3) What would shift on the surface if the ukhu shifted? (4) What can I do to access or sense the ukhu — slow down, listen for silences, look at the back of the weave, observe what is consistently *not* said? The practice doesn't generate answers directly; it disciplines patience until the inside reveals itself.

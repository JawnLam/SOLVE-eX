# Originalism (Constitutional Interpretation)

**One-line summary:** Constitutional provisions should be interpreted according to their original public meaning at the time of ratification — text-anchored, history-anchored interpretation.

**When to reach for it:** Constitutional interpretation in originalist legal traditions; arguments about whether contemporary readings honor or depart from constitutional structure.

## Purpose

Originalism is a family of constitutional-interpretation schools (original intent, original public meaning, original methods) sharing the commitment that constitutional meaning is fixed at ratification. The discipline anchors interpretation in historical research about what the words meant to the public at the time. Critics argue the historical recovery is uncertain and that constitutional text is necessarily applied to novel circumstances. The school remains a major force in American constitutional law.

## How To Use

1) Identify the constitutional provision in question. 2) Research the public meaning of the words at ratification (dictionaries, contemporaneous usage, legal context). 3) Apply that meaning to the contemporary question. 4) Resist expansionary readings not supported by original meaning. 5) Use historical sources rigorously; recognize uncertainty.

# Hohfeldian Rights Analysis

**One-line summary:** Wesley Hohfeld's (1913) decomposition of the loose word "right" into four jural correlatives (right/duty, privilege/no-right, power/liability, immunity/disability) — surfacing what kind of right is actually at stake.

**When to reach for it:** When a legal or political dispute hinges on what someone has the "right" to do, and the ambiguity of that word is doing concealed work. Useful in property, constitutional, and human-rights analysis; also as a clarifying frame in non-legal ethics debates.

## Purpose

"Right" is a portmanteau term. Hohfeld showed it bundles four conceptually distinct relations: a claim-right entails a duty in another; a privilege entails no-right in another; a power changes another's legal position; an immunity blocks another's power. Confusing them is the source of much sloppy rights-talk. The framework forces precision.

## How To Use

1. **Identify the alleged "right."** State it plainly.
2. **Classify it.** Which of Hohfeld's four is it?
   - Claim-right: I am owed something (you have a duty).
   - Privilege: I am free to do something (you have no claim against me).
   - Power: My action changes your legal position (you are subject to my power).
   - Immunity: You cannot change my legal position (your power is disabled).
3. **State the correlative.** For each Hohfeldian, who holds the opposite?
4. **Test conclusions.** If switching the type changes the conclusion, the dispute was actually about *which kind of right*.

# Geoffrey Moore Chasm Segmentation

**One-line summary:** A market-segmentation model for technology diffusion that names the discontinuity between early adopters and the early majority — the "chasm" — and prescribes a beachhead-and-bowling-alley strategy for crossing it.

**When to reach for it:** Early-stage technology products with strong enthusiast traction but stalled mainstream growth, B2B SaaS go-to-market planning, internal innovation rollouts that have champions but no momentum, and any diffusion problem where the prevailing strategy assumes a smooth adoption curve when the data shows a discontinuity.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

In 1962, Everett Rogers's *Diffusion of Innovations* mapped a smooth bell-curve of adoption: innovators (2.5%), early adopters (13.5%), early majority (34%), late majority (34%), laggards (16%). Geoffrey Moore's 1991 contribution — *Crossing the Chasm* — was the empirical observation that the curve is not smooth in technology markets. Between early adopters and the early majority sits a **chasm**: a discontinuity in buying psychology that defeats most products despite strong early-adopter traction.

The discontinuity has a clear cause. Early adopters (visionaries) buy on the *promise* of strategic advantage; they tolerate roughness, integrate the product into other moving parts themselves, and reward novelty. The early majority (pragmatists) buy on *proof of value within their reference community*; they want a solid, complete, supported product with credible peer success stories. The two groups have incompatible buying processes, references, and evidence preferences. A go-to-market built for visionaries does not work on pragmatists.

Moore's prescription is operational, not just diagnostic:

- **Pick a beachhead segment.** Not "small businesses" or "the financial sector" — a specific niche of pragmatists who share a common compelling problem, common information sources, and common references. The segment must be small enough to dominate quickly.
- **Build a whole product** for that segment. The whole product is everything around the core technology that the pragmatist needs to succeed: integrations, services, training, references, ecosystem partners. Visionaries fill these gaps themselves; pragmatists will not.
- **Dominate the beachhead.** Become the obvious leader in the segment — Moore quantifies "dominate" loosely as 50%+ market share within the niche. This generates the references, case studies, and peer endorsements that unlock adjacent pragmatist segments.
- **Bowl the next pin.** Adjacent segments fall in sequence (the "bowling alley") because each beachhead's pragmatists are credible references for the next adjacent segment's pragmatists. Then a tornado phase: the broader pragmatist market tips and growth becomes general.

The non-obvious operational insight: **the chasm cannot be crossed by trying to serve everyone.** Most products fail in the chasm by spreading thin across multiple "promising" segments rather than concentrating force on one beachhead. The discipline is segment commitment, not market expansion.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **Visionary-extrapolation failure.** Strong early-adopter traction is read as product-market fit. The team scales sales and marketing assuming the rest of the market will buy the same way. Pragmatists do not, and the company runs out of runway with confused messaging and a fragmented customer base.
2. **Beachhead diffusion.** The team picks too broad a "first segment" — by industry, by company size, or by geography — and cannot dominate any sub-segment within it. References stay thin, peer signals stay weak, and the next segment never tips.
3. **Whole-product blind spot.** The technology is excellent and the team treats the surrounding ecosystem (integrations, training, support, partners) as future work. Pragmatists, who do not assemble the whole product themselves, churn or never adopt. The team interprets this as a sales problem rather than a product-completeness problem.

For consulting and strategy work, the framework is also a useful diagnostic on internal change initiatives: a new tool, process, or methodology has visionary champions inside the organization but cannot reach mainstream adoption. The chasm explanation usually fits, and the prescription (pick a beachhead team, deliver complete value there, generate internal references, then expand) is operationally directly transferable.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Diagnose where you are on the curve. Are your customers / users visionaries or   |
|      | pragmatists? (Visionaries: tolerate roughness, integrate gaps themselves, value  |
|      | novelty. Pragmatists: want references, completeness, peer validation.)          |
|    2 | If you have visionary traction but mainstream stall, the chasm is the diagnosis. |
|    3 | Identify candidate beachhead segments. Each candidate must have: (a) a           |
|      | compelling reason to buy this year, (b) a shared peer reference network,        |
|      | (c) an identifiable budget owner, (d) small enough to dominate within ~12-18mo. |
|    4 | Pick ONE beachhead. The discipline is commitment — you are choosing not to       |
|      | serve other segments in the near term, even if they look promising.             |
|    5 | Whole-product audit: for the chosen beachhead, list everything a pragmatist      |
|      | needs to succeed. What's missing? Build or partner for the gaps before scale.   |
|    6 | Pragmatist messaging: rewrite all positioning for proof and reference, not       |
|      | promise and novelty. Lead with case studies, ROI, integration depth.            |
|    7 | Dominate the beachhead. Win it visibly. 50%+ share within the niche; widely     |
|      | known case studies; conference presence; analyst coverage in the niche.         |
|    8 | Adjacent expansion: identify the next pragmatist segment whose references       |
|      | overlap with the beachhead. Repeat the whole-product → dominate cycle.          |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE ROGERS / MOORE ADOPTION CURVE WITH CHASM

       innovators   early adopters    [ CHASM ]   early majority   late maj.   laggards
         (2.5%)        (13.5%)                       (34%)         (34%)      (16%)

       buy on:        buy on:                       buy on:        buy on:    buy on:
       novelty        strategic                     proof          risk-      necessity
                      advantage                     within         minimization
                                                    reference
                                                    community

   The chasm sits between early adopters and the early majority because the
   buying psychologies are incompatible. A go-to-market that worked on the
   left side of the chasm will not work on the right side without redesign.

THE BEACHHEAD-SEGMENT TEMPLATE

   Candidate segment: __________________________________________________

   1. Compelling reason to buy THIS YEAR (not "would benefit from"):
       _________________________________________________________________

   2. Shared peer reference network (where do they look for proof?):
       _________________________________________________________________

   3. Identifiable budget owner (specific role, specific authority):
       _________________________________________________________________

   4. Size: small enough to dominate in 12-18 months, large enough to
      justify a whole-product investment:
       _________________________________________________________________

   5. Adjacent segments whose references overlap (the next bowling pins):
       _________________________________________________________________

   Verdict: pursue / defer / reject

   The discipline is to pick ONE. Multiple beachheads = no beachhead.

THE WHOLE-PRODUCT AUDIT

   For the chosen beachhead, list every component the pragmatist needs:

   [ ] Core product / technology                   (likely covered)
   [ ] Generic ecosystem (3rd-party integrations)  __________________
   [ ] Application-specific services               __________________
   [ ] Training / onboarding                       __________________
   [ ] Implementation support                      __________________
   [ ] Ongoing support / SLA                       __________________
   [ ] Reference customers in this segment         __________________
   [ ] Documented case studies / ROI data          __________________
   [ ] Analyst / press coverage in the niche       __________________
   [ ] Peer / community / user-group presence      __________________
   [ ] Standards / certifications relevant here    __________________

   For each gap: build / partner / accept (and explain why).

VISIONARY vs PRAGMATIST MESSAGING (the rewrite)

                       VISIONARY                  PRAGMATIST
   Lead with...        promise / vision           proof / reference
   Evidence type...    demos, beta access         case studies, ROI
   Tolerance for...    roughness, gaps            zero gaps
   Decision speed...   fast, individual           slow, committee
   Budget source...    discretionary / strategic  operational / approved
   Reference need...   none ("I'll be first")     deep ("who's like me?")
   Sales motion...     consultative, technical    proof-driven, reference

   Same product. Different language. Different evidence. Different
   sales motion. The product team often resists this rewrite because
   the visionary version "feels truer to what we're really doing."
   The pragmatist will not buy the visionary version.

THE TORNADO (the payoff phase)

   When the bowling alley has knocked down enough adjacent segments, the
   broader pragmatist market tips and adoption becomes general. Strategy
   in the tornado is different again: shift from beachhead depth to
   breadth, race for share, defer customization, win the standard.

   Most companies do not get to the tornado. The discipline of the chasm
   is what makes the tornado possible.

THE INTERNAL-CHANGE TRANSLATION

   This framework applies to *internal* adoption of new tools, processes,
   or methodologies inside an organization. Read the curve as:

       innovators       — the experimentation team
       early adopters   — the few teams that volunteer
       chasm            — most teams will not adopt because the early
                            adopters' use case feels foreign
       early majority   — adopt when the methodology has been adapted
                            to a team like theirs and references exist
                            from within the company

   Beachhead strategy: pick one team or function whose pain is acute,
   deliver a complete solution to them, generate internal references,
   then expand to the adjacent function.
```

> **Operational notes:** Four disciplines. (1) The hardest move is segment commitment — saying "we will not pursue these other promising customers" while runway burns. The framework is correct that splitting effort across multiple "first" segments is what kills most products in the chasm, but the political cost of committing is high. Pre-commit on paper, with named segments excluded, before sales pressure makes the commitment feel like a constraint to relax. (2) The whole-product audit reveals work the engineering team is allergic to (integrations, services, partner ecosystem, certifications). That work is the actual barrier to the chasm; product excellence in isolation does not cross it. (3) The framework is descriptive of *technology-discontinuous* products. For incremental innovations (better-faster-cheaper versions of established categories), Rogers's smooth curve fits and the chasm is less load-bearing. Diagnose which case you are in before applying the prescription. (4) Moore's later books (*Inside the Tornado*, *Living on the Fault Line*, *Zone to Win*) extend the framework into post-chasm dynamics; the original *Crossing the Chasm* is still the core operational reference for the chasm-phase decision.

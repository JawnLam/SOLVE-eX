# Kaizen (Continuous Improvement)

**One-line summary:** A culture-level commitment to small continuous improvements, by everyone, every day — distinct from large episodic transformation initiatives.

**When to reach for it:** Building improvement culture in any organization; teaching that small ongoing improvements compound over time; contrasting with large-bang transformation approaches.

## Purpose

Imai contrasted Western 'innovation' (large jumps via episodic transformation) with Japanese 'kaizen' (continuous small improvements). The two are not mutually exclusive, but kaizen alone — small improvements by frontline workers, every day, on their own work — produces compounding gains that match or exceed innovation programs. The discipline requires sustained leadership commitment, frontline empowerment, and explicit time for improvement. The cultural translation is harder than the technique itself.

## How To Use

1) Empower every employee to identify and propose small improvements to their own work. 2) Provide explicit time, tools, and recognition for improvement. 3) Implement small improvements quickly rather than perfecting large ones slowly. 4) Track and celebrate cumulative effect over time. 5) Leadership models the discipline visibly.

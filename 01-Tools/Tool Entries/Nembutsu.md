# Nembutsu (Pure Land Recitation)

**One-line summary:** Pure Land contemplative practice centered on recitation of Amida Buddha's name — enacting a shift from self-power (jiriki) to other-power (tariki).

**When to reach for it:** Engaging contemplative practice where self-power (effortful striving) feels exhausted or arrogant, and a tradition centered on radical reliance (other-power) is sought.

## Purpose

Nembutsu is the central practice of Pure Land Buddhism, refined in the Japanese traditions of Honen (12th c.) and Shinran (13th c.). The practitioner recites 'Namu Amida Butsu' (Namo Amituofo in Chinese), the name of Amida Buddha. The recitation is not an effort to earn liberation but consent to Amida's already-given vow to save all beings. The cognitive payload: shift from jiriki (self-power, the deluded notion that liberation must be earned by individual striving) to tariki (other-power, recognition that the bond with awakening was made by Amida, not by self). The practice enacts the shift.

## How To Use

In quiet or in motion, recite 'Namu Amida Butsu' (or in Chinese contexts, 'Namo Amituofo'). The recitation can be vocal, whispered, or mental. Don't focus on counting or on achieving a specific state — the recitation itself is the practice, the consent. When the self-power instinct arises (wanting to earn, wanting results), notice it and continue the recitation. Over time, the practice deepens not into a different state but into a different relationship with practice — from working-for to consenting-to. May be practiced briefly throughout the day or in concentrated sessions. Particularly useful for those for whom effortful concentration practices have become a subtle ego-project.

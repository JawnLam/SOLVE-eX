# Quaker Discernment (Clearness Committee / Sense of the Meeting)

**One-line summary:** Friends Meeting practice for collective discernment — Clearness Committees for personal decisions, Meetings for Business with "sense of the meeting" as decision protocol (vs voting). Silence-grounded; trust in inward Light.

**When to reach for it:** When a Quaker or Quaker-style community faces a personal or collective decision and seeks to discern rather than vote. Adaptable to non-religious groups committed to consensus and contemplative process — increasingly used in academic, nonprofit, and intentional-community contexts.

## Purpose

Voting forces individuals to take positions before they may understand. Quaker discernment instead seeks the "sense of the meeting" — the discernment that arises from collective silent listening. Decisions emerge rather than being made. The form trains participants to speak only when moved, to listen for what's underneath, and to let go of personal investment in outcomes.

## How To Use

**Clearness Committee (personal decision)**:
1. **Focus person** brings a question, writes a brief statement.
2. **Committee** (4-6 trusted Friends) gathers; opens with silence.
3. **Asking only honest, open questions** (not advice, not leading questions, not statements disguised as questions).
4. **Focus person responds** to what arises; silence between responses.
5. **Continues 1-2 hours**; close with silent reflection.

**Meeting for Business (collective decision)**:
1. **Item presented**; silence.
2. **Friends speak as moved** — one at a time, brief, considered.
3. **Clerk** listens for the sense of the meeting.
4. **Clerk proposes a minute** capturing what has been discerned; meeting tests it.
5. **If unity** (not unanimity — willingness to stand aside), the minute is approved.

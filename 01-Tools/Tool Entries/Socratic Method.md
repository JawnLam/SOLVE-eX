# Socratic Method

**One-line summary:** A structured dialogical method using probing questions to surface contradictions in beliefs, leading interlocutors to recognize the limits of their understanding.

**When to reach for it:** Teaching critical thinking; legal pedagogy; philosophical dialogue; coaching conversations where insight must come from the client; testing belief structures.

## Purpose

The Socratic Method is structured questioning that exposes inconsistency, vagueness, or unwarranted certainty in beliefs. The questioner doesn't deliver content; they probe. Through layered questions ('What do you mean by X?', 'How do you know?', 'What if Y?'), the interlocutor's reasoning is laid bare for examination. Done well, the method produces aporia (recognition of one's own ignorance) which is the precondition for genuine inquiry. The method is taught in law school, philosophy, and increasingly in clinical and coaching contexts.

## How To Use

1) Identify the belief or claim to examine. 2) Ask for clarification: 'What do you mean by...?' 'Can you give an example?' 3) Probe assumptions: 'What are you assuming there?' 'Why do you assume that?' 4) Probe reasons and evidence: 'How do you know?' 'What's your evidence?' 5) Probe implications: 'If that's true, what follows?' 6) Examine alternatives: 'What might someone disagree with?' 7) Let the interlocutor draw conclusions.

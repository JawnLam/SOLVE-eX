# Occam's Razor

**One-line summary:** Among competing hypotheses that fit the evidence equally well, prefer the one that requires the fewest additional assumptions.

**When to reach for it:** Comparing explanations, debugging, hypothesis selection, theory choice; whenever a complex story competes with a simple one.

## Purpose

Occam's razor is a tiebreaker, not a truth criterion. When two hypotheses make identical predictions, the simpler one is preferable because it carries fewer unverified commitments, is easier to falsify, and tracks the Bayesian intuition that complex hypotheses have lower prior probability. The razor does not say the simpler hypothesis is true — only that it should be the working assumption until evidence forces complication.

## How To Use

1) List candidate hypotheses. 2) For each, list the assumptions it requires. 3) Identify the hypothesis with the fewest independent assumptions consistent with the evidence. 4) Adopt it as the working hypothesis. 5) Be ready to abandon it the moment evidence forces a more complex explanation. 6) Resist adding epicycles to save a preferred hypothesis.

# Cialdini Influence Principles (6+1 Checklist)

**One-line summary:** The seven principles of influence — reciprocity, commitment-consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity, unity — used both offensively (ethical persuasion) and defensively (recognition of manipulation).

**When to reach for it:** Designing persuasion campaigns; analyzing why an influence attempt succeeded or failed; teaching people to recognize and resist manipulation; structured negotiation preparation.

## Purpose

Cialdini's empirical research distilled influence into seven principles operating at the level of automatic response: reciprocity (we feel obligated to return favors), commitment-consistency (we want to align with prior commitments), social proof (we follow what similar others do), liking (we are influenced by those we like), authority (we defer to apparent expertise), scarcity (we value what's limited), unity (we ally with our in-group). The checklist serves both ethical-influence design and defensive-recognition. The principles are not manipulation tricks — they're the underlying psychology that any persuasion attempt engages.

## How To Use

1) For an influence task, scan each principle: is it operative? could it be leveraged ethically? 2) For receiving an influence attempt: scan whether the principles are being weaponized inappropriately. 3) Design communications that activate principles consistent with truth and the recipient's interest. 4) Avoid principles that exploit asymmetries (scarcity manufactured artificially, false authority).

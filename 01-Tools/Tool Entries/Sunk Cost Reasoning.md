# Sunk Cost Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Past costs that cannot be recovered should not influence forward decisions — only the comparison of expected forward costs and benefits matters.

**When to reach for it:** Continuing-vs-pivoting decisions on projects with significant historical investment; ongoing relationships, careers, or investments where 'we've already put so much in' threatens to override forward analysis.

## Purpose

Sunk costs feel salient because of loss aversion and identity attachment, but they are economically irrelevant — they're paid either way. The rational forward question is: from this point, do the expected future returns of continuing exceed the expected future returns of pivoting? The tool is the explicit re-anchoring of the decision frame to 'forward only.'

## How To Use

1) State the decision in forward-only terms: 'starting from now, with these resources, would I commit them to this project?' 2) If the answer is no, the historical investment is irrelevant — pivot. 3) If yes, continue, but note that the past investment did not justify the continuation; the forward analysis did. 4) Use pre-mortem to test for motivated continuation reasoning.

## Sources

- Thaler, R. (1980) 'Toward a positive theory of consumer choice.' *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*.

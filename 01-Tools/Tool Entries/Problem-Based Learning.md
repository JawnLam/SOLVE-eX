# Problem-Based Learning (PBL)

**One-line summary:** Curriculum organized around authentic problems — small groups work through real cases, identifying learning needs, researching, and applying — rather than lecture-based content delivery.

**When to reach for it:** Professional education (medical, law, engineering); curricula that aim at integrated application rather than discrete knowledge; teaching self-directed learning.

## Purpose

PBL originated at McMaster Medical School in 1968 as a corrective to lecture-heavy medical education that produced students who couldn't apply knowledge to clinical situations. PBL flips the structure: students encounter a clinical case, identify what they don't know, research it, and apply it. The method develops integrated case-based knowledge, self-directed learning skills, and small-group collaboration. Evidence shows PBL students may have somewhat lower factual recall but better long-term retention and stronger applied skills.

## How To Use

1) Develop authentic problem cases (e.g., clinical vignettes). 2) Small groups (5-8) tackle each case. 3) Group identifies what is known, what's unknown, what to learn. 4) Distribute research tasks among members. 5) Reconvene to apply research to case. 6) Faculty tutors facilitate but don't lecture. 7) Iterate across many cases over the curriculum.

# Saamya (Equality / Even-Mindedness Cultivation)

**One-line summary:** Bhagavad Gita's discipline of even-mindedness across pairs of opposites (pleasure-pain, gain-loss, victory-defeat) — cultivated through repeated application until inner state stabilizes regardless of outcome.

**When to reach for it:** Working in karma-yoga mode where outcomes are uncertain, attachment to results would distort action, and a cultivated equanimity is the desired interior posture.

## Purpose

Saamya ('equality' or 'even-mindedness') is the central practice of karma-yoga as taught in the *Bhagavad Gita*. The text instructs: act without attachment to results; remain even-minded across pairs of opposites (sukha-duhkha, pleasure-pain; labha-alabha, gain-loss; jaya-ajaya, victory-defeat). This is not numbness, not indifference — it is a cultivated stability of inner state across external fluctuation. The stance enables sustained, well-aimed action because the interior is not buffeted. Considered foundational to skill-in-action (yoga karmasu kaushalam).

## How To Use

In daily activity, notice when outcomes have arrived: a small win, a small loss, praise, criticism. Pause. Observe the interior reaction. Then, without suppressing it, return to even-mindedness — neither inflated by the win nor deflated by the loss. The practice is not pretending the outcome doesn't matter; it is recognizing that the inner state's stability is the practitioner's responsibility, separate from outcome. Over time, the stable interior becomes default; sharp reactions become rarer. Pair with karma-yoga ethic: act fully and well, but release attachment to specific results.

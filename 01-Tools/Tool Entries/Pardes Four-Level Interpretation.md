# Pardes Four-Level Interpretation

**One-line summary:** Jewish four-level hermeneutic — Peshat (literal), Remez (allusive), Drash (homiletic), Sod (mystical) — treating a text as inviting four distinct interpretive registers simultaneously.

**When to reach for it:** When approaching a text dense enough to reward multiple readings — sacred text, literary classic, philosophical work. The practice generalizes beyond Jewish scripture to any text where surface, allusion, homily, and mystical reading all yield distinct insight.

## Purpose

Modern interpretive habits collapse to one register at a time — usually peshat (what does this literally say?) or sometimes drash (what does this mean for us?). The Pardes discipline is to hold all four registers open as legitimate, generative, and non-competing. The text becomes a multidimensional object; reading becomes traversal through dimensions.

## How To Use

1. **Peshat** — Read literally. What does the text say in its plain sense, in its historical context?
2. **Remez** — Read for allusion. What other texts does this echo? What patterns? What hints at deeper meaning?
3. **Drash** — Read homiletically. What ethical or practical teaching emerges? What does it ask of the reader?
4. **Sod** — Read mystically. What does the text say about ultimate reality, the divine, the inner structure of being? (Reserved for advanced practice; warned against by tradition for the unprepared.)

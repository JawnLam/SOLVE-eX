# Meadows 12 Leverage Points

**One-line summary:** A ranked hierarchy of system-intervention points — from low-leverage (changing parameters) to high-leverage (paradigms and the power to transcend paradigms).

**When to reach for it:** System-design decisions, policy interventions, organizational change strategy; deciding where to apply scarce influence in a complex system.

## Purpose

Meadows's classic ranking: numbers and parameters have less leverage than buffers, which have less than stocks-and-flows, which have less than delays, which have less than feedback-loop strengths, which have less than information flows, which have less than rules, which have less than self-organization, which have less than goals, which have less than paradigms, which have less than the power to transcend paradigms. The ranking is heuristic but the directional claim — that paradigm-level interventions have outsized leverage — is robust. The tool gives change agents a vocabulary for selecting intervention targets.

## How To Use

1) Map the system. 2) For each candidate intervention, locate it on the leverage-points hierarchy. 3) Bias toward higher-leverage points where feasible. 4) Recognize that high-leverage interventions are also harder, slower, and more contested. 5) Sometimes the right move is multiple low-leverage interventions; sometimes one high-leverage one.

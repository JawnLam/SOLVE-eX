# Beers Criteria (Polypharmacy Review)

**One-line summary:** AGS-maintained list of potentially inappropriate medications for older adults — used as a structured decision-aid when reviewing an elder's medication list.

**When to reach for it:** Reviewing an older adult's medication list for potential problems — especially when polypharmacy is suspected or when the patient is showing symptoms (falls, confusion, urinary retention) that might be medication-induced.

## Purpose

The Beers Criteria, maintained by the American Geriatrics Society and regularly updated (most recently 2023), is a list of potentially inappropriate medications for older adults. The criteria are organized by drug class with specific concerns: medications to avoid in older adults regardless of condition; medications to avoid in older adults with certain conditions; medications to use with caution; drug-drug interactions to avoid; medications that should be avoided or dose-reduced based on renal function. Each entry includes the rationale (e.g., increased fall risk, anticholinergic burden, accumulation due to age-related pharmacokinetics).

## How To Use

For an older adult's medication list, work through each medication and check against the current Beers Criteria. For each match, examine the patient-specific situation: is the indication current? Is there a safer alternative? Can the dose be reduced? Is the cumulative anticholinergic burden too high? Is renal-adjusted dosing being used? The criteria are a decision-aid, not a mandate — some patients legitimately need Beers-listed medications. The discipline: each medication on the list is examined and decided about explicitly, rather than continued by default. Particularly important at transitions of care (hospital admission, discharge, nursing home placement) when medication lists tend to accumulate.

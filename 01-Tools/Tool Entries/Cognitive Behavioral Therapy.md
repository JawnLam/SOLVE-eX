# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

**One-line summary:** A structured, present-focused therapeutic approach in which clients learn to identify automatic thoughts, examine the evidence supporting them, and substitute more balanced cognitions — thereby altering associated emotions and behaviors.

**When to reach for it:** Treatment of anxiety, depression, OCD, PTSD, insomnia, and chronic-pain coping (clinical context); self-application for moderate distress, performance anxiety, or rumination; foundation for many derivative protocols.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

CBT is built on the *cognitive triangle*: thoughts, feelings, and behaviors interact such that intervening on any one influences the others. Beck's central observation — that depressed clients' automatic thoughts contained systematic distortions (catastrophizing, all-or-nothing, mind-reading) and that examining the evidence for those thoughts could shift the depression — became the foundation of contemporary clinical psychology.

The non-obvious operational insight is that CBT is *technical* and *brief*. Standard protocols are 12–20 sessions, structured around specific exercises (thought records, behavioral experiments, exposure hierarchies for anxiety). The active ingredient is between-session homework — the client practices the techniques in vivo, not just discusses them in session. This makes CBT among the most *teachable* and *self-applicable* therapies; many techniques work outside a formal therapeutic relationship.

The evidence base is the strongest of any psychotherapy across many conditions. Modern derivatives (DBT for borderline pattern, ACT for psychological flexibility, MBCT for depression relapse) refine specific elements. The core remains: thoughts are testable, distortions are common, and structured intervention produces durable change.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes structured CBT prevents:

1. **Thought-as-fact.** Untrained, people treat their automatic thoughts as accurate descriptions of reality. The thought-record discipline reveals them as hypotheses to be tested.
2. **Avoidance-driven anxiety.** Anxiety conditions strengthen via avoidance. CBT's behavioral-experiment / exposure component breaks the avoidance loop.
3. **Vague therapeutic processing.** Open-ended exploration without structured technique can confirm patterns rather than change them. CBT's protocols ensure the work is corrective.

For self-applied use (as a thinking tool, not full therapy), the thought-record technique alone is high-leverage — most everyday distress contains identifiable distortions that examination weakens.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Notice the situation: what just happened? Be specific.                          |
|    2 | Identify automatic thought(s): what went through your mind? Get the actual      |
|      | sentence, not a paraphrase.                                                     |
|    3 | Identify the emotion(s) and intensity (0–100).                                  |
|    4 | Examine the evidence FOR the thought.                                           |
|    5 | Examine the evidence AGAINST the thought.                                       |
|    6 | Identify any cognitive distortions (see list in artifact).                      |
|    7 | Construct a balanced thought that fits the evidence.                            |
|    8 | Re-rate the emotion intensity. Often shifts substantially even from this        |
|      | written exercise alone.                                                         |
|    9 | For anxiety / phobia: build a behavioral exposure hierarchy and work it.        |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THOUGHT RECORD (the canonical CBT artifact)

    Date: ___________  Situation: ____________________________________________

    AUTOMATIC THOUGHT
      Exact sentence as it occurred:
      "_____________________________________________________________________"
      Belief in this thought: ____ %

    EMOTION(S)
      Emotion 1: __________  Intensity: ____ / 100
      Emotion 2: __________  Intensity: ____ / 100

    EVIDENCE FOR THE THOUGHT
      1. ___________________________________________________________________
      2. ___________________________________________________________________

    EVIDENCE AGAINST THE THOUGHT
      1. ___________________________________________________________________
      2. ___________________________________________________________________
      3. ___________________________________________________________________

    COGNITIVE DISTORTIONS PRESENT (check any that fit)
      [ ] All-or-nothing thinking         [ ] Catastrophizing
      [ ] Overgeneralization              [ ] Mental filter (negative-only)
      [ ] Disqualifying the positive      [ ] Mind reading
      [ ] Fortune-telling                 [ ] Emotional reasoning
      [ ] "Should" statements             [ ] Labeling
      [ ] Personalization                 [ ] Magnification / minimization

    BALANCED THOUGHT
      A more accurate, evidence-based thought that acknowledges what's true
      and corrects distortion:
      "_____________________________________________________________________"
      Belief in this new thought: ____ %

    RE-RATE EMOTION
      Emotion 1: __________  New intensity: ____ / 100
      Emotion 2: __________  New intensity: ____ / 100

BEHAVIORAL EXPERIMENT (for anxiety / avoidance)

    Belief to test:
      "If I do X, then Y will happen, and I won't be able to cope."

    Experiment design:
      Action: ___________________________________________________________
      Prediction (specific): ____________________________________________
      Predicted distress level: ____ / 100

    After the experiment:
      What actually happened: ___________________________________________
      Actual distress: ____ / 100
      Time for distress to peak and then drop: ____ minutes
      What this tells me about the original belief: _____________________

EXPOSURE HIERARCHY (for phobias / OCD)

    Item                                          | Anticipated distress (0–100)
    ----------------------------------------------|------------------------------
    1. (easiest)                                  |   20
    2.                                            |   30
    3.                                            |   45
    4.                                            |   60
    5.                                            |   75
    6. (hardest, the goal)                        |   90

    Work bottom-up, repeating each item until distress drops by ~50%.

CORE BELIEFS WORKSHEET (for deeper work)

    Identify a recurring automatic thought across many situations.
    What core belief does it imply about self / others / world?
      Self:    "I am ____________________________________________"
      Others:  "Others are ______________________________________"
      World:   "The world is ____________________________________"

    Origin: when did this belief seem to take hold? __________________
    Evidence supporting:    __________________________________________
    Evidence contradicting: __________________________________________
    More balanced core:     __________________________________________
```

> **Operational notes:** Three disciplines. (1) Get the *exact* automatic thought, not a polite paraphrase. The crude form ("I'm useless and everyone secretly hates me") is the actual cognitive content; the polite paraphrase ("I'm having some self-doubt") loses the operative material. (2) Do behavioral experiments, not just thought records. Cognitive change without behavioral test produces intellectual insight that doesn't transfer. The experiment is what convinces the affective system. (3) For clinical conditions (clinical depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD), self-application is supplementary, not sufficient. Work with a CBT-trained clinician; the protocols have specific structures (e.g., exposure-and-response-prevention for OCD) that benefit from skilled guidance. Fourth: not all distress is from distorted thinking. Sometimes the "negative" thought is *accurate* — e.g., "I'm in a job that doesn't fit me." CBT applied to such cases incorrectly tries to soften an accurate appraisal; the right response there is action (change the situation), not cognitive restructuring.

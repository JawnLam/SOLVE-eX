# ACT Acceptance / Willingness Practice

**One-line summary:** Willingness to have unwanted internal experiences — thoughts, feelings, sensations — without struggle, while continuing toward chosen values.

**When to reach for it:** When experiential avoidance is the maintenance mechanism for psychological distress; teaching ACT clients to make space for difficult emotions rather than suppress them.

## Purpose

ACT identifies experiential avoidance — the struggle to control or eliminate unwanted internal experiences — as a primary driver of suffering. Acceptance (the alternative) is willingness to have the experience while not being controlled by it. The stance is not endurance, resignation, or detachment; it is open contact with the experience as it is, without struggle, while continuing valued action. The practice is recurring and skill-based: through breath, somatic awareness, and metaphor, clients build willingness over time.

## How To Use

1) Notice the unwanted experience (thought, feeling, sensation). 2) Notice the urge to control, suppress, or eliminate it. 3) Practice making room for it: breath, body openness, naming. 4) Stay in contact without struggle. 5) Continue toward valued action while holding the experience.

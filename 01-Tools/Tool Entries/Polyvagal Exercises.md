# Polyvagal Exercises

**One-line summary:** A family of small-dose body-based practices — vagal-pathway-engaging movements, sounds, and breath patterns — that shift the practitioner from sympathetic-arousal or dorsal-shutdown states into the ventral-vagal social-engagement state where cognitive flexibility returns.

**When to reach for it:** Stuck in anxious arousal that cognitive moves cannot dispel; shutdown / dissociation / numbness that interpretation cannot reach; before high-stakes interactions where you need to be socially engaged rather than defended; in therapy work where the body needs to settle before deeper processing.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

Porges's Polyvagal Theory (1995, 2011) distinguishes three autonomic states:

- **Ventral-vagal** (social-engagement): the integrative state. Eye contact, vocal prosody, present-moment attention, capacity for connection.
- **Sympathetic** (fight-flight): mobilized arousal. Heart rate up, tunnel vision, action-orientation.
- **Dorsal-vagal** (shutdown): immobilization. Numbness, dissociation, conservation of energy.

The non-obvious operational claim: **autonomic state is upstream of cognition.** You cannot reason your way out of sympathetic arousal; the body's autonomic system has already pre-empted the prefrontal cortex. Conversely, specific body-based moves can *shift* the autonomic state directly — and once shifted, the cognitive capacity returns automatically.

Polyvagal exercises target the ventral-vagal pathway specifically. The vagus nerve has branches innervating the face, ears, voice box, heart, and gut. Engaging these branches via deliberate practice tones the vagal pathway and signals "safe" to the autonomic system.

Common practices:

- **Voo chant.** Long, low "voo" vocalization with extended exhale. Engages the vagus via the laryngeal branch.
- **Basic Exercise (Rosenberg).** Lie on back, interlace fingers behind head, eyes look right (without head turn) for 30 sec, then left. Engages cervical vagal pathways.
- **Half-salamander.** Tilt head sideways while looking opposite. Same family.
- **Social-engagement gaze.** Soft eye contact with a trusted person.
- **Cold-water face stimulation.** Activates the mammalian dive reflex, which has strong vagal component.
- **Extended exhale.** Exhale longer than inhale (4 in, 8 out). Vagal tone via respiratory sinus arrhythmia.

A second insight: **the exercises are dose-dependent.** Single short engagements help acutely; tone-building requires daily practice. Both have value; conflating them produces frustration ("I tried it once, it didn't work").

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the practice prevents:

1. **Cognitive-only intervention for autonomic dysregulation.** Trying to think your way out of fight-flight or shutdown. The body must shift first.
2. **Symptomatic suppression.** Treating the felt-signal (anxiety, numbness) as something to override. Polyvagal practices treat it as autonomic data to respond to, not noise.
3. **Generic "calming down."** Without targeting the autonomic pathway specifically, generic relaxation may not engage. Polyvagal exercises engage the specific vagal pathway that produces social-engagement.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | Recognize the current state: ventral (engaged), sympathetic (mobilized), or     |
|      | dorsal (shutdown). Body cues distinguish: tight chest / racing thoughts =       |
|      | sympathetic; numb / empty / heavy = dorsal.                                    |
|  2   | Pick a practice matched to the state.                                           |
|      |   Sympathetic → extended exhale, voo chant, cold-face.                          |
|      |   Dorsal      → basic exercise, social gaze, gentle movement.                   |
|  3   | Do 3-5 minutes of the practice. Don't rush.                                     |
|  4   | Re-check state. Has it shifted? If partially, repeat or stack practices.        |
|  5   | If acute dysregulation persists despite practice, seek skilled support.        |
|      | These are tools, not substitutes for clinical care in trauma contexts.          |
|  6   | For tone-building: 10-15 min daily, not just acute episodes.                    |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
Polyvagal State Map + Practice Selector

CURRENT STATE (body cues):
   [ ] VENTRAL (engaged, curious, present)        → no practice needed
   [ ] SYMPATHETIC (tight, mobilized, urgent)     → calming practices
   [ ] DORSAL (numb, heavy, dissociative)         → activating practices
   [ ] MIXED                                       → start with extended exhale

PRACTICE LIBRARY:

  EXTENDED EXHALE
    Inhale 4 sec, exhale 8 sec. 10 cycles.
    Best for: sympathetic. Tones vagal via respiration.

  VOO CHANT (Levine / Porges)
    Long low "voo" sound on exhale; sustain as long as comfortable.
    8-10 cycles. Best for: sympathetic, mild dorsal.

  BASIC EXERCISE (Rosenberg)
    Lie on back. Interlace fingers behind head. Hold head still.
    Eyes look right for 30 sec or until natural sigh/yawn.
    Repeat looking left. Engages cervical vagal pathway.

  HALF-SALAMANDER
    Tilt head right while eyes look left, hold 30 sec. Reverse.
    Companion to basic exercise.

  COLD-WATER FACE
    Splash cold water on face, OR submerge face in cold water bowl 30 sec.
    Activates mammalian dive reflex. Strong sympathetic-down move.

  SOCIAL-ENGAGEMENT GAZE
    Soft eye contact with trusted person for 1-2 minutes.
    Best for: dorsal shutdown. Requires a co-regulator.

SESSION LOG:
   Date: ______
   State before: ____________
   Practice(s): _____________
   State after: _____________
   Duration: ______ min
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **State recognition is the prerequisite.** If you can't tell whether you're sympathetic or dorsal, the practice selection is guesswork. Start by paying attention to body cues over 2-3 weeks before practicing dosing. (2) **Trauma context warning.** For complex trauma, some practices (especially extended eye contact) can be activating rather than regulating. Work with a trauma-informed therapist. (3) **The practices feel small.** A 30-second eye exercise doesn't seem like it should do anything. The mechanism is in the vagal-pathway engagement, not the felt magnitude of the practice.

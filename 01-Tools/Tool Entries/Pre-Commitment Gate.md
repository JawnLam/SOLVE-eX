# Pre-Commitment Gate

**One-line summary:** A Phase 6 commitment ritual that operationalizes the result of a Pre-Mortem as a binding artifact — before the high-stakes commitment, the decider writes the exit / abort / divest criteria for each downstream review point, signs them, and locks them, so that at each gate during execution the decision is against the pre-committed criteria rather than against the candidate / partner / position whose specifics now carry sunk-cost and charisma weight.

**When to reach for it:** At the commitment moment of a high-stakes decision where (a) the deciding party will continue to interact with the chosen candidate / partner / position over time, (b) the cost of the wrong choice compounds slowly (long ramp, gradual divergence, latent failure mode), and (c) the post-commitment self will face structural pressure to discount early failure signals — through sunk-cost bias, charisma overweighting, missed-red-flag pattern, or relationship-as-analytic-weight. Common cases: first sales hire (slow ramp + charisma), strategic partnership (multi-month entanglement + reciprocity pressure), capital allocation (irreversible spend + commitment escalation), exclusive-distribution agreement, key-employee promotion to leadership track.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

**Pre-Commitment Gate** is a commitment-stage ritual that takes the output of a Pre-Mortem (what would go wrong) and converts it into binding instructions for future decision points. The core insight: at the commitment moment, the decider sees clearly. By month three of execution, the decider sees the specific person they hired, the specific partner they chose, the specific deal they committed to — and the analytic clarity has been displaced by relational presence and sunk cost. Pre-commitment exists to **make the pre-bias self the binding party**.

Pre-Mortem identifies the failure mode: *"we will hesitate to fire Marcus if his ramp signals fail because he's charismatic and we'll have integrated him into the team."* Pre-Mortem produces the diagnosis. Pre-Commitment Gate produces the prescription: *"At month 60 of Marcus's tenure, regardless of how integrated he is, if leading-indicator signals X / Y / Z have not appeared, we initiate the exit conversation. Signed: [decider, dated [pre-start])."*

The distinction:

- **Pre-Mortem** — *"What would go wrong?"* The diagnostic. Output: a list of failure modes, ranked by probability and impact.
- **Pre-Commitment Gate** — *"Given the failure modes, what are the exit criteria at each review point, and how do we bind the future self to them?"* The prescription. Output: a signed, time-stamped, locked criteria document used at each gate.

The tool's load-bearing move is **decide against criteria, not against the candidate**. The post-commitment self will be evaluating the specific person / partner / position; the pre-commitment self has the analytic distance to evaluate against generic criteria. At each gate, the pre-commitment criteria are the binding party. The post-commitment self can override them — but only by acknowledging that they are overriding, not by re-evaluating from scratch.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the framework prevents:

1. **Sunk-cost-driven exit delay.** The decider continues a failing commitment longer than the pre-commitment self would have endorsed, because the post-commitment self has integrated the choice into ongoing operations. Pre-commitment criteria are the binding artifact that makes the exit timely.
2. **Charisma overweighting at gate points.** At each review, the decider is evaluating the specific person whose charisma is now familiar and integrated. The pre-commitment criteria — written before the warm impressions consolidated — are the calibration against this drift.
3. **Goalpost migration.** Without pre-commitment criteria, the decider implicitly migrates the goalposts at each review: *"well, technically the ramp is slow but Marcus is doing X, Y, Z that we hadn't anticipated…"* The pre-commitment criteria fix the goalposts at the pre-bias moment. Migration becomes visible.

For founders and operators making high-stakes commitments with extended downstream entanglement (first hires, key partnerships, exclusive deals), this tool is the bridge between analytic clarity at the commitment moment and the relational reality of execution. Without it, the analytic clarity dissipates as the relational reality takes over.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|    1 | Run a Pre-Mortem on the commitment. Identify the top 2-3 failure modes the      |
|      | decider expects could arise from this commitment.                                |
|    2 | For each failure mode, identify the leading indicators. What would be visible    |
|      | at month 30, month 60, month 90, and month 150 (the pre-peak point — before     |
|      | the failure compounds into a crisis)? Specific signals, not vibes.              |
|    3 | Write the exit / abort / divest criteria. "If at month 60, leading-indicator    |
|      | signal X has not appeared, the decision to continue requires explicit           |
|      | re-justification documented in writing." The criteria must be specific enough   |
|      | that a third party reading the document at month 60 could apply them.           |
|    4 | Sign and date the criteria document at the pre-commitment moment. Lock it.      |
|      | The signed document is the binding artifact — store it where the decider will   |
|      | look at each gate (calendar reminder, decision journal, shared with a           |
|      | structural pressure-tester).                                                    |
|    5 | At each gate (month 30 / 60 / 90 / 150), re-read the criteria document. Decide  |
|      | against the criteria, not against the candidate. If the criteria say "exit,"    |
|      | the burden is on the post-commitment self to explicitly override — and to       |
|      | document the override.                                                          |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
THE PRE-COMMITMENT GATE PROTOCOL

   AT COMMITMENT MOMENT (before the start date / signing / wire):

      1. Pre-Mortem complete? If not, run it first. The Gate
         operationalizes the Pre-Mortem; it does not replace it.

      2. Identify the gate points. Typical pattern for a hire:
         month 30, 60, 90, 150 (pre-peak). For a partnership:
         quarter 1, quarter 2, end of pilot, decision point.
         For a capital allocation: tranche 1, tranche 2, etc.

      3. For each Pre-Mortem failure mode, write the leading
         indicators visible at each gate. Be specific.
         BAD:  "Marcus isn't performing."
         GOOD: "Marcus has not closed his first deal by month 90"
               OR "Marcus's pipeline coverage at month 60 is < 2x
               quota" OR "Marcus has not built a reciprocal
               relationship with engineering by month 60."

      4. Write the exit / abort / divest criteria. State them as
         conditional rules:
         "IF at month 60, [signal X] AND [signal Y] have not
         appeared, THEN exit conversation begins by month 75 or
         is explicitly deferred in writing with new criteria."

      5. Sign. Date. Lock. Store in [agreed location].
         Recommended: share with one structural pressure-tester
         (co-founder, advisor, peer in similar role) who has
         standing to check at each gate.

   AT EACH GATE (month 30 / 60 / 90 / 150):

      1. Re-read the criteria document.
      2. Decide against the criteria. Not against the candidate.
         Not against the relationship. Not against sunk cost.
         Not against the integration the post-commitment self has
         built.
      3. If the criteria say "exit / abort / divest," the burden
         is on the post-commitment self to explicitly override.
         The override must be written, dated, and justified.
         No silent goalpost migration.
      4. Post-decision, update the criteria document with what
         actually happened — a record for the next commitment.

THE BINDING PRINCIPLE

   The pre-commitment self has the analytic distance the decision
   deserves. The post-commitment self has the relational
   presence the execution requires. Both selves are legitimate.
   The Pre-Commitment Gate makes the pre-commitment self the
   binding party at moments of structural pressure.

   Sunk-cost bias is structural, not personal. Pre-commitment
   exists to make the pre-bias self the binding party, not the
   post-bias self.

THE COMMON FAILURE MODES

   1. CRITERIA TOO VAGUE TO APPLY
      "If Marcus isn't working out by month 60..." has no
      binding force because "working out" is interpretable at
      gate time.
      Recovery: rewrite criteria with specific leading
      indicators that a third party could apply.

   2. SKIPPING THE GATE READS
      The decider does not actually re-read the criteria
      document at each gate point. The signed artifact becomes
      decorative.
      Recovery: set explicit calendar reminders for each gate
      with the criteria document attached. Share with a
      structural pressure-tester who has standing to ask.

   3. SILENT GOALPOST MIGRATION
      At gate time, the decider notices that the criteria say
      "exit" but rationalizes that "things have changed since
      we set these." Sometimes true. Often a sign that the
      post-commitment self is re-bargaining with the
      pre-commitment self.
      Recovery: require WRITTEN override. The act of writing
      forces the rationalization to be explicit; explicit
      rationalizations are visible to the decider's own
      review.

   4. APPLYING THE TOOL TO LOW-STAKES OR SHORT-CYCLE DECISIONS
      Pre-Commitment Gate's overhead is justified only when (a)
      the commitment has extended downstream entanglement, (b)
      structural pressure on the post-commitment self is
      predictable, and (c) the cost of the wrong choice
      compounds slowly.
      Recovery: confirm the three preconditions before applying.
      For one-shot / fast-cycle / reversible decisions, the
      tool is overhead without payoff.

THE WORKED EXAMPLE — TESSA HOLLIS / FIRST SALES HIRE
(Sprint 10 panel test, hire-vs-stay case)

   Decision: Hire first sales lead (Marcus) at $120K against
   $300K ARR.

   Pre-Mortem failure mode identified:
      "Marcus is charismatic and we'll hesitate to fire him
      even if the ramp signals fail. Six months in, we'll have
      integrated him into the team, the engineering team will
      have learned his pipeline rhythm, and we'll be reluctant
      to start over."

   Pre-Commitment Gate criteria (written before start date):

      Month 30: At least one closed deal OR pipeline coverage
      ≥ 1.5x first-year quota.
      Month 60: At least two closed deals OR pipeline coverage
      ≥ 2x first-year quota AND positive engineering feedback
      on reciprocal relationship.
      Month 90: First-quarter quota attainment ≥ 60%.
      Month 150 (pre-peak point — before sunk cost
      compounds): First-half-year quota attainment ≥ 70%; if
      not, exit conversation by month 165.

   Signed by Tessa, dated [pre-start], stored in the company
   decision journal, shared with [co-founder / board advisor /
   structural pressure-tester].

   Outcome (the worked example's hypothetical, not the panel
   test's actual outcome):

      Month 60 arrives. Marcus has integrated well, the team
      likes him, pipeline coverage is 1.3x (below the 2x
      criterion). The post-commitment self wants to extend.
      The pre-commitment criteria say "below criterion;
      explicit override required if continuing."

      Tessa writes the override: "Marcus's pipeline at 1.3x is
      below criterion; we are extending to month 90 because
      [specific reasoning about Marcus's engineering integration
      that we hadn't anticipated]. New criterion at month 90:
      pipeline coverage ≥ 2x AND at least one closed deal."

      The override is explicit. The goalpost migration is
      visible. The decision is documented. Tessa retains the
      ability to course-correct because she retains the analytic
      distance.
```

> **Operational notes:** Five disciplines. (1) Always run Pre-Mortem first; Pre-Commitment Gate operationalizes its output. (2) Criteria must be third-party-applicable — specific leading indicators, not vibes. (3) Sign, date, lock, store visibly. The artifact is binding only if the decider sees it at each gate. (4) Decide against criteria, not against candidate. The candidate's specifics are now relational; the criteria are analytic. (5) Require WRITTEN override at any gate where criteria say exit but the decision is to continue. The act of writing forces rationalization to be explicit.

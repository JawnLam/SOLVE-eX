# Ijtihad (Independent Legal Reasoning)

**One-line summary:** The stance of a qualified Islamic jurist (mujtahid) exercising independent jurisprudential reasoning, distinguished from mere following of authority (taqlid).

**When to reach for it:** Operating in Islamic jurisprudence as a qualified scholar facing a novel question, where the alternative to ijtihad is taqlid (acceptance of prior authority) and the question warrants fresh derivation.

## Purpose

Ijtihad is not a discrete technique but a methodological stance — the qualified exercise of all the tools of usul al-fiqh (qiyas, istihsan, maslaha, etc.) by a mujtahid to derive a ruling. It is distinguished from taqlid (following the rulings of past authorities without independent derivation). The famous debate over whether 'the gate of ijtihad was closed' (an over-simplified Western narrative) concerns whether independent reasoning was foreclosed in classical Sunni Islam after the 10th c. — Hallaq and others have shown it was not. Contemporary Islamic legal-thought debates whether and how ijtihad should respond to modern conditions.

## How To Use

As stance (not technique): possess qualified expertise across Quran, hadith, Arabic linguistic-rhetorical analysis, the methodologies of usul al-fiqh, and the rulings of past schools. Facing a novel question, do not simply consult prior rulings — exercise independent derivation using the methodological tools. Cross-check derived rulings against prior scholarship to identify where they agree and disagree. The stance presumes both rigor (one is not free to invent) and courage (one must derive even where prior authority has not). Contemporary applications: new medical-ethical questions, financial instruments, gender questions — all require ijtihad rather than mechanical taqlid.

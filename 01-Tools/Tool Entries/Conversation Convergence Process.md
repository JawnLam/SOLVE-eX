# Conversation Convergence Process

**One-line summary:** A states → B paraphrases → B clarifies → switch → chart agreements/disagreements.

**When to reach for it:** A conflict in a meeting is rooted in misunderstanding rather than substantive disagreement.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

The purpose of the "Conversation Convergence Process" thinking tool is to facilitate effective communication and problem-solving in situations where conflict arises from misunderstandings. It is designed to guide participants through a structured dialogue that clarifies positions, fosters mutual understanding, and identifies common ground.

This tool is particularly useful in meetings or discussions where differing viewpoints may lead to conflict, and there is a need to align on objectives and understand each other's perspectives. The goal is to move from conflict or divergence in conversation to a point of convergence, where participants agree on a shared path forward or at least understand the nature of their disagreements.

By methodically exchanging views, repeating back what was heard for confirmation, asking questions to clarify, and systematically documenting points of agreement and disagreement, the tool aims to reduce misunderstandings, highlight shared goals, and facilitate a collaborative search for solutions. It ultimately aims to transform conflict into constructive dialogue, leading to actionable outcomes and improved group dynamics.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Conflicts often happen because people don't understand each other. Here's what to do:
- Ignore the conflict if it's not about what the meeting is for.
- Make sure everyone knows what the problem is.
- Make the goals of the meeting clear so that people aren't working against each other.
- Figure out if the disagreement is really about the problem or just about how people work, their personalities, or their values.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

For this thinking tool, start a conversation about the conflict using this method to help solve it.

Steps:
1. **Initial Position Statement:** Participant A presents their view on the problem without interruption.
2. **Recap & Understanding:** Participant B repeats what they heard from Participant A, without adding new information.
3. **Clarifying Questions:** Participant B asks clarifying questions to fully understand Participant A's view. No challenges are made here; the focus is on comprehending the stance.
4. **Perspective Switch:** Now, Participant B presents their perspective on the problem.
5. **Position Acknowledgment:** Participant A repeats Participant B's view, ensuring accurate understanding.
6. **Deepening Understanding:** Participant A asks Participant B clarifying questions about their perspective.
7. **Joint Discussion.** Engage in a dialogue to explore:
   - Shared views and agreements.
   - Differences in understanding the problem.
   - Potential solutions or ideas.
   - Agreed upon action steps or solutions.
   - Unresolved issues and continuing points of contention.
8. **Visual Mapping:** Create a Problem Difference Chart detailing the key points of agreement and disagreement.
9. **Solution Exploration:** Discuss the differences charted to identify potential solutions.

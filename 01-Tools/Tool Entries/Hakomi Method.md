# Hakomi Method

**One-line summary:** Body-centered, mindfulness-based psychotherapy (Ron Kurtz, 1970s-80s) that uses gentle "experiments in mindfulness" to surface and shift unconscious somatic-emotional patterns. Built on five principles: Unity, Organicity, Mindfulness, Non-violence, Mind-Body Holism.

**When to reach for it:** When working with attachment wounds, developmental trauma, or limiting core beliefs that haven't shifted via cognitive or talk-based methods. Used by therapists trained in somatic and trauma-informed modalities.

## Purpose

Hakomi distinctively uses mindfulness *not* to calm or distract but to *study* — inviting the client into a state of quiet observation where unconscious somatic memories and emotional imprints become accessible. The therapist offers small "probes" (a touch, a phrase, a gesture) and tracks the client's micro-responses; the body's reaction reveals what the conscious mind doesn't yet know.

## How To Use

1. **Establish loving presence** — therapist's grounded, non-directive attention is the working medium.
2. **Track the client** — notice posture, breath, micro-expressions, what the client says vs. what the body says.
3. **Enter mindfulness** — invite the client into a quiet, curious internal state.
4. **Offer a small experiment** — a probe phrase ("You are safe here"), a gesture, a question. Note what arises in the body.
5. **Process what emerges** — the body's response is data about the implicit pattern. Surface it; integrate it.
6. **Close with integration** — what was discovered? How does it land?

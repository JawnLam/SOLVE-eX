# Continuum Movement

**One-line summary:** Emilie Conrad's somatic exploration treating the body as fluid intelligence. Uses breath sounds (long vowels) and micro-movements ("dives") to access cellular and embryological resonance.

**When to reach for it:** Movement education for dancers, somatic therapists, performers; trauma recovery work; chronic-pain support; or as a contemplative-somatic practice for anyone with the patience for very slow inquiry. Best learned through workshops or extended training.

## Purpose

Most movement work assumes a body of bones and muscles. Continuum starts from the observation that the body is mostly water — fluid, undulant, responsive. By emphasizing breath sounds (long vowels: "o", "e", "u") that create subtle internal vibration, and very slow movements ("dives") that follow rather than direct, practitioners access embodied states distinct from voluntary muscular activity. The practice cultivates a different cognitive register — one of receptivity, internal listening, biological intelligence.

## How To Use

1. **Set up gently** — lying or supported seated position, quiet space.
2. **Sound breathing** — long, sustained vowel sounds on the exhale ("oooo", "eeee"). Notice internal vibration.
3. **Wait for movement to arise** — don't initiate; notice the body's spontaneous responses to the breath-sound resonance.
4. **Dive** — follow a movement impulse very slowly into deeper internal exploration.
5. **Pause** — rest between dives; integrate what arose.
6. **Close** — return to ordinary movement; notice differences.

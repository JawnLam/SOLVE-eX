# Pratyabhijna Recognition Practice

**One-line summary:** Kashmir Shaivism's 'recognition' method — the practitioner recognizes that they are already Śiva (universal consciousness) rather than learning a new fact.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging contemplative inquiry where the path is not acquisition of a state but recognition of one's already-present nature, and where philosophical analysis is the means rather than ritual or somatic technique.

## Purpose

Utpaladeva's Pratyabhijna ('recognition') method (9th c.) is the philosophical pole of Kashmir Shaivism, complementing the more practice-oriented Spanda tradition. The method: trace any cognition back to its illuminating source, which is shown to be the same awareness across all cognitions, which is Śiva. The recognition is not the learning of a new fact but the seeing-again of what was always already the case. Theistic (Śiva-identified) rather than abstract-impersonal (Brahman-as-pure-awareness), and instantaneous-philosophical rather than gradual-meditative.

## How To Use

In quiet attention, take any cognition — a sensation, a thought, a memory. Ask: what illumines this? Some awareness must be present for the cognition to be a cognition at all. Now take a different cognition. What illumines it? Is the illuminating awareness the same or different? Sustain this inquiry across many cognitions until the answer becomes self-evident: the awareness is the same. That awareness is what one is, has always been. The recognition stabilizes through repetition. Traditionally transmitted teacher-to-student; texts function as pointers, not propositions.

# Inquiry-Based Learning

**One-line summary:** Pedagogy where students drive learning by posing questions, investigating, and constructing understanding — rather than receiving pre-packaged content.

**When to reach for it:** Science education, project-based curricula, gifted-and-talented programs; teaching the practices of inquiry rather than just the conclusions; building self-directed learners.

## Purpose

Inquiry-Based Learning (IBL) frames the curriculum around questions that students investigate rather than content they receive. The method develops both subject-matter understanding and the metacognitive skills of formulating questions, designing investigations, evaluating evidence, and revising beliefs. Variants include guided inquiry (teacher provides the question, students investigate), open inquiry (students formulate the question), and structured inquiry (students follow a defined procedure to find a predetermined answer).

## How To Use

1) Pose or invite a genuine question. 2) Students generate hypotheses and sub-questions. 3) Design investigations to gather evidence. 4) Conduct investigation. 5) Analyze findings; revise hypotheses. 6) Communicate conclusions. 7) Reflect on the inquiry process itself.

# Jazz Improvisation Chord-Substitution Reasoning

**One-line summary:** Jazz musician's cognitive method of substituting chords during improvisation — tritone substitutions, diminished passing chords, modal interchange — each opening different scalar choices.

**When to reach for it:** Improvising on a chord progression where playing the given chords would produce generic harmonic content, and where substituting harmonically-related chords opens new melodic and emotional possibilities.

## Purpose

Jazz Chord-Substitution Reasoning is the improvising musician's cognitive method of substituting chords during real-time playing. Common substitutions: tritone substitution (replace a dominant chord with the dominant a tritone away — they share the critical guide tones); diminished passing chords (insert a diminished chord between two diatonic chords); modal interchange (borrow a chord from a parallel mode for color); secondary dominants (treat any chord as if it were V of the next chord). Each substitution opens different scalar choices for the soloist — modes, altered scales, bebop scales. The cognitive payload: trains hearing-thinking-playing integration where harmonic possibility is computed in real time and reflected in melodic choice.

## How To Use

Learn the standard substitution categories with their theoretical basis. Practice each: take a simple progression and apply the substitution. Hear how it changes the available scales. Practice the new scales over the substituted chord. Over time, the substitutions become available in real-time improvisation. During a solo: when the chart calls for a specific chord, listen for whether the substituted chord would open more interesting melodic options. Use the substitution; play scales appropriate to it; resolve to the next chord coherently. The discipline is fast: in real-time improvisation, the substitution is made in milliseconds. Trained jazz musicians make these choices below conscious deliberation, but the underlying cognitive method is teachable and learnable.

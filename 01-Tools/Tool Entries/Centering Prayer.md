# Centering Prayer

**One-line summary:** Method of Christian silent prayer developed by Trappist monks (Pennington, Keating, Meninger) at St. Joseph's Abbey ~1975. Uses a "sacred word" as a consent-to-presence anchor; on noticing thoughts, gently return to the sacred word.

**When to reach for it:** For Christian practitioners (or anyone open to a theistic frame) seeking a structured 20-minute daily silent practice. Adapted from *The Cloud of Unknowing*; revives apophatic Christian contemplative tradition for lay practice.

## Purpose

Centering Prayer is not concentration on the sacred word (as in Buddhist mantra) — it is consent to God's presence and action within. The sacred word marks the intention to return to that consent whenever thoughts arise. The discipline is in the gentle return, not in the absence of thoughts. The result, over time, is what Keating calls the "divine therapy": material from the unconscious surfaces and is released in the silent receptivity.

## How To Use

1. **Choose a sacred word** (Abba, Love, Peace, Jesus, etc.) — a short word that expresses your intention.
2. **Sit comfortably**, close eyes, settle briefly.
3. **Introduce the sacred word** silently, as a symbol of consent to God's presence.
4. **When thoughts arise** (and they will), gently return to the sacred word.
5. **Continue for 20 minutes** (timer optional but typical).
6. **Close** with a few minutes of silence with eyes still closed (avoid jolting back to ordinary mind).
7. **Practice twice daily** ideally — morning and afternoon/evening.

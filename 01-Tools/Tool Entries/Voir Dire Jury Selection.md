# Voir Dire Jury Selection Strategy

**One-line summary:** Pre-trial questioning of potential jurors to identify bias and shape the deciding body — strategic frame including open-ended vs leading questions and peremptory vs cause challenges.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing for or conducting jury selection in adversarial trial proceedings where the composition of the jury will materially shape verdicts and the attorney has limited but real tools to influence it.

## Purpose

Voir Dire ('to speak the truth' — pre-trial questioning of potential jurors) is the strategic moment where trial attorneys identify bias and influence jury composition. Strategic frames include: open-ended questioning (to surface attitudes) vs leading questioning (to anchor and educate); peremptory challenges (limited number, no cause required — strategic deselection) vs challenges for cause (unlimited, requires demonstrated inability to be impartial); demographic profiling (age, race, occupation as predictors — increasingly restricted by Batson v. Kentucky and progeny) vs attitudinal profiling (responses about case-relevant beliefs and experiences). The cognitive method: shape the decision-making body before evidence is presented.

## How To Use

Pre-trial preparation: develop a juror profile — who is most likely to be sympathetic to the case, who is most likely to be hostile? Develop voir dire questions targeting case-relevant beliefs and experiences rather than only demographics. Conduct: use open-ended questions early (e.g., asking what they think when they hear the word negligence) to surface attitudes that closed questions would miss. Use leading questions later to anchor favorable framings. Listen carefully for tells — both verbal and nonverbal. Reserve peremptory challenges for jurors most likely to be hostile based on the cumulative pattern, not single answers. Document the basis for any challenge to defend against Batson reverse-discrimination challenges. The strategic discipline is identifying real bias risk versus surface heuristics.

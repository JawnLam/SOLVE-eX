# Shikantaza (Just Sitting)

**One-line summary:** Soto Zen's signature practice — sitting without object, without technique, without seeking — letting sitting itself BE the meditation.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging contemplative practice where technique itself has become an obstacle, and the work is to release doing-something in favor of being-with what already is.

## Purpose

Shikantaza ('just sitting') is the central practice of Soto Zen as articulated by Dogen (1233-1253). Distinct from concentration practices (shamatha, with a specific object) and from insight practices (vipassana, with an investigative method), shikantaza is sitting without object, technique, or seeking. The 'just' is doing the work: the practitioner releases doing-anything-special and lets sitting BE meditation. Dogen's claim: the sitting is not preparation for awakening; the sitting is itself the embodiment of the awakened state. Posture, breath, and presence are sufficient.

## How To Use

Sit in a stable upright posture — traditionally cross-legged on a cushion, but a chair is acceptable. Hands in cosmic mudra (one palm on the other, thumbs lightly touching). Eyes half-open, gaze soft, directed downward. Breath natural — no special technique. When thoughts arise, don't engage and don't fight. Don't try to clear them. Don't try to maintain a state. Don't try to attain anything. Just sit. The mind will wander; without comment, return to the posture and the present. Practiced 25-50 minutes per period, often 2-4 periods per day in serious practice. Surprisingly difficult because it offers nothing to do; therein lies its function.

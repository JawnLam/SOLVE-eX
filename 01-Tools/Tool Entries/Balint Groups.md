# Balint Groups

**One-line summary:** Case-discussion method (Michael Balint, 1957) in which doctors present difficult patients to a peer group that explores the doctor-patient relationship rather than clinical content.

**When to reach for it:** When clinicians want to develop self-awareness about how they react to patients (transference and countertransference) and to surface unconscious patterns shaping their care decisions. Standard in many psychiatry, family-medicine, and GP training programs.

## Purpose

Conventional case conferences focus on what the doctor should do (clinical content). Balint groups focus on what the doctor *feels* in the encounter — the emotion is data about the patient's unconscious communication and the doctor's countertransference. By making the doctor-patient dyad the object of inquiry, Balint groups surface clinically-relevant material that disease-focused review misses.

## How To Use

1. **Presenter** offers a case where the doctor-patient relationship felt difficult, confusing, or stuck. No notes, no preparation — what they remember matters.
2. **Group asks questions** — but only about feelings/dynamics, not clinical facts.
3. **Group reflects aloud** while the presenter listens silently — speculation about what the patient and doctor were experiencing.
4. **Presenter returns** with what landed, what didn't.
5. **Leader closes** — names a pattern, never prescribes a fix.

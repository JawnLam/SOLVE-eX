# Viewpoints (Bogart / Overlie)

**One-line summary:** Mary Overlie's (and Anne Bogart's) ensemble-training method based on six viewpoints — Space, Story, Time, Emotion, Movement, Shape — practiced alone and in combination to develop ensemble responsiveness.

**When to reach for it:** Training a performance ensemble in here-and-now spatial-kinesthetic awareness, especially when psychological-method approaches have produced internally-rich but ensemble-disconnected performers.

## Purpose

The Viewpoints method, developed by Mary Overlie (the original six 'physical' viewpoints) and elaborated by Anne Bogart and Tina Landau (expanded to nine 'viewpoints of time and space'), trains ensemble performance through attention to six (or nine) discrete viewpoints: Space, Story, Time, Emotion, Movement, Shape (Overlie's set) — and Bogart's expanded version including Tempo, Duration, Kinesthetic Response, Repetition, Topography, Spatial Relationship, Architecture, Shape, Gesture. Performers practice attention to each viewpoint alone, then in combinations. The cognitive payload: develops here-and-now spatial-kinesthetic awareness and ensemble responsiveness, distinct from Stanislavski's psychological method. Particularly useful for non-naturalistic theater, ensemble work, and contemporary performance.

## How To Use

Lead an ensemble through Viewpoint exercises. Begin with single viewpoint focus: 'Move through the space attending only to Tempo — speed of movement.' Then 'Attend to Kinesthetic Response — how do you respond to other performers' movements?' Then 'Spatial Relationship — distance and angle between performers.' Progress to combining: 'Maintain tempo awareness while adding kinesthetic response.' Eventually all viewpoints are active simultaneously, available as the performer needs. Use in rehearsal as both training and generative method — Viewpoints-based exploration can generate stage compositions and ensemble behaviors that scripted blocking cannot. Distinct from improv-comedy 'yes-and': Viewpoints work is physical-spatial-temporal rather than narrative-character.

# Harkness Discussion Method

**One-line summary:** Students sit around an oval table to discuss assigned text; the teacher facilitates minimally; students take responsibility for collective learning.

**When to reach for it:** Discussion-based seminars; humanities and social-science teaching; college-prep education that aims at intellectual autonomy.

## Purpose

The Harkness method, named for Phillips Exeter Academy's benefactor Edward Harkness, structures class as student-led discussion around a literal oval table. The geometry (everyone faces everyone) and the teacher's restrained role (facilitate, don't lecture) build students' capacity to advance discussion themselves. Students learn to listen carefully, build on each other's contributions, support claims with evidence, and disagree productively. The method is taught explicitly: students reflect on their discussion practice and the group analyzes its own dynamics.

## How To Use

1) Assign text in advance with study questions. 2) Begin discussion with a student-posed question. 3) Teacher contributes minimally — primarily to invite quiet voices, summarize, or redirect. 4) Track participation and discussion patterns. 5) End with reflection on the discussion itself. 6) Iterate over many discussions; students develop discussion skill over time.

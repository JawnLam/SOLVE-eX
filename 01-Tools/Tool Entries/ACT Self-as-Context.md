# ACT Self-as-Context

**One-line summary:** The observing perspective — the 'you' that has been continuously present across all your experiences, distinct from any specific thought or feeling.

**When to reach for it:** Defusion work in ACT; teaching clients the experiential difference between self-as-content (the narrating I) and self-as-context (the witnessing I); contemplative practice.

## Purpose

Clients often identify with the contents of consciousness — their thoughts, feelings, narratives, roles. Self-as-Context is the recognition that there is a witnessing aspect of self that has been continuously present across all those changing contents. Accessing Self-as-Context provides psychological flexibility: when you are aware of the perspective from which thoughts and feelings are observed, the contents lose their gripping quality. The experience is contemplatively related to traditions of pure awareness; ACT operationalizes it for therapy.

## How To Use

1) Reflect on early childhood memories. 2) Notice the 'you' that was present then. 3) Reflect on recent memories. 4) Notice the same 'you' that's present now. 5) Recognize the continuity of the observer despite changing content. 6) Practice resting in that perspective.

# Sri Yantra Meditation

**One-line summary:** Sri Vidya tantric contemplative method using the Sri Yantra — nine interlocking triangles forming 43 sub-triangles around a central bindu — as a visual entry to consciousness.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging visualized-geometric contemplative practice where structured visual form carries cognitive-spiritual meaning, and where graded entry from periphery to center serves as a developmental path.

## Purpose

The Sri Yantra is the central diagram of the Sri Vidya tradition of Hindu Tantra. Nine interlocking triangles (four pointing up = Śiva/consciousness; five pointing down = Śakti/energy) form 43 smaller triangles around a central bindu (point). Each triangle has a presiding goddess; the structure as a whole represents the unfolding of cosmos from consciousness-source. The contemplative method: enter the yantra from outside inward, meditating on each layer in turn, until reaching the bindu. The geometric structure is not decorative — it is the contemplative path made visible.

## How To Use

In a Sri Yantra image or memorized visualization, begin at the outermost square (bhupura, the earth-gate). Spend extended attention on each enclosure as you move inward: the lotus circles, the 16-petaled lotus, the 8-petaled lotus, the 14-triangle line, the 10-triangle line (twice), the 8-triangle line, the inner triangle, the bindu. Each enclosure has associated mantras and presiding shaktis to be invoked. The full traversal takes weeks of accumulated practice; abbreviated forms exist. Tradition requires initiation; outside formal lineage, study the diagram as an object of contemplative attention and architectural-symbolic understanding.

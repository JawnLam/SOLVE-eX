# Motivational Interviewing

**One-line summary:** A collaborative conversation style for strengthening intrinsic motivation to change — built on OARS skills (open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries) and four processes (engaging, focusing, evoking, planning).

**When to reach for it:** When someone is ambivalent about a change (smoking cessation, medication adherence, behavior modification) — i.e., motivation is the bottleneck, not knowledge or skill. Widely used in healthcare, addictions, and coaching.

## Purpose

The "righting reflex" — the helper's urge to fix or persuade — is the most common cause of failure in change conversations. MI inverts the pattern: the helper evokes the person's *own* reasons for change, then strengthens "change talk" via reflective listening. The motivation comes from the speaker, not the helper.

## How To Use

1. **Engage** — Establish a collaborative atmosphere (OARS skills, no judgment).
2. **Focus** — Agree on what change matters (don't impose; offer a menu).
3. **Evoke** — Use directional questions to draw out the person's own reasons for change. Reflect change talk; let it grow.
4. **Plan** — Once readiness emerges, negotiate concrete next steps.

**OARS**: Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections (simple and complex), Summaries.

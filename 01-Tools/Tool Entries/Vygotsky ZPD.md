# Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development

**One-line summary:** The gap between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with skilled support — the zone where instruction is most effective.

**When to reach for it:** Diagnostic teaching, coaching, mentoring; choosing the right level of challenge for a learner; designing scaffolding that fades as competence grows.

## Purpose

Vygotsky distinguished two developmental levels: actual development (what the learner can do independently) and potential development (what the learner can achieve with help). The ZPD between them is the productive teaching zone — too easy and learning is minimal; too hard and the learner can't even use help. Effective teaching identifies the ZPD and provides scaffolding that gradually fades as the learner's actual development advances. The concept underlies cognitive apprenticeship, scaffolded instruction, and modern formative assessment.

## How To Use

1) Assess the learner's current independent capability. 2) Identify what the learner could do with appropriate support. 3) Provide instruction or scaffolding in that zone. 4) Reduce support as the learner develops. 5) When the learner is independent, the ZPD shifts upward — re-assess and teach the new ZPD.

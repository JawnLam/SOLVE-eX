# Marginal Analysis

**One-line summary:** Compare the cost and benefit of the next unit of input rather than averages — optimal allocation is where marginal cost equals marginal benefit.

**When to reach for it:** Resource-allocation decisions, pricing, scaling-up vs scaling-down debates, hiring N+1th employee, advertising budget setting; any context where averages obscure the relevant comparison.

## Purpose

Average-based analysis conflates infra-marginal and marginal effects. The economically relevant question is almost always: what does the next unit cost vs. what does it produce? Continuing to add until marginal cost meets marginal benefit produces the optimum. The discipline is forcing the question from 'is the total worth it?' to 'is the next unit worth it?'

## How To Use

1) Identify the unit of decision (employee, dollar, hour, customer). 2) Estimate the cost and benefit of one more unit at the current scale. 3) If marginal benefit > marginal cost, add. 4) If marginal benefit < marginal cost, stop or remove. 5) Re-evaluate marginal calculus at each new equilibrium — it shifts as you move along the curve.

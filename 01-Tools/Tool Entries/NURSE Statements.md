# NURSE Empathic Statements

**One-line summary:** Five-style mnemonic for articulating empathy in clinical encounters — Name, Understand, Respect, Support, Explore the emotion.

**When to reach for it:** A patient or family member expresses strong emotion that the clinician must acknowledge before continuing — and unstructured response would either skip past the emotion or compound it.

## Purpose

NURSE (Back, Arnold, Tulsky) is a mnemonic for five empathic responses to clinical emotion: (N)ame — 'It sounds like you're feeling [emotion].' (U)nderstand — 'I can imagine why you'd feel that way.' (R)espect — 'You've been working incredibly hard at this.' (S)upport — 'I'm going to be with you through this.' (E)xplore — 'Can you tell me more about what's weighing on you most?' Each is a discrete response; clinicians may use one or several depending on the situation. Used as a communication scaffold when patients express emotion that needs acknowledgment before any other clinical work proceeds.

## How To Use

When a patient or family member expresses strong emotion (named explicitly, signaled by tears, voice change, or stark statement): pause your clinical agenda. Choose one or more NURSE responses. Name: 'It sounds like you're feeling angry/scared/overwhelmed.' (Naming alone is often enough.) Understand: 'Given everything that's happened, that makes complete sense.' Respect: 'I want you to know I see how hard this has been.' Support: 'You're not alone in this — I'm going to be with you through whatever comes next.' Explore: 'What's weighing on you most right now?' After the emotion is met, the clinical conversation can resume. The protocol takes 30-90 seconds and changes the rest of the encounter.

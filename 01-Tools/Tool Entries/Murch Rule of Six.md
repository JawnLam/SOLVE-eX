# Walter Murch's Rule of Six (Film Editing)

**One-line summary:** Walter Murch's prioritized criteria for an ideal film cut — Emotion (51%), Story, Rhythm, Eye-trace, Planarity, Spatial continuity — sacrifice the lower if conflict with higher.

**When to reach for it:** Making editing decisions in film, video, audio, or any time-sequenced artistic medium where the question 'where to cut?' has many candidate criteria that may conflict.

## Purpose

Walter Murch (editor of *Apocalypse Now*, *The English Patient*, *The Godfather III*) articulated his prioritized criteria for the ideal film cut in *In the Blink of an Eye* (1995, 2001). Six criteria in priority order: (1) Emotion — 51% of the decision. Does the cut serve the emotional moment? (2) Story — does the cut serve the narrative? (3) Rhythm — does the cut work musically with the surrounding pace? (4) Eye-trace — where is the audience looking? Does the cut accommodate or fight that? (5) Planarity — does the cut respect the screen-as-2D-composition? (6) Spatial continuity — does the cut maintain coherent 3D space? Critically: if lower-order criteria conflict with higher, sacrifice the lower. Spatial continuity is the least important; emotion is paramount. The hierarchy guides every cut.

## How To Use

At each candidate cut point, walk through the six criteria in order. Start with emotion: does this cut serve the emotional moment? If yes, weight strongly. Then story: does it serve narrative? Then rhythm, eye-trace, planarity, spatial continuity. If a cut is right for emotion but wrong for spatial continuity, take the cut — sacrifice spatial continuity to preserve emotion. If a cut is wrong for emotion but right for spatial continuity, do not take it. The hierarchy disciplines the decision against the editor's tendency to over-weight spatial continuity (the most teachable and the easiest to evaluate) at the cost of emotion (the most important and the hardest to articulate). Generalizes to any sequenced-artistic-decision context: write down the priority order, then trust it under conflict.

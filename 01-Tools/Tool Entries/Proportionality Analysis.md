# Proportionality Analysis (Constitutional Method)

**One-line summary:** Four-step constitutional review (legitimate aim, suitability, necessity, proportionality stricto sensu) used in German Basic Law jurisprudence and exported globally to constitutional courts.

**When to reach for it:** Adjudicating whether a state action limiting a fundamental right is constitutional. Now standard in Germany, Canada, South Africa, Israel, the European Court of Human Rights, and many constitutional democracies.

## Purpose

When a right and a state interest conflict, blanket rules over-favor one or the other. Proportionality is a structured balancing — it forces the court to articulate *every* step in the rights-vs-aim analysis rather than collapsing the question into a single intuitive judgment. The four-step structure makes the reasoning auditable: an opinion that fails at step 2 has been falsified at step 2, not at the end.

## How To Use

1. **Legitimate aim** — Does the measure pursue a constitutionally legitimate aim?
2. **Suitability** — Is the measure rationally connected to the aim? (Means-end fit.)
3. **Necessity** — Is there a less-restrictive means that would achieve the aim equally well? (If yes, the measure fails.)
4. **Proportionality stricto sensu** — Are the benefits of achieving the aim proportionate to the burden on the right? (Balancing in the narrow sense.)

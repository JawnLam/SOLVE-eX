# Transtheoretical Stages of Change (Prochaska-DiClemente)

**One-line summary:** The 5-stage model of behavior change — Precontemplation, Contemplation, Preparation, Action, Maintenance (plus Relapse) — used to match intervention to stage.

**When to reach for it:** Diagnosing readiness for change in clinical or coaching contexts; designing interventions that match the client's current stage rather than presuming readiness for action.

## Purpose

The model recognizes that interventions effective in one stage are ineffective or counterproductive in another. Pushing someone in Precontemplation toward Action produces reactance; offering Contemplation-style exploration to someone in Action wastes time. Matching intervention to stage improves outcomes. The five stages are not strictly linear — relapse is normal and clients cycle through. The framework gives clinicians and coaches a stage-aware vocabulary for assessment and intervention selection.

## How To Use

1) Assess the client's current stage. 2) Precontemplation: raise awareness, no pressure. 3) Contemplation: explore pros and cons, build motivation. 4) Preparation: plan specific actions, set timing. 5) Action: support behavior change, reward attempts. 6) Maintenance: build relapse prevention. 7) Treat relapse as normal, recycle through stages.

# Status Work (Johnstone)

**One-line summary:** Improvisational training method from Keith Johnstone exploring high-status vs low-status physical and verbal behaviors. Reveals invisible power dynamics; participants practice raising and lowering their status.

**When to reach for it:** Performance training (theatre, improv, public speaking, sales) and leadership development. Increasingly used in conflict-transformation work, where seeing status patterns surfaces stuck dynamics. Best learned in workshop with skilled facilitator.

## Purpose

Status is the most basic structure of social interaction — every gesture, sentence, and pause raises or lowers status. Most people do this unconsciously; performers and leaders need conscious control. Johnstone's exercises make status visible, then flexible. Participants discover they can play any status; the playing reveals how status drives most of what passes for personality.

## How To Use

1. **High-status walk vs low-status walk** — Walk around the room playing high status (commanding space, slow, eye contact); switch to low (tentative, smaller, deferential).
2. **Status mismatch scenes** — Two players, both told to play high; both told to play low. Watch what emerges.
3. **Sliding status** — Within a scene, one player gradually raises while the other lowers.
4. **Status by object** — A high-status player can play with a low-status object (e.g., a king carrying a chamber pot); status comes from the relationship, not the prop.
5. **Real-world transfer** — Apply to meetings, presentations, difficult conversations. Notice your own habitual status; play with shifting it.

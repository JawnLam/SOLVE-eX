# Direct Instruction (Engelmann)

**One-line summary:** Highly scripted explicit teaching with carefully sequenced examples, frequent student response, and immediate corrective feedback — empirically strongest results for low-prior-knowledge learners.

**When to reach for it:** Teaching foundational skills (early reading, math, vocabulary); working with learners who have struggled in less-structured environments; large-scale skill-acquisition programs.

## Purpose

Direct Instruction (DI), developed by Engelmann, is the most empirically-validated instructional method for basic skill acquisition. The method is unusual in its level of scripting: every teacher utterance is specified, examples and non-examples are precisely sequenced to logically determine the concept, students respond chorally and individually, and errors are corrected immediately. Project Follow Through, the largest US educational evaluation, found DI produced the strongest academic gains for low-SES students. The method is unfashionable in progressive-education circles but empirically powerful.

## How To Use

1) Use a published DI program (e.g., Reading Mastery, Connecting Math Concepts). 2) Follow the script precisely. 3) Move at a brisk pace. 4) Elicit frequent student response (group choral, individual). 5) Correct errors immediately with a model-test-retest sequence. 6) Provide cumulative review.

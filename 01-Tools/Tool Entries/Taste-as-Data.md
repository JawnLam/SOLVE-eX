# Taste-As-Data

**One-line summary:** Cooking-as-inquiry method — taste in iterations through preparation, treating each taste as data and each adjustment as hypothesis; trains read-and-respond rather than execute-recipe.

**When to reach for it:** Working in any domain where in-progress feedback can be gathered continuously and used to inform incremental adjustment, and where recipe-following or fixed-plan execution would produce mediocrity.

## Purpose

Taste-as-data is the cognitive method underlying experienced cooking. Throughout preparation, the cook tastes — not at the end, repeatedly through process. Each taste yields data: what does this need now? Salt? Acid? Fat? Sweetness? Time? Each adjustment is a hypothesis to be tested by the next taste. Distinct from recipe-following (which prescribes specific quantities) and from intuition-as-mystery (which obscures the iterative testing). Trains a cognitive-sensory skill of reading-and-responding rather than executing. Generalizes to any iterative-feedback domain: writing (read aloud, listen for what's flat, adjust); design (look at the work, identify what's not working, adjust); coaching (notice the response, calibrate).

## How To Use

Begin preparation. Early — long before the dish is 'done' — taste. Don't ask 'is it ready?' Ask 'what does this need now?' Specifically: is the salt level pulling flavors forward, or is the dish flat? Is there enough acid to brighten? Is the fat adequate to carry the flavors? Is the sweetness too low (making it sharp) or too high (cloying)? Is the texture right? Adjust based on what you taste. Then taste again 5 minutes later. Continue until the dish has converged. The discipline: trust your own palate as the primary instrument; the recipe is a starting hypothesis, not a final truth. The method develops with practice — early on, the data is hard to interpret; with reps, the read-respond loop becomes faster and more accurate.

# Pomodoro Technique (Cirillo)

**One-line summary:** Work in 25-minute focused intervals separated by 5-minute breaks; after 4 pomodoros, take a longer 15-30 minute break.

**When to reach for it:** Building focus capacity; resisting distractions; managing energy through a workday; teaching beginners how to focus without burning out.

## Purpose

Cirillo developed the technique in university using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer (hence pomodoro). The structure addresses two opposed failure modes: insufficient focus (constant distraction) and unsustained focus (exhaustion). The 25-minute interval is long enough for meaningful work but short enough to enforce starting; the 5-minute break prevents accumulating fatigue. The technique externalizes focus management into a clock-based ritual that beginners can adopt without willpower. More advanced practitioners use it strategically for specific tasks rather than as a default.

## How To Use

1) Choose a task. 2) Set a 25-minute timer. 3) Work on the task with no interruptions; if interrupted, note and return. 4) When timer rings, take a 5-minute break — get up, move, look away from screen. 5) Start the next pomodoro. 6) After 4 pomodoros, take a 15-30 minute longer break. 7) Track completed pomodoros.

# Red Queen Hypothesis

**One-line summary:** Where competitors co-evolve, you must run as fast as you can just to stay in the same place — relative fitness erodes if you stop adapting.

**When to reach for it:** Competitive markets where rivals continuously improve; arms-race dynamics in advertising, security, finance; biological co-evolution analysis; any context where standing still equals falling behind.

## Purpose

Named for Lewis Carroll's Red Queen, the principle articulates why competitive advantage requires sustained investment rather than one-time positioning. Each competitor's improvement raises the bar for survival; firms that don't keep improving lose relative position even if their absolute capabilities are unchanged. The strategic implication: 'we're already best in class' is a snapshot claim that erodes if R&D, talent, or capability investment slows.

## How To Use

1) Identify whether your environment has Red Queen dynamics — competitors continuously improving rather than stable. 2) If yes, treat capability investment as a recurring requirement, not a one-time project. 3) Measure relative position regularly, not absolute capability. 4) Allocate budget to maintaining position, separately from growth investments.

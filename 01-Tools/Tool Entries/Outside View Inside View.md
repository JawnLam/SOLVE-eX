# Outside View / Inside View

**One-line summary:** Switch from inside-view (case-specific features, intuition) to outside-view (base rates of similar cases) — the latter is usually better-calibrated.

**When to reach for it:** Estimation under uncertainty, especially when insider enthusiasm or detail-knowledge may be producing overconfidence; planning fallacy contexts.

## Purpose

Inside-view reasoning leverages the specific richness of the case (features, plans, intent) and feels informative — but is reliably overconfident because it neglects base rates. Outside-view reasoning treats the case as one draw from a population of similar cases and uses the population statistics. The discipline is forcing a conscious switch: ask 'how have similar cases turned out?' before relying on 'how do I think this case will turn out?'

## How To Use

1) Notice when you're reasoning from case specifics (inside view). 2) Force a switch: ask 'what is the reference class, and what's its track record?' 3) Anchor the prediction on the outside view's central tendency. 4) Treat the inside view's deviations from the outside view as motivated reasoning until proven otherwise. 5) Practice the switch as a default discipline, not an exception.

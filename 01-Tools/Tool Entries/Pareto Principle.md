# Pareto Principle

**One-line summary:** The 80/20 rule — many outcome distributions are heavily skewed, with a small share of inputs driving most outputs. A heuristic for finding leverage.

**When to reach for it:** Whenever you face a long list of possible interventions, customers, problems, or causes and need to prioritize the small subset that drives most of the outcome.

## Purpose

Pareto's empirical observation about wealth distribution generalized to a broad family of skewed phenomena (the heaviest-tailed customers drive most revenue; the worst bugs cause most outages; the top contributors do most of the work). The principle is descriptive (such distributions exist) and prescriptive (concentrate effort on the heavy end). It does not require an exact 80/20 split; the operative claim is that the underlying distribution is non-uniform and the asymmetry is exploitable.

## How To Use

1) Identify the outcome metric. 2) Rank inputs/causes/contributors by contribution. 3) Compute cumulative share. 4) Locate the inflection (often ~20% of inputs explain ~80% of output). 5) Focus disproportionate attention on the heavy end; deprioritize or batch the long tail. 6) Re-check when the system evolves — Pareto distributions shift.

## Sources

- Pareto, V. (1896) *Cours d'économie politique*.
- Juran, J.M. (1941) popularization of the 80/20 rule in quality engineering.

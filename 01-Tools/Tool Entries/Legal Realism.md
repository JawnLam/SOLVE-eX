# Legal Realism

**One-line summary:** The law is what courts will actually do, not what doctrines say they should — predict legal outcomes by understanding the social, political, and psychological influences on judges.

**When to reach for it:** Practical lawyering (predicting how a court will actually rule); legal education emphasizing the gap between doctrine and practice; sociological analysis of law.

## Purpose

Legal Realism emerged as a corrective to formalist legal theory that treated law as a self-contained system of doctrine. Holmes's bad-man perspective ('the prophecies of what the courts will do in fact') reframes law as predicted judicial behavior. Cardozo emphasized the multiple influences on judicial decisions: precedent, logic, sociology, politics. The school's practical implication: lawyers should study how courts actually behave, not just what doctrine prescribes.

## How To Use

1) For a legal question, identify the doctrine that would seem to apply. 2) Identify the courts and judges likely to decide. 3) Consider the social, political, and psychological factors that influence those judges. 4) Predict the actual outcome — which may diverge from doctrine. 5) Argue strategically based on the prediction rather than just the doctrine.

# Mise en Place

**One-line summary:** Culinary cognitive scaffold — putting in place every ingredient, tool, and step before cooking begins, externalizing thinking into the workspace.

**When to reach for it:** Preparing for any complex, time-pressured execution where mid-task thinking would degrade quality and where pre-execution preparation can convert thought into arrangement.

## Purpose

Mise en place ('putting in place') is the French culinary discipline of preparing every ingredient, tool, and step before cooking begins. Every onion is diced, every measurement made, every pan within reach. The cognitive function: the workspace becomes an 'external brain' — thinking is externalized into the arrangement, freeing the cook to attend fully to in-action judgment during execution. Translates beyond cooking to any complex, time-pressured work: Tiago Forte's 'mise-en-place for knowledge workers' applies the discipline to project preparation; Dan Charnas's *Work Clean* extends it as a life methodology. The principle: prepare-now reduces think-later under pressure.

## How To Use

Before beginning execution: list every step of the task and what each requires. Stage all required materials within reach in the order they will be needed. Pre-process everything that can be pre-processed (chop, measure, format, organize). Clear the workspace of anything not required. Test the staging: walk through the execution mentally, checking that each step has its required materials and tools. Begin execution only when mise en place is complete. Used in cooking; transferable to surgical preparation, public speaking setup, code-writing environment, complex document preparation, any time-pressured complex work. The discipline is the pre-commitment to staging — refusing to start until mise en place is genuinely complete.

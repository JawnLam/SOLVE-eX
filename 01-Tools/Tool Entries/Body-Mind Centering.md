# Body-Mind Centering

**One-line summary:** Bonnie Bainbridge Cohen's experiential study of body systems (skeletal, muscular, organs, glands, fluids, nervous, etc.). Each system has a distinct movement quality and cognitive coloring; patterning of developmental sequences re-organizes movement.

**When to reach for it:** Movement education, dance training, somatic therapy, infant/child developmental support. Used in modern dance pedagogy, occupational therapy, and somatic-psychotherapy training. Best studied in workshops with certified BMC practitioners.

## Purpose

Most movement training works with the muscular and skeletal systems. BMC adds: organs, fluids, glands, nerves each have distinct experiential qualities and contribute differently to movement. A skeletal-driven movement differs from an organ-driven one — sensorially, expressively, and functionally. The practice teaches access to each system, then integrates them. The developmental sequences (yield, push, reach, pull, grasp) recapitulate the infant's organization and can re-pattern adult movement that bypassed them.

## How To Use

1. **System-by-system experiential anatomy** — Study one body system; explore its movement quality through palpation, imagery, and improvisation.
2. **Developmental movement** — Re-traverse the infant developmental sequences (yield, push, reach, pull, grasp) consciously, noticing which feel under-developed.
3. **Patterning** — Where a developmental movement is under-developed, work with a practitioner to re-pattern via hands-on guidance and repetition.
4. **Integration** — Allow body systems to inform each other in complex movement (dance, daily activity).

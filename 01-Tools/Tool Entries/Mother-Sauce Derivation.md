# Mother-Sauce Derivation Logic

**One-line summary:** Classical French culinary reasoning — five mother sauces (béchamel, velouté, hollandaise, tomate, espagnole) generate hundreds of derivatives through small variations; master parent, derivatives become tractable.

**When to reach for it:** Approaching a domain with many surface-different variants and seeking a cognitive frame that reveals which few foundational forms generate the rest — accelerating mastery by mastering parents rather than memorizing children.

## Purpose

The classical French culinary system, codified by Escoffier in *Le Guide Culinaire* (1903), organizes the universe of sauces around five mother sauces: béchamel (milk + roux), velouté (light stock + roux), hollandaise (egg yolk + butter emulsion), tomate (tomato-based), and espagnole (brown stock + roux). Every other sauce is a derivative — a small variation on one of these parents (béchamel + cheese = mornay; espagnole + red wine + shallots = bordelaise). The cognitive method: master the parent thoroughly; derivatives become tractable. Generalizes as a frame for any domain with rich variants over few foundations — programming patterns from base classes, musical genres from basic forms, persuasion techniques from foundational structures.

## How To Use

For the domain in question: identify the small set of foundational forms that generate the variants. In cooking, the mother sauces. In programming, base classes or fundamental algorithms. In writing, foundational structures (the elevator pitch, the executive summary, the case study). In any craft, ask: what are the parents from which the children derive? Master the parents thoroughly — not just executing them but understanding what makes them work, where they fail, why their structure is what it is. Then explore the children with the parent always in view: this is béchamel with X added; this is bordelaise = espagnole with these variations. The discipline shortens the mastery path dramatically. The risk: prematurely identifying the wrong parents and forcing derivative-thinking on cases that don't fit.

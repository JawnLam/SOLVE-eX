# Futures Wheel

**One-line summary:** Jerome Glenn's structured-brainstorming visual tool. Write a trend or event in the center; surround it with first-order consequences; surround those with second-order; continue outward to surface cascade implications.

**When to reach for it:** Any time a foresight question requires identifying second- and third-order consequences of a trend or decision — "if we automate driving, then…" or "if antibiotic resistance accelerates, then…". Strong for policy analysis, scenario development, and technology assessment.

## Purpose

Most analysis stops at first-order effects. The Futures Wheel disciplines the analyst to push outward systematically — second-order (consequences of consequences), third-order, etc. Often the second-order ring contains the strategic insights; the first-order ring is obvious. The visual structure also makes the analysis collaborative: different participants can fill in different rings.

## How To Use

1. **Write the trend/event in the center.** "Universal basic income implemented at $X/month."
2. **First-order ring** — Direct consequences. Spoke outward to ~6-10 immediate effects.
3. **Second-order ring** — For each first-order effect, what does *it* cause?
4. **Third-order ring (optional)** — For high-leverage second-order effects, push one more level.
5. **Highlight** — Which consequences are desirable, undesirable, surprising, leverage-rich?
6. **Synthesize** — Story across the rings; the most strategically interesting effects are often 2-3 hops out.

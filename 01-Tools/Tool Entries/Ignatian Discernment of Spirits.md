# Ignatian Discernment of Spirits

**One-line summary:** A structured protocol — Ignatius's 22 Rules — for reading the felt-tonality of inner movements ("consolation" vs "desolation"), distinguishing authentic guidance from false guidance, and making decisions in alignment with one's deepest commitments rather than surface preferences.

**When to reach for it:** Major life decisions where competing inner pulls are present; periods of spiritual consolation that may be misleading or desolation that may be informative; vocational or relational decisions where "what feels right" needs structural examination; in spiritual direction or therapy when working with felt-sense as a decision input.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

Ignatius's central operational claim: **inner movements have characteristic signatures, and those signatures can be read.** The discernment practitioner learns to attend to:

- **Consolation** — movements that draw one toward life, faith, hope, charity; an inner peace, a sense of "Yes" that doesn't depend on circumstances; an increase in capacity for love and service.
- **Desolation** — movements toward darkness, restlessness, disturbance, separation from one's deepest commitments; loss of hope, attraction to lower pursuits.

The non-obvious operational insight: **not all inner pull is authentic.** Both the "good spirit" and the "enemy of human nature" can produce felt-good states. Authentic consolation has a steady-state quality (peace beyond circumstances); false consolation has a brittle, anxious-grasping quality even when it feels positive. The rules give you tests to distinguish.

A second insight: **decisions made in desolation should generally not be made.** Ignatius is firm here: when you're in desolation, return to whatever you decided in your last clear consolation. Don't restructure your life based on a felt-state of disturbance. Wait until clarity returns.

A third insight: **discernment is a practice, not a one-time event.** The rules assume a daily examen (review of the day's inner movements), iterative learning of one's own patterns, and ideally a spiritual director who provides outside perspective.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the practice prevents:

1. **Treating felt-pull as authoritative.** Strong inner pull is often the enemy's strategy, not God's. The rules teach you to *test* the pull rather than obey it.
2. **Acting from desolation.** Major decisions made in dark periods often unravel when consolation returns. The rules counsel patience in desolation.
3. **Missing genuine guidance.** Equally, dismissing all felt-experience as unreliable means missing the authentic consolation that should inform direction. The rules navigate between credulity and dismissal.

The framework is theologically Christian but the structural insights about reading inner movement transfer to any practice of life-discernment, including secular vocational reflection.

## How To Use This Thinking Tool

```
|======|=================================================================================|
| Step |                                     Action                                      |
|======|=================================================================================|
|  1   | Daily examen (15-20 min, ideally evening):                                      |
|      |   a. Recall the day with gratitude.                                              |
|      |   b. Notice the felt-movements: where was consolation? Where was desolation?    |
|      |   c. Trace the cause: what was happening when the movement arose?               |
|      |   d. Ask: which movements drew me toward life, which toward isolation?          |
|  2   | When facing a decision: name the options. For each, imagine living it for a    |
|      | week and notice the inner movement that arises.                                 |
|  3   | Test for authenticity:                                                          |
|      |   - Does this "good feeling" hold under scrutiny (peace beyond circumstances)? |
|      |     OR does it have a grasping/anxious quality?                                 |
|      |   - Does the proposed action align with deepest commitments, or with a         |
|      |     lower-order preference dressed up as one?                                   |
|  4   | If in desolation: do not make major decisions. Return to last clear            |
|      | consolation. Wait.                                                              |
|  5   | If in clear consolation: act, but cautiously — false consolations can mimic.    |
|  6   | Seek outside witness (spiritual director, trusted friend, therapist) for major |
|      | decisions. Self-confirmation bias is the chief failure mode.                    |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

## The Actual Thinking Tool

```
Daily Examen Worksheet

DATE: _______

1. GRATITUDE — three things from today:
   - _______________________
   - _______________________
   - _______________________

2. INNER MOVEMENTS — where in today was there:

   CONSOLATION (movement toward life, peace, capacity):
   _________________________________________________

   DESOLATION (movement toward isolation, restlessness, despair):
   _________________________________________________

3. CAUSES — what triggered each movement?
   Consolation: _____________________________________
   Desolation: ______________________________________

4. DISCERNMENT TEST (for any consolation):
   [ ] Steady, peaceful, holds under scrutiny? → authentic
   [ ] Brittle, grasping, anxious-elated? → suspect; test further

5. PATTERN over recent days:
   - Recurring consolations point toward:
     _________________________________________
   - Recurring desolations point toward:
     _________________________________________

6. ACTION (if decision is pending):
   In desolation? → DO NOT decide; wait.
   In consolation? → cross-check with deepest commitments and a witness.

---

DECISION DISCERNMENT TEMPLATE (for a specific choice)

OPTIONS being weighed:
  A: _______________________
  B: _______________________

For each option:
  Imagined-future inner movement: ______________
  Test (does the consolation hold under scrutiny?): ______________
  Alignment with deepest commitments: ______________

Outside witness consulted:  [ ] yes  [ ] no
Witness's read: ______________________________________
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **Discernment is slow.** Resist the urge to discern quickly. Major decisions take weeks or months of iterative discernment, not single sessions. (2) **The framework is theologically loaded** but the structural insights work secularly — substitute "deepest commitments" for "God's will" if helpful, and "the part of me that resists my best self" for "the enemy." (3) **A spiritual director or trusted witness is structurally important** — the felt-test alone is too easy to game. (4) **The rules vary by spiritual life stage** — Ignatius distinguishes Week 1 rules (for beginners, where good/bad pulls are obvious) from Week 2 rules (for advanced, where false consolations become subtle). Start with Week 1.

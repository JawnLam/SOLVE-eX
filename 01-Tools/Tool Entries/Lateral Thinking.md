# Lateral Thinking (de Bono)

**One-line summary:** A family of provocation-techniques — random word stimulation, "po" provocations, assumption-challenging, concept-escape — that deliberately moves cognition sideways from established patterns to generate options that vertical (logical-deductive) thinking cannot reach.

**When to reach for it:** Stuck on a problem where more analysis is producing the same answer; ideation phases where you need genuinely new options not just variations on the obvious; teaching contexts where students default to canonical answers; product development that needs to break out of category orthodoxy.

---

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool

De Bono's central operational distinction: **vertical thinking** moves step-by-step along established patterns, evaluating each move (does this follow?). **Lateral thinking** deliberately moves sideways, *suspending evaluation*, to generate alternative patterns. Both are needed; modern education over-trains vertical and under-trains lateral.

The non-obvious operational insight: **provocation is a method, not just a stance.** Telling someone "think outside the box" doesn't work. De Bono's contribution was systematizing provocation into specific techniques the user can deploy on demand:

1. **Random Word Technique.** Pick a random noun from a list. Force a connection between the random word and the problem. The randomness short-circuits the brain's tendency to retrieve only relevant prior solutions.
2. **"Po" Provocation.** State a deliberate impossibility, signal it with "po" (provocative operation), then explore where the impossibility leads. "Po: cars have square wheels." Don't dismiss; explore. The exploration generates real ideas via the impossibility's adjacencies.
3. **Challenge Assumptions.** List the unstated assumptions in the current framing. Negate each one. Each negation is a provocation.
4. **Concept Escape.** Identify the dominant concept (the lens you can't see through). Deliberately drop it. Re-approach the problem without it.
5. **Reverse the Problem.** Flip the goal. ("How do we make customers buy more" → "How do we make customers buy less" → reveal what's currently driving purchases.)

A second insight: **lateral and vertical alternate; they don't compete.** Lateral generates options; vertical evaluates them. The protocol's value is keeping them separate. Most failed ideation alternates within a single move — you generate, then immediately evaluate, then generate evaluated-only-versions; the evaluation suppresses the genuine laterals.

## Why Use This Thinking Tool

Three failure modes the toolkit prevents:

1. **Vertical lock-in.** Continuing to drill the same pattern even when no new options are appearing. More effort, fewer breakthroughs.
2. **Pseudo-lateral.** "Brainstorming" that stays within category orthodoxy because evaluation is sneaking in. The provocations are explicit enough to prevent this — you cannot evaluate "po: cars have square wheels" and stay in normal-car space.
3. **Generic creativity advice.** "Think different" / "be creative" / "challenge yourself" without a method. The techniques are operational, repeatable, and teachable.

This tool's evidence base is corporate-trainer-empirical (de Bono's methods are widely taught in design, advertising, and consulting since the 1970s) more than academic. The framework has been criticized for over-claiming on "creativity"; the steel-man defense is that it provides repeatable provocation methods that demonstrably produce non-obvious options, whether or not "creativity" is the right word.

```
|  7   | REVERSE: Flip the goal. Reveal hidden drivers.                                  |
|  8   | Collect options without evaluation. After the lateral phase, switch to vertical |
|      | for evaluation.                                                                 |
|======|=================================================================================|
```

> **Operational notes:** (1) **Suspend evaluation completely during the lateral phase.** Even a "hmm, that's silly" thought collapses the technique. Note the silliness without acting on it. (2) **Random word lists work best when the words have many semantic dimensions.** Concrete nouns (mirror, river, bicycle) outperform abstract (justice, truth, beauty) for stimulation. (3) **"Po" is the hardest technique** because the brain resists holding an impossibility. Persist; the adjacencies appear after a minute or two of staying with it. (4) **Lateral thinking is not a substitute for domain expertise** — the options it generates have to be assessed by someone who knows the field. The tool produces candidates; the field-knowledge filters them.

## Related Tools and Frameworks

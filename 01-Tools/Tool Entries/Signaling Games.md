# Signaling Games / Spence Signaling

**One-line summary:** A two-player asymmetric-information game — a sender of a costly signal conveys private information to a receiver who acts on it.

**When to reach for it:** Job-market signaling (education as credible-type indicator); product warranties as quality signals; venture-capital signaling; marriage proposals as commitment signals.

## Purpose

Spence's insight: in asymmetric-information settings, players with good private information can credibly signal it by taking actions costly enough that low-type players wouldn't imitate. Education-as-signaling, warranties-as-quality-signals, and many other phenomena are instances. The equilibrium concept (perfect Bayesian equilibrium) refines Nash for these games. The framework is the foundation of information economics and many institutional designs that elicit private information.

## How To Use

1) Identify the asymmetric information structure (sender knows type, receiver doesn't). 2) Specify signal options and their costs by type. 3) The separating equilibrium has each type sending a distinct signal that the other type wouldn't pay to imitate. 4) The pooling equilibrium has all types sending the same signal. 5) Choose between separating and pooling based on cost structure.

# Second-Order Thinking

**One-line summary:** Reason past the first effect of an action to the second-, third-, and Nth-order consequences — including how other actors will adjust to your action.

**When to reach for it:** Strategic decisions where competitors, regulators, customers, or markets will react; policy decisions with non-trivial feedback loops; any context where 'and then what?' is the operative question.

## Purpose

First-order thinking stops at the immediate consequence. Second-order thinking asks what happens after the system absorbs the action — how counterparties adjust, how incentives shift, how feedback loops kick in. Investment outperformance, strategic surprise, and many policy successes come from playing the second move better than the consensus first move.

## How To Use

1) State the action and its first-order effect. 2) Ask: who else is affected, and how will they respond? 3) Apply the same question to those responses. 4) Continue until effects stabilize or become uncertain. 5) Compare the cumulative consequence to the first-order expectation; act on the difference.

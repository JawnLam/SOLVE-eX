# Tibetan Chöd (Cutting Through)

**One-line summary:** Tibetan tantric practice (Machig Labdron, 11th-12th c.) — visualizing offering one's own body as nourishment to demons, deities, and all beings, radically reversing survival-grasping at body-self.

**When to reach for it:** Working with chronic fear, ego-attachment to bodily safety, or charnel-ground retreat conditions where ordinary contemplative methods cannot reach the grip of body-identification.

## Purpose

Chöd ('cutting through') was originated by the female master Machig Labdron (11th-12th c. CE) and is unique among Tibetan practices for being a female-founded lineage of significant authority. The practitioner visualizes offering their own body — dismembered, transformed into nectar, made into a feast — as nourishment to demons, deities, hungry ghosts, ancestors, and all sentient beings. The visualization radically reverses the basic survival instinct's grasping at the body-self. Used to cut through ego-fixation; powerful for working with chronic fear, terminal illness, or charnel-ground retreats. Distinguished by its dramatic visualization, ritual instruments (damaru drum, thigh-bone trumpet), and chanted melodies.

## How To Use

Within formal Tibetan Buddhist lineage transmission: receive empowerment and instruction from a qualified teacher. The full practice involves preliminary refuge and bodhicitta, visualization of the practitioner's consciousness ejecting through the crown, transformation of the body into nectar/feast, multiple invitations (to the four guests: deities, dharma protectors, ancestral spirits, hungry beings), the offering, and dedication of merit. Accompanied by specific melodies, drum, and bell. Daily practice may take 30-90 minutes. Often performed in charnel grounds, caves, or other 'fear places' to test the cutting-through. Outside formal lineage transmission, study the philosophy and symbolism rather than attempt the visualization.

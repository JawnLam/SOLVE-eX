# Whakapapa Relational Genealogy

**One-line summary:** Māori method of layered genealogical reasoning — every entity has a whakapapa (lineage of origin) that, when traced, reveals its kinship structure and inherited relations.

**When to reach for it:** Investigating a phenomenon, idea, or decision where its provenance, kinship structure, and inherited obligations are not yet visible — and where origin-tracing reveals what abstract analysis misses.

## Purpose

Whakapapa extends the cognitive method of human genealogy to all reality. Mountains, rivers, ideas, institutions, decisions — each has a lineage of origin that can be recited. To know something is to know its whakapapa: where it came from, what relations it inherits, what obligations attach. The method generates situated understanding rather than abstract characterization. Used in Māori research methodology (kaupapa Māori), in ecological co-governance, in legal proceedings (Waitangi Tribunal), and as a personal practice of self-situating.

## How To Use

Identify the entity of inquiry (a place, an idea, a decision, an organization). Recite or trace its whakapapa: from what did it descend? What were its formative relations? Which kinship lines is it part of? What does it inherit by virtue of its descent — obligations, predispositions, gifts? Use the recitation as a frame for understanding present-day character and as a check against decontextualized analysis. Approach with appropriate humility: in Māori practice, whakapapa belongs to specific peoples and is shared on terms; outsider use should be descriptive and respectful, not extractive.

# Mahasi Vipassana Noting

**One-line summary:** Mahasi Sayadaw's (1904-1982) Burmese insight-meditation technique: silently note arising sensations and mental events with quick mental labels ("rising… falling… hearing… thinking…"). Provides structure for sustained concentration on the six sense doors.

**When to reach for it:** Insight (vipassanā) meditation practice, especially for practitioners who find unstructured awareness drifts toward distraction. Used widely on intensive retreats; adaptable to daily practice. Foundational in the IMS (Insight Meditation Society) lineage and many lay vipassanā schools.

## Purpose

Awareness without structure tends to dissolve into mind-wandering. The Mahasi technique provides a minimal verbal scaffold — a one-word note — that anchors awareness without elaborating it. "Hearing" rather than "someone outside is having an argument and I wonder what about." The note keeps the practitioner on the bare sensation; over time, the noting itself fades and bare awareness remains.

## How To Use

1. **Primary anchor**: attend to the rising and falling of the abdomen with the breath. Silently note "rising… falling…" with each in-out.
2. **When a stronger sensation arises** (sound, sensation, thought, emotion), shift the note to it: "hearing," "itching," "thinking," "sadness."
3. **Note three times** if the sensation persists; return to the primary anchor when the sensation passes.
4. **Keep the note short** — one word maximum. The note labels the experience without elaborating it.
5. **In retreat conditions**, the practice is sustained for many hours; in daily practice, 30-60 minute sittings are standard.

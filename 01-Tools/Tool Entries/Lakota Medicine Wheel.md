# Lakota Medicine Wheel as Reasoning Frame

**One-line summary:** Lakota (and broader Plains-Indigenous) circular four-direction model where each direction carries distinct qualities; the practitioner cycles through to ensure balanced consideration.

**When to reach for it:** Engaging life-stage reflection, decision-making, healing work, or ceremonial inquiry where balanced consideration across multiple dimensions is the work, and Western analytic frameworks feel partial.

## Purpose

The Medicine Wheel is a circular cognitive frame with four directions (East, South, West, North), each carrying associated qualities — typical Lakota attributions include dawn-spring-childhood-yellow for East, noon-summer-youth-red for South, dusk-autumn-adulthood-black for West, midnight-winter-elder-white for North. The practitioner cycles through directions to ensure each is honored in turn. Used for decision-making, healing, life-stage reflection, and ceremonial reasoning. Specific quadrant-attributions vary by nation; the structural method is widely shared across Plains and other Indigenous traditions.

## How To Use

Sit or stand within an imagined or constructed wheel oriented to the four cardinal directions. For the question being addressed: turn first to the East and ask what this direction's qualities reveal (new beginnings, illumination, vision). Then South (growth, action, emotion). Then West (introspection, dreaming, death-of-old). Then North (wisdom, completion, ancestors). Each turn invites particular questions; balance comes from honoring all four. Approach with respect: medicine wheel work belongs to specific peoples and ceremonies; non-Indigenous use should be descriptive and acknowledge tradition, not extractive.

# Akan Sankofa Method

**One-line summary:** Akan (Ghana) principle: 'san-ko-fa' — go back and fetch. When stuck, return to past, ancestral wisdom, or earlier stages and retrieve what's needed.

**When to reach for it:** Facing a decision or stuck point where the answer is not forward-oriented innovation but recovery — of an ancestral practice, a forgotten lesson, an earlier stage of one's own journey.

## Purpose

Sankofa is one of the Akan adinkra symbols — a bird with its head turned backward, retrieving an egg from its back. The literal meaning: 'san' (return), 'ko' (go), 'fa' (fetch). The cognitive principle: when one is stuck, lost, or facing a question that forward-only reasoning cannot answer, the answer is to return to what came before — ancestral wisdom, earlier stages of one's own journey, forgotten resources, traditional practices abandoned in haste. Widely used in African and African-diasporic education, ethics, and decision-making. The principle rebuts the modernist assumption that progress requires only forward motion.

## How To Use

Facing a stuck point: pause the search-forward instinct. Ask sankofa questions: What did our predecessors do facing similar circumstances? What ancestral practice addressed this kind of trouble? What did I myself know or do earlier in my life that I have forgotten? What traditional resource — material, relational, ceremonial — exists for this kind of question? Then go and fetch: read the predecessors, ask the elders, revisit the earlier self's journals, recover the traditional practice. The forward path resumes after the recovery, often re-oriented. As a daily practice, sankofa disciplines the recency-bias that treats only the newest as the most relevant.

# Mental Status Exam (MSE)

**One-line summary:** Structured systematic observation of a patient's current mental functioning across appearance, behavior, speech, mood, affect, thought process/content, perception, cognition, insight, judgment.

**When to reach for it:** Conducting a psychiatric or psychological assessment where systematic structured observation across mental functioning dimensions is required, and unstructured impression would miss critical signs.

## Purpose

The Mental Status Examination is foundational to psychiatric assessment — it teaches what to observe. The examiner systematically attends to: (1) Appearance — grooming, build, apparent age, distinguishing features; (2) Behavior — eye contact, motor activity, mannerisms; (3) Speech — rate, volume, prosody, fluency; (4) Mood — patient's self-reported state; (5) Affect — observed emotional state, range, congruence; (6) Thought process — coherence, flight, blocking, tangentiality; (7) Thought content — preoccupations, delusions, suicidal/homicidal ideation; (8) Perception — hallucinations, illusions; (9) Cognition — orientation, memory, attention, executive function; (10) Insight — awareness of illness; (11) Judgment — capacity for sound decision-making.

## How To Use

During the clinical encounter (or immediately after), systematically observe and record across each dimension. Some are observable throughout the interview (appearance, behavior, speech, affect, thought process); some require specific questions (suicidal/homicidal ideation, perceptual experiences, cognitive testing); some are inferred from the encounter as a whole (insight, judgment). Document with specific descriptive language rather than vague labels — 'disheveled, with mismatched socks' rather than 'unkempt'; 'reports feeling like a balloon untethered' rather than 'sad mood.' The structured form ensures no dimension is omitted; the descriptive specificity makes the MSE useful for longitudinal comparison and inter-rater reliability.

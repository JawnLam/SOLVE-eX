# Midrashic Reading

**One-line summary:** A creative-amplification hermeneutic — reading texts (especially Torah) by elaborating gaps, juxtaposing distant passages, and generating new meaning rooted in the text's openness.

**When to reach for it:** Sacred-text study in Jewish tradition; teaching close reading that produces new meaning rather than just recovering original meaning; literary reading that engages textual gaps generatively.

## Purpose

Where modern hermeneutics often aims at authorial intent or original meaning, midrashic reading sits comfortably with multiple coexisting meanings and treats gaps and silences in the text as invitations to generative interpretation. The method is intertextual: passages are read in light of each other across the canonical corpus. Each generation's midrashic readings build on prior generations while making their own contributions. The tradition gives a model for creative-faithful interpretation.

## How To Use

1) Read the text closely, noting gaps, silences, repetitions, anomalies. 2) Bring other passages into dialogue with it. 3) Generate possible meanings consistent with the text's structure. 4) Hold multiple meanings simultaneously without forcing reduction. 5) Build on prior generations' readings.

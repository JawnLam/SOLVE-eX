# Trauma-Releasing Exercises (TRE)

**One-line summary:** David Berceli's series of seven physical exercises that fatigue the psoas and related deep muscles, inducing neurogenic tremors — the body's natural shaking-discharge mechanism for survival activation.

**When to reach for it:** For trauma decompression in non-clinical settings (refugee populations, military, first responders), or as adjunct to trauma therapy. Used cross-culturally; works without verbal processing, making it useful where language is a barrier.

## Purpose

Stress and trauma activate survival musculature (especially the psoas, the body's "fight or flight" muscle). In animals, the activation discharges through shaking. Humans typically inhibit the shaking — and the activation stays stuck. TRE bypasses inhibition: by fatiguing the muscles via specific exercises, the body resumes the natural tremor and discharges. The result is parasympathetic recovery without cognitive processing of the trauma narrative.

## How To Use

1. **Find a safe space** — a place where shaking is socially acceptable.
2. **Perform the seven exercises** in sequence — calf stretching, ankle rocking, psoas-engagement positions, and the wall-sit. Each fatigues specific muscles.
3. **Final position** — supine with knees bent, feet flat, knees lightly inward. Wait.
4. **Tremor begins** — small involuntary shaking in the legs first, possibly spreading. Don't suppress, don't amplify. Just allow.
5. **15-20 minute initial sessions**; longer with experience. Stop if overwhelmed (the body will tremor when ready).
6. **Slow practice ramp** — 2-3 times per week; not daily initially.

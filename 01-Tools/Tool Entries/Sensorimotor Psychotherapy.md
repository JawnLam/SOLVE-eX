# Sensorimotor Psychotherapy

**One-line summary:** Pat Ogden's bottom-up trauma therapy integrating Hakomi mindfulness, attachment theory, and Van der Kolk-influenced body-based processing. Tracks autonomic arousal; uses small movement-experiments to widen the window of tolerance.

**When to reach for it:** Treating trauma where the client's autonomic dysregulation (hypoarousal, hyperarousal) prevents cognitive or talk therapy from being effective. The body's chronic survival responses are the working material.

## Purpose

Conventional talk therapy assumes the client can stay in their thinking mind — but for trauma survivors, autonomic activation derails cognitive work. Sensorimotor therapy first stabilizes the body's arousal, then uses small movement experiments to update implicit procedural memory. The bottom-up approach changes the body's defensive patterns; the top-down cognitive work follows.

## How To Use

1. **Track autonomic state** — Is the client in optimal arousal (window of tolerance), hyperarousal, or hypoarousal?
2. **Resource the client** — Build grounding skills, contact with the present moment, sensory anchors.
3. **Approach the trauma material in small doses** — Brief contact with distressing material; return to safety; titrate.
4. **Movement experiments** — Where survival responses (fight, flight, freeze) were truncated, complete them in small somatic gestures.
5. **Integration** — Connect the body's new pattern to cognitive understanding.

# Atma Vichara (Ramana Maharshi Self-Inquiry)

**One-line summary:** Ramana Maharshi's (1879-1950) self-inquiry practice: hold attention on the "I"-thought, ask "Who am I?" or "Where does this I come from?", and let attention rest in the source. A direct-pointing variant of Advaita Vedanta inquiry.

**When to reach for it:** Within Advaita Vedanta-influenced contemplative practice. Particularly suited for practitioners who have hit the limits of conceptual approaches and want a method that bypasses cognitive elaboration. Best with periodic guidance from a teacher familiar with the practice.

## Purpose

Most spiritual inquiry seeks an answer — "What is the self?" Atma vichara reverses the move: instead of seeking an answer, it traces the question back to its source. The "I"-thought arises from somewhere; trace attention to *where*. The thought-of-I dissolves in the trace; what remains is the awareness that was looking. The method is famously simple and famously difficult — its simplicity is precisely what allows ordinary mind to slip past it.

## How To Use

1. **Begin with awareness** of any thought, sensation, or feeling.
2. **Notice the implicit "I"** — "I am thinking," "I am feeling," "I am aware."
3. **Turn attention to the I itself** — "What is this I? Where does it come from?"
4. **Don't seek a verbal answer.** Hold the question; let attention rest in the inquiry.
5. **When attention drifts to ordinary thoughts**, gently note them and return to the I-source.
6. **In time**, the I-thought dissolves into its source — pure awareness without object. Rest there.
7. **Continue** — through formal sitting and through ordinary activity. The practice is unrelenting attention to the I-source.

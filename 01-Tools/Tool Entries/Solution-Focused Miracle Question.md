# Solution-Focused Miracle Question

**One-line summary:** Single-prompt method from de Shazer/Berg's Solution-Focused Brief Therapy: "Suppose tonight while you slept, a miracle happened and the problem you came in for were solved — but you didn't know. How would you discover it? What would you notice?"

**When to reach for it:** When a client is fixated on the problem and cannot imagine the solution. Or when planning conversations stall on what's wrong rather than what's wanted. Useful in therapy, coaching, design, organizational change.

## Purpose

Problem-focused conversations explore why the problem exists. Solution-focused conversations explore what the solution looks like. The Miracle Question bypasses the problem-fixation by inviting the client to articulate the solution-state in concrete, observable terms. The articulation surfaces what would actually change — often revealing that pieces of the solution are already present.

## How To Use

1. **Set up gently** — "I want to ask you a kind of strange question."
2. **Ask the question slowly** — "Suppose tonight, while you were sleeping, a miracle happened and the problem you brought here today was solved. But because you were asleep, you didn't know the miracle happened. When you wake up tomorrow, what would be different? What would tell you that the miracle had occurred?"
3. **Hold space for the answer** — long pause; let the client construct.
4. **Concretize** — "What else? What would your partner/colleague/family notice that was different about you?"
5. **Look for exceptions** — "Has any small piece of that already happened, even briefly?"
6. **Bridge** — "What would help you take one small step toward that miracle, this week?"

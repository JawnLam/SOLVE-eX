# Causal Chain Breaker.


## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Domino Sequence Breaker" thinking tool is to identify the most effective point at which to intervene and solve a complex problem. Recognizing that not all problems can be solved at their origin, this tool helps to trace the sequence of causes that led to the problem, allowing one to consider where they have the capacity and resources to implement a solution.

This tool is especially useful when direct action on the root cause is not feasible or when control over the initiating factors is limited. By working through the causal chain, one can pinpoint where actions can be taken to disrupt the sequence and resolve the problem or mitigate its effects.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool:
This tool helps find the best place to solve a problem. It might not be possible to fix the problem right where it starts. To find the right place to solve it, ask, "Where in the series of events leading to the problem can I actually make a change, given what I can control and the resources I have?"


## How To Use This Thinking Tool:
|======|==========================================================================================================================================|
| Step |                                                                  Action                                                                  |
|------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    1 | Quickly describe the situation needing a cause-and-effect analysis.                                                                      |
|    2 | Make a list of possible causes (like Cause A, Cause B, Cause C, and so on).                                                              |
|    3 | For each possible cause, create a cause-and-effect chain. Ask, "Why did this happen?" Answer with, "It happened because of this reason." |
|    4 | For each "because of this reason" part, ask yourself, "Can I start solving the problem from here?"                                       |
|    5 | Keep doing this until you find where you can effectively start solving the problem.                                                      |
|    6 | Do this for each cause (Cause B, Cause C, and so on).                                                                                    |
|    7 | Look at all the cause-and-effect chains to figure out the best place to start fixing the problem.                                        |
|======|==========================================================================================================================================|

# Conversation Convergence Process.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool: 
The purpose of the "Conversation Convergence Process" thinking tool is to facilitate effective communication and problem-solving in situations where conflict arises from misunderstandings. It is designed to guide participants through a structured dialogue that clarifies positions, fosters mutual understanding, and identifies common ground.

This tool is particularly useful in meetings or discussions where differing viewpoints may lead to conflict, and there is a need to align on objectives and understand each other's perspectives. The goal is to move from conflict or divergence in conversation to a point of convergence, where participants agree on a shared path forward or at least understand the nature of their disagreements.

By methodically exchanging views, repeating back what was heard for confirmation, asking questions to clarify, and systematically documenting points of agreement and disagreement, the tool aims to reduce misunderstandings, highlight shared goals, and facilitate a collaborative search for solutions. It ultimately aims to transform conflict into constructive dialogue, leading to actionable outcomes and improved group dynamics.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Conflicts often happen because people don't understand each other. Here's what to do:
- Ignore the conflict if it's not about what the meeting is for.
- Make sure everyone knows what the problem is.
- Make the goals of the meeting clear so that people aren't working against each other.
- Figure out if the disagreement is really about the problem or just about how people work, their personalities, or their values.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool:
For this thinking tool, start a conversation about the conflict using this method to help solve it.

Steps:
1. Initial Position Statement: Participant A presents their view on the problem without interruption.
2. Recap & Understanding: Participant B repeats what they heard from Participant A, without adding new information.
3. Clarifying Questions: Participant B asks clarifying questions to fully understand Participant A's view. No challenges are made here; the focus is on comprehending the stance.
4. Perspective Switch: Now, Participant B presents their perspective on the problem.
5. Position Acknowledgment: Participant A repeats Participant B's view, ensuring accurate understanding.
6. Deepening Understanding: Participant A asks Participant B clarifying questions about their perspective.
7. Joint Discussion. Engage in a dialogue to explore:
- Shared views and agreements.
- Differences in understanding the problem.
- Potential solutions or ideas.
- Agreed upon action steps or solutions.
- Unresolved issues and continuing points of contention.
8. Visual Mapping: Create a Problem Difference Chart detailing the key points of agreement and disagreement.
9. Solution Exploration: Discuss the differences charted to identify potential solutions.

# Critical Consensus Constructor.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Critical Consensus Constructor" thinking tool is to facilitate a process whereby a group can reach a decision collaboratively, with each member feeling committed to the outcome. This tool is designed to guide a team through the nuances of consensus-building, which is not about unanimous agreement but about creating collective decisions that everyone considers to be the best possible solution given the circumstances.

The tool emphasizes the importance of open dialogue, shared understanding, and the recognition of common objectives. It's structured to help teams navigate through differences, identify where they agree, find solutions to where they don't, and ultimately agree on a course of action that all feel invested in. This tool is particularly valuable in complex decision-making scenarios where buy-in from all team members is crucial for effective implementation.

The steps and corresponding actions in the tool ensure that every team member's voice is heard, that all relevant information is considered, and that the team moves forward together, even if the decision reached isn't each individual's preferred choice. The final consensus statement acts as a recorded agreement that reflects the collective will and commitment of the team.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Consensus is a highly interactive process used to develop team decisions with a high level of commitment. Consensus is not a way to obtain total agreement among team members. It may not represent everyone’s first choice of solutions, but every team member is convinced that the decision represents the "best" solution to the problem at hand.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
Consensus is about group interaction and discussion, not just following a set of ordered steps.

# Delphi Forecasts and Predictions.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Delphi Forecasts and Predictions" thinking tool is to harness the collective intelligence of experts to generate accurate forecasts and strategic plans for the future. The Delphi technique is especially useful when direct knowledge is dispersed and the subject matter is complex or involves uncertainty. It aims to achieve a convergence of opinion on future events or outcomes, relying on the iterative gathering of expert judgments.

The anonymity of participants reduces the influence of authority, reputation, or personality on the group's predictions, allowing for unbiased information exchange. The process encourages independent thinking and gradual consensus, taking advantage of diverse expert insights without the pressure of conformity or groupthink.

This tool is structured around multiple rounds of questionnaires, where experts provide their forecasts, assumptions, and reasoning. Their responses are collected, summarized, and shared with the group in subsequent rounds. This iterative feedback is designed to refine and evolve the group's forecasts towards a more accurate prediction, culminating in a final report that represents the collective judgment of the experts involved.

The multi-round approach, combined with the ability to revise opinions based on aggregated group responses, distinguishes the Delphi method from other forecasting techniques and contributes to its effectiveness in yielding reliable and nuanced insights into future scenarios.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
The Delphi technique is a method developed to improve group predictions by gathering the opinions of experts who do not know each other's identities. It's like a bigger version of brainstorming where experts share their thoughts on what might happen in the future. These thoughts are sent to a coordinator, who shares them with the group to refine the ideas and reach a common prediction.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
This technique involves asking experts for their opinions in several rounds of questioning. The key elements of this process are: keeping the experts anonymous if needed, showing their opinions in a way that uses numbers or statistics, and sharing the reasons behind their opinions with the group.

# Dynamic Feedback Mapper.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Dynamic Feedback Mapper" thinking tool is to systematically collect and utilize feedback to navigate and adapt to changes within systems or processes. It facilitates the identification of areas for improvement, helps prevent the recurrence of problems, and encourages the continuous enhancement of practices or operations.

This tool provides a structured approach for initiating and maintaining a productive dialogue around feedback from various sources, such as customers, employees, suppliers, and stakeholders. By addressing the types of feedback needed, assessing the effectiveness of solutions, and identifying gaps in feedback, the tool aims to refine strategies and actions that align with the organization's goals.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Feedback is a method used to adjust to changes in systems. This set of questions is designed to find ways to keep improving, stop problems from happening again, prevent new issues, and keep getting better over time.

The "Dynamic Feedback Mapper" guides users through:
- Determining the sources and types of feedback required.
- Evaluating the effectiveness of implemented solutions.
- Discovering areas where feedback is lacking and developing methods to obtain it.
- Deciding who should receive feedback and for what purpose.
- Measuring aspects of the operations that require feedback for improvement.
- Selecting the appropriate tools to gather feedback effectively.
- Leveraging existing systems as feedback mechanisms.
- Processing the received feedback to inform decision-making.
- Validating the feedback data for accuracy and relevance.
- Handling negative feedback constructively.

Moreover, it emphasizes individual feedback dynamics, promoting a culture where feedback is not only given and received effectively but also where open communication is encouraged, and honest feedback is valued. The tool ensures that feedback translates into actionable insights that drive progress and facilitate positive change.

# Constructive Contrarian Guide.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Constructive Contrarian Guide" thinking tool is to strengthen ideas and decision-making processes within a group by incorporating critical analysis and diverse perspectives. The tool leverages the role of a devil's advocate—someone who deliberately challenges the group's ideas—to ensure that a wide range of opinions are considered, which can lead to more robust, well-rounded, and innovative solutions.

Using this thinking tool involves selecting a respected and experienced member of the team to serve as the devil's advocate. Their task is to rigorously scrutinize proposals, encourage looking at issues from different angles, including those of competitors or dissenting viewpoints, and to foster an environment where critical examination is welcomed and valued. This contrarian role is not to criticize for the sake of opposition but to drive the group to consider potential pitfalls and hidden opportunities in their thinking.

The guide outlines a clear process for the devil's advocate to follow, from questioning the initial assumptions and ideas to challenging the working group to refine their proposals. It encourages a dynamic where feedback, even if perceived as negative, is used constructively to refine and enhance the group's output. This approach not only improves the end results but also contributes to a culture of continuous improvement and adaptive thinking.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Having someone in meetings to question and challenge ideas can make good ideas even better. This person, called a devil's advocate, helps consider different opinions and not just the usual or easy ones.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
Pick someone with a lot of experience and respect in the group for this role. Make sure everyone knows the devil's advocate is there to help by looking at all sides of an idea, even if it seems like they're just being critical. This helps improve ideas and avoid missing important points.


## The Actual Thinking Tool:

| Step |                                      Action                                     |
|======|=================================================================================|

|      | a. Challenge ideas to test group thinking.                                      |
|      | b. Think from competitors' perspectives for strategy.                           |
|------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    2 | Choose the devil's advocate:                                                    |
|      | a. One or two people as the devil's advocate.                                   |
|      | b. Split the team into a working group and a devil's advocate group.            |
|------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    3 | Working group outlines solutions, noting all underlying assumptions and ideas.  |
|------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

|      | Note: Challenges must be well-founded.                                          |
|------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    6 | Working group revises their proposals based on the devil's advocate's feedback. |
|------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    7 | Optionally switch roles if time allows and the issue is significant.            |
|======|=================================================================================|



# Collaborative Conversation Navigator.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Collaborative Conversation Navigator" is to enhance the group decision-making process through dialogue—a form of communication that prioritizes mutual understanding over persuasion or agreement. This tool is aimed at merging diverse viewpoints to discover overarching insights that may not be evident through conventional discussion methods.

The thinking tool is employed by first ensuring that all members are on the same page regarding the intention behind the dialogue—to learn from each other and synthesize collective wisdom. It involves selecting individuals willing to put aside their biases and listen genuinely to others' ideas. This kind of environment allows a group to develop a shared understanding that surpasses individual perspectives.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Dialogue is a way of talking where the goal isn't to win or agree, but to understand each other better. It combines different ideas to find a higher truth.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
The first goal is to make sure everyone feels free to speak their mind. This happens when people are ready to put aside their own strong opinions and are open to being influenced by what others say. When everyone is open in this way, the group can reach a new level of shared understanding.

The "Collaborative Conversation Navigator" consists of a series of actions:
1. Establish the use of dialogue within the meeting, pausing if the conversation drifts into argumentative territory.
2. Clearly define what constitutes dialogue and set ground rules that discourage disagreement for the sake of debate.
3. Shift focus from personal convictions to collective rightness, thus minimizing ego and fostering openness.
4. Lay bare any underlying personal agendas to ensure transparency.
5. Refrain from imposing personal views, allowing for unbiased exploration of ideas.
6. Encourage the open expression of beliefs without judgment to facilitate genuine dialogue.
7. Introduce moments of silence throughout the dialogue to allow for reflection on the discussion.
8. Encourage participants to be mindful of their biases, particularly when emotional responses are triggered.
9. Urge participants to share experiences without defensiveness, welcoming new insights.
10. Practice deep listening and resist any impulses to challenge others' perceptions, fostering an atmosphere where all views are seen as valuable contributions to understanding.

# Holistic Perspective Harmonizer.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Holistic Perspective Harmonizer" thinking tool is to enrich the understanding of a situation by embracing the diversity of viewpoints. Recognizing that individuals may see problems and opportunities through different lenses, this tool seeks to compile these varied perspectives to form a more complete picture. By doing so, it aims to facilitate a more effective and empathetic problem-solving approach.

The "Holistic Perspective Harmonizer" is used by actively soliciting the views and experiences of different stakeholders involved or affected by the issue at hand. This includes not only those responsible for resolving the problem but also those who experience its impact, those who are distant from the situation, and even those who may disagree with the prevailing opinion. ## Why Use This Thinking Tool:

This thinking tool simplifies problem-solving by focusing on tackling the problem bit by bit, starting with the most impactful parts. It also helps in realizing the amount of control or responsibility you have over the problem, encouraging a step-by-step approach to find a solution.

# Ease-Impact Assessment Matrix.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Ease-Impact Assessment Matrix" is to provide a quick and efficient way to evaluate and prioritize ideas or alternatives based on two critical factors: their impact and the ease of implementation. This thinking tool is especially valuable in scenarios where decision-makers need to focus on options that will have a significant effect while being feasible to execute. The tool's functionality can be broken down as follows:
1. Idea Organization: It starts with listing out all potential ideas or alternatives, encouraging a comprehensive view of available options.
2. Impact and Ease Scoring: Each idea is scored on a scale from 1 to 9, with higher scores indicating high impact and ease of implementation. This scoring system helps in objectively assessing the potential value and practicality of each idea.
3. Visual Prioritization: By plotting these ideas on a matrix, the tool visually distinguishes between high-value, easy-to-implement options and those that are less feasible or impactful. This aids in quickly identifying the "low hanging fruit" - ideas that offer the greatest impact with the least implementation challenges.
4. Strategic Focus: The matrix assists in directing resources and efforts towards ideas that are most likely to succeed and provide significant benefits, ensuring efficient use of time and resources.
5. Action Planning: Following the assessment, it provides a framework to develop actionable plans for the top-prioritized ideas, thereby facilitating their practical implementation.
In essence, the "Ease-Impact Assessment Matrix" is an indispensable tool for strategic decision-making, allowing teams and leaders to identify and act on the most promising and practical ideas swiftly.

# Prioritized Purpose Planner.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The "Prioritized Purpose Planner" is a thinking tool designed for structured problem-solving, especially in a workshop or collaborative setting. Its main purpose is to guide participants through a systematic process of identifying, prioritizing, and addressing the various reasons or purposes behind a problem. This thinking tool is designed to aid in the structured resolution of problems, particularly in a collaborative workshop environment. Its application is multifaceted:
1. Clarify the Problem: It helps participants clearly define and articulate the main problem they are facing, ensuring that everyone has a common understanding of the issue at hand.
2. Identify and Prioritize Reasons: By listing and prioritizing all the reasons or purposes for solving a problem, the tool encourages participants to consider a wider range of factors and motivators. This approach helps in identifying the most critical aspects that need addressing.
3. Develop Comprehensive Solutions: Understanding the full spectrum of reasons behind a problem allows for the development of more comprehensive and effective solutions. It ensures that proposed solutions are not just addressing superficial symptoms but are tackling the root causes.
4. Foster Collaboration and Engagement: The tool promotes collaborative thinking and discussion. By encouraging participants to contribute their perspectives and ideas, it fosters a sense of ownership and engagement in the problem-solving process.
5. Create Actionable Goals: The tool guides participants in formulating a clear, overarching goal based on identified purposes, as well as subsequent sub-goals or intermediate objectives. This step is crucial for turning abstract ideas into actionable plans.
6. Visual and Interactive Exploration: Features like the Vision Mapping Canvas and the Deep Dive Discussion Zone offer interactive and visual methods to explore the problem and solutions, enhancing creativity and engagement.
7. Facilitate Structured Problem-Solving: The structured approach of the tool, with its clearly defined sections and purposes, helps in methodically breaking down complex problems into manageable parts.
The goal is to find more effective, comprehensive solutions by understanding the full spectrum of purposes driving the need for a solution. Here's an overview of how to use the "Prioritized Purpose Planner":
1. Listing Reasons/Purposes: Participants use the tool to list all the reasons for solving the situation, starting each purpose with "To" followed by an action verb. This step helps to clearly articulate all the drivers behind the need for a solution.
2. Prioritize Needs: Once all the reasons or purposes are listed, they are organized and prioritized according to their importance and relevance to the problem at hand. This prioritization helps to focus efforts on the most critical aspects of the problem.
3. Top Purpose Expansion: The most important purpose is selected for further exploration using the Purposes Hierarchy Template. This step helps in identifying more detailed and specific purposes related to the main one, offering a deeper understanding of the problem.
4. Goal Statement Development: Based on the listed purposes, a goal statement is developed. This statement encapsulates the main objectives identified and serves as a guiding beacon for the problem-solving process.
5. Sub-Goal Formation: To support the main goal, 3-5 sub-goals or intermediate goals are created. These sub-goals are specific and actionable, ensuring that the primary goal is attainable and that progress can be measured.
Each section of the tool serves a specific purpose, from defining the problem and identifying needs to formulating actionable goals. This structured approach not only aids in tackling the problem more effectively but also ensures that all participants are aligned and working towards a common objective. The tool is particularly useful in settings where a clear and organized approach to problem-solving is needed, such as in business strategy sessions, team workshops, or collaborative project planning. In summary, the "Prioritized Purpose Planner" is a valuable asset in any problem-solving scenario, particularly in settings that require a thorough, collaborative approach to identifying and addressing issues. Its focus on understanding the underlying reasons for a problem and developing aligned goals makes it a powerful tool for effective decision-making and strategy development.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Clarifying all the reasons for solving a problem can lead to better solutions. Knowing the purposes makes the situation clearer and helps think of broader, more comprehensive ideas or solutions. Usually, we find what we're looking for. If we only look for a solution to one specific purpose, that's all we'll find. But if we consider multiple purposes, we're more likely to find solutions that address several objectives at once. Also, understanding all the reasons behind a problem increases the chances of tackling the actual issue. It's important to remember: People usually work harder and are more motivated by understanding the purposes behind a task, rather than just following rules or instructions.

# Critical Question Mapping.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Critical Question Mapping" thinking tool is to enhance effective thinking in problem-solving situations by identifying the most impactful or "perfect" questions. This approach uses the technique of "Question Brainstorming," which is similar to traditional brainstorming but focuses on generating questions rather than ideas. After brainstorming, the tool guides participants in categorizing, prioritizing, and analyzing these questions to determine the most critical ones for their specific situation. By doing so, it aids in uncovering key insights and solutions, steering the problem-solving process towards a more structured and comprehensive understanding of the issue at hand.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
To enhance effective thinking, it's essential to ask the right questions. The goal is to identify the "perfect" questions, but how can you achieve this? The technique of "Question Brainstorming" can help. This method is similar to traditional brainstorming but concentrates on generating questions instead of ideas. After listing all possible questions, choose the top 3-4. Then, apply a structured question hierarchy to these selected questions. This process aids in pinpointing the most impactful or "perfect" questions to ask for your specific situation.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
To effectively utilize this thinking tool in a facilitated problem-solving workshop, follow these steps:
1. Initial Engagement and Brainstorming:
- Introduce the tool's concept and explain the types of questions (e.g., Broad Scope Exploration, Original Question Identification).
- Conduct a brainstorming session focused on generating diverse questions related to the problem, without immediate judgment or dismissal.
- Encourage participants to ask as many questions as possible without seeking to answer them immediately.
- Document all questions without filtering.
2. Categorization of Questions:
- Once a substantial list of questions is generated, group the brainstormed questions into predefined categories for structured analysis as outlined in the thinking tool (e.g., Broad Scope Exploration, Narrow Scope Focus, etc.).
- Eliminate duplicates and streamline the process by focusing on unique, impactful questions.
- This helps in organizing the questions into meaningful groups for deeper analysis.
3. Prioritization Process:
- Use criteria like relevance, impact, and urgency to prioritize questions, focusing on those aligning with workshop goals.
- Facilitate in-depth discussions for each category to refine questions and identify actionable insights.
- From each category, select the top 3-4 questions that seem most relevant, impactful, or critical to addressing the core problem.
- Utilize the "Prioritization of Key Questions" table for this process.
- Employ criteria such as relevance, impact, and urgency to prioritize the questions.
- Focus on questions that align closely with the workshop's goals and objectives.
4. Selection of Key Questions:
- Utilize the "Prioritization of Key Questions" criteria to identify and agree upon the most critical questions, ensuring a consensus among participants.
- Organize these key questions in a hierarchical order, progressing from broad to specific topics.
5. Impact Analysis and Discussion:
- Perform an impact analysis on the selected questions to determine their potential influence on the problem-solving process, focusing on how answers to each question could yield specific outcomes or insights.
- Facilitate in-depth discussions for each type of question, using this stage not only to refine them but also to identify those most likely to lead to actionable insights.
6. Development of a Hierarchical Question Structure:
- Apply a structured hierarchy to the selected key questions, starting with broad topics and progressively narrowing down to more specific ones.
- This approach facilitates a deeper understanding, starting from general concepts and moving towards targeted insights, thereby uncovering root causes and potential solutions to the problem.
7. Identification of the "Perfect" Questions:
- After applying the hierarchical structure, identify the questions that are most critical to understanding and resolving the problem.
- These "perfect" questions should be those that, when answered, provide the most significant insights or solutions.
8. Action Plan for Answering Key Questions:
- Develop a comprehensive plan that details the steps and methodologies—like research, data collection, and expert consultation—necessary to address the key questions.
- Ensure the plan's alignment with the workshop's objectives and the problem-solving process, with clearly defined responsibilities and established timelines.
- Proceed with implementing the plan, diligently tracking progress, and systematically collecting information or insights that respond to the key questions.
9. Review and Iteration:
- Conduct regular reviews of the answers and insights obtained from the key questions, maintaining detailed records of discussions, decisions, and their impact on the problem-solving process.
- Stay flexible and ready to revise the questioning strategy as new information or changing circumstances arise, ensuring that the approach remains relevant and effective in addressing the problem-solving objectives.
10. Documentation:
- Keep a detailed record of all questions, discussions, and the rationale behind the selection of key questions.
- Document the insights and answers obtained, along with the impact on the problem-solving process.
11. Feedback and Closure:
- At the end of the workshop, consolidate feedback to evaluate the effectiveness of the questioning process.
- Engage in a discussion to understand how the insights derived from the key questions will shape future strategies and decision-making.
By adhering to these steps, the "Critical Question Mapping" tool offers a systematic and thorough approach to problem-solving. This method not only deepens understanding of the issue at hand but also guides participants towards actionable solutions. It combines comprehensive analysis with collaborative decision-making, ensuring that strategic planning is both efficient and effective through structured questioning.

# Perspective Pivot Workshop.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The "Perspective Pivot Workshop" is designed to ensure that the correct problem is being addressed in business problem-solving. It aims to break free from familiar thinking patterns and preconceived models by reframing the problem in various ways. The tool facilitates viewing problems from new angles and perspectives, similar to viewing a picture in a different frame, which can significantly change perceptions. This approach aligns with the idea that problems cannot be solved within the same framework in which they were created. The tool is especially beneficial in shifting mindsets and identifying core issues by looking at them through different lenses, thereby addressing the right challenges more effectively.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
This tool helps you ensure you're tackling the right problem. People often fall back on familiar thinking and try to fit new problems into old patterns. To break out of set patterns, "Reframe" your problem. Reframing means looking at your problem from a new angle or perspective. Think about it like looking at a picture in a different frame — it can change how you see it. This is based on the idea, often attributed to Albert Einstein, that you can't solve a problem by thinking about it in the same way it was created.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool: 
Reframe your problem in new ways to ensure you've correctly identified the core issue and are addressing the right challenge.

# FOCUS Ideation.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the FOCUS Ideation thinking tool is to facilitate effective problem-solving and strategic planning in complex, long-term projects. This tool is designed to help teams and individuals systematically address each aspect of a project or problem, ensuring that all elements are considered both individually and as part of the overall objective. The process involves:
1. Foundation: Assessing the current situation to understand the starting point.
2. Objective: Clearly defining the end goal or desired outcome of the project.
3. Constraints: Identifying and acknowledging obstacles that may impede progress.
4. Understanding: Gaining deeper insights into the challenges and overall context of the situation.
5. Strategy: Developing actionable ideas and plans to navigate through the challenges and achieve the objectives.
This structured approach allows for a comprehensive understanding of the situation, clear goal setting, identification of barriers, in-depth analysis, and strategic planning. By breaking down the process into these distinct areas, FOCUS Ideation enables teams to develop coordinated, effective strategies that align with the overarching goals of their projects.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
When dealing with complex, long-term projects, focusing on specific aspects one at a time is beneficial, while also keeping in mind how these aspects fit into the overall picture. This helps develop coordinated ideas that collectively work towards the overarching goal. The FOCUS technique uses brainstorming to help clarify, pinpoint, and develop ideas by addressing barriers to achieving the goal. This method organizes information into these distinct areas:
1. Foundation: Defines the current situation or starting point.
2. Objective: Outlines the ultimate goal or desired outcome.
3. Constraints: Identifies obstacles or hurdles in the way.
4. Understanding: Gathers insights and deeper understanding of the challenges and the overall situation.
5. Strategy: Proposes ideas and actions needed to overcome challenges and move towards the goal.


## How To Use This Thinking Tool:

# Holistic Systems Mapper.

## Purpose Of This Thinking Tool:
The purpose of the "Holistic Systems Mapper" thinking tool is to provide a comprehensive and structured approach to understanding complex systems and processes. It's designed to help users:
1. Identify and examine the central process of focus to gain a deep understanding of its intricacies.
2. Map direct interactions and assess immediate elements that influence the central process.
3. Evaluate related processes that indirectly impact the central focus area.
4. Assess the influence of external factors that shape the overall environment in which the process operates.
5. Analyze outcomes and feedback loops to understand the effectiveness of the process and areas for improvement.
6. Engage in hierarchical mapping to visualize the process within the broader context and identify dependencies.
7. Synthesize insights from group discussions to uncover patterns and potential innovations.
8. Develop an action plan to address identified issues and enhance the central process and its connected elements.
This tool is particularly useful for visualizing a process within its broader operational and strategic context, allowing for more effective planning and problem-solving. It's suitable for workshops and collaborative sessions where a diverse group of stakeholders can contribute their perspectives, leading to a more nuanced and holistic understanding of complex systems.


## Why Use This Thinking Tool: 
Getting a conceptual grasp of the entire picture is not an easy task. Often times we can get myopic due to our intense focus on our own domain of expertise. Having a specialty is important. But when solving problems, it's important to make sure the solutions we propose will be well integrated into the entire system as a whole. This thinking tool will help you keep the following perspectives in mind as you go about your problem-solving process.
1. Central Process: Identify the key process you're focusing on. For example, instead of "Manufacturing of smartphones," consider "Assembly of electronic devices."
2. Immediate Elements: Note elements directly interacting with your process. Replace "Suppliers of smartphone components" with "Providers of electronic parts."
3. Related Processes: Consider processes that are connected to but not directly a part of your central process. Swap "Quality control of smartphone manufacturing" for "Inspection of electronic assembly."
4. External Influences: Identify broader factors impacting your process. Instead of "Global demand for smartphones," think "Worldwide market trends for electronics."
5. Outcome and Feedback: Determine the results and feedback loops of your process. Change "Consumer satisfaction with smartphones" to "User satisfaction with electronic devices."
This approach helps you visualize your process within its broader system, aiding in comprehensive understanding and strategic planning.

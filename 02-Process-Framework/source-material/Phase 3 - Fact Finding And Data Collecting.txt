STEP 3 - FACT FINDING AND DATA COLLECTING.

There is a positive correlation between people & teams who develop more and better ideas/solutions and those who understand all the relevant facts about the situation. These questions will help you acquire the proper knowledge about your situation/problem.

Step 3.1 - Define The Set Of Current Facts And Data.

Analyzing and effectively resolving any situation is difficult without completely understanding all the "relevant" data. Generally, the more a problem is understood, the more influential the thinking will be. Be careful, however, of analysis paralysis, where you collect too much data and information.

Recommended Thinking Tools:

Journalistic Questions: Basic Who, What, When, Where, Why, How questions.

Prime Questions:
What do you know for sure about the problem or situation?
What factors have contributed most to the problem?
Do other people see the facts differently? How?
What key facts need to be known and are currently not known?
Can you go forward without knowing the past data?
What are the top 10 facts about this situation/problem?
Understand the data by asking Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How questions.
How has this problem evolved over time, and what trends can be identified?
What external factors, such as market trends or regulatory changes, are influencing this situation?
How do the perspectives of various stakeholders differ on the key issues of this problem?
What are the financial implications or costs associated with this problem?
What technological factors might be influencing or exacerbating this problem?
What are the potential future developments that could impact this situation?
What information is being overlooked or undervalued in understanding this problem?
What similar problems have been successfully resolved in the past, and what can be learned from them?
What industry insights or expert opinions can shed light on this problem?
How is this problem being reported or perceived in the media or public domain?
What are the demographic or psychographic factors that play a role in this problem?
How does this problem impact different segments of the market or customer base?
What are the legal or compliance aspects related to this problem?
What are the unseen or indirect effects of this problem on other business areas or functions?

Fundamental Questions:
Would it be a waste of time to know everything about this situation/problem?
What are the key assumptions that drive this problem or situation?
What are the key performance measurements behind this problem/situation?
What are vital opinions, interpretations, and mere guesses but not facts?
What facts do you assume to be accurate but are not?
What is assumed to be not true but is true?
What are all the reasons for collecting data?
When will the problem stop being a problem?
What is the principle underlying the problem?
What aspects of this situation require outside help?
What aspects of the problem are controllable but are not being controlled?
What must you accept about this situation?
What looks false here?
What internal resources (personnel, technology, finances) do we have, and how do they relate to this situation?
How is this problem perceived differently within various departments of the organization?
What previous attempts have been made to address this problem, and what were their outcomes?
What data or metrics are currently being used to measure the impact or severity of this problem?
What cultural or organizational factors are influencing the perception or impact of this problem?
Are there any misconceptions or myths surrounding this problem that need to be addressed?
What lessons can be learned from similar problems faced by other organizations or industries?
What historical organizational decisions or policies might have contributed to this problem?
What are the immediate and long-term financial costs of not solving this problem?
How might this problem affect employee morale, productivity, or turnover?
What are the technological constraints or opportunities related to solving this problem?
How could current market trends or consumer behaviors influence the resolution of this problem?
What are the potential supply chain or logistical challenges associated with this problem?
What is the impact of this problem on our business partnerships and collaborations?

Nutcracker Questions:

What baseline data needs to be reviewed and understood?
What biases may be distorting the facts?
What is not known about the situation that needs to be known?
What would a deep dive into historical data reveal about the origins and progression of this problem?

How might emerging technologies or future societal changes affect the dynamics of this problem?

What innovative or disruptive business models could emerge from solving this problem?
How might resolving this problem influence our organization's role in the community or society at large?
What new business opportunities or markets could be created by solving this problem?

Step 3.2 - Define The Set Of Past Facts And Data.

Past or "transient" data is the data that describes the changes that occurred that led to the current problem/situation. Knowing this type of data helps identify the driving forces affecting the current situation and possible solutions.

Recommended Thinking Tools:

Causal Chain: Determine where solutions can best be applied.

Prime Questions:
What change was made just before the problem started?
Who left the situation or was brought in just before things changed?
What things are being done differently now than they were done before?
What successful actions stopped or dropped off?
Where have new or revised policies or procedures been put in place?
Where have priorities shifted?
How have customer feedback or expectations changed over time leading up to this problem?
What changes in the market or industry trends preceded the onset of this problem?
What internal or external events coincided with the beginning of this problem?
Were there any significant technological updates or changes before the problem emerged?
How has the organizational structure or leadership changed prior to this problem?
What were the financial conditions of the organization leading up to this situation?
Have there been changes in regulatory or compliance environments that may have influenced this situation?
What were the key performance indicators before the problem, and how have they changed?
What customer or stakeholder feedback was received prior to the problem, and how did it shift?
How have employee roles or responsibilities evolved leading up to this problem?
Were any new technologies or systems implemented that could have influenced the situation?
How did the competitive landscape look before this problem arose?
Were there any significant policy or regulatory changes in the industry before the problem occurred?
What were the market conditions like, and how might they have contributed to the situation?

Fundamental Questions:
Have changes occurred that people have not been adjusted to?
Is there anything that makes the problem temporarily disappear?
Can you replicate the situation to get the data needed?
How has the workload shifted? Are priorities shifting?
Are orders coming from different directions?
Is there a new vendor?
When does a current situation happen?
When did the situation first start?
When is the situation triggered?
How pervasive or widespread is it?
How quickly is the problem spreading or developing?
What historical successes or failures in the organization are similar to this problem?
Has there been a shift in employee skills, morale, or engagement levels prior to this problem?
What changes in processes or workflows have occurred that might have led to this problem?
Have there been any significant shifts in supplier or partner relationships?
What were the communication patterns like before the problem, and how have they changed?
Were there any warning signs or minor issues that were overlooked before the problem became significant?
What were the prevailing business strategies or goals before this problem arose?
What were the resource allocations before the problem, and how have they changed?
How has communication within the organization altered leading up to this issue?
Were there any changes in customer demographics or behaviors before the problem?
What were the staff training and development scenarios prior to the issue?
Were there any changes in supplier quality or delivery times that could have impacted the situation?
How did the company's marketing strategies or campaigns change prior to the problem?
What technology or process inefficiencies were present before the problem emerged?

Nutcracker Questions:
Is there any evidence of any cycles or patterns?
Are conflicting orders coming from different directions?

What standard or norm has been violated?
What recurring challenges or issues in the past might have contributed to this problem?

Were there any cultural shifts within the organization or its environment that precipitated this issue?
How did past financial decisions or budget allocations contribute to the current situation?
What were the patterns in employee turnover or satisfaction rates before the issue, and how might they be related?
How have past successes or failures in innovation or adaptation influenced the current problem?

Step 3.3 - Define The Set Of Facts And Data Related To The System.

Typically, most of us apply analytical thinking habits that consider problems in isolation; however, problems and opportunities generally do not exist in isolation. These questions help provide a systems understanding to better ensure you are developing whole solutions and not causing damage to another part of the system.

Recommended Thinking Tools:

System Hierarchy: Identify the system in which the problem resides. 

Prime Questions:
What system surrounds the problem or situation?
How does the problem/situation interact with everything around it?
What does the problem indicate about the system?
What other problems or situations are interconnected with this one?
How can the system be changed so the problem conditions don't arise?
Might resolving this situation hurt the system?
What historical decisions or events have shaped the current system in which the problem exists?
How does the problem reflect the overall health or effectiveness of the system?
What feedback mechanisms are currently in place within the system, and how do they affect the problem?
How do external factors like market trends, regulations, or technological advancements impact the system?
What are the resilience and adaptability features of the system in response to problems like this?
In what ways could solving this problem potentially create new opportunities within the system?
How does the current problem align with the long-term goals or vision of the system?
What are the external environmental factors influencing the system?
How does the current problem correlate with the overall lifecycle of the system?
What are the unintended consequences of previous solutions within this system?
How does this problem interact with the system’s risk management processes?
What role does organizational culture play within this system concerning the problem?
What are the dependencies within the system that could impact problem-solving?
How have changes in stakeholder expectations influenced the evolution of the system?

Fundamental Questions:
What are all the relevant system components or parts?
How does this situation/problem interact with other processes? Customers? Systems? Other problems?
What minor problems add up to this one or make it worse?
What is the purpose of the system and its components? What better purpose should they have?
Where does this situation fit in the more extensive system?
How is the problem area a sub-system?
Can solving this problem be used to solve other problems?
What are the direct and indirect costs associated with this problem within the system?
How do different stakeholders within the system perceive this problem?
What are the current constraints or limitations of the system that affect the problem?
How do communication and information flow within the system contribute to the problem?
What role do leadership and management styles play in the system relative to this problem?
How does this problem impact resource allocation and prioritization within the system?
What are the short-term and long-term effects of this problem on the system's sustainability?
What are the knowledge gaps within the system that contribute to the problem?
How do internal policies and procedures within the system impact the problem?
What are the resource flow and allocation patterns within the system?
How does the problem impact employee roles and responsibilities within the system?
What are the existing communication channels in the system, and how do they affect the problem?
How has the technology used within the system contributed to or mitigated the problem?
What are the training and development processes within the system, and how do they relate to the problem?

Nutcracker Questions:
From what viewpoint can the problem's relationship to the whole be observed?
What are the system boundaries (inputs and outputs) of the system that surround this problem?
Does this problem situation indicate a need for a system change?
How does the input/output of the problem relate to the input/output of the system?
What systemic patterns or trends can be identified that have contributed to this problem?
How might changing one part of the system unexpectedly impact other parts?
Is there a possibility that this problem is symptomatic of a more significant systemic issue?
How might solving this problem influence the system's evolution or transformation?
What are the potential ripple effects of various solutions to this problem throughout the system?
How could the system be restructured or redesigned to prevent similar problems in the future?
What are the ethical and social implications of the problem and potential solutions within the system?
How do power dynamics within the system influence the problem and its potential solutions?
What are the long-term implications of the problem for the sustainability of the system?
How does the problem reflect broader societal or industry trends impacting the system?
What are the potential challenges in aligning the system’s goals with the problem resolution?
How could resolving the problem impact the system's competitive advantage?
What strategic shifts might be necessary within the system to address the problem effectively?
What are the potential ethical dilemmas posed by the problem within the context of the system?

Step 3.4 - Define The Set Of Future Facts And Data.

This question set helps predict what the facts will look like in the desired or ideal future state. This type of data helps expand thinking and develop better innovative solutions.

Recommended Thinking Tools:

Futures Thinking: Anticipate and plan for the future.

Prime Questions:
What will current data/facts look like once the situation/problem is resolved?
What are the facts, as they most likely will exist in the future?
What will current data/facts look like in an ideal (desired) future state?
What current data/facts might not exist in the future?
What facts/data might exist in the future that do not exist today?
What changes do you need to make today to reach the ideal future?
How will customer/client needs and expectations evolve in the ideal future state?
What technological advancements are expected, and how will they impact the future data/facts?
How will the resolution of this problem influence the market or industry trends in the future?
What will the competitive landscape look like in the ideal future?
How will employee roles, skills, and work culture evolve in the future state?
What new types of collaborations or partnerships might emerge in the ideal future?
How will regulatory and compliance landscapes change, and how will this affect future facts/data?
How will changes in consumer lifestyles and behaviors impact the future data/facts?
What new market segments or customer demographics could emerge in the future?
How might the company's product or service offerings evolve in the ideal future?
What role will digital transformation play in shaping the future state of the organization?
How will global environmental changes influence the business and its future strategies?
What new forms of data or metrics will become important in the future state?
How will evolving societal values and norms be reflected in future business practices and data?

Fundamental Questions:
What are the primary reasons for creating the ideal future state?
Does the desired state really solve the problem or hide it?
What will be the advantages of the ideal future?
What are the strengths that will help create the ideal future?
What worries you most about reaching the ideal future?
What things are done well today that should be part of the ideal state?
What things are done poorly today that should not be part of the ideal state?
What opportunities are envisioned to be in the ideal future?
What trends are driving future opportunities? What if the trend stops?
What trends might connect with each other and influence the future?
What are the three biggest challenges to reaching the ideal future state?
What resources will be required to transition to the ideal future state?
What internal processes or policies need to change to achieve the ideal future?
How will the organization's mission and values be reflected in the future state?
What sustainable practices will be integrated into the ideal future?
How will the ideal future impact the organization's social and environmental footprint?
What kind of training or development will be necessary for employees to thrive in the future state?
What are the potential economic impacts of the ideal future on the organization?
What are the anticipated shifts in employee expectations and work-life balance in the future?
How will the organization's supply chain and logistics evolve in the future state?
What changes in leadership and governance structures are anticipated in the ideal future?
How might the organization's brand and reputation be perceived in the future?
What role will automation and AI play in the organization's future operations?
What are the expected changes in regulatory landscapes and how will they impact the organization?
What are the implications of future technological innovations on the organization's business model?

Nutcracker Questions:
If you could create the ideal future state, what would be happening? What would the data/facts look like?
What threats could destroy the ideal future? What is their probability of happening?
Who or what could be working against an ideal future? Why?
How will changes in global economic and political landscapes affect the ideal future?
What are the potential barriers to innovation that might hinder reaching the ideal future?
How can the organization adapt to unforeseen challenges or disruptions in achieving the ideal future?
What ethical considerations need to be addressed in pursuit of the ideal future?
How will the ideal future align with the evolving needs and values of society?
What role will emerging technologies play in shaping the ideal future state?
How will the organization maintain its relevance and leadership in the ideal future scenario?
What potential global conflicts or geopolitical shifts could impact the ideal future state?
How might advancements in science and technology redefine the organization's industry?
What are the potential ethical dilemmas the organization might face in the future state?
How could the organization's ideal future state influence or shape industry standards and practices?
What long-term legacy would the organization aim to create in this ideal future?
How will changing demographics and population trends affect the organization in the future?
What are the potential scenarios where the organization's future plans could be disrupted or challenged?

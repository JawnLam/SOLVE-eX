STEP 2 - DEFINE THE GOAL STATE.

Goal setting is generally a prerequisite for success. Without a clear goal, people will likely go down the wrong thinking path and develop ineffective solutions. Use these questions to develop a goal statement that describes what the situation/problem will look like after it has been resolved.

Step 2.1 - Articulate the reasons for resolving the problem.

Understanding the reasons and purposes for wanting to resolve the problem or situation helps expand thinking to produce all-encompassing ideas/solutions. Knowing the purpose is a significant motivator, and people should be encouraged to continually ask, "What is the purpose for wanting to resolve this situation?"


Recommended Thinking Tools:
Purposes & Goals: Helps produce all-encompassing ideas/solutions.

Prime Questions:
What are the main reasons/purposes for wanting to resolve this situation?
What is the ideal purpose that would make everyone happy?
What are the organizational, group, and personal reasons for resolving this situation?
What are the customer's purposes and their customer's purposes?
What are the intangible reasons for wanting to solve this situation?
What are the purposes of others who want to resolve this situation?
Are any purposes related to other projects or activities?
How does resolving this situation align with the organization's long-term vision and strategic direction?
In what ways could resolving this situation redefine our approach to similar challenges in the future?
How can we ensure that the resolution of this situation is sustainable and adaptable to future changes?
What would be the broader societal or industry impact of successfully resolving this situation?
How does this situation present an opportunity for innovation or transformation within our organization?
What lessons learned from this situation can be applied to other aspects of our operations or strategy?
How can resolving this situation contribute to strengthening our organizational culture and values?
How does solving this problem advance our competitive edge or market position?
What are the potential benefits to employee morale and engagement from resolving this situation?
How might resolving this issue enhance our reputation for corporate social responsibility?
What new partnerships or collaborations could emerge from solving this problem?
In what ways could solving this problem contribute to industry-wide best practices or standards?
What would be the environmental impact of successfully resolving this situation?
How could this resolution potentially shape future policies or regulations in our industry?

Fundamental Questions:
How would you or your team prioritize the purposes from most important to least?
Can you group more minor purposes into a larger purpose?
What is the larger purpose of this purpose?
Do you really know, and have you listed all the purposes for resolving this problem?
What if you question the assumptions underlying the purposes?
What will the problem or situation look like when it is resolved?
What larger purpose might eliminate the need to achieve this more minor purpose?
Who else can be asked to develop more purposes?
What larger purpose has caused this situation to come into being?
Do all team members understand the reasons for needing to resolve this situation?
What specific, measurable outcomes would indicate that the situation has been resolved successfully?
How will the resolution of this situation affect our internal processes and team dynamics?
What risks or potential trade-offs should be considered in pursuing the resolution of this situation?
How can we ensure that the resolution is inclusive and considers the needs of all stakeholders?
What timeline is realistic for achieving the goals associated with resolving this situation?
How will we communicate the progress and outcomes of this resolution process to relevant parties?
What benchmarks or milestones can be established to track progress toward resolving this situation?
What are the financial implications (cost savings, revenue generation) of resolving this problem?
How will resolving this issue improve customer satisfaction and loyalty?
What impact would solving this problem have on our supply chain or logistical operations?
How might this resolution foster innovation in our products or services?
What are the potential risks to data security or privacy in resolving this problem?
How can resolving this problem support the professional development and skill-building of our team?
What changes in consumer behavior or market trends make resolving this problem timely and relevant?

Nutcracker Questions:
Expand the purposes as far as possible by asking three times, "What is the larger intent of this purpose?"
Are there contrary or crossing purposes or goals for resolving this situation?
What standard are you trying to reach by resolving this problem?
Is there a clear, realistic understanding of what needs to be accomplished?
How does the resolution of this situation align with or challenge current industry norms and practices?
What would be the long-term consequences, both positive and negative, of resolving this situation?
How could the resolution of this situation potentially disrupt current market dynamics or consumer expectations?
What ethical considerations must be taken into account in defining the goal state for this situation?
How does this situation offer an opportunity to rethink or challenge prevailing assumptions or beliefs within the organization?
What would be the ideal legacy of resolving this situation for the organization and its stakeholders?
How can we frame the goal to inspire innovation and encourage a break from traditional thinking patterns?
How might this resolution influence our long-term business model or strategy?
What are the potential risks of innovative or unconventional solutions to this problem?
How could solving this problem challenge or disrupt established norms or hierarchies within the organization?
What are the broader geopolitical or economic factors that might impact or be impacted by this resolution?
How might this resolution contribute to the larger discourse on sustainability or ethical business practices?
What would be the implications for our organization's agility and adaptability in a rapidly changing market?
How could this situation be leveraged to foster a more inclusive and diverse organizational culture?

Step 2.2 - Write the goal statement.

The goal statement represents a final outcome. It describes what the problem or situation will look like after its resolution. A clear goal statement increases the probability of developing appropriate solutions and ideas.


Recommended Thinking Tools:
Group Goal Setting: Helps teams reach a common goal.

Prime Questions:
How would a goal statement be written in 15 words or less?
What is the purpose of reaching this goal?
What will success look like once the goal has been reached?
Should the goal be quantified? Ask questions like how much or how many.
Consider starting the goal statement with the words, How can I/We… or How will…
Does the goal statement describe the ideal scene you are trying to achieve?
How can the goal statement be restated to make it clearer?
How does this goal align with the broader mission and values of the organization?
What innovative approaches or perspectives does this goal encourage?
How does the goal address the situation's symptoms and root causes?
What stakeholder needs and expectations does this goal encompass?
How does this goal foster organizational growth or learning?
What are the key metrics or indicators that will signify the achievement of this goal?
How does this goal balance ambition with feasibility?
What specific behaviors or actions will indicate progress towards achieving this goal?
How will achieving this goal improve our service or product quality?
What unique aspect of our organization can be leveraged to achieve this goal?
How can this goal inspire innovation or creative problem-solving within the team?
What collaborative opportunities might emerge as a result of working towards this goal?
How will this goal enhance our adaptability to future market changes or challenges?
What new skills or knowledge will the team acquire in the process of achieving this goal?

Fundamental Questions:
How might others involved state the goal?
Will everyone who reads the goal statement have the same understanding?
Will accomplishing the goal solve, in part or whole, the stated problems?
Do most involved parties agree with the goal?
Is the scope of the goal limited enough so that it can be reached?
Is the goal set too low? Should it be set higher?
Is the goal a portion of a larger goal?
Is the goal too broad to scatter ideas around?
Is the goal so vague or so far ahead that it is impossible?
What objectives should be set to mark progress to the goal?
Does everyone intuitively feel the goal is attainable?
What are the potential obstacles or challenges in achieving this goal, and how can they be overcome?
How does this goal integrate with other ongoing initiatives or projects?
What resources (time, personnel, budget) are required to achieve this goal?
How can the progress toward this goal be monitored and evaluated?
What are the incremental milestones that indicate progress toward this goal?
How can the goal be communicated effectively to ensure alignment and buy-in from all involved parties?
What contingency plans should be in place if the goal encounters unforeseen challenges?
What immediate impacts will be noticeable once we start making progress towards this goal?
How can we align this goal with the individual goals and motivations of team members?
What are the measurable economic impacts of achieving this goal?
How can this goal foster stronger relationships with our stakeholders?
What smaller, interim goals can be set to maintain momentum and motivation?
How can technology be utilized to facilitate the achievement of this goal?
What feedback mechanisms are necessary to ensure ongoing alignment with this goal?

Nutcracker Questions:
Does the goal create a short-term gain but a long-term loss?
Is the goal within the control and ability of those who need to reach it?
Are you sure this is the final core goal, not just an intermediate objective?
To ensure you have reached the core goal, ask the question, "What is the purpose of reaching the goal?" five consecutive times.
What long-term changes or transformations does this goal aim to achieve in the organization or industry?
How does this goal challenge existing assumptions or paradigms within the organization?
What are the broader societal, environmental, or ethical implications of achieving this goal?
How might achieving this goal change the competitive landscape or market dynamics?
What potential unintended consequences should be considered when pursuing this goal?
How does this goal contribute to the personal and professional development of those involved in its achievement?
What would be the historical significance or legacy of achieving this goal for the organization?
How might this goal influence our business model or strategy in the long term?
What are the risks of being too ambitious or too conservative in setting this goal?
How can we ensure this goal remains relevant amidst rapid industry or technological changes?
What are the ethical responsibilities we must consider in pursuit of this goal?
How can this goal serve as a catalyst for broader organizational change or cultural shift?
What legacy will the achievement of this goal create for future generations within the organization?
How can we ensure that the pursuit of this goal is inclusive and considers diverse perspectives and needs?

Step 2.3 - Establish the requirements.

Documenting requirements (outcomes or deliverables) is probably one of the most overlooked steps in thinking. It is difficult or impossible to develop good ideas or solutions if you do not have some agreement on the end requirements. Use these questions to identify requirements that "must" be included in any proposed idea/solution.


Recommended Thinking Tools:
Idea Requirements: Requirements to help focus creative thinking.

Prime Questions:
What are all the primary requirements that must be included in any proposed idea or solution?
What is the most essential requirement?
What requirements would be considered a want and not a need?
Are there any legal requirements that must be met?
Is there anything that must not be included in any idea/solution?
Who are all the stakeholders? Have their requirements been considered?
How do these requirements align with the long-term strategic objectives of the organization?
What impact will each requirement have on the overall success of the proposed solution?
Are there any conflicting requirements among different stakeholders, and how can they be reconciled?
What are the potential risks or challenges associated with each requirement?
How do these requirements cater to customers' or clients' evolving needs and expectations?
What benchmarks or standards need to be met by these requirements?
How can we ensure that the requirements are adaptable to future changes in the business environment?
How do these requirements support innovation and creative problem-solving within the organization?
What are the environmental impacts of these requirements, and how can they be mitigated?
How do these requirements align with the organization's values of diversity and inclusion?
What are the implications of these requirements for long-term customer relationships and loyalty?
How might these requirements influence the organization's competitive advantage in the market?
What are the implications of these requirements on employee well-being and job satisfaction?
How will these requirements be measured and tracked to ensure they are being met effectively?

Fundamental Questions:
Who needs to participate in requirements development?
Who approves requirements?
Are there any individual requirements that must be included?
Have all parties agreed and signed off on all the requirements?
Is each requirement stated clearly so everyone will have the same understanding?
Is each requirement stated clearly so everyone will have the same understanding?
Are resources available to work on this situation?
What would prohibit working on this situation?
Is it too early to begin working on this situation?
Is there anything unethical or wrong here?
Any special interest requirements?
What is the process for revising or updating requirements as new information becomes available?
How will the requirements be communicated to all involved parties to ensure clarity and alignment?
What measures are in place to ensure that all requirements are feasible within the given constraints?
How will compliance with legal and ethical standards be ensured in these requirements?
What methods will be used to validate that each requirement has been successfully met?
What feedback mechanisms are in place to assess the effectiveness of meeting these requirements?
What contingency plans are in place for requirements that may be challenging to fulfill?
What internal or external resources are needed to meet these requirements, and how will they be secured?
How will changes in the market or industry trends affect the relevance of these requirements?
What are the potential legal or compliance risks associated with these requirements?
How can these requirements be communicated to ensure universal understanding across departments and teams?
What training or skill development will be necessary to meet these requirements?
How will these requirements be integrated into existing workflows or processes?
What is the process for gathering and incorporating feedback on these requirements from relevant stakeholders?


Nutcracker Questions:
Have all stated goals, purposes, and outcomes been reflected in the requirements?
Are you sure there is no "gold plating" or planning to deal with more requirements than needed?
How will you handle requirements scope creep (a requirement not linked with a need)?
How do these requirements contribute to a sustainable and socially responsible solution?
Are there any overlooked requirements that could lead to significant consequences if not addressed?
How will conflicting or competing requirements be prioritized and managed?
What innovative approaches can be applied to meet complex or challenging requirements?
How do the requirements address potential future scenarios or market changes?
What is the process for addressing requirements that may become obsolete or irrelevant?
How will the requirements be adapted to accommodate technological advancements or digital transformations?
How might addressing these requirements lead to transformative change within the organization or industry?
What are the potential ethical dilemmas or conflicts that could arise from these requirements?
How can these requirements foster long-term sustainability and corporate social responsibility?
What are the broader societal impacts of meeting these requirements, and how can they be addressed?
How do these requirements challenge conventional thinking or practices within the organization?
What are the potential global implications or impacts of these requirements?
How will these requirements be evaluated and adjusted in response to future technological advancements and innovations?

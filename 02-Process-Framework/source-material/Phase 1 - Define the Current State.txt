# PHASE 1 - ARTICULATE THE CURRENT STATE.


## Introduction. 
Thought processes are influenced by definitions. People frequently start solving problems too quickly without properly identifying the actual problem or the appropriate solution. This leads to the challenge of attempting to solve the right problem but ending up creating an excellent solution for an incorrect problem.

Effective problem-solving in a business context is deeply rooted in understanding and accurately defining problems. It's common for teams to rush into solution mode without thoroughly analyzing the core issue, leading to misapplied efforts and resources. To enhance problem-solving effectiveness, it's crucial to focus on the problem-definition phase. This involves:
Step 1.1 - Describe the Situation: Begin by thoroughly describing the situation where the problem exists. This description should include all relevant details, context, and any initial observations. It's essential to be as objective and comprehensive as possible, avoiding assumptions or biases. This step sets the foundation for a clear understanding of the environment in which the problem exists.
Step 1.2 - Write the Problem Statement: Once the situation is clearly described, articulate the problem statement. This statement should be concise and specific, clearly defining what the problem is without suggesting a solution. A well-crafted problem statement should provide clarity and focus, guiding the solution development process and ensuring that efforts are well-directed.
Step 1.3 - Frame the Situation: After defining the problem, frame the situation in a broader context. This involves understanding the implications of the problem, its significance, and how it affects different aspects of the business. Consider the impact on stakeholders, business processes, and long-term objectives. Framing the situation helps in prioritizing the problem and aligning the solution with broader business goals.

By methodically working through these steps, business managers can approach problem-solving with a structured and effective mindset. This approach not only ensures that the real issues are addressed but also paves the way for more innovative and relevant solutions, ultimately leading to better decision-making and organizational success.


## Step 1.1 - Describe the situation.

### Intro.
Employ facilitator questions to create a brief background description of the present situation or problem. This description aids in comprehending the characteristics, extent, and possible effects of the situation. It should highlight the unique aspects of the situation to prevent relying on generic solutions.

When addressing complex business problems, it's crucial to have a clear understanding of the situation at hand. A structured approach to defining the problem can lead to more effective and tailored solutions. This can be achieved by using facilitator questions to guide the writing of a concise background description. The process involves the following steps:
1. Identify Key Facets of the Situation: Use facilitator questions to uncover the essential elements of the current situation or problem. Questions should explore the who, what, when, where, why, and how of the situation. This helps in gaining a comprehensive understanding of the problem.
2. Assess the Scope and Impact: Evaluate the extent of the problem and its potential impact on different areas of the business. Understand how it affects various stakeholders, processes, and the organization's goals. This assessment helps in prioritizing the issue and planning an appropriate response.
3. Highlight Uniqueness: Focus on identifying what makes this situation unique. Understanding the specific characteristics that differentiate this problem from others prevents falling into the trap of applying generic, 'one-size-fits-all' solutions. This encourages more creative and effective problem-solving.
4. Write a Concise Background Description: Based on the insights gained, write a brief background description of the problem. This description should be clear, focused, and encompass all critical aspects identified. It serves as a reference point for the problem-solving process, ensuring that all discussions and solutions are relevant and targeted.
5. Encourage Broad Participation: In a workshop setting, encourage participation from all attendees. Diverse perspectives can add depth to the problem understanding and contribute to more innovative solutions.

This approach ensures that the problem is thoroughly understood before jumping into solutions. By focusing on a clear, concise background description shaped by facilitator questions, managers can approach problem-solving with a more strategic and effective mindset, leading to solutions that are both innovative and closely aligned with the unique aspects of the problem.

Step 1.2 - Write the problem statement.

Developing a good problem statement may be the most crucial task in problem-solving. A correctly and clearly stated problem focuses people’s thinking, energies, and actions. A precise statement enhances proper diagnosis and resolution. A vague statement has people thinking all over the place.

In a problem-solving workshop for business managers, understanding the significance of a well-crafted problem statement is crucial. The process involves:

1. Clarity and Precision: Emphasize the need for clarity and precision in defining the problem. A clear problem statement guides managers to focus their thinking and directs their efforts efficiently. It prevents ambiguity and misdirection in the problem-solving process.
2. Influence on Problem-Solving Process: Highlight how a well-defined problem statement impacts the entire problem-solving cycle. It ensures that energies and resources are channeled appropriately, leading to a more effective diagnosis of the issue and the development of suitable solutions.
3. Techniques for Developing Problem Statements: Teach techniques to formulate effective problem statements. This includes breaking down the problem into its core components, using specific and unambiguous language, and avoiding technical jargon that might confuse the understanding.
4. Examples and Practice: Provide real-world examples of both good and poor problem statements. Encourage participants to practice rewriting vague statements into clear and concise ones, enhancing their skills in problem identification.
5. Impact of Vague Statements: Discuss the consequences of vague problem statements, such as misdirected efforts, wasted resources, and inefficient problem-solving. This helps managers understand the importance of precision in problem formulation.
6. Feedback and Collaboration: Encourage feedback and collaboration in developing problem statements. A collective approach can help ensure that different perspectives are considered, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of the problem.
By focusing on these aspects are part of learning the craft of effective problem statements. This is a skill that is fundamental to successful problem-solving in any organizational context. This approach ensures that the problem is understood accurately, leading to targeted and effective solutions.

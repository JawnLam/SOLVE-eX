# Phase 1.3 — Frame the Situation

For Step 1.3: making the framing explicit. ("One way to look at this is…")

**See also:**
- `phase-1-1-describe-situation.md` — the description these frames operate on.
- `by-clarification-need/clarification-on-origin.md` — frame ambiguity often is origin ambiguity in disguise.
- `meta-questions/asking-the-right-question.md` — when the frame question itself needs more refinement.
- `phase-2-2-write-goal-statement.md` — natural next-step once the frame settles.

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_OPENING_QUESTIONS -->
- What's the lens you've been viewing this through?
- "One way to look at this is…" — finish that sentence.
- What kind of problem do you think this is — a values problem, a logistics problem, a relational problem, something else?
- What's the metaphor that fits the situation, if any?
- What's the frame you keep returning to when you think about this?
- What category of decision is this in your mind?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_CLARIFYING_QUESTIONS -->
- Is there a different frame that fits the same facts?
- What's the frame that other people in your life seem to use about this?
- What's the frame you wish you could use but can't quite?
- What's a frame from another domain (work, sport, family, history) that maps onto this?
- What's the implicit narrative you're operating in — hero, victim, observer, deciding agent?
- Who set the frame you're currently using? You, someone else, circumstance?
- What's the time frame you're operating in (immediate, weeks, years, lifetime)?
- What's the unit of analysis — you, a relationship, a system, a moment?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_STRESS_TEST_QUESTIONS -->
- What does the current frame let you see clearly?
- What does the current frame hide from view?
- If the frame is wrong, what would the truer frame look like?
- Is your frame about the right problem, or the right symptom?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_REFRAMING_QUESTIONS -->
- Try the same situation from the opposite frame — what do you see?
- If this were a problem of identity, what would change in the framing?
- If this were a problem of timing, what would change?
- If this were a problem of resources, what would change?
- If this were a problem of permission, what would change?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_FRAME_INHERITANCE_PROBES -->
- Where did this frame come from — your training, your family, your industry, somewhere else?
- What's the frame your past self would have used here, and how does it differ?
- What's the frame someone five years older than you in this position would use?
- What's the frame the most cynical person you respect would use?
- What's the frame the most optimistic person you respect would use?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_FRAME_FIT_DIAGNOSTICS -->
- Does the frame match the size of the question, or is the frame too big / too small?
- Does the frame match the timescale, or is it borrowed from a different timescale?
- Is the frame load-bearing, or is it decorative?
- What predictions does the frame make that you could test in the next 30 days?
- Where in the data does the frame already fit awkwardly?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_1_3_CLOSING_QUESTIONS -->
- Are you settled on the frame, or do you want to hold it loosely for now?
- Is the frame solid enough to move into goal-setting?
- What's the frame you're committing to for now, even if you revise later?
- Anything about the frame that you'd want to revisit later?
<!-- /SECTION -->

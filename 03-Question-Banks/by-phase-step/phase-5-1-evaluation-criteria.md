# Phase 5.1 — Define Evaluation Criteria

For Step 5.1: the criteria the user will judge options against.

**See also:**
- `meta-questions/probing-questions.md` — when a stated criterion is thin and needs depth ("what does *important* actually mean here?").
- `by-clarification-need/clarification-on-path.md` — when the criteria conversation reveals confusion about *what would constitute a good outcome*.
- `phase-5-2-score-options.md` — natural next-step once criteria are named; applying them to the option set.
- `phase-2-1-articulate-reasons.md` — back-reference when the criteria surface unstated reasons the user actually cares about.
- `phase-2-2-write-goal-statement.md` — back-reference when criteria reveal the goal statement needs sharpening.

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_5_1_OPENING_QUESTIONS -->
- What matters most when evaluating these options?
- What are your top three criteria?
- What's the one criterion that, if violated, rules out an option? (the disqualifying criterion — see Binary Criteria Screen pattern)
- What's a criterion that's easy to measure?
- What's a criterion that's important but hard to measure?
- What's the criterion you most often forget to apply?
- If you had to explain to a friend why one option is better than another, what would you point to first?
- What does "the right call here" feel like — what makes it the right call?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_5_1_CLARIFYING_QUESTIONS -->
- Are the criteria yours, or someone else's?
- How would you weight the criteria — are they equal, or is one or two more important?
- Which criteria measure outcome (what happens), and which measure process (how it happens)?
- Are there criteria about fit-with-values that are separate from fit-with-goals?
- What's a criterion related to reversibility?
- What's a criterion related to other people's wellbeing?
- What's a criterion related to your own energy and sustainability?
- What's a criterion about timing?
- Is there a criterion you've been applying but haven't named?
- Which of your criteria are binary (pass/fail) vs. which are scalar (more/less)?
- Are there criteria that matter for the next quarter vs. criteria that matter for the next decade?
- Which criteria are about the option's risk of failure, and which are about its size of payoff if it succeeds?
- Is there a criterion that's actually a constraint (must-have), not a preference (nice-to-have)?
- How would you know, six months from now, that the criterion was satisfied?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_5_1_STRESS_TEST_QUESTIONS -->
- Are any criteria in tension with each other?
- If you had to drop a criterion, which would it be?
- Are you over-weighting a single criterion at the expense of others?
- Are these criteria from this version of you, or an older version?
- Would your criteria look different at a different point in your life?
- If your top-weighted criterion turned out to be empirically wrong (the option you thought maximized it actually didn't), how much of your decision-confidence collapses?
- Is there a criterion you're applying that you'd be embarrassed to defend in public?
- Which criterion would your most respected mentor say you should add — or remove?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_5_1_REFRAMING_QUESTIONS -->
- What's the criterion that would matter most to a friend evaluating from outside?
- What's the criterion you'd apply if someone you respected was the decision-maker?
- What's the criterion that doesn't sound right when said aloud but is operating anyway?
- What's the deepest criterion that the surface criteria are pointing at?
- If you reframed all the criteria as "what would I regret missing?", which jumps to the top?
- If you reframed them as "what would I be proud of?", does the ordering change?
- Are the criteria measuring the actual decision, or the felt-sense of the decision?
<!-- /SECTION -->

<!-- SECTION: PHASE_5_1_CLOSING_QUESTIONS -->
- Do these criteria capture what matters?
- Are they prioritized clearly enough that you could explain the priority order?
- Ready to choose how to apply them — which decision tool fits? (Binary Criteria Screen for disqualifying-style criteria; Weighted Decision Matrix for trade-off scoring; Pre-Mortem for risk-weighted stress testing.)
- Is there a criterion you've added in this conversation that wasn't on your list before — and how confident are you it's load-bearing?
- Of the criteria, which one will be the tiebreaker if two options score the same on the others?
- Have you named both the criteria that say "yes proceed" and the criteria that say "no stop"? Both directions matter.
<!-- /SECTION -->
